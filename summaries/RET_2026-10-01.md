[LEAD] Walton Centre has dropped out of NHS Oversight Framework segment 1 for the first time, moving to segment 2 in the Q1 2026/27 league tables, ranked 20th of 134 providers.
[Evidence] "Historically, The Walton Centre has maintained a Segment 1 position within the framework. However, following publication of Quarter 1 2026/27 data the Trust moved into Segment 2." Average metric score 1.57 (Q1 25/26), 1.58, 1.78, 1.79, then 2.05 (Q1 26/27). "No financial override was applied during this period." The worst-scoring metrics are named: clinical trials set up within 90 days (score 4.0), relative cost difference / National Cost Collection Index (3.4), temporary staffing cost band (3.0), sickness absence (3.01), flu vaccination (2.54); C. difficile and E. coli "both indicators currently attract scores of 4". p.110-113, [NOF Segmentation Report, Item 10]. CEO report: "The Walton Centre has been placed in segment 2, ranked 20 out of 134 organisations", p.24, [CEO Report, Item 5]. The paper also says the trust should not "become overly focused on improving segmentation performance alone", p.114.

[LEAD] Trust's own WRES report shows ethnic minority staff were 10 times more likely to enter formal disciplinary proceedings in 2025/26, and bullying from managers doubled to more than twice the national average; the paper calls the position "unacceptable" and says anti-racism work is "urgently required".
[Evidence] "The percentage of BME staff entering the formal disciplinary process was 2.35% compared to 0.23% for White staff; this equates to BME staff being 10.16 times more likely to enter the formal disciplinary process." (7 of 298 BME staff vs 3 of 1,297 white staff, p.161.) "21.3% of BME staff who responded to the Staff Survey reported experiencing at least one incident of harassment, bullying or abuse from managers. This is a significant increase from last year (10.5%) and significantly higher than the National average (8.58%)." Bullying from staff: 31.3% vs national average 20.02%. "Significant deterioration can be seen in many areas of the report". "The Trust recognises the inequalities in the experiences of our staff and note that this is unacceptable." "Focused anti-racism work is urgently required". p.154-158, [WRES 25.26, Item 13]. BPC key issues report flags it as an alert: "the latest surveys indicate a marked decline, especially for WRES ... This setback, albeit consistent with a national trend, warrants a deeper Board focus." p.63, [BPC Key Issues Report, Item 8]. Metric 9: BME board members "were non-executives and there is therefore a -18.52% percentage difference when comparing BME executive members of the Board to the overall workforce", p.157. WDES: "There are no disabled members of staff on the Trust Board", p.171.

[LEAD] Guardian of safe working says an estimated £15,000 to £20,000 of fines ringfenced for resident doctors has never been spent and the trust cannot confirm the balance; the statutory Guardian role has no deputy, no admin support and was uncovered during a long-term absence.
[Evidence] "an estimated £15,000 to £20,000 collected for resident doctors is unconfirmed and unspent, and the Forum has not been able to discharge its scrutiny role." (p.187) "Fine monies are ringfenced for resident doctors' education, training, rest facilities and wellbeing, so an unspent balance of this size is a benefit the Trust has collected but not passed on." "The statutory role has no deputy and was uncovered during long-term absence this year; and it has no specified administrative support". "The Trust holds no detriment survey and no external triangulation, so nil returns from six specialties cannot be tested." Fines: 14 hours across four occasions, all neurology registrars breaching the 5-hour overnight rest rule on non-resident on-call, driven by "the volume of overnight calls generated by the thrombectomy pathway"; £793.52 paid to doctors, £1,322.44 to the fund; no breach since December 2025 after stroke nurse specialists took the overnight calls. "Partial assurance is offered for 2025/26." p.181-188, [Guardian of Safe Working Hours Annual Assurance Report 2025-26, Item 15].

[LEAD] Trust's mortality surveillance group did not meet for two months, leaving five deaths unreviewed at quarter end; chief medical officer offers only partial assurance on learning from deaths.
[Evidence] "The Mortality Surveillance Group did not meet in May or June 2026; both meetings were cancelled at short notice because of workload pressures and annual leave. The consequence was a backlog in the completion and presentation of initial mortality reviews, and five reviews remained outstanding on 30 June 2026." "Acceptable assurance should not be offered until the backlog is cleared and compliance with the eight-week review standard is restored." 24 inpatient deaths in Q1; for one elective death (arachnoid cyst fenestration, inquest pending) "MSG identified slight evidence of avoidability"; two deaths escalated to PSIRG and a PSII commissioned for a death from wound infection after earlier elective surgery. p.117-132, [Learning from Deaths Report Q1 2026/27, Item 11].
