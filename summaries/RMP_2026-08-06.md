# Tameside and Glossop Integrated Care FT / Stockport FT — Joint Board (South East Sector Healthcare Partnership) — 6 August 2026

**This is a SHARED JOINT pack covering BOTH trusts.** Tameside and Glossop Integrated Care NHS FT (TG ICFT / RMP) and Stockport NHS FT (SFT / RWJ) run a single Joint Board under the South East Sector Healthcare Partnership (SESHP), with one chair (David Wakefield), one chief executive (Karen James) and one chief finance officer (John Graham) across both organisations. Findings below state which trust they relate to where the pack distinguishes them.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Both Stockport and Tameside & Glossop have told NHS England they cannot confirm compliance with two of the five mortuary assurance statements demanded of every provider board after the final Ockenden report into Nottingham — including that they have not reviewed progress against the Fuller Inquiry recommendations through a board process.
[Evidence] "The organisation has completed the Board Assurance Statements, as required by NHSE, with non-compliance for both organisations in 2 of the 5 Assurance Statements" — p.179, [Board Briefing: Mortuary and After-Death Care Learning from the Ockenden Review (2026), Item 16.2]. TG ICFT's signed return answers "No" to the roles-and-responsibilities statement, adding: "Whilst there is not an up-to-date Mortuary Policy or SOP that describes roles and responsibilities… roles and responsibilities have not been formally tested on a regular basis" — p.180. Stockport's return also answers "No" twice, stating "A full review will be completed against the 29 recommendations of the Fuller inquiry (21 of which are relevant to Trusts) by the end of September 2026" — p.183. Returns were signed off by Quality Committee, not the board, to meet NHSE's 31 July 2026 deadline: "The Quality Committee approved the submission on behalf of the Board to achieve the deadline of the 31.07.26" — p.179.

[LEAD] Stockport's paediatric audiology service remains suspended, with no agreed date to restart: the trust has pushed "exit criteria" for a safe resumption out to Q3 2026/27, having told the June board that an August reopening was already at risk. Two corporate risks relating to it are scored at the maximum-but-one level of 20.
[Evidence] "SFT: Paediatric Audiology Service paused - Agree exit criteria for Paediatric Audiology Service to safely resume — Chief Nurse — Q3 2026/27" — p.210, [Board Assurance Framework 2026/27, Principal Risk 1.4, Item 19]. Corporate risk register: "3018 Surgery There is a risk to patients in the Stockport locality due to the pause of the paediatric audiology service — 4 5 20" and "2650 Surgery Risk of harm to paediatric patients if the audiology service does not comply with best practice recommendations — 4 5 20" — p.225, [Appendix 3: SFT Significant Risks]. June minutes: "The SFT Chief Operating Officer advised that the August target date was at risk, noting that the reopening would only proceed if it could be achieved safely" — p.10, [Minutes 4 June 2026, item 14/26].

[LEAD] Both trusts would sit in NHS Oversight Framework segment 2 were it not for their deficits, the joint board was told — and their board assurance framework score for delivering the 2026/27 financial plan doubled from 8 to 16 in the first quarter of the year.
[Evidence] "The SFT Chief Operating Officer confirmed that under the NHS Oversight Framework, both Trusts would have been placed in Segment 2 were it not for their financial deficits" — p.8, [Minutes 4 June 2026, item 10/26]. BAF principal risk 6.1 ("If we fail to deliver the annual revenue and capital financial plans, there is a risk regulatory intervention increases"): Q4 2025/26 score 8 (4x2) at both trusts, rising to 16 (4x4) at both at Q1 2026/27 — p.204 and p.220, [BAF 2026/27]. Risk 6.2, medium-term financial sustainability, has been at 16 (4x4) at both trusts since Q4 2025/26 — p.205.

[LEAD] Neither trust has identified enough savings to hit its 2026/27 efficiency target: Stockport has schemes worth £17.5m risk-adjusted against a £26.8m target, with delivery forecast at £11.8m non-recurrent, and Tameside £11.9m risk-adjusted against £20.6m.
[Evidence] "The total efficiency (STEP) target for the year is £26.8m. At Month 03 the Trust total value of schemes identified are now £23.3m (87%), risk adjusted down to £17.5m (65%). Delivery in year is forecast £11.8m (44%) non-recurrent, which also adds pressure to the underlying financial position." / "The total efficiency (TEP) target for the year is £20.6m. As at the end of June, schemes totalling £17.7m (£11.9m when risk adjusted) were in various phases of development vs the annual plan of £20.6m." — p.72, [Finance Report as at Month 3 2026/27, Item 11]. The Joint Finance & Performance Committee formally alerted the board to "Continued significant risk to delivery of the 2026/27 financial plans across SFT and TGICFT due to the scale, phasing and deliverability of the Cost Improvement Programmes (CIP)… albeit with a substantial proportion of savings remaining backloaded, particularly at TGICFT, and limited quantified mitigations identified should planned savings not materialise" — p.34–35, [Joint Finance & Performance Committee AAA Report, 18 June and 23 July 2026].

[LEAD] Corridor care and 24-hour ED waits continue at scale across both trusts in June, with Tameside recording 172 patients given corridor care for more than 45 minutes and 292 patients in its emergency department for more than 24 hours — both up on May.
[Evidence] Tameside: "Corridor Care (>45-minute) and the number of patients in ED for more than 24hrs both deteriorated marginally month-on-month, increasing to 172 and 292 respectively (from 158 and 289)… As Corridor Care reporting commenced in Mar-26, earlier data is not included but in April the figure was 541." — p.57, [Integrated Performance Report, Operations | UEC, Escalation and Crowding, Item 10]. Stockport: "A total of 155 patients remained in the Emergency Department for over 24 hours, while 84 patients received corridor care for more than 45 minutes during the reporting period… Sustained use of corridor care continues to impact patient experience, privacy, dignity and operational resilience" — p.56. Tameside four-hour performance was 62.81%, "in the bottom quartile nationally" (p.55); Stockport 68.1% (p.54).

[LEAD] Stockport is running at nearly double its target for patients with no criteria to reside — 85 a day against a target of 45 — and the joint board has been told it cannot say when this will improve.
[Evidence] "the average number of patients with No Criteria to Reside (NCTR) remaining above target at 85 patients per day compared with the target of 45" — p.60, [Integrated Performance Report, Operations | Patient Flow & Discharge, Item 10]. June minutes: "The SFT Chief Operating Officer advised that it was not yet possible to provide a definitive timeframe" for improvement on NCTR — p.11, [Minutes 4 June 2026, item 14/26].

[LEAD] Tameside & Glossop recorded 11 deaths in 2025/26 where care was judged more likely than not to have contributed to the outcome — down from 13 the year before, but described in the trust's own annual report as "an unacceptable figure".
[Evidence] "Whilst the figures represent an improvement on 2024/25, when 13 deaths (2%) were judged to be deaths where care was judged more likely than not to have contributed to the outcome, focus on learning from deaths remains a priority as this remains an unacceptable figure." — p.163, [Learning from Deaths Joint Annual Report 2025/26, Item 14]. New 2025/26 themes at TG ICFT include "sepsis recognition and screening at triage… and Emergency Department capacity affecting ownership and decision-making" — p.165. Stockport reviewed 35% of its 1,369 hospital deaths and recorded three initial ratings where care likely contributed — p.160.

[LEAD] Both trusts are carrying a board assurance framework risk on their estate scored at 20 — the highest score on the BAF — and have written into the framework that the target score cannot be reached for up to a decade.
[Evidence] Principal risk 7.3, estate fit for purpose: score "20 (5x4)" at both SFT and TG ICFT at Q4 2025/26 and Q1 2026/27, with the note: "The target score will not be realised in year as to achieve this will require significant investment over 5-10 years." — p.223, [BAF 2026/27]. Gap in control recorded as "Inability to deliver required levels of estates backlog maintenance due to lack of funding" — p.223. Stockport corporate risk register carries "586 Estates & Facilities There is a risk of deterioration of the hospital site due to a significant increase in Estate Backlog Maintenance — 4 5 20" and "2596 Corporate – IT There is a risk of total failure of the cooling in the Beech House Data Centre — 5 4 20" — p.225.

[WORTH WATCHING] Tameside & Glossop is in an unresolved contract dispute with Derby and Derbyshire ICB that has been escalated to NHS England, and the trust's own income assumptions depend on the outcome.
[Evidence] "income assumption from Derbyshire ICB the Trust is still awaiting the outcome of the contract dispute process from NHSE" — p.76, [Finance Report Month 3 2026/27, Financial Position – Key Messages, Tameside]. BAF gap in control against risk 6.1 lists the required action "Conclude escalation process with Derby & Derbyshire ICB — Chief Finance Officer — Q3 2026/27" — p.219, [BAF 2026/27].

[WORTH WATCHING] Reported bullying and harassment incidents at Tameside & Glossop rose 54% in a year, to 210, with staff-to-staff harassment reports up 62% and a new sexual harassment category recording nine incidents in its first year.
[Evidence] "There was a total of 210 incidents reported using this cause group in 2025/26… There has been a new subcategory added (Harassment – Sexual Harassment) to which nine incidents have been reported. There has been a 38% increase in incidents related to the category of 'Harassment – Patient to Staff' and a 62% increase of incidents within the category of 'Harassment - Staff to Staff' when compared to 2024/25. Although the total number of bullying and harassment incidents in 2025/26 (210) is higher than it was in 2024/25 (136)…" — p.316, [TG ICFT Health and Safety Annual Report 2025/26, Item 21]. [WORTH WATCHING] The joint EPR programme has no data sharing agreement between the two trusts, no independent assurance, and its delivery partner Altera has told them the approved resourcing is not enough.
[Evidence] "Approved resourcing is insufficient to deliver the programme within prescribed timeframes (R025), including SFT development/integration team resourcing pending approval at Staffing Approval Group. This gap has been raised by delivery partner (Altera)." / "No formal inter-organisational Data Sharing Agreement in place between SFT and TG ICFT, needed to support EPR design, testing and deployment activity. — Finalise and execute a Data Sharing Agreement between the two Trusts — EPR Programme Director — Immediate" / "No independent (Level 3) assurance in place for the EPR Programme." — p.222, [BAF 2026/27, new Principal Risk 7.2].

[WORTH WATCHING] Tameside & Glossop has been identified as a national outlier for delirium recognition and management in the National Audit of Dementia.

In response, new delirium pathways, training resources and learning events were introduced in July 2025" — p.239, [TG ICFT Integrated Safeguarding Annual Report 2025/26, Item 20.2]; repeated at p.244: "the Trust was previously identified as an outlier for delirium".
