# Norfolk and Suffolk ICB board — 15 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.norfolkandsuffolk.icb.nhs.uk/about/governance/board-meetings/
- **Pack:** 1 file (304pp combined agenda and reports)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-09T00:00:00Z

Context: this is the newly merged Norfolk and Suffolk ICB (formed 1 April 2026 from Norfolk & Waveney ICB and Suffolk & North East Essex ICB); this is one of its first board meetings. CEO Ed Garratt; Chair Will Pope. £4.59bn commissioning budget.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Staff survey slide pack labelled internal-only ("for EMT and RemCom only due to sensitivity") was nonetheless published in the public board pack — and shows the legacy Norfolk & Waveney ICB's staff engagement now the worst in its benchmark group on multiple People Promise measures.
[Evidence] "This slide pack is intended for EMT and RemCom only due to sensitivity." — p.245, [NHS Staff Survey 2025, Item 16]. Same pack: "SNEE staff engagement score was 6.83 and NW 5.96 – the comparable average was 6.28." — p.244

[LEAD] The former Norfolk & Waveney ICB's scores fell below benchmark median across the board and its "We are always learning" score (4.75) was its lowest single result, with morale showing the "sharpest decline of any of the People Promise elements."
[Evidence] "the score of 4.75 does reflect the lowest individual score for Norfolk and Waveney ICB in the 2025 Staff Survey." — p.254; "the Norfolk and Waveney score showing the sharpest decline of any of the People Promise elements from this year's results" (Morale) — p.257, [NHS Staff Survey 2025, Item 16]

[LEAD] The ICB's balanced 2026/27 plan is contingent on delivering a £101.9m efficiency/CIP programme, of which delivery is rated Amber with a c.£7.2m unforecast shortfall risk already flagged at Month 2.
[Evidence] "The total savings target for 2026/27 is £101.9m... There is a potential shortfall of c£7.2m not yet reflected in the forecast driven by underperformance in a small number of programmes." — p.273, [Summary Finance Report Month 02, Item 18]. Two programmes rated red: "Clinical Threshold Policies continues with a high delivery risk, with potential for no savings." — p.273

[LEAD] The ICB's own risk register carries 32 live risks, five scored at the top level (20) — including Elective Recovery, the RTT objective and the diagnostics 6-week standard — reflecting a new organisation entering the year with elective performance as its dominant risk.
[Evidence] "5 have a mitigated score of 20"; risks at 20 listed as "TB Service Resilience and Capacity... Neuro-Developmental Service (NDS) Community Paediatrics... Elective Recovery... RTT (Referral to Treatment) Objective... Diagnostics 6-Week Standard" — pp.288–289, [Risk Management and Board Assurance Framework, Item 21]

[LEAD] The ICB's Mental Health Strategic Plan confirms plans, with NSFT, to design and implement two new Mental Health Emergency Departments in Norwich and Ipswich, and to recommission the c.£6m crisis-pathway estate.
[Evidence] "we will work in partnership with NSFT and other system partners to design and implement two new Mental Health Emergency Departments in Norwich and Ipswich"; "This encompasses about £6m of ICB expenditure, encompassing existing crisis hubs/sanctuary houses, steam cafes and community connectors." — p.218, [Strategic Plan for Mental Health 2026-2031, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] The new ICB has concluded it lacks the capacity to lead a mental health collaborative and is stepping back from standing, ICB-led collaboratives — an explicit admission of reduced commissioning bandwidth after the merger and running-cost cuts.
[Evidence] "there should not in future be permanent, ICB-led mental health collaboratives"; driven by "the very significant reduction in ICB staffing levels" — pp.228, 224, [Strategic Plan for Mental Health 2026-2031, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board minutes record the ICB has commissioned external support to develop proposals on elective waiting-list growth after "poor performance on change in the size of waiting list in elective care" was identified as a two-year priority.
[Evidence] "noted the poor performance on change in the size of waiting list in elective care. This was an area that had been identified as a priority over the next two years and the ICB had commissioned external support to help develop proposals" — p.30, [Minutes of 20 May 2026 meeting, Item 6]

[WORTH WATCHING] A Community Stroke Services business case approved by the Strategic Commissioning Committee is projected to release over 7,000 hospital bed days by shifting care closer to home, and is being held up internally as the exemplar for the ICB's new commissioning model.
[Evidence] "would provide a significant return on investment by releasing over 7,000 hospital bed days by delivering care closer to home... an exemplar of the new strategic commissioning approach" — p.279, [Committee Highlight Reports, Item 19]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB flags an unresolved 2026/27 contract dispute with Cambridge University Hospitals FT, now escalated to NHS England, plus ADHD/ASD diagnosis and treatment costs as its most significant activity risk.
[Evidence] "There is still a risk with the contract dispute with CUHFT for 2026/27 now escalated to NHS England for determination"; "The most significant risk area remains in ADHD and ASD diagnosis and treatment costs" — p.272, [Summary Finance Report Month 02, Item 18]

[FOI] Board considered the Lord Mann Review into antisemitism and racism in the NHS (all 36 recommendations accepted by Government 4 June 2026); the pack summarises but does not disclose the ICB's own WRES/WDES data or its Remuneration Committee's discussion of leadership accountability.
[Evidence] "Jewish staff experience 'routine ostracism' and that discrimination against this group has been increasing"; Remuneration Committee action "to include clear indicators on regular Staff Matters reporting to highlight any cases linked to racism, non-compliance or poor leadership behaviour" — pp.238, 240, [Lord Mann Review, Item 15]
