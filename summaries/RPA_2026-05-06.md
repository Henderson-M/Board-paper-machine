## Top lines

[LEAD] Medway closed 2025/26 with a £54.9m deficit, £50m worse than its £4.9m-deficit plan, after delivering only £10.8m of a £45.4m efficiency target.
[Evidence] "The Trust has reported a year-end deficit of £54.9m, which is £50m adverse to the originally agreed Plan of £4.9m deficit for 2025/26… During the year, the Trust delivered £10.8m of efficiencies." — p.83, [Finance Report Month 12, Conclusions]; "The Trust set a £45.4m efficiency plan for FY26 but only delivered £10.8m." — p.76, [Finance Report Month 12]

[LEAD] The trust drew £48.4m of cash support / borrowing from NHSE in Q4, which permanently raises its cost base through 3.5% interest on public dividend capital.
[Evidence] "Total cash support/borrowing is therefore £48.4m… Cash support (unlike DSF) increases our Public Dividend Capital (PDC) and thus attracts interest at 3.5%; this increases the Trust cost base in perpetuity (higher PDC dividend payments)." — p.76, [Finance Report Month 12]; applications of £30.1m (Jan), £8.7m (Feb), £9.6m approved for March.

[LEAD] PA Consulting has been engaged to support the trust's financial recovery alongside a System Recovery Director, with the underlying deficit (stripping out Deficit Support Funding) put at £79.6m.
[Evidence] "Without immediate and concerted action the Trust will be at risk of non-delivery of its financial plans in 2026/27… This work is being supported by PA Consulting and the System Recovery Director." — p.73/74, [Finance Report Month 12 slides]; "After removing the non-recurrent Deficit Support Funding (DSF), this creates an underlying £79.6m deficit (average £6.6m deficit per month)." — p.81, [Finance Report Month 12]

[LEAD] Medway has entered NHSE's National Provider Improvement Programme after rating itself red against the Oversight Framework; the NPIP assessment phase runs to early summer.
[Evidence] "The Board rated itself red in recognition of the long-standing challenges… we welcome the support of the National Provider Improvement Programme (NPIP), NHS England's intensive improvement programme, which replaced the Recovery Support Programme (RSP). The NPIP team has been working with us since March and is now moving into its assessment phase, which will run through to early summer." — p.14, [Chief Executive's report: May 2026]

[LEAD] A wrong-site surgery never event is under investigation, with the Quality Assurance Committee warning against "normalisation" of never events.
[Evidence] "The Committee received an initial report on a recent 'never event' involving wrong site surgery. This is going through the escalation and investigation process, noting contributing factors such as visibility, surgeon position, and checklist information. The Committee discussed the need for robust assurance on checklist use and avoidance of normalisation of Never Events." — p.145, [Quality Assurance Committee chair's report]

[LEAD] The trust's SHMI is the highest it has ever recorded and is rated its single highest-scoring extreme risk; three deaths in March were declared as patient safety incident investigations.
[Evidence] "SHMI remains higher than expected and is currently at the highest value seen by the Trust" — p.49, [IQPR]; "SHMI for the period of December 2024–November 2025 is 1.30 and higher than expected"; "Three preventable deaths were identified in March 2026 — all three have been declared PSIIs." — p.38, [IQPR]; Risk 1684 "Risk of elevated SHMI mortality indicator" carries a current rating of 20 against a target of 4. — p.132, [Trust Risk Register]

[LEAD] Two executive directors — the Chief Medical Officer and Interim Chief Operating Officer — are leaving at the end of June, with four substantive executive posts to be filled by an as-yet-unappointed permanent CEO.
[Evidence] "I would like to express my sincere thanks to Alison Davis, Chief Medical Officer, and Frances Woodroffe, Interim Chief Operating Officer, who will be leaving us at the end of June… the new Chief Executive will lead the recruitment to key substantive leadership roles, including Chief Medical Officer, Chief Operating Officer, Chief Finance Officer and Chief People Officer." — p.12, [Chief Executive's report]. [INFERENCE] This leaves the trust recruiting four substantive board-level executives simultaneously.

[LEAD] The trust is submitting a 2026/27 breakeven plan requiring £39m of efficiency savings and "difficult decisions across the organisation".
[Evidence] "For 2026/27, we have submitted a breakeven plan requiring £39 million of efficiency savings, which will involve difficult decisions across the organisation." — p.13, [Chief Executive's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Corridor care is running at roughly 40 patients a day in the emergency department and is one of the trust's named extreme risks.
[Evidence] "Corridor care has continued to be in use with lodging averaging 40 patients per day (approx.). This is not a sustainable model of care" — p.61, [IQPR]; Risk 2672 "Risk of patient harm due to demand exceeding organisational capacity (provision of care in corridors)" rated 15. — p.132, [Trust Risk Register]. CEO report notes a new national 45-minute corridor-care definition and daily reporting from March (p.12).

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust took over the local Urgent Treatment Centre from 1 September 2026 as an "emergency contract award", with a flagged risk of proceeding "without finalised financials".
[Evidence] "MFT has received formal notification of UTC takeover from 1st September 2026 as an Emergency contract award" — p.23, [Pre-72 Front Door UEC Programme highlight report]; "Risk of proceeding with the UTC takeover by 1 September without finalised financials, with a potential cost deficit at delivery" — p.23.

[WORTH WATCHING] Neurology consultant numbers have halved from four to two, flagged as a patient-safety concern and an overdue extreme issue.
[Evidence] "Highlighted Risk ID 2666, the reduction in neurology consultants from 4 to 2, raising concerns about staff pressure and patient safety" — p.148, [People Committee report]; logged as "Critical Depletion of Neurology Consultant Workforce Impacting Service Capacity and Patient Care", action status Red. — p.135, [Trust Risk Register].

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff survey results fell across every "people promise" area alongside a 7% drop in the response rate.
[Evidence] "A decline in staff survey response rate of 7% and scores across all people promise areas. Have faced challenges from organisational instability and national pressures." — p.148, [People Committee report]

[WORTH WATCHING] ED was hit by a meningitis outbreak and a major incident in March, with attendances up around 20%.
[Evidence] "UEC performance was strong in March, but adversely impacted by the meningitis outbreak, with 20% daily increases in attendances, and the need to reconfigure the bed-base rapidly" — p.38, [IQPR]; "Performance continued to improve despite a major incident and increased attendances. 17,789 attendances." — p.61, [IQPR]. Note: the CEO report separately attributes pressure to "a now-resolved norovirus outbreak in one area" (p.12) — two distinct outbreak references in the pack.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has £40.2m of capital bids in for UEC and diagnostics plus £18.7m to the Estates Safety Fund, partly to replace "end of life" scanners and address fire compartmentalisation.
[Evidence] "The Trust has submitted bids under these headlines amounting to £40.2m… Additional bids have also been submitted to the Estates Safety Fund equating to £18.7m for fire compartmentalisation and AHU schemes… supported by NHSE and awaiting treasury approval." — p.140, [External Capital Bids: Update Paper]; CT/MRI bids are to "replace end of life scanners" (p.142).

[WORTH WATCHING] Of 103 approved risks, eight are extreme and 14 carry actions overdue by more than six months; some live risks date back to 2019.
[Evidence] "Risk Profile (103 approved risks): 8 Extreme, 67 High… 14 risks have actions overdue by more than 6 months… 7 risks and 33 issues are older than 3 years." — p.126–127, [Trust Risk Register and Issues Log]; oldest live risk: "1376 28/11/2019 Risk of Falls from Height in Multi-Level Car Park". — p.136.

## FOI leads

[FOI] Independent financial "deep dive" by Simon Worthington — request the report. The Finance, Planning and Performance Committee discussed "Simon Worthington's deep dive review" but the pack only records that findings "have been acknowledged" without summarising them. Request from Medway FT (and/or NHSE Kent & Medway) the deep-dive terms of reference, the full report, and any associated action plan.
[Evidence] "Deep Dive Report - from Simon Worthington's deep dive review - The Committee was assured that the findings of the independent deep dive have been acknowledged, that arrangements are being put in place to strengthen grip and control." — p.151, [Finance, Planning and Performance Committee report]

[FOI] Provider Licence undertakings on governance — request the undertakings. Multiple papers reference existing enforcement undertakings on Medway's provider licence specifically relating to governance structure, requiring completion by end of Q1 2026/27, but the substance is not set out. Request from NHSE the text of the licence undertakings and the trust's compliance plan.
[Evidence] "There are existing undertakings on Medway Foundation Trust's Provider licence that specifically relate to the governance structure of the Trust… which require completion by the end of Quarter one in the 2026/27 period." — p.86 [Risk Management Strategy] and p.153 [Annual review of Committee Terms of Reference]

[FOI] Wrong-site surgery never event — request the investigation report. The QAC has "requested further information on the Never Event action plan and learning"; the pack discloses only contributing factors. Request from Medway FT the patient safety incident investigation report (redacted for patient data) and action plan.
[Evidence] "an initial report on a recent 'never event' involving wrong site surgery… The Committee has requested further information on the Never Event action plan and learning." — p.145, [Quality Assurance Committee chair's report]

[FOI] Coroner concerns on mortality — request the correspondence. QAC noted "Coroner's concerns about staff attitudes and tick-box documentation" in relation to preventable deaths, but does not quote or date the coroner material. Request from Medway FT any Regulation 28 / prevention of future deaths reports received and the trust's responses.
[Evidence] "Coroner's concerns about staff attitudes and tick-box documentation were highlighted." — p.144–145, [Quality Assurance Committee chair's report]
