## Top lines

[LEAD] The trust's Orbis programme is rated red with no agreed go-live date after a fourth delay to data migration, and has been escalated to NHS England.
[Evidence] "The Orbis programme remains rated red (RAG), with no agreed go-live date due to a fourth delay in the delivery of the required data migration functionality. While a third of the identified gaps have now been resolved, significant concerns remain regarding several outstanding major gaps. Escalation to NHSE has resulted in senior-level recognition of the issues and challenges associated with Orbis. A comprehensive options appraisal will be presented to SLT and F&R in October and November." Same entry records that the committee "welcomed the review to be undertaken by Internal Audit" — p.297, [Finance and Resource Committee Assurance Report, 3 September 2026, Enc. 14b]

[LEAD] The adult acute bed base is down 14 beds to 50, described in the pack as the lowest level of available beds since September 2020, with nine inappropriate out-of-area placements at month end.
[Evidence] "The adult acute bed base is reduced due to Project Chrysalis, with 50 beds operational, a reduction of 14 beds. This represents the lowest level of available beds since September 2020." Same page: "At the end of M04, there were 9 reportable inappropriate OAPs attributable to adult acute bed capacity constraints and flow issues" and "At the end of M04, 5 patients who were clinically ready for discharge (CRFD) remained within adult acute inpatient services. As a result, inpatient wards are operating at capacity with CRFD patients blocking new admissions" — p.238, [IQPR M04, Enc. 12]. PIP data records 17 inappropriate OAPs during the month against a target of 2 — p.232

[LEAD] The trust is not adopting the IHRA definition of antisemitism outright, despite an NHS England requirement to confirm its position by 31 July 2026, and its board paper cites expert criticism of the Lord Mann Review's implementation.
[Evidence] Status against the NHSE requirement "Adoption of IHRA definition of antisemitism" is recorded as "Under review" — p.87, [Lord Mann Review Board Briefing, Enc. 6, Appendix 1]. The paper states: "The Trust's approach has been to consider the IHRA working definition of antisemitism alongside other recognised definitions and guidance where appropriate, on a case-by-case basis" — p.83. And: "EDI Advocates (e.g., Roger Kline's, BMJ) have argued that whilst antisemitism is real and serious and must be tackled robustly, there is a risk that implementation of the Mann Review recommendations in their current form, could unintentionally create a perceived hierarchy of discrimination if antisemitism is addressed separately from wider anti-racism work" — p.84. The board is asked to approve the Race and Health Observatory Seven Anti-Racism Principles but only to "continue to review its position" on the Mann recommendations — p.80

[LEAD] An Ofsted inspection has raised concerns about controlled drug administration overnight at the trust's children's respite service, and the board is being asked to fund a second night nurse at £144k a year.
[Evidence] "A recent OFSTED inspection has also identified concerns associated with the administration of controlled medications during the night shift, which require 2 registered nurses to administer and witness in order to comply with the Trusts policy for the Safe administration of Controlled Drugs" — p.101, [Safer Staffing 6 Monthly Review, Enc. 7]. "Failure to increase overnight Registered Nurse capacity presents ongoing risks to patient safety, medication governance, regulatory compliance and service resilience" — p.101. Senior leaders have proposed limiting the uplift: "SLT made a proposal to support option 3, however on a fixed term basis for 6 months" — p.103. The appendix records the review was triggered "due to challenges associated with staffing fill rate and medications management for children during night shifts" — p.112

[LEAD] Race equality data shows recruitment disparity widening sharply and the disciplinary indicator rising more than tenfold in a year.
[Evidence] "This means White applicants were 1.62 times more likely to be appointed from shortlisting than Global Majority applicants" (up from 1.23 in 2025). "Global Majority applicants represented 48.9% of all applications... However, their representation fell to 27.8% of shortlisted candidates and 19.1% of appointments" — p.209, [WRES Report 2026, Enc. 11]. "The Trust's disciplinary indicator increased from 0.41 in 2025 to 5.38 in 2026, indicating that Global Majority staff were over five times more likely than White staff to enter a formal disciplinary process during the reporting period." The paper notes formal cases fell from 17 to 6 and cautions the measure is volatile at small numbers — p.210

[LEAD] The trust's forecast £154k surplus depends entirely on mitigations including balance-sheet flexibilities and a PFI unavailability claim; the unmitigated forecast is a £0.5m deficit.
[Evidence] "The Trust's month 4 forecast submission to NHSE reflects a surplus of £154k, in line with the agreed plan. However, the current underlying forecast indicates an unmitigated deficit of £0.5m... the submitted position is dependent upon the successful delivery of identified mitigations and financial flexibilities... a residual delivery risk remains." Also: "15% (£1.2m) of the CIP target is classified as high risk" — p.277; bank expenditure £2,063k against £1,684k plan, an adverse variance of £379k — p.276
