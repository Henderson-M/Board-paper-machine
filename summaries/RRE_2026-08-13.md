## Top lines

[LEAD] MPFT has been invited by NHS England into the assessment process to become one of the next Advanced Foundation Trusts.
[Evidence] "Earlier this month we announced that MPFT has been invited by NHS England to enter the assessment process to become one of the next Advanced Foundation Trusts. This places us amongst a small number of organisations nationally recognised as being ready to demonstrate the leadership, governance and organisational maturity required to operate with greater autonomy within the NHS." — p.27, [Chief executive's corporate report, Enc. C]

[LEAD] The trust is telling NHSE it will break even while its own internal forecast is a £6.4m deficit, with the gap closed by high-risk savings and contingency releases.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported (internally) an unmitigated adjusted deficit of £6.4m. Recognising downside and upside risks, the Trust is continuing to report to NHSE that the planned break-even position will be delivered at the financial year end... To deliver the breakeven forecast reported to NHSE, the Trust is assuming the delivery of £3.8m high risk transformation savings (estates rationalisation, medicines management, stretch procurement opportunities, and securing additional ICB funding for service growth), and the release of £2.6m additional non-recurrent contingencies throughout the financial year." — p.122, [Financial Performance 2026/27 – M3 (June 2026), Enc. M]

[LEAD] Part of the £30.8m efficiency target is being met by re-labelling existing underspends — including pay vacancies — as savings.
[Evidence] "The Trust continues to report to NHSE a forecast outturn saving of £30.8m in line with the efficiency target. This is through assuming the delivery of further high-risk transformation savings - estates rationalisation (£0.5m), medicines management (£0.5m), stretch procurement opportunities (£0.5m), securing additional ICB funding for service growth (£2.3m), and re-categorising other existing underspends (e.g. pay vacancies) against the efficiency target." — p.123, [Financial Performance 2026/27 – M3, Enc. M]

[LEAD] The Reach Out forensic provider collaborative's long-term financial viability is in question, with 71 patients placed outside the collaborative and NHSE control of forensic beds blocking repatriation.
[Evidence] "We are in ongoing discussions with the partners in the Reach Out Provider collaborative about the long-term future. Increased demand combined with the limited bed capacity and length of stay of patients has led to increased out of area placements (currently 33 outside of the West Midlands with a further 38 at either BSMHFT or St Andrews). This has called into the question the long-term financial viability and although measures are being taken to increase flow with greater community support it is unclear if this will be enough to reduce costs by the required amount. There are ongoing discussions with NHSE as they are responsible for bed management across the Forensic estates, this limits our ability to repatriate patients." — p.96, [Specialist Care Group report, Enc. L]

[Evidence] "IBPC recommend Trust Board to approve the tender submission for Essex Talking Therapies (NHS TT) and Psychological Therapies for severe mental health problems (PTSMHP) services, which is within delegated authority limits for Board approval (contract income £31M+ per annum, 3+2 years)." — p.142, [Integrated Business and Performance Committee Highlight Report – July 2026, Enc. [WORTH WATCHING] Freedom to speak up: staff confidence that the trust will act on broader concerns has fallen to 64%, and two-thirds of those coming to the guardian had already raised the issue with a manager and seen nothing change.
[Evidence] "Staff confidence in raising concerns about unsafe clinical practice remains high at 80.3%, while 74.5% feel safe to raise broader organisational concerns... However, confidence that the organisation will act on broader concerns has reduced to 64%, and in the latest quarter around two-thirds of those approaching FTSU reported that they had previously raised the matter with their manager and felt that nothing had changed. Reported experience of disadvantage or detriment following speaking up also requires continued oversight." — p.64, [Freedom to Speak Up Assurance Report, Enc. G]

We have also confirmed that Claire Bailey will lead both the Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent and Children and Families Care Groups for the next 12 months, strengthening collaboration between the two care groups during a period of significant change... Alex Brett has made an outstanding contribution to MPFT over many years, leading our people agenda... Mark Large has likewise played a significant role... through his two terms as a Non-Executive Director." — pp.27-28, [Chief executive's corporate report, Enc. C]
