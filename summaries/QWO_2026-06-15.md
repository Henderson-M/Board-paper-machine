# West Yorkshire ICB board — 15 June 2026

- **Source:** https://www.westyorkshire.icb.nhs.uk/meetings/integrated-care-board/nhs-west-yorkshire-icb-15-june-2026/
- **Pack:** 2 files (agenda + Annual Report and Accounts 2025/26)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-11

> Pack composition: the 15 June meeting is a 30-minute virtual sitting whose sole business is approval of the 2025/26 Annual Report and Accounts. All material below is sourced from that combined 232-page document. Jonathan Webb (Interim CE since 16 April 2026), Ian Currell (Interim Director of Finance) and Jane Madeley (Audit and Risk Committee Chair) are the lead presenters.

## Top lines

[LEAD] West Yorkshire ICB has identified £14.337m of incorrect Out-of-Hours payments made to Wakefield Personal Medical Services GP practices between 2016/17 and 2024/25, and is "exploring options to recover the outstanding amounts".
[Evidence] "The ICB has identified that certain deductions required for GP out of hours payments were not processed correctly for the Wakefield Personal Medical Services GP Practices. This has resulted in £14,337k of payments made incorrectly to Practices between 2016/17 and 2024/25. This represents approximately 0.3% of spend with all West Yorkshire GP practices. The ICB was established in July 2022, and the error was identified in 2024. Deductions were made correctly in line with national guidelines in 2025/26 and onwards. The ICB is exploring options to recover the outstanding amounts." — p.123, [Annual Report, Control Issues]. Two internal audit reports — a Wakefield Out-of-Hours Fact Finding Review (low assurance) and a still-draft Wakefield Out-of-Hours Payment Investigation (limited assurance) — landed during the year. The investigation found "lack of timely escalation of a material overpayment and potential loss to the Board due to the issue initially being incorrectly identified as a budget variation rather than a significant overpayment or potential loss. This led to a missed opportunity to seek to recover retrospective overpayments during 2025/26." — p.135.

[LEAD] The ICB agreed 294 exit packages costing £17.79m in 2025/26 — 290 of them voluntary redundancies/early retirements — driving total termination benefits charged to the income statement to £17.79m vs zero the prior year and pushing staff costs from £79.1m to £101.5m.
[Evidence] "TOTALS 4 46,194 290 17,744,541 294 17,790,735" — p.163, [Annual Report, Exit packages]. Band detail: 80 packages of £50,001–100,000 totalling £5.83m; 45 packages of £100,001–150,000 totalling £5.46m; 18 packages of £150,001–200,000 totalling £2.85m; nil above £200,000. Staff costs jumped: "Termination benefits 17,791" (£'000) vs "—" the prior year — p.156, [Staff numbers and costs].

[LEAD] Three of four Director of Nursing post-holders are exiting the ICB between February and April 2026 — a serious-rated (16) board risk warning the gap could leave the ICB unable to meet its statutory safeguarding duties and unable to attend West Yorkshire's statutory safeguarding boards.
[Evidence] "There is a risk of insufficient strategic leadership at Director level to meet ICB statutory requirements for quality and safeguarding before new structures are enacted. Due to the Interim Executive Director of Nursing of the ICB arrangements coming to an end in April 2026. Three out of four current Director of Nursing post-holders are exiting the organisation due to retirement, secondment ceasing and a new job between February and April 2026. This could result in the inability to enact risk responses and escalation as per the National Quality Boards requirements for ICBs, maintain SEND statutory duties and inspection requirements, and the ICB not meeting its statutory safeguarding requirements to safeguard and promote the wellbeing of children and adults due to insufficient seniority, competency and capacity resulting in non-attendance at Statutory Safeguarding Boards across West Yorkshire." — Risk 2593, p.118, [BAF/risk register]. Two interim Director of Nursing posts secured on a 6-month basis.

[LEAD] Staff turnover at the ICB ran at 21.66% in 2025/26 and the staff engagement score is reported as "6.30%" on the People Promise — a figure that requires checking (likely 6.30/10 or a typo) but in either reading is a significant outlier worth questioning.
[Evidence] "The staff turnover rate for the period 1 April 2025 – 31 March 2026 was 21.66%. … The staff engagement score for the ICB taken from the NHS Staff Survey 2026 was 6.30% in relation to the People Promise." — p.157, [Staff Report]. NHS Staff Survey response rate 62.95%, 717 from 1,139 employees — p.158.

[LEAD] West Yorkshire's system carried a £33.2m "system risk" value on the ICB's books to deliver 2025/26 breakeven, against an efficiency requirement of £429m (6.6% of allocation); the ICB ultimately reported a £21.4m surplus and £106.7m of efficiencies delivered.
[Evidence] Risk 2521 (rated 20 — critical): "There is a risk that the ICS will not deliver the 2025/26 financial requirement of break-even (as submitted to NHS England on 27 March 2025). This is due to the significant level of risk contained within organisational plans (including a £33.2m 'system risk' value, currently held within the ICB), and the fact that delivery is predicated on delivering efficiencies of £429m of efficiencies (6.6% of allocation)." — p.117. Outturn: "For 2025/26, the ICB reported a surplus of £21.4m, supported by the delivery of £106.7m in efficiencies." — p.124. Bradford District & Craven Place separately notes it was "asked by NHS West Yorkshire ICB to take on an extra share of the West Yorkshire overall deficit" against the backdrop of Airedale FT and Bradford Teaching Hospitals deficits — p.96.

[LEAD] Two internal audit reports received Low Assurance and two further received Limited Assurance in 2025/26 — the worst tranche of audit opinions for the ICB to date, covering Bradford CHC year-end accounts, the Wakefield out-of-hours payments review, Medicines Optimisation and LeDeR (learning from deaths of people with a learning disability).
[Evidence] Head of Internal Audit (Audit Yorkshire, Helen Higgs) draft opinion lists "2 low assurance opinions" and "2 limited assurance opinions" — p.132–133. "The review provided Low Assurance for Bradford District and Craven Health and Care Partnership (BDCHCP) in the management of Continuing Healthcare (CHC) finances for year-end financial statements and reporting." — p.133. "An opinion of Limited Assurance has been given for our review of Medicines Optimisation … The review contains six moderate recommendations." — p.133. "Overall, we have provided limited assurance on the … (LeDeR) policy, as a result of current significant resource constraints within the LeDeR team." — p.133. The LeDeR review notes the ICB has been "without a dedicated Local Area Contact role holder since early 2025" — p.89.

[LEAD] West Yorkshire was assessed as the second-least digitally mature ICB in England in 2024; the 2025 assessment lifted its overall What Good Looks Like score from 1.8 to 2.5 — "broadly in line with regional peers" — with Primary Care doubling from 1.6 to 3.1.
[Evidence] "In 2024, West Yorkshire ICB was assessed as the second least digitally mature ICB in the Country. By 2025, the system's overall digital maturity score had increased from 1.8 to 2.5, bringing West Yorkshire broadly in line with regional peers." — p.41–42, [Annual Report, Digital Maturity Assessment]. "West Yorkshire Primary Care digital maturity score increasing from 1.6 in 2024 to 3.1 in 2025." — p.42.

[WORTH WATCHING] The West Yorkshire and Harrogate Cancer Alliance moved from ICB hosting to WYAAT/Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust from 1 April 2026 — a notable governance shift coinciding with the new National Cancer Plan (February 2026).
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, the ICB hosted the West Yorkshire and Harrogate Cancer Alliance. A change of organisational host occurred from 1 April 2026 and the West Yorkshire Association of Acute Trusts (WYAAT) – Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust now fulfils this role." — p.23, [Performance, Cancer]. £11m of cancer transformation SDF spend in 2025/26.

[WORTH WATCHING] Wakefield Place is permanently confirming the suspension of the freestanding midwifery-led birthing unit at Pontefract, following a 14-week consultation; the board paper notes it had only ever served up to 350 women versus its 500 ambition before alongside-MLU opened at Pinderfields in 2016.
[Evidence] "the Committee was asked to support the proposal to make permanent the temporary suspension of the birthing facility at Pontefract … At its peak it offered services to 350 people (versus an ambition of 500) but numbers tailed off when the alongside midwife-led unit at Pinderfields was opened in 2016. Formal consultation with the public was carried out over a 14-week period from February to May 2025. In total, 762 people engaged in the consultation with 437 individual completed surveys returned. … The proposal was supported with 11 members voting in favour and three abstentions." — p.102–103, [Wakefield Place Committee annual report].

[WORTH WATCHING] ICB had 83 off-payroll engagements above £245/day at 31 March 2026 (62 of them for under a year) and 119 temporary off-payroll workers over the course of the year — alongside the use of multiple interim director appointments.
[Evidence] "Number of existing engagements as of 31 March 2026: 83 … for less than one year at the time of reporting: 62" — p.160. "No. of temporary off-payroll workers engaged between 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026: 119" — p.161. Three off-payroll senior board engagements (Tuddenham, Patten, Clennell) operate via staff-sharing arrangements with their primary employers.

[WORTH WATCHING] Interim Director of Finance/Deputy CE Jonathan Webb — who becomes Interim CE on 16 April 2026 — drew a 2025/26 banded total remuneration of £350,000–£355,000, including £152,500–£155,000 of pension-related benefits, the highest single-figure on the board.
[Evidence] "Jonathan Webb / Director of Finance and Deputy Chief Executive … Salary 195-200 … All Pension Related Benefits 152.5-155 … Total 350-355" — p.150, [Single Total Figures Remuneration Table]. Rob Webster's CE total was £225,000–230,000 (0.8 WTE, FTE-equivalent £280,000–285,000). Pay multiplier: highest-paid director vs median salary ratio 5.16 (vs 5.72 in 2024/25).

## FOI leads

- **Wakefield Out-of-Hours GP Payment Investigation — full report.** The draft "Limited Assurance" investigation report (Audit Yorkshire ref XX/2026) sits behind the £14.337m disclosure but had not been finalised at the time of the Audit and Risk Committee. Request the final report, the Wakefield CCG/NHSE/ICB handover documents referred to, and any correspondence with NHSE about the recovery strategy. Body: West Yorkshire ICB. Why it matters: identifies who knew what when on a £14m payment error that may not be recoverable.
- **The "system risk value" arithmetic.** Risk 2521 references a £33.2m system risk and £429m efficiency requirement; the report doesn't disclose place-by-place breakdown. Request the underlying ICB Finance Committee paper(s) setting out the place-level efficiency targets and the system risk distribution at the start and end of the year. Body: West Yorkshire ICB.
- **Director of Nursing departures and replacement arrangements.** The annual report names Beverley Geary (to 28 Sept 2025, then seconded to LTH) and "three out of four" Director of Nursing post-holders departing Feb–Apr 2026 but does not name the others, the reasons, or the substantive recruitment plan. Request the workforce/transition papers identifying the four post-holders and any settlement/secondment agreements. Body: West Yorkshire ICB.
- **Limited assurance audit reports — full text.** Request the unredacted internal audit reports for 10/2026 Medicines Optimisation; 11/2026 LeDeR; 03/2026 BDCHCP CHC Year-End; 13/2026 Wakefield Out-of-Hours Fact Finding Review; and the still-draft investigation report. Body: West Yorkshire ICB.
- **Bradford District & Craven place finances.** BD&C committee notes it was "asked by NHS West Yorkshire ICB to take on an extra share of the West Yorkshire overall deficit". Request the ICB-to-place letter and any side agreements with Airedale FT and Bradford Teaching Hospitals on deficit support. Body: West Yorkshire ICB.
- **Salary overpayments thematic.** Audit Yorkshire's report 08/2026 "reviewed six ICBs' gross salary overpayments data, for the financial years 2022/23, 2023/24 and 2024/25" — request the underlying figures for West Yorkshire ICB. Body: West Yorkshire ICB.
