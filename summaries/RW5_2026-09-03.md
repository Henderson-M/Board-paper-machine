## Top lines

[LEAD] Chief executive Chris Oliver is leaving Lancashire and South Cumbria for the top job at Greater Manchester Mental Health Foundation Trust; an interim takes over from 7 September with formal interviews not until November.
[Evidence] "Chris Oliver, CEO has been appointed as the new CEO of Greater Manchester Mental Health NHS Foundation Trust (GMMH)... His leaving date from LSCFT is 06 September 2026. On 07 September 2026, Dr Gareth Thomas will become the Interim Chief Executive for LSCFT... The formal recruitment process for the Chief Executive role was launched in August 2026, with formal interviews scheduled to take place in early November 2026." — p.134, [Trust Chair's Report TB175]

[LEAD] The deficit has widened well beyond plan four months into the year, driven almost entirely by slippage in the trust's £17.7m efficiency programme, with medical agency costs also rising.
[Evidence] "At month 4, the Trust is returning a deficit of £1.9m against a plan of £0.5m deficit, so £1.4m beyond plan at this point. The position is driven almost exclusively by efficiency, and either its delayed delivery against plan phasing, or slippage in programmes that, now quality impact assessed, are deemed to require replacement." — p.173, [Month 4 Finance Summary TB178]. "Efficiency is currently £1.8m behind plan... the risk rated position is £5.2m." — p.175, [Month 4 Finance Summary TB178]

[WORTH WATCHING] Inpatient pressure spiked in July: the trust declared OPEL 4 twice, restraints hit their highest level in over a year, and long-stay ("super-stranded") patient numbers kept climbing.
[Evidence] "Significant pressure continued across inpatient flow, urgent care and complex discharge pathways during July, when the Trust declared OPEL 4 on two occasions... the number of super-stranded patients rose slightly to 132... Restraints within mental health inpatient services increased to 397, the highest level since May 2025, with activity predominantly linked to a small number of patients with high levels of acuity." — p.188, [Trust Performance Report Month 4 TB182]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's own risk register records a 2025 staff survey showing most People Promise domains declining, and says BAME staff faced more public discrimination in 2026.
[Evidence] "The 2025 National Staff Survey (NSS) provides the evidence base for this programme... Within the NSS most People Promise domains declined, with the largest drops in career development, learning opportunities, wellbeing and staffing levels. Engagement, morale and Friends and Family Test scores also trended negatively... In 2026 our B.A.M.E colleagues experienced an increase in discrimination from the public." — p.48, [Strategic Dashboard / Board Assurance Framework TB169]

[WORTH WATCHING] Follow-up to the trust's CQC regulatory breach on inpatient safer staffing: rather than roll out a new multidisciplinary staffing model across wards, the trust says services need more stabilisation first and will pilot the new model on a single ward.
[Evidence] "Whilst the Trust remains committed to a more integrated MDT model, it is recognised that further service stabilisation is required before large-scale workforce redesign can be safely implemented. The report therefore proposes a pilot MDT model on a single ward within the Bay Network." — p.81, [Cultural Plan Update / Integrated Safer Staffing report TB171]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board is asked to self-assess against the 2026 Ockenden maternity review even though the trust runs no maternity services, on the basis the findings apply to any provider board.
[Evidence] "Although LSCFT does not provide maternity services, the review has direct relevance for all NHS provider Boards, including mental health and community trusts. National leaders have been explicit that the findings reflect systemic leadership, governance, cultural and assurance failures that can arise in any care setting if known risks are normalised, voices are not heard, and Boards rely on reassurance rather than insight." — p.295-296, [Ockenden Independent Review paper TB188]

[FOI] A new trust-wide "Recovery Management Framework" has been created to put services with quality, safety, regulatory or workforce concerns into enhanced oversight and escalation — but the pack does not say which services, if any, are currently in it.
[Evidence] "The Trust requires a consistent, proportionate and improvement-focused approach for services experiencing significant incidents, quality concerns, performance deterioration, patient safety risks, safeguarding issues, regulatory concerns, workforce instability or recurring gaps in assurance... A policy has been developed which sets out a Trust-wide framework for enhanced oversight, escalation and recovery groups." — p.135-136, [Chief Executive Report TB176]. **Request:** the Recovery Management Framework policy, and confirmation of which services/wards, if any, are currently subject to it, from LSCFT.

[FOI] An external review of the Initial Response Service made nine recommendations, but only the fact of the review — not its findings — reached the Board via committee minutes.
[Evidence] "The Committee further noted the findings of the IRS review, which included nine recommendations it was agreed that the review would be shared with the Committee." — p.143, [Board Committee Report TB177, Finance and Performance Committee chair's report, 27 July 2026]. **Request:** the Initial Response Service review report and its nine recommendations, from LSCFT.

[FOI] The trust and the ICB have an unresolved funding dispute over Independent Sector Beds, with the committee backing an escalation approach if the ICB misses an agreed deadline — detail not given.
[Evidence] "The Committee also noted the unresolved funding position relating to Independent Sector Beds (ISBs)... The Committee supported the proposed approach to the ISB funding dispute should a satisfactory response not be received from the ICB by the agreed deadline." — p.143-144, [Board Committee Report TB177, Finance and Performance Committee chair's report, 27 July 2026]. **Request:** correspondence between LSCFT and the Lancashire & South Cumbria ICB on Independent Sector Beds funding, from LSCFT and/or the ICB.
