[LEAD] Birmingham and Solihull and Black Country ICBs paid out more than £8m in exit packages in 2025/26 as the two organisations clustered and cut senior posts.
[Evidence] "Total exit packages... 70... £5,161,687" for Black Country ICB in 2025/26, up from 6 packages / £78,365 in 2024/25, p.423, [Black Country ICB Annual Report 2025/2026, Exit packages table]. [LEAD] NHS England's newly-issued annual assessment letters, published in full in this pack for the first time, rate both predecessor ICBs' safety domain in the bottom quartile nationally, and record that Black Country ran a £107.5m system-wide deficit last year, £12.5m above its deficit-support-funded plan, even though the ICB itself delivered a balanced position.
[Evidence] "Performance in the safety domain was in the bottom quartile" (Birmingham and Solihull), p.32, [NHS England annual assessment letter, BSol ICB, 3 August 2026]. "there was a system-wide deficit of £107.5m at year-end, which was £12.5m above the deficit support funded plan and the system efficiency plan under-delivered by £24.1m", p.39, [NHS England annual assessment letter, Black Country ICB, 3 August 2026]. Also: "The ICB ended the year as the most challenged system nationally for the number of people with LDA in inpatient beds, with a rate of 69 per million against the standard of less than 30 per million" (BSol), p.32

[LEAD] Chief executive David Melbourne's report to the board discloses that combined four-hour A&E performance across the cluster has fallen to 71%, down from 76% a year ago, cancer 62-day performance is at 54% against a 75% target, and lost ambulance handover hours in Black Country have nearly doubled year-on-year.
[Evidence] "Combined four-hour performance across the cluster stands at 71%, below the 76% we achieved at the same point last year... "ambulance handover hours lost above fifteen minutes rose to 6,902 in the Black Country in July, against 3,564 in the same month last year", p.25-26, [Chief Executive's Report]
