# Lincolnshire ICB board, 17 September 2026

- **Source:** https://lincolnshire.icb.nhs.uk/about-us/our-board-and-committees/icb-board-meetings/

**Note on scope:** this is a meeting in common of Derby and Derbyshire ICB, Lincolnshire ICB and Nottingham and Nottinghamshire ICB (the "DLN cluster"), chaired by Kathy McLean with Amanda Sullivan as cluster chief executive. Every paper is a joint paper covering all three ICBs. Page references are PDF page numbers within each of the two pack files, given as [part 1] (agenda items 1 to 11, 136pp) and [part 2] (agenda items 12 to 17, 219pp).

## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England's neighbourhood health readiness assessment scores the three-ICB cluster 39/100, puts every one of the five national goals below the England average and rates no place above "MOBILISING".
[Evidence] "Cluster mean 2.03, every Goal below the England mean of 2.48. Strongest G1 2.42, weakest G5 1.58." And: "No Place is rated above MOBILISING, because no neighbourhood footprint has yet been determined or agreed at a Health and Wellbeing Board." Place scores: Lincolnshire 44, Nottingham and Nottinghamshire 38, Derby and Derbyshire 34. All seven Stage 2 readiness steps rated "[EARLY STAGE]" (p.78, [Chief Executive's Report, Appendix B annex, part 1]; corroborated at p.84, [Population Health Strategy Progress Report: Neighbourhood Health, part 1]: "The national self-assessment gives DLN a combined baseline of 39/100")

[LEAD] The same NHS England annex records that the cluster has no ring-fenced neighbourhood health investment and that 2026/27 transformation money has been pulled to cover acute pressures.
[Evidence] "No ring-fenced cluster NbH investment line. Devolved budgets at 2 of 4 N&N PBPs (£3.28m, £3.5m); none in Lincolnshire. Transformation fund stated at roughly £33m a year, 2026/27 funds diverted to acute pressures." On workforce: "No cluster NbH workforce plan and no named NbH SRO in any cluster-supplied document." (p.78, [Chief Executive's Report, Appendix B annex, part 1])

[LEAD] The three systems are £31.2m adverse to plan four months into the year, with the entire variance in Nottinghamshire.
[Evidence] "At month 4, the DLN ICS is reporting a deficit of £101.3m against a planned deficit position of £70.1m, an adverse variance of £31.2m." Drivers named as "Nottingham University Hospitals (£17.6m adverse), reflecting pay cost pressures and under-delivery of efficiencies, Sherwood Forest Hospitals (£6.9m adverse)... and Nottinghamshire Healthcare (£6.8m adverse)". Efficiency delivery is "£11.5m behind the planned £150.5m", with University Hospitals of Derby and Burton £5.3m adverse. All organisations still forecast full-year plan delivery. (p.141, [Finance Report, Item 14, part 2])

[LEAD] Five cluster trusts sit in NHS Oversight Framework segment 4, and two of the largest national ranking falls in the quarter happened here.
[Evidence] "At Q4 2025-26 there are 5 trusts in segment 4, 5 trusts in segment 3 and 1 trust in segment 2." "UHDB fell 28 places in the ranking and moved to segment 4. 5th in the trusts with the largest reductions nationally". "SFHT fell 22 places in the ranking and moved to segment 3. 9th in the trusts with the largest reductions nationally." "EMAS dropped 2 segment levels, from 2 to 4". "Overall position: The overall position of the providers across DLN has worsened." Segment 4 trusts: Nottinghamshire Healthcare, Nottingham University Hospitals, United Lincolnshire Hospitals, Chesterfield Royal Hospital, University Hospitals of Derby and Burton. (p.106, [Quality Oversight Report, Quality Performance Report August 2026, part 1])

[LEAD] Derbyshire Community Health Services has served notice on four Derbyshire urgent treatment centres, logged as a new high-scoring operational risk.
[Evidence] "RR165 (NEW) Joint. Notice has been received by DCHS on the delivery of 4 Derbyshire Urgent Treatment Centre services. Failure to commission alternative services within required timescales may result in a risk of disruption to same day urgent care provision in the county". Scored high, 16 (I4 x L4). (p.210 to 211, [Committee Highlight Reports, Appendix 7, part 2])

[LEAD] The ICBs' quality committee took "limited assurance" over perinatal oversight and discussed racism within maternity services.
[Evidence] "They discussed concerns regarding organisational culture and experiences of racism within maternity services and highlighted the importance of triangulating performance data with family, patient and staff experience." And: "While oversight arrangements were in place and progress was evident, the Committee took limited assurance regarding the ICBs' ability to secure sustained improvement where provider performance remained below expectations. It sought greater clarity on the contractual and commissioning levers available to secure timely improvement". Committee met 9 September 2026, chaired by Sharon Robson. The pack's own definition of "Limited" assurance is: "Achievement of outcomes will not be achieved or are significantly off-track for achievement... Achievement of strategic objectives and system priorities will be impaired." (p.189 to 190 and p.204, [Committee Highlight Reports, Appendix 2 and Appendix 6, part 2])

[LEAD] The winter plan goes to the board with no new money, £22.9m of transformation funding absorbed into acute baselines and a residual 77-bed gap in January.
[Evidence] "System partners and providers are aware there is no additional funding for winter". "In developing the winter plan acute trusts were asked to ensure they acknowledged the position in relation to the Transformation Funding which has been included in acute baseline positions this year totalling £22.9 million" (United Lincolnshire Hospitals £7.9m, Sherwood Forest £7m, University Hospitals of Derby and Burton £4m, Chesterfield Royal £2m, Nottingham University Hospitals £2m). The bed gap table shows a DLN total of minus 11 in October and minus 77 in January. The paper says "a residual bed gap remains in parts of the winter period, particularly in October and January". (p.2, p.4, p.5 and p.6, [Winter Plan 2026/27, Item 12, part 2])

[LEAD] Lincolnshire ICB is ranked last of 36 ICBs on diagnostic waits, and United Lincolnshire Hospitals 133rd of 134 providers, on the pack's own national benchmarking.
[Evidence] "In June, the proportion waiting over six weeks was 27.4% in Derbyshire against a 17.1% plan, 52.1% in Lincolnshire against 32.8%, and 29.3% in Nottingham and Nottinghamshire against 22.6%." The benchmarking table (ranking "from best (1) to worst") puts LICB 36/36 and ULH 133/134 on diagnostics over six weeks for June 2026; LICB is also 35/36 on 62-day cancer and 34/36 on 18-week RTT. (p.110 and p.120, [Commissioning Delivery Oversight Report, Item 13, part 2])

[LEAD] ICB cluster turnover has hit 34.6% as voluntary redundancy exits run, with appraisal completion down to 42.5%.
[Evidence] "Overall turnover is 34.6% a rise of 12.7% compared to May. This is linked to the ongoing management of change process. Numbers leaving organisations through voluntary redundancy is now increasing, as exits began in April. We expect this to continue to over the next 2 months." Also: "The ICBs' whole time equivalent (WTE) has decreased by 153.1WTE compared to May"; "42.5% of staff have completed appraisals across the cluster a decrease of 5.9% compared to May"; sickness 4.3%, with "Anxiety, stress and depression" the most common cause. (p.70, [Chief Executive's Report, Appendix A workforce report, part 1])

[LEAD] Commissioners have put formal contract and recovery machinery on four acute trusts, including contract performance notices at Sherwood Forest and United Lincolnshire.
[Evidence] Provider oversight table: UHDB "The ICB has requested a Shadow RAP from the Trust targeting the 4 programme areas of concern, followed by a fortnightly improvement progress discussion for 8 weeks, following which a decision will be taken as to whether to progress to a CPN"; ULTH "The ICB had issued a CPN specifically for diagnostics prior to the ICB Performance escalation process"; SFHT "entered the ICB Escalation process on the 3rd July, being issued a CPN for UEC and Diagnostics"; CRH "PROM paused for Rapid Quality Review process duration". Across the board, "61 headline metric instances failing against required targets or plans". (p.107 and p.117, [Commissioning Delivery Oversight Report, Item 13, part 2])

[WORTH WATCHING] United Lincolnshire Hospitals has reported a rise in stillbirths above the national threshold, with every case under review.
[Evidence] "ULHT stillbirth rate (2.94 per 1,000 births) increase in stillbirth rates has been identified and is currently above national threshold. All cases are undergoing detailed review via PMRT, supported by thematic analysis at trust and system level." Separately, Nottingham University Hospitals is "reported as higher than expected" on SHMI at 1.1677 against an England baseline of 1.000, with the caveat that the trust "has a high percentage of invalid diagnosis codes". (p.119 and p.110, [Quality Oversight Report, Quality Performance Report August 2026, part 1])
