## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust booked £1.5m of elective income above actuals to report an "on-plan" position, while the underlying deficit runs well ahead of plan
[Evidence] "In order to report an on-plan position, the Trust has booked elective income in line with plan, rather than actuals - this means that £1.5m of income has been recognised in the position year to date in excess of actuals." — p.9, [Chief Executive's Report, item 7]. Finance cover: reported YTD deficit £5.7m but "underlying deficit year to date £8.3m (plan £6.4m deficit)"; achieved "through the use of non-recurring items which cannot be sustained through 2026/27." — pp.1, 3, [Financial Report June 2026, item 9/paper 05]

[LEAD] CFO gives the board only "limited assurance" on the full-year plan; trust could be £6.1m adrift by end of Q2
[Evidence] "the material variance between the current underlying monthly run rate and the required financial trajectory means the Board can take only limited assurance that the Trust will deliver its planned full year financial position" — p.4; "the Trust could face a £6.1m gap to plan by the end of Q2 if the underlying position remains unchanged" — p.3, [Financial Report June 2026]. Paper self-rated Limited Assurance.

[LEAD] Trust expects to need £10m of NHSE deficit support from January 2027; cash covers just over 15 days
[Evidence] "the Trust anticipates the need for £10m of deficit support from January 2027"; "a structurally tight liquidity position that has been stretched further by a confirmed repayment of £6.9m FY25/26 PDC to DHSC by the end of Q3" — p.7; "the Trust held sufficient cash to cover just over 15 days of operating expenditure. While this is below the preferred level, it remains adequate to avoid requesting revenue support before January 2027" — p.8, [Financial Commentary Report June 2026]. Closing cash £31.3m.

[LEAD] Trust exited NHSE Recovery Support Programme in January 2026, but Sept 2023 enforcement undertakings remain under NHSE review, with the recovery-plan undertaking still only "Partial compliance"
[Evidence] "HHFT exited its RSP programme formally on the 14/1/2026"; undertaking 7.1.1 (single system Recovery Plan) assessed "Partial compliance" — the only undertaking not marked "Fully met and complied" — [Enforcement Undertakings self-assessment, Appendix 3, PDF pp.89-91]. "NHS England are reviewing the Undertakings given to us when we entered the RSP… We will report to Board in due course on whether NHSE decide to remove or amend the Undertakings." — p.9, [Financial Commentary Report June 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] CIP delivery £1.8m behind at month 3; only 69.4% of the £45.2m savings programme is even developed
[Evidence] YTD CIP delivery £5.9m against plan £7.9m; forecast "fully developed CIPs of £19.0m and plan in progress CIPs of £6.9m, totalling £25.9m… This represents 69.4% of the total CIP programme. No assumptions have been made for undeveloped schemes. Failure to delivery these savings will result in increased revenue support requirement." — pp.5, 7; "Workforce is the largest delivery gap, with £0.688m identified against a £3.55m target, despite locum spend reducing by 34%." — p.6, [Financial Commentary Report June 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board assurance framework carries three risks at the top score of 20 — including "run out of cash", stalled new-hospital programme, and estate condition
[Evidence] Risk 4.1: "If we do not deliver the financial plan and/or run out of cash, then externals controls over expenditure will be applied" — net 20, up from previous target 16; Risk 5.1 Service reconfiguration (MOHHS / new hospital programme) — 20; Risk 1.2 Infrastructure — 20. — [Board Assurance Framework report, item 8, PDF p.20]

[WORTH WATCHING] Urgent and emergency care among the most challenged in England; heatwave critical incident declared in June
[Evidence] "overall 4-hour performance was 63.6% in June, against a planned target of 75.1%, while 12-hour performance was 6.27%, compared with a planned target of 2.4%… Four-hour performance is amongst the most challenged nationally, (111 out of 118)"; "On Wednesday 3 June, the Trust declared a Critical Incident after a significant level of pressure, highly unusual for this time of year" — pp.4-5, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] 52-week waits running at four times plan and the trust ranks 109/118 nationally; waiting list has grown to 50,470
[Evidence] "The volume of patients waiting over 52 weeks is greater than expected (2.56% vs 0.6%), and nationally an area where the Trust ranks poorly (109 out of 118)"; "there are more patients waiting for elective care than expected (50,470 vs 44,869)" — p.6, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust pressing ahead with turning its in-house facilities arm (HHCS) into a Managed Service Provider after an NHSE-consultation pause
[Evidence] "Following approval by the Board in February to pursue an alternative approach to developing Hampshire Hospitals Contract Services (HHCS) and in light of the pending NHSE consultation, progress has resumed… Recruitment to the Service Lead role is due to commence shortly, which will be a key enabler for the subsequent recruitment of supervisory and domestic colleagues." — p.7, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Healthcare support worker backpay offer collapsed after Unite rejected it; staff side has written to the chair asking for a higher settlement
[Evidence] "While Unison and the Royal College of Nursing accepted the offer, Unite rejected it. As a result, the Joint Staff Side confirmed the offer could not proceed and wrote to the Chair and CPO asking them to consider a higher settlement." — p.7, [Chief Executive's Report]
