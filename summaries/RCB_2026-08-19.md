# York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust board — 19 August 2026 (Extraordinary)

**Note on the meeting:** this is an *extraordinary* board meeting, called for 30 minutes (2:15pm–2:45pm) with one substantive item — the future of Bridlington Care Unit. It was not previously in our meeting state and had not been alerted.

## Top lines

[LEAD] York and Scarborough's board is being asked on 19 August to approve the permanent closure of Bridlington Care Unit, after 91 per cent of people responding to its own engagement survey opposed the closure.
[Evidence] The board is asked to "Approve the permanent closure of Bridlington Care Unit and the cessation of the current inpatient step-down service model" — p.5, [Future of Bridlington Care Unit (BCU), Item 4]. Survey result: "There was strong opposition to the closure of the unit: 91% of respondents said they do not support at all or mostly do not support the proposal" — p.24, [Bridlington Engagement Report, Appendix A]. Full breakdown: "Do not support at all 367 82.8 … Mostly do not support 35 7.9 … Total responses 443" — p.50, [Bridlington Engagement Report].

[LEAD] The trust admits it briefed its own staff about the closure before it had notified the council scrutiny committee, the local MP and other stakeholders — a sequencing failure it concedes in writing.
[Evidence] "Despite best intentions of colleagues, the correct sequence of events for this engagement was not followed as colleagues were advised of the change before stakeholders had been briefed, resulting in them learning of the plans before being formally notified and before discussions could take place" — p.21, [Bridlington Engagement Report, Appendix A].

[LEAD] Neither the trust nor Humber and North Yorkshire ICB classes the closure as a substantial variation in service — the classification that would trigger a fuller formal consultation route.
[Evidence] "Although neither the Trust or the Humber and North Yorkshire Integrated Care Board (ICB) regard the closure as a substantial variation in service, there is still an expectation that engagement takes place with key stakeholders including East Riding Council's Health, Care and Wellbeing Overview and Scrutiny Sub-Committee, the local MP, and other interested parties" — p.21, [Bridlington Engagement Report, Appendix A]. `[INFERENCE]` The consequence of that classification for the trust's statutory consultation obligations is not stated in the pack and is not asserted here.

[LEAD] The MP for Bridlington and the Wolds, Charlie Dewhirst, handed the trust a 5,784-signature petition to keep the unit open; three East Riding councillors and three MPs from outside the area also wrote in.
[Evidence] "Charlie Dewhirst, the MP for Bridlington and the Wolds presented a petition to the Trust with 5,784 signatures calling for the BCU to remain open. The petition was formally received by the Board of Directors at its May 2026 meeting." Also: "The Trust received letters from three East Riding of Yorkshire Councillors raising concerns about the closure of the BCU" and "Three letters were received from MPs outside of the Bridlington area raising constituent concerns about the closure of BCU, as part of a coordinated campaign" — p.37, [Bridlington Engagement Report, Appendix A].

[LEAD] Commissioning funding for the unit stopped at the end of March 2026 — the closure decision comes after the money had already gone.
[Evidence] "the commissioning arrangements which originally supported BCU have changed, with funding ceasing for the unit at the end of March 2026, requiring the Trust and wider health and care system to consider the most appropriate long-term model of care for this patient cohort" — p.3, [Future of Bridlington Care Unit (BCU)].

[WORTH WATCHING] The board agreed a three-month pause in May after the East Riding scrutiny sub-committee intervened — but staff were redeployed during the pause, and the paper concedes reversing that would now be a problem.
[Evidence] The OSC "recommended a delay to implementation, further assurance regarding community readiness and a programme of engagement" and the board "agreed a three-month pause" — p.3. On Option 1 (continue operating): "Since the original implementation plans were paused, workforce changes and redeployments have taken place and consideration would need to be given to whether previously redeployed staff would be required to return to the service" — p.14, [Options Appraisal].

[WORTH WATCHING] The unit's bed base was cut to a maximum of 15 in May 2026 and has been running at about 10 patients a day since.
[Evidence] "Although the bed base was reduced to a maximum of 15 beds in May 2026, actual utilisation has remained significantly below this level, with the unit operating at an average occupancy of approximately 10 patients per day since May" — p.9, [Future of Bridlington Care Unit (BCU)].

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's assurance that closure is safe rests on Humber and North Yorkshire ICB's description of a future model, not on delivered capacity — and the community services concerned are run by another provider.
[Evidence] The board is asked to "Note the assurance provided by Humber and North Yorkshire Integrated Care Board regarding the continued development of community, neighbourhood health, rehabilitation and Home First pathways" — p.4. The trust states it is "working in partnership with the providers on community services and adult social care in the Bridlington locality, as the trust is not the provider of these services" — p.12. Named provider: "The NHS commissioned community services, run by City Healthcare Partnership (CHCP)" — p.12.

[WORTH WATCHING] Respondents backed the principle of Home First while rejecting this closure — the pack's own reading is that the gap is confidence in community capacity.
[Evidence] "when asked 'to what extent do you agree that people should be supported to return home as soon as it is safe to do so, rather than remaining in hospital longer than necessary?' 89% tended to agree or strongly agreed" — p.25. And: "Their concerns are focused less on the principle of care at home, and more on whether community services, social care packages, rehabilitation, communication with families and discharge arrangements are sufficiently robust to make this safe and reliable in practice" — p.11.

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the full board paper and minutes of the private board meeting of 27 May 2026 at which the pause was agreed, from York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals FT.
[Evidence] "York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust's Board of Directors discussed the OSC's recommendations and wider feedback from stakeholders and the Bridlington community at its private meeting on 27 May 2026" — p.21. The decision to pause was taken in private; the reasoning is not in the public pack.

[FOI] Request from York and Scarborough FT, and from Humber and North Yorkshire ICB, all correspondence between the two bodies on whether the BCU closure constitutes a substantial variation in service, including any legal or governance advice.
[Evidence] The pack asserts the shared position — "neither the Trust or the … ICB regard the closure as a substantial variation in service" — p.21 — but records no analysis supporting it.

[FOI] Request the letters from the three East Riding councillors and the three out-of-area MPs, plus the Bridlington Health Forum correspondence and the trust's replies.
[Evidence] "The Bridlington Health Forum wrote to the Trust on a number of occasions raising concerns about the closure of the BCU" and the councillor/MP letters at p.37 — contents are not reproduced in the pack.

[FOI] Request the workforce impact assessment and the redeployment records for BCU staff covering May–August 2026 (headcount redeployed, from and to which services).
[Evidence] "workforce changes and redeployments have taken place" during the pause — p.14 — with no numbers given anywhere in the pack.
