> **Joint board.** This is a single combined pack for the **RWT/WHT Group Board of Directors Meeting held in Public**, covering **The Royal Wolverhampton NHS Trust (RL4)** and **Walsall Healthcare NHS Trust (RBK)**. The identical file is published on both trusts' board pages. Group chair: Sir David Nicholson. Group CEO: Joe Chadwick-Bell. Page refs below are pages of the combined pack PDF.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Nearly half the group's £81.3m savings programme has been rated high risk of not being delivered, two months into the year.
[Evidence] "Total savings requirement for the Group of £81.3m presents a significant delivery challenge. NHS England require Trusts to identify 100% of the savings plan by 31st May 2026." / "£36m of CIP schemes have been assessed as high risk of non/reduced delivery (£29m RWT, £6m WHT)" — p.60, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 7, Finance & Productivity Committee, May]. Split confirmed at p.92: "The total efficiency challenge in 2026/27 for the group is £81.3m; RWT £53.8m, WHT £27.5m" — [Group Finance & Workforce Report, Enclosure 10.1].

[LEAD] Seven patients have come to harm in Royal Wolverhampton's glaucoma service, which has 10,000 patients waiting and 1,500 rated red.
[Evidence] "The Glaucoma service at RWT is facing significant demand pressures... Approximately 10,000 patients are on the waiting list, 5,500 of whom are overdue and RAG-rated (2,000 Green, 2,000 Amber, 1,500 Red). Seven patients have come to harm and harm reviews and associated learning have been requested." — p.152, [Integrated Performance Report — RWT, Enclosure 10.3, Quality, Safety & Patient Experience Executive Summary]. The May minutes tie part of this to the new patient administration system: "issues had been identified with the implementation of the PAS system, specifically affecting ophthalmology patient waiting lists" and "the timeliness of reviews meant a number of patients remained at risk" — p.7, [Minutes of 19 May 2026, item 171/26].

[LEAD] The CQC issued a Section 29A warning notice to Royal Wolverhampton's emergency department in February and then withdrew it after the trust challenged it — because the notice itself was defective.
[Evidence] "On 28th May 26 we received communication from the CQC stating they have upheld the Trust Representations regarding the 29A Warning notice, issued to the Emergency Department (ED) at New Cross Hospital in February 26, concluding that the notice was not sufficiently detailed to meet the legal threshold for enforcement." — p.149, [Integrated Performance Report — RWT, Enclosure 10.3]. Repeated at p.145, [Managing Director Summary, Gwen Nuttall]. Committee report puts it as: "Following representations by the Trust, the anticipated Section 29A Warning Notice was withdrawn." — p.63, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 7, Quality Committee].

[LEAD] Three never events at Royal Wolverhampton in two months, including two wrong-site surgeries in the same ophthalmology directorate.
[Evidence] "There were 2 Never Events reported in month, one in the Ophthalmology Directorate under the description of wrong site surgery which resulted in the patient receiving bilateral PRP (Pan-retinal Photocoagulation) laser treatment on 11th April 26, the error was identified at follow-up on 11th June 26. A previous wrong site surgery in Ophthalmology was also reported in April 26. The second Never Event occurred in the Gastroenterology Directorate under the description of wrong route medication on 26th May 26, when oral medication was mistakenly administered via a Hickman line" — p.149, [Integrated Performance Report — RWT, Enclosure 10.3].

[LEAD] The group's new board assurance framework says failure on the £81m savings programme would hit its CQC well-led rating, as £60m of national deficit support is withdrawn.
[Evidence] "If we do not deliver the 2026/27 cost improvement programme of £81m (6.4%) and transform how services are delivered while national deficit support funding (£60m) reduces each year, then the Group will not live within its c.£1.5bn resource envelope, resulting in an unsustainable financial position, reduced ability to invest in services, colleagues and estate, increased regulatory intervention and an adverse impact on the CQC well-led assessment." — p.74, [Board Assurance Framework, Enclosure 7.1, Risk TCT3 — Financial Sustainability and Efficient Use of Resources; current score 15]. Listed negative assurances include "Reliance on non-recurrent measures in prior-year delivery" and "Run rate above plan in some divisions, with temporary workforce spend above target."

[LEAD] Royal Wolverhampton dropped into NHSE oversight segment 4 and was pulled back out after the ICB agreed to inject £8m.
[Evidence] "RWT has fallen into Segment 4 having seen the overall average score fall from 2.49 to 2.71. The primary driver for RWT's deterioration into Segment 4 is the deterioration in scoring within the Finance and Productivity segment – specifically the variance to financial to plan. The Trust was reporting a negative variance to plan at the end of Quarter 3 when the snapshot was taken for oversight scoring. This was immediately prior to an agreement being reached with the ICB (as reported at the last PRM meeting) to inject £8m. This has supported the Trust to deliver its financial plan for 2025/26." — p.176, [Integrated Performance Report — RWT, Enclosure 10.3, National Oversight Framework]. The CEO report confirms RWT returned to segment 3 for Q4 (average metric score 2.47, league position 90 of 134); WHT scored 2.21, segment 3, position 58 — p.25, [Group Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 6].

[LEAD] Both trusts declined on every one of the nine People Promise indicators in the 2025 staff survey, with falls steeper than the national trend.
[Evidence] "She summarised that both Trusts had seen a decline across all nine People Promise indicators, as well as the two overarching measures of staff engagement and morale. She said all scores were below national averages, with declines that were steeper than national trends. She noted that response rates had also fallen across both organisations." Areas of concern included "increased reports of discrimination and unwanted behaviours, rising work pressure, a higher proportion of staff considering leaving and a marked drop in advocacy scores" — p.8, [Minutes of 19 May 2026, item 173/26 NHS National Staff Survey Results, Dr Claire Radley]. RWT's staff survey engagement theme score is ranked 123/134 nationally and its raising-concerns sub-score 115/134 — p.177, [NOF Dashboard — RWT].

[WORTH WATCHING] Walsall's Freedom to Speak Up rate is more than three times Royal Wolverhampton's, and nearly half its concerns come from staff who won't say what job they do.
[Evidence] "WHT recorded 6.79 concerns per 100 staff, compared with 2.09 concerns per 100 staff at RWT" — p.35. "At WHT there was a high volume of concerns from undisclosed professional groups (149) across Q1 – Q4 2025/26, potentially indicating a fear of detriment." — p.37. "During Q1–Q4 2025/26, WHT received 70 concerns alleging discrimination, while RWT recorded 38 allegations of discrimination" — p.38, all [Joint WHT RWT Freedom to Speak Up Annual Report, Enclosure 6.2].

[WORTH WATCHING] Four Black Country trusts have formally recommitted to their group model "due to recent media reports", while leaving the door open to closer managerial alignment.
[Evidence] "She emphasised the intention by RWT and WHT to recommit to the Group model and said the organisations did not wish to disperse back into standalone Trusts, nor was there a proposal to move towards a single Black Country-wide group at this stage and that the report sought to formalise ongoing Group arrangements." Sir David Nicholson "added that due to recent media reports regarding Group arrangements, it was timely and sensible for the four Black Country organisations to reaffirm their shared position, set out a clear strategic direction, and establish firmer foundations for future collaboration. He said this included exploring, in time, whether any organisations wished to pursue closer managerial alignment." — p.6, [Minutes of 19 May 2026, item 170/26 Black Country Acute Trusts – Future Group Structure Arrangements]. The paper went to Dudley Group FT and Sandwell and West Birmingham the following day.

[WORTH WATCHING] Two fire safety enforcement notices remain in force across the group's estate, one on a maternity block.
[Evidence] "Discussions continue with West Midlands Fire Service regarding safety works within Block 32 Maternity Services at New Cross Hospital, with particular focus on risk assessments. The Fire Safety Enforcement Notice remains in force" and "Discussions continue with Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service regarding outstanding works at Cannock Chase Hospital, which remains subject to a Fire Safety Enforcement Notice. Whilst formal written confirmation has not been received, the Fire Service has indicated that they are satisfied with progress to date." — p.59, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 7, Quality Committee]. A third notice, on Nucleus Theatres in Block 55 at New Cross, "has been lifted following completion of the required works" — p.61.

[WORTH WATCHING] The people committee has recorded that there is no assurance the group's 2026/27 workforce plan will be delivered.
[Evidence] "Deploying bank WTE relative to the 26/27 workforce plan remains a challenge and there is currently no assurance that the workforce plan will be achieved." / "M2 26/27: Total WTE deployed was 202.70 WTE adverse to plan." / "Despite reductions, WHT is 92.1WTE and RWT is 110.6WTE above plan at M2." — p.61, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 7, People Committee].

[WORTH WATCHING] Walsall's 2026/27 plan is built on running a c.£10.5m deficit in the first half of the year and recovering all of it in the second, on savings that were unidentified at the start of the year.
[Evidence] "The WHT plan assumed that unidentified CIP at the start of the financial year would be achieved in the 2nd half of the financial year. As a result, the plan has a deficit of c£10.5m in the first half of the financial year and then a surplus of c£10.5m in the second half of the year." — p.110, [Integrated Performance Report — WHT, Enclosure 10.2, Managing Director Summary, Amelia Godson]. At RWT, 91 per cent of month 2 savings were non-recurrent: "In Month 2 the Trust has achieved £3.6m of CIP in month, however £3.3m of this was achieved non-recurrently, which is 91% of the in-month achievement... The current forecast indicates a significant gap against the target, which is across all divisions." — p.174.

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts' 2025/26 annual accounts and annual reports have been signed off — with clean audit opinions, but Walsall picked up an amber value-for-money assessment across all three criteria.
[Evidence] WHT: "Under delegated authority from the WHT board the committee agreed to adopt the 2025/26 annual accounts." / "The External Auditors reported they will give a 'clean opinion' on the 25/26 financial statements." / "The External Auditors assessment of Value for Money was an amber rating (No significant weakness, improvement recommendations made) across all three criteria: Financial Sustainability, Governance and Improving economy, efficiency and effectiveness." — p.224, [Exception Report from Audit Committee, Enclosure 11.1, Mary Martin]. RWT: "The External Auditors presented their findings reports, noting their intention to give an unqualified opinion on the Trust's financial statements and value for money. Their ISA 260 report to those charged with governance was received, noting adjusted and unadjusted audit misstatements. No high priority recommendations were raised." — p.225, [Exception Report from Audit Committee, Enclosure 11.1, Julie Jones]. Auditor named as Grant Thornton at p.224. [INFERENCE] The full annual report and accounts documents themselves are not in this pack — only the audit committee exception reports confirming approval and submission to NHSE.

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England is refusing to fund the group's industrial action costs.
[Evidence] "Overall, the Group position is behind plan by £1.0m year-to-date, with RWT £0.6m behind plan, and WHT £0.4m behind plan, related to industrial action costs in April. Both Trusts are on plan in month. NHSE are currently holding a position that this will not be funded." — p.92, [Group Finance & Workforce Report, Enclosure 10.1]. Earlier estimate of the pressure: "Industrial action cost pressures (£1.5m) are currently based on estimates" — p.60.

## Other items noted (not flagged)

- Corridor care in use at both trusts: WHT "the corridor space was used to support peak periods of ambulance offloads on 13 separate days, averaging 3.3 hours per episode" (p.110); "corridor care continued to be utilised during May" at both (p.64).
- "Formal complaints at RWT have increased by 50% compared with last year" (p.64). WHT complaint response compliance "reduced to 32%, with Surgical Services achieving 9%" (p.65, p.110).
- RWT UEC escalation: "the Trust's usual Level 4 response actions failed to have the required impact" during the June heatwave (p.163).
- HSE Notice of Contravention issued August 2025 over Entonox exposure in delivery suites at both RWT and WHT; RWT has "submitted a business case for permanent destruction unit" (p.59).
- Human Tissue Authority inspection of December 2025: WHT as licence holder has had its corrective action plan accepted; "only four of the original 18 standards remain open" (p.110); three open corrective actions relate to "Mortuary security access and CCTV (RWT)" (p.28).
- Lord Mann Review into Antisemitism and Racism in the NHS (published 4 June 2026): board asked to adopt the IHRA definition of antisemitism "with immediate effect" and to take board-level training (p.46, Enclosure 6.3).
- A stroke mortality alert covering April 2024–March 2025 at RWT is under review; "Local data does not currently suggest the Trust is an outlier and concerns have been escalated to the national team" (p.65).
- Long-term financial plan: "the long-term financial plan required the Group to eliminate a £37m deficit over the next two years" (p.11, minutes). RWT's 2026/27 plan "is breakeven following national deficit support of £16.6m" (p.171).
- Group Standing Financial Instructions and Standing Orders merged into a single group policy "for the first time" (p.226, Enclosure 11.2).
- Partnerships & Transformation Committee "note concerns regarding Executive capacity in relation to transformation programme and potential NHS Anchor" (p.66).
