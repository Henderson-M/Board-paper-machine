## Top lines

[LEAD] Both trusts closed 2025/26 with reported deficits of £46.4m against breakeven plans, but underlying deficits (excluding lost deficit support funding) were £85.3m each.
[Evidence] UHL: "The YTD position for the Trust is a deficit of £46.4m (subject to audit) against a plan of £0m. The main drivers are loss of DSF funding £25.9mA and non delivery of CIP £19.4mA. Excluding DSF, this position equates to a deficit of £85.3m, which is in line with the position notified to NHSE." — p.32, [Paper D2, UHL IPR Executive Summary]; UHN draft accounts "confirm UHN has delivered the forecast outturn position it agreed with NHSE at month 10... Underlying deficit results in non compliant financial plan for 2026/27 – 2028/29" with control total deficit of £46.386m (excl. £97m impairment) — p.85, p.76, [Paper D3, UHN IPR]

[LEAD] UHN's board was asked to approve June cash draw-downs of £5.399m (KGH) and £3.5m (NGH) because the trusts have no deficit support funding under a non-compliant financial plan.
[Evidence] "The UHN Boards are asked to approve the cash draw-down requests for both NGH and KGH for June. KGH at £5.399m and NGH at £3.5m which reflects the lack of deficit support funding (DSF) given the non-compliant financial plan." — p.23, [Paper D1, IPR Cover]

[LEAD] UHL's 2026/27 cost improvement target is around £99m, with "limited assurance" on the savings still to be found in bottom-up plans.
[Evidence] "RDO report – high level CIP plan 'best case' broadly aligned to target of £99m. Detailed planning for individual CIP still underway with CMGs – limited assurance given size of CIP 'to find' in bottom up plans." — p.155, [Paper E2, UHL Finance and Investment Committee escalation, 29 April 2026]

[LEAD] A coroner has issued a Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths notice to UHL, the MHRA and Medtronic after a patient died from a dialysis catheter device failure at a UHL satellite dialysis unit; a similar non-fatal event occurred at another satellite unit last November.
[Evidence] "Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths Notice. HM Coroner Lincolnshire issued this notice on 1st April to MHRA, UHL and Medtronic Ltd. A death resulted from a device failure in an indwelling haemodialysis catheter in a satellite dialysis unit. Investigation revealed a similar non fatal event at a different satellite unit in November last year." — p.151, [Paper E1, UHL Quality Committee escalation, 30 April 2026]

[LEAD] Kettering General Hospital's maternity service remains under the NHS England Maternity and Neonatal Intensive Support Team (MatNeoIST) and a national/ICB Improvement Oversight & Assurance Group, working toward regulatory exit under a "single regulatory recovery framework" aligning CQC actions.
[Evidence] "The implementation of the Perinatal Safety Improvement Programme (PSIP)... has created a single, coherent regulatory recovery framework. This aligns PSIP, CQC actions, MatNeoIST milestones and wider national requirements... maternity services progressing toward sustained regulatory recovery and MatNeoIST exit, subject to continued embedding"; "NHS England and LNR ICB facilitated Improvement Oversight & Assurance Group (IOAG): most recent meeting 22 April 2026." — p.176–179, [Paper F, KGH Maternity and Neonatal Intensive Support Team Programme]

[LEAD] UHN was reporting a £55m deficit at Month 11, £42m adverse to plan, with NHSE having approved an emergency cash support request, and its National Oversight Framework rating had moved to segment 4.
[Evidence] "UHN was reporting a deficit of £55m at month 11 which was 42m adverse to plan, with removed deficit support funding and under delivery of Cost Improvement Plan (CIP) the main reasons. The level of deficit exacerbated cash pressures with a request for cash support now having been approved by NHSE"; "The NOF rating for UHN had moved to a rating of 4, limited assurance." — p.7, p.9, [Paper A, minutes of 9 April 2026 meeting]

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts are in NHSE Tier 1 for 62-day cancer performance, all sites missing the 70% standard, with UHL at 54.6% year-to-date.
[Evidence] "62-day cancer performance significantly challenged - UHL 53.0% (target 70%), KGH 62.5%, NGH 58.2% (target 70%); both in Tier 1 for 62-day performance nationally." — p.26, [Paper D1, IPR Cover]; "Cancer 62 Day Combined... 70% [target]... 54.6% [YTD]" — p.38, [Paper D2, UHL Oversight Framework metrics]

[WORTH WATCHING] UHL's elective waiting list stood at around 114,000 with RTT performance at 59% and roughly 2,500 patients waiting over 52 weeks — more than double the under-1% plan.
[Evidence] "The final submitted figures for UHL for March are a total waiting list of c. 113,973 and RTT performance of 59.09%... The current expected final March position for 52-week waits is c.2,500 patients... which would represent 2.17% of the total waiting list size... The plan for 25/26 and 26/27 is to deliver less than 1%." — p.31, [Paper D2, UHL IPR]

[WORTH WATCHING] Resident-doctor exception reports at Northampton General doubled over the year to 671, with 24 Guardian's fines issued and persistent rota gaps in General Medicine, Oncology and Haematology.
[Evidence] "NGH: 671 exception reports submitted; volume doubled over the year. This is largely attributable to rota gaps and additional hours worked by doctors... 652.7 additional hours claimed (16.3 working weeks). 62% resolved by payment; 24 Guardian's fines issued"; common themes "Persistent rota gaps, particularly in General Medicine, Oncology, and Haematology." — p.192, [Paper H, UHN Guardians of Safe Working Consolidated Annual Report 2025-26]

[WORTH WATCHING] Northampton General's hospital standardised mortality ratio is rated "above expected," which the trust attributes to data and coding factors rather than care quality.
[Evidence] "HSMR and SMR are both 'above expected' ranges for NGH... current analysis indicates that recent HSMR movement is driven primarily by data and methodological factors rather than an increase in observed mortality or evidence of deteriorating care quality." — p.88, p.97, [Paper D3, UHN IPR Caring/Effective domains]

[WORTH WATCHING] UHN's KGH/NGH audit committees took "no assurance" on the board assurance framework deep-dive and "limited assurance" on overdue internal-audit actions, with Estates Strategy and Salary Overpayments actions past their due dates and no update given to internal audit.
[Evidence] "actions from the other two reports [Salary Overpayments and Estates Strategy], plus a number of other significant actions were now beyond, at, or fast approaching their due dates, with no update having been given to the IA team. The committees stressed the need for these actions to receive greater management focus, which the CFO and CEO agreed to take up" — p.173–174, [Paper E7, UHN Audit Committees upward report, 22 April 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] UHL forgoes around £28m a year of income for over-performing on emergency activity because of its block contract.
[Evidence] "Emergency/Non Elective inpatient activity continues to over-perform; at M12 the additional activity over the year equated to £28m of income that won't be received due to the block arrangement however the cost of delivery will be in the Trust's non-pay." — p.32, [Paper D2, UHL IPR]
