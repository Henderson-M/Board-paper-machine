# NHS South Yorkshire Integrated Care Board — 2 September 2026

## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has withheld South Yorkshire ICB's quarter 2 deficit support funding, and set a 100%-of-CIP test before any more is released.
[Evidence] "Award Q1 funding for the ICB in recognition of the progress made to date. **Withhold Q2 funding for the ICB until further progress is demonstrated on de-risking the delivery of the plan (including CIP delivery).**" — [Enclosure 15, Appendix 1 — NHS England letter, 24 July 2026, ref 068_SA_RD]
[Evidence] "To qualify for further DSF funding, the requirements are that the ICB must demonstrate 100% of the value of CIP is fully developed and being implemented, with the ICBs internal risk adjusted forecast demonstrating confidence in delivery of forecast (as independently reviewed by region), supported by Board assurance from the Chair and CEO." — [same]
[Evidence] The decision point is imminent: "eligibility for Q2 DSF, along with any decision to award Q3 DSF, will be re-assessed later in the year using month 5 results and the decision will be required to be determined by region by 30th September." — [same]
[Evidence] The withheld money is the ICB's entire reported deficit: "the Integrated Care Board (ICB) is reporting a year-to-date and forecast position of £1.79m deficit which solely relates to the Deficit Support Funding for Month 4 that has not yet been agreed." — [Enclosure 14 — Month 4 Finance Report]

[LEAD] NHS England has placed South Yorkshire ICB in a named regional risk category — "Watch: Targeted support" — under a new North East and Yorkshire regime that replaces earned autonomy with "earned intervention".
[Evidence] "Your organisation has been assessed regionally as 'Watch: Targeted support'. We will now work with you on the support, actions and intervention which will support and ensure plan delivery within the framework shown at Appendix 1." — [Enclosure 15, Appendix 1 — NHSE letter, 24 July 2026]
[Evidence] The annex sets out the shift: "Current Approach: Earned autonomy for Boards over time. **New Approach: Earned intervention for Boards** – i.e. all organisations with accepted plans have autonomy and earn intervention through under delivery" — [Enclosure 15, Appendix — "Delivering the NEY 2026/27 Financial Plan Delivery Approach", presented by Tarryn Lake, Regional Director of Finance]
[Evidence] Five stratification bands are set out, from "On track - earned autonomy" through "Financial Recovery Required and 'watch' approach by Region", "Financial Recovery Required with active involvement by Region", to "Mandated national support or non-compliant plan"; stratification is "refreshed monthly using a blended view of plan delivery, run-rate trajectory, cash, underlying deficit and recovery confidence." — [same]

[LEAD] NHS England's own regional letter puts the North East and Yorkshire 2025/26 outturn at a £378.6m deficit, £145.3m off plan — which it calls one of the worst results of any region in England.
[Evidence] "The NEY regional finance position for the year ended 2025/26 was a deficit of £378.6m. This was £145.3m off plan representing one of the most challenged levels of performance against plan across all regions." — [Enclosure 15, Appendix — Delivering the NEY 2026/27 Financial Plan]
[Evidence] The current year is already adrift: "At month 3 the region reported a £152.7m deficit (excluding the impact of deficit support funding), against a planned £119m deficit, resulting in a £33.7m adverse variance to plan... Thirteen of the 33 providers in the region reported an adverse variance to plan at month 3, with ICBs reporting on plan." — [Enclosure 15, Appendix 1 — NHSE letter, 24 July 2026]
[Evidence] "Across the region, cost improvement plans were behind plan at month 3 by £35m. This is a deterioration from month 2 where cost improvement plans were behind plan by c£24m." — [same]
[Evidence] "All organisations continue to forecast delivery of the full year £1.6bn efficiency plan for the region with £287m delivered at month 3, leaving 82% of the full year plan to be delivered over the remaining 9 months of the year." — [same]
[Note] This letter is addressed to South Yorkshire ICB but its finance annex describes the whole NEY region — it is regional data surfacing in a single ICB's public pack.

[LEAD] NHS England's statutory annual assessment says South Yorkshire ICB failed two statutory financial duties in 2025/26 on a £48.9m deficit.
[Evidence] "Financial performance is the most significant area of deterioration in the year. The ICB reported a deficit of £48.9m, contributing to a system deficit of £54.6m and **did not meet two statutory financial duties.** Although the ICB delivered its savings plan the year-end position for 2025/26 deteriorated from plan and from the previous year, reflecting underlying cost pressures and the scale of the financial challenge." — [Enclosure 17, Appendix 1 — NHS England 2025/26 Annual Assessment of South Yorkshire ICB, 24 July 2026, ref 064_SA_RD, signed Samantha Allen CBE, Interim Regional Director NHSE North East & Yorkshire]

[LEAD] The same assessment says South Yorkshire has become one of the worst systems in England for keeping autistic adults and adults with a learning disability out of hospital.
[Evidence] "There are continued challenges in reducing reliance on inpatient care for autistic people and under-18s with learning disability and autism needs and **South Yorkshire ICB has become one of the poorest performing systems in England in respect of adults, with a particularly high rate of admissions.**" — [Enclosure 17, Appendix 1 — NHSE 2025/26 Annual Assessment]

[LEAD] ADHD "right to choose" activity is growing at about 10% a month and is now forecast to overspend by £11.6m this year.
[Evidence] "Activity information is now available relating to ADHD Right to Choose activity and this has continued to grow at approximately 10% per month resulting in a YTD pressure of £3.8m and a forecast of £11.6m overspend. A detailed action plan is in place to manage and contain this activity across finance, contracting and commissioning." — [Enclosure 14 — Month 4 Finance Report]
[Evidence] The forecast break-even relies on offsetting it: "The forecast at Month 4 is break even due to limited information being available in some areas and assumes the full DSF of £21.5m is awarded in year. This forecast also includes £11.4m risk mitigation linked to the ADHD pressure and £1m for IS contracts." — [same]

[LEAD] The ICB is carrying £59.2m of gross financial risk against a £119.5m efficiency programme, with continuing healthcare delivering barely half its target.
[Evidence] "As part of planning, £96m of risks were identified, mainly relating to efficiency, prescribing and CHC and other activity pressures. Risks have been reviewed and are now £59.2m (£60.7m at Month 3). This risk includes under delivery against the efficiency plan of £19.6m." — [Enclosure 14 — Month 4 Finance Report]
[Evidence] "CHC and S117 schemes have been developed but there is a significant risk to delivery including £1.4m of opportunities that do not have a clear plan to deliver. **CHC efficiency is estimated to deliver 53% (£9.7m of the £18.4m target). NHS England have been asked to review the programme to provide assurance** on the focus on the plan for CHC and Individual placements and also support actions to increase the pace of deliverability of plans." — [same]
[Evidence] "The efficiency schemes are now divided into four main programme areas with a total plan of £119.5m" with "£41m delivered in full, mainly relating to zero based budgeting, where contracts have been defunded (£12.2m) and technical [schemes]" — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] Sheffield Teaching's electronic patient record has knocked the trust — and the South Yorkshire aggregate — out of national elective reporting for more than a year.
[Evidence] "Elective performance was impacted by the implementation of a new Electronic Patient Record (EPR) at Sheffield Teaching Hospitals in July 2025, which led to the Trust's live data being removed from national reporting and their pre-July position being imputed instead." — [Enclosure 17, Appendix 1 — NHSE 2025/26 Annual Assessment]
[Evidence] Still unresolved in the current IPR: "STH data is not currently available as a result of ongoing data validation following their recent installation of their Electronic Patient Record system. This is affecting aggregated South Yorkshire position, these figures have been removed." — [Enclosure 11 — South Yorkshire: Delivering our Performance Objectives (IPR), p.12 note]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board is being asked to collapse a board assurance framework of 87 risks into a handful of principal risks.
[Evidence] "A significant simplification of the Board Assurance Framework was proposed, moving from 87 existing risks to a smaller set of strategic objectives and principal risks." — [Enclosure 09a — BAF 2026/27, Board Development Session 5 August 2026]
[Evidence] The July minutes record the board finding the existing framework "comprehensive but unwieldy" — [Enclosure 04a — Unadopted minutes, 1 July 2026]
[Evidence] The proposed principal risks include "There is a risk that ICB culture, leadership and consequent behaviour limits success." — [Enclosure 09a]

[WORTH WATCHING] Community waiting lists over 52 weeks stand at 9,582 across South Yorkshire — 9,547 of them at Sheffield Children's, almost all neurodiversity assessments.
[Evidence] "Community Waiting List over 52 weeks... SYICB Actual Jun-26 9,582, Standard 0. Actual: DBTHFT 0, RDASH 0, SCH 9,547, SHSC 0, STH 2, SWYPFT 0, TRFT 33" — [Enclosure 11 — IPR]
[Evidence] "The long waiters over 52 weeks are primarily driven by neurodiversity waits... The large number of long neurodiversity assessment waits are not unusual nationally, but SCH is one of few providers to code them as community waits." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] RDaSH, Yorkshire Ambulance Service and Leeds Community have been put into a national sickness reduction programme.
[Evidence] "Three providers (RDASH, YAS and Leeds Community) have been put forward for a national sickness reduction programme and we will look to spread learning across the region from this programme." — [Enclosure 15, Appendix 1 — NHSE letter, 24 July 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2025/26 annual report and accounts are adopted today, after the audit committee twice deferred approval over unresolved audit issues.
[Evidence] "It was noted that, at its meeting on 18 June, the Audit and Risk Committee deferred formal approval and adoption of the Annual Report and Accounts pending resolution of the outstanding audit issues and receipt of the final external audit opinion. Following a further meeting on 14 July, the Committee received and reviewed the audited Annual Report and Accounts and subsequently recommended them to the Board for approval" — [Enclosure 12 — 2025/26 ICB Annual Accounts and Annual Report]
[Evidence] "the ICB's annual accounts and report have not yet been submitted due to outstanding audit queries, with all responses provided and an extension agreed with NHS England." — [Enclosure 04a — Unadopted minutes, 1 July 2026, minute 93/26]
[Evidence] External auditor is KPMG; internal auditor 360 Assurance. "Regulations required a final, complete draft of the ICB's Annual Report and Accounts to be submitted to NHS England and to our external auditors, KPMG, by 9.00 am on Monday 27 April 2026." — [Enclosure 12]
[Note] The pack states the accounts are audited and recommended for adoption but **does not reproduce the audit opinion or the VfM conclusion in the papers provided** — "Due to the size of the documents, they have not been [attached]". See FOI below.

## FOI leads

[FOI] **The full 2025/26 annual report and accounts, the KPMG audit opinion and the value-for-money conclusion.** The pack adopts the accounts but does not attach them, and does not state the audit opinion or whether VfM was qualified — despite NHSE saying the ICB missed two statutory financial duties. *Request from South Yorkshire ICB:* the audited annual report and accounts, KPMG's ISA 260 / Annual Auditor's Report to those charged with governance, the VfM conclusion and any statutory recommendation, and the audit committee minutes of 18 June and 14 July 2026 recording the "outstanding audit issues".

[FOI] **Which two statutory financial duties were breached, and NHSE's supporting analysis.** *Request from South Yorkshire ICB and NHS England (North East & Yorkshire):* correspondence identifying the specific duties under the NHS Act 2006 that were not met in 2025/26 and any consequent action.

[FOI] **The full regional risk stratification.** The letter says NEY now stratifies every organisation monthly into five financial risk bands and that SY ICB sits in "Watch: Targeted support". *Request from NHS England (North East & Yorkshire):* the current stratification for all 33 providers and the region's ICBs, the monthly refreshes since April 2026, and the deficit support funding award/withhold decisions for each organisation for Q1 and Q2 2026/27. *Why it matters:* this is a named, ranked list of which northern organisations NHSE thinks are failing financially.

[FOI] **The board assurance letters from chairs and chief executives.** DSF eligibility now requires "Board assurance from the Chair and CEO". *Request from NHS England (NE&Y) and South Yorkshire ICB:* the board assurance letters submitted for Q1 and Q2 2026/27 DSF, and the region's independent review of the ICB's risk-adjusted forecast.

[FOI] **The CHC efficiency programme NHSE has been asked to review.** *Request from South Yorkshire ICB:* the terms of reference and output of the NHS England review of the continuing healthcare and individual placements efficiency programme, and the scheme-level plan showing the £8.7m of the £18.4m target that is not forecast to deliver.

[FOI] **The ADHD right-to-choose action plan.** *Request from South Yorkshire ICB:* the "detailed action plan... to manage and contain this activity", the provider-level right-to-choose spend, and any proposals to restrict or re-contract right-to-choose ADHD assessment.
