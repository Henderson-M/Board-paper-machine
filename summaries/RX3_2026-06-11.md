[LEAD] Trust still does not have a compliant bank-cost reduction plan to meet an NHSE Medium Term Plan condition due by end of June, with bank spend having risen to £16.16m in 2025/26.
[Evidence] "the Trust does not yet have a fully compliant bank reduction plan, with additional work continuing to the end of June to identify actions to deliver the full recurrent value of required bank cost reduction (which includes recovering 2025/26 growth). This means the bank condition is not yet fully met at the time of writing." / "bank expenditure rose to approximately £16.16m in 2025/26" — p.47, [Medium Term Plan – Conditions, Assurance and Mobilisation Update]

[LEAD] NHSE assessed the trust's Medium Term Plan as "Compliant with Conditions," with the regional director setting three requirements (sickness plan, bank cost plan, minimal unidentified savings) to be met before end of June 2026.
[Evidence] "NHS England has assessed the Trust's Medium Term Plan (MTP) as 'Compliant with Conditions' and, in the letter from the Regional Director NHS England – North East & Yorkshire, set out specific requirements to be addressed prior to the end of June 2026." — p.44, [Medium Term Plan]

[LEAD] Delayed discharges are costing the trust an estimated £18m a year, with an average 73 inpatient beds (14% of acute capacity) occupied in April by patients clinically ready for discharge but stuck awaiting external care.
[Evidence] "an average of 73 inpatient beds in April were occupied by patients who were clinically ready for discharge but unable to leave due to delays in external care placements and packages. These patients comprised 14% of all adult and older adult acute beds in use … driving an estimated direct cost pressure of circa £18 million annually." — p.54, [Integrated Quality & Performance Report]

[LEAD] Internal audit's draft Head of Internal Audit Opinion for 2025/26 is set to fall to "reasonable assurance," with substantial-assurance reports down from 7 to 3, two limited-assurance reports (EPRR and clinical supervision) and an "adverse outcome" from the cyber Data Security and Protection Toolkit advisory audit.
[Evidence] "it is likely that the Head of Internal Audit Opinion (HOIAO) will be reasonable assurance … two limited assurance reports (for EPRR and clinical supervision) … An adverse outcome from the Data Security and Protection Toolkit Assessment advisory audit … was also considered. It is unlikely that further work … will change the assurance due to the reduction in 'substantial' ratings (down from 7 to 3 currently)." — p.194, [Audit and Risk Committee Key Issues Report]

[LEAD] An independent peer review by Mersey Care Foundation Trust has been commissioned into the trust's adult learning disability inpatient service, where restraint/seclusion hit a "special cause concern" of 1,058 instances in April and the service has been designated as "Requiring Additional Support."
[Evidence] "Trust-wide, 1058 instances of restraint or seclusion were recorded in April – a special cause concern … largely attributable to the Adult Learning Disability (ALD) inpatient service … DTVFCG's ALD service has been formally designated a 'Service Requiring Additional Support' … The Trust has also commissioned an independent peer review by Mersey Care NHS FT to advise on any further improvement actions" — p.55, [Integrated Quality & Performance Report]

[LEAD] The trust received a Regulation 28 (prevention of future deaths) notice from a coroner over a patient death on leave, concerning communication with carers and hospital-leave planning; a formal response was sent on 5 April 2026.

[LEAD] Mental Health Act detention data shows Black/Black British people are detained at roughly twice the local rate of White people, with Black/Black British women "disproportionately affected"; the findings have been escalated to the Mental Health Legislation Committee.
[Evidence] "detention rates in the Trust above national benchmarks: White patients are 1.79 times more likely to be detained than the national average, and Black/Black British female patients are detained at roughly twice the expected rate, indicating potential racial and socio-economic inequalities in crisis care and risk management." — p.56, [Integrated Quality & Performance Report]
