## Top lines

[LEAD] CUH is six months into a multi-year, multi-million-pound recovery programme after the independent Verita investigation found "missed opportunities to identify and avoid potential harm to paediatric orthopaedic patients", overseen by a regulator panel of NHSE, CQC, GMC and Healthwatch.
[Evidence] "In October 2025, Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (CUH) published Verita's independent investigation report into missed opportunities to identify and avoid potential harm to paediatric orthopaedic patients. The investigation report was produced with oversight from NHS England" — p.87, [Verita action plan six-month update, Item 9]; "the report has been subject to external scrutiny through the Paediatric Orthopaedic Incident Oversight Board, comprising representatives from NHS England, the Care Quality Commission, the General Medical Council and Healthwatch" — p.12, [Minutes 11 March 2026, item 10/26]

[LEAD] The investigation made 23 recommendations — all accepted — generating 34 actions over 12 months, several described as "multi-million-pound, multi-year programmes of work".
[Evidence] "the investigation made 23 recommendations all of which were accepted by the Trust. In response the Trust had committed to 34 actions to be delivered over a 12-month period" — p.12, [Minutes, item 10/26]; "several represent multi-million-pound, multi-year programmes of work" — p.87, [Verita action plan update]

[LEAD] Freedom to Speak Up declined across every measure in the 2025 staff survey — reversing prior improvement and falling faster than the national trend — with the sharpest drop in confidence that concerns will be acted upon.
[Evidence] "Staff survey results indicate a decline across all FTSU measures in 2025, reversing previous improvement. The most significant issue is reduced confidence that concerns will be acted upon. While performance remains above national benchmarks, the rate of decline is greater than the national trend." — p.~110, [FTSU Annual Report 2025/26, Item 10]; "303 cases raised 2025/26 (increased from 295 in 2024/25)" — same paper

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is being run with an acting chief executive and an interim chief operating officer — interim senior leadership while it works through the Verita fallout.
[Evidence] Board attendance list: "Ms N Ayton — Acting Chief Executive"; "Mr N Kirby — Interim Chief Operating Officer" — p.4, [Minutes 11 March 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Month 1 was a £3.0m deficit, £2.0m adverse to plan, with the full variance attributed to April's resident doctor strike — against a 26/27 plan that still requires a £59.2m efficiency programme to hit breakeven.
[Evidence] "The Month 1 financial position is a £3.0m deficit for performance management purposes — this is £2.0m adverse to plan due to the estimated £2.0m adverse impact" of industrial action — p.~33, [Finance report]; "The Trust needs to deliver an efficiency programme (PEP) of £59.2m in 26/27 ... Failure to deliver the programme as planned could impact ... financial performance" and "could threaten the planned breakeven financial performance in 26/27" — p.~57, [Finance report key risks]; "the Trust is still reporting a gap in identified values of £3.7m for cost reduction" — p.~33

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust SLAs for 26/27 are unsigned, with "formal disputes regarding the local pricing of activity" heading to national arbitration — a live income risk.
[Evidence] "Trust SLAs are not yet signed with formal disputes regarding the local pricing of activity meaning the Trust could receive less income that was planned for"; mitigation requires the trust to "Obtain a good outcome from the national arbitration for the Trust SLAs" — p.~57, [Finance report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Diagnostic six-week waits deteriorated sharply against plan, and four-hour A&E performance slipped with the trust's national ranking falling to 103rd.
[Evidence] "a further decline in performance for patients waiting over 6 weeks for their diagnostics moving from 29.9% to 37.86% which is significantly behind the plan of 22.0%" — p.~25, [IPR]; "4 hour performance in April 2026 was 69.1%, a decline from 71.5% in March 2026. CUH's national ranking for 4-hour performance declining to 103 in April"; "940 patients were waiting over 52 weeks which is significantly behind the plan position" — p.~25, [IPR]

## FOI leads

[FOI] The number of paediatric orthopaedic patients harmed / recalled is not stated in this pack. Request from CUH: the cohort size identified by the Verita investigation and clinical review, and the harm gradings. Also request the full "Learning, Accountability and Change" action plan and the costed business case referenced ("a business case has been approved, recruitment will take time" — p.12).

[FOI] A separate "ongoing clinical review" is referenced as distinct from the Verita investigation ("The Board noted the findings of the independent Verita investigation, which is separate from the ongoing clinical review" — p.12). Request its terms of reference, scope and reporting timeline from CUH.

[FOI] The independent medical-workforce "structured listening exercise" was due to report end-May/early-June 2026 ("culminate in a report and playback session ... delivered end of May/beginning of June" — p.88). Request the report and the named independent partner commissioned to run it.

[FOI] SLA "formal disputes regarding the local pricing of activity" referred to national arbitration — request from the Cambridgeshire & Peterborough ICB / CUH the disputed contract values and the services in dispute.
