## Top lines

[LEAD] The trust says it is unlikely to meet NHS England's 10 per cent bank staff spending reduction target this year, with a forecast overshoot of £12.9m.
[Evidence] Target "£53.35m max. exp. target", year-end forecast £66.3m, variance "(12.9)", RAG "Red": "Current performance is adverse to plan - the forecast outturn based on current run-rate (adjusted for £1.0m n/r IA expenditure extrapolates to a potential additional £12.9m of bank expenditure above the annual target threshold. It is unlikely the Trust will be able to meet this target in 26/27." Also: "Year to date bank expenditure exceeds the monthly NHS England expenditure limits for 26/27." — p.122 and p.118, [Finance Report Jul-26]

[LEAD] CUH won a national arbitration against its commissioners over 2026-27 elective pricing but says the ruling still has not been honoured, and its contracts remain unsigned.
[Evidence] "Following arbitration of the 26/27 elective pricing dispute with Central East ICB and Norfolk and Suffolk ICB (as an associate to the contract), NHS England has ruled in favour of the Trust's position. The Trust continues to seek full implementation of the binding arbitration outcome – to date, the outcome has not been appropriately reflected in revised contract offers." (p.85, [Chief Executive and Executive Team Report]). CFO version: "The ICB has queried the outcome with NHSE and the Trust is working with them to bring this to a close" (p.118). Listed as a key financial risk: "Whilst the Trust was successful in a national contract arbitration process, contracts for 26/27 remain unsigned with further negotiation required and other disputes to resolve." — p.111, [Integrated Performance Report July 2026]

[LEAD] The Andrew Kennedy KC clinical review of paediatric orthopaedic cases from 2012 to 2024 is nearing completion and will be published this year, and the trust's own papers warn it may identify further concerns.
[Evidence] "the Andrew Kennedy KC clinical review of paediatric orthopaedic cases from 2012 to 2024 is nearing completion and will be published this year. We are working closely with affected patients and families, including through our Patient Advisory Board to support them over this period including around the publication itself" (p.81, [Chief Executive and Executive Team Report]). Risk section of the Verita response paper: "The publication of the clinical review of cases in the autumn may identify further concerns and generate significant scrutiny. There is a risk that this overshadows the progress and improvements made through the programme, affecting confidence among patients, families, staff and external stakeholders." — p.160, [Learning, Accountability and Change action plan, 9 month progress update, Item 10]

[LEAD] CUH has opened a 31-day consultation on a new operating model that puts 61 staff at risk and creates three clinical care groups led by new managing directors.
[Evidence] "On the 3 of September the Trust began a 31 day consultation on the new Target Operation Modal and associated structures. There are 61 people affected by the proposed changes. The launch of the consultation was supported constructively by staffside." (p.84). CEO summary: "the proposals to move from our existing structure to a new target operating model that will enable the shift of resources, accountability and decision making power to three clinical care groups... Recruitment to the three Managing Director posts has begun and further appointment processes will follow later in the Autumn subject to the consultation outcome." (p.81). Implementation "planned from April 2027". — p.159, [Learning, Accountability and Change action plan]

[LEAD] The trust's estates risk entry says fire safety decant works ordered by the fire service are "off track by some margin" and that capital beyond 2027 is a "funding cliff edge", with high-risk backlog maintenance heading to £27m by 2029.
[Evidence] "The risk score is 20 against a target of 16, with no improvement trajectory projected over the next 12 months. 6FACET survey projections indicate High risk backlog maintenance liability will grow to £27m by 2029; current capital allocation trajectories reduce this to approximately £3.3m", described as "a material residual exposure that requires Board acknowledgement". On fire safety: "the compartmentation works that require wards to be decanted cannot be completed until there is capacity to do so. The planned works in C8 has been deferred due to operational capacity constraints. There is a requirement overseen by the CFRS to concl.de the stage 2 (decant) works by 2027. Currently, the works are off track by some margin". And: "Ring-fenced statutory fire safety capital beyond 2027 has not been confirmed; this represents a material funding cliff edge that will directly increase the fire safety risk trajectory if left unresolved." The entry also notes "The Fire Safety Order 2005 creates personal criminal liability for the Trust's designated Responsible Person." — p.52, [Board Assurance Framework, BAF006 Estate environment compliance and resilience, Item 8]

[LEAD] Beds lost to discharge delays at CUH are running roughly a third higher than a year ago, and the trust says it is 41 medical beds short.
[Evidence] "The numbers and length of time of patients waiting for discharge beyond their discharge ready date worsened in July, with 233.2 beds lost to external delays compared with 176 in July 2025. CUH is using winter capacity to maintain safe care (although baseline demand and capacity analysis completed earlier this year indicates the Trust is currently shortly 41 medical beds)." (p.83, [Chief Executive and Executive Team Report]). IPR detail: "medically ready patients waiting 7 days or more increasing from 40 to 51 patients, including 16 patients waiting over 21 days"; Pathway 1 beds lost rose "from 63.9 to 70.9 bed days per day". — p.104, [Integrated Performance Report July 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Two board committees have challenged the "substantial" assurance ratings the trust has given itself on access and estates, and the finance director is reworking the financial sustainability risk.
[Evidence] "The aligned BAF entries were presented to the Quality Committee and Performance and Finance Committee on 2 September. In discussion it was agreed that the ratings of substantial assurance for BAF items 004 (Access) and 006 (Estates) should be reviewed. The Chief Finance Officer is also undertaking a review of the financial sustainability risk." ## Other specifics worth having

- Month 4 position: £0.4m surplus year to date on an NHS financial performance basis, in line with plan, but including £4.4m of cost pressures, £2.3m of non-recurrent benefits and £3.8m of favourable operational variances. Reported gross deficit of £7.0m year to date against a planned £4.5m surplus, the gap driven by a £6.8m "loss recognised on peppercorn lease disposals" that is added back. Cash £40.8m against plan of £52.7m, blamed on "delays in commissioner payments under Payment by Results". (p.118, p.121)
- Efficiency: PEP target £59.2m including £25.1m cost reduction. £16.6m delivered at month 4, but "a gap of £2.0m in identified scheme values for cost reduction schemes" and the year to date "is driven by higher non-recurrent scheme delivery (£9.0m) and masks £1.0m of lower productivity benefits than planned". Monthly requirement rises to £5.5m in months 7 to 12. (p.133, p.134)
- Financial sustainability BAF entry: "While the Board has approved a breakeven plan for 2026/27, years two and three of the medium-term plan currently show deficits", and "Reliance on non-recurrent financial support in 2026/27 does not secure a sustainable exit financial rate into 2027/28". (p.70, p.73)
- NHS Oversight Framework: segment 2, rank 28 at Q4 2025-26; access to services and effectiveness and experience domains both in segment 3; finance and productivity segment 1. National Cost Collection Index for 2024-25 was 106, "meaning that costs were 6% higher than expected", giving an estimated score of 3 on the new relative cost difference metric. (p.89, p.95, p.111, p.122)
- Access: 4-hour performance 68.43% in July, "6% lower than July 2025"; 838 patients waiting over 52 weeks against a plan of 201, with 52 waiting over 65 weeks; RTT waiting list 60,206; 18-week performance 65.3%; diagnostic 6-week breaches 26.6%; corridor care in ED 525 in July. (p.97, p.100, p.101, p.103)
- Infection control: one hospital onset MRSA bacteraemia in July, two this year; E. coli rate "towards the higher end of the Shelford Group benchmark range"; "The Trust has limited side-room capacity, and this remains a longstanding and ongoing risk"; "Despite escalations and the introduction of the rolling deep clean programme, cleaning standards remain variable and inconsistent across the Trust"; "Poor medical representation limits the impact of the MRSA Learning Review". (p.105, p.106)
- Guardian of safe working, Q1 2026-27: 358 exception reports, up from 304 in Q4, with 8 relating to immediate patient safety; locum shift fill rate 91.87%; fines of £1,403.79. Detriment survey drew only 17 responses from 1,100 resident doctors (1.54%), of whom 6 per cent reported actual detriment and 24 per cent threat of detriment, with 47 per cent feeling they were not discouraged from exception reporting. Note the response rate before using these figures. (p.161, p.165, p.167)
- Research: BAF009 warns of "failure to secure NIHR BRC re-designation, with associated loss of funding (contract worth ~£86m with CUH overheads ~19%, status and influence)", with designation renewal due in 2028; the outline bid was due in September 2026. Clinical trials set up within 90 days at 35.6 per cent in July, "below the national average". (p.66, p.88)
- Corporate risks scored 20 named in the BAF include healthcare associated infections, radiopharmacy services (risk of losing the MHRA "Specials Manufacturing Licence"), cyber security, ATC FM contract performance and the ATC PFI contract. (p.30, p.47, p.51)
- Two July regulatory inspections: an unannounced Human Tissue Authority inspection of the mortuary "found no critical issues; minor and moderate findings concerned the estate and traceability", and a CQC IR(ME)R inspection "identified no regulatory breaches requiring an action plan". Formal reports awaited. (p.82)
- Capital projects: Cambridge Cancer Research Hospital full business case due for board approval by December 2026, construction September 2027 to end 2030; Cambridge Children's Hospital Building Safety Act submission January 2027, "The most significant risk to these timelines is securing the full capital funding required". (p.86, p.87)
