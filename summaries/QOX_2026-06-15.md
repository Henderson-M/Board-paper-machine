# BSW, Dorset & Somerset ICB Cluster Board (meeting in public) — 15 June 2026

- **Source:** https://bsw.icb.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/06/260615_Cluster-Board-Meeting-in-Public-Pack.pdf
- **Pack:** 2 files (combined pack 162 pages + public-question response)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-16T00:00:00Z
- **NOTE — shared cluster board:** This is a single "Cluster Board" now covering THREE ICBs jointly — NHS Bath & NE Somerset, Swindon and Wiltshire (BSW, ods QOX), NHS Dorset (QVV) and NHS Somerset (QSL). The three ICBs share one board, one executive team, one set of committees and (in this pack) consolidated finance and performance reporting, ahead of a planned full legal merger into a single ICB in **April 2027**. Correspondent: Joe.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Three south-west ICBs confirm they are merging into one ICB from April 2027, with a 45-day staff consultation now closed and recruitment into the new, smaller structure starting 10 June.
[Evidence] "On the 1 May 2026, the 45-day consultation on the new structures closed... over one thousand separate pieces of individual feedback... On 1 June 2026, the new structures were briefed and published to all ICB colleagues. The recruitment and selection process to populate roles in the new structure will begin from 10 June 2026, once the full People Impact Assessment (PIA) is complete." — p.5-6, [CEO Public Report, item 4]; merger date "full ICB merger in April 2027" — p.27, [Developing our Strategic Ambitions, item 5]

[LEAD] CEO frames the reorganisation as a single streamlined commissioning organisation and concedes the scale of change is destabilising staff.
[Evidence] "Our three ICBs are now entering a significant new phase in this transition. We must continue to deliver our statutory functions while also evolving into a more streamlined, strategic commissioning organisation. A change process on this scale is hugely unsettling." — p.6, [CEO Public Report, item 4]

[LEAD] The cluster carries a £207.4m combined in-year savings programme for 2026/27 across the three ICBs, with a reported net financial risk of £27.9m at month 1.
[Evidence] "There is no variance reported at month 1 against the savings programme forecasted to deliver £207.4m this financial year. The reported net risk position remains at £27.9m which is in line with planning submission." — p.49, [Cluster Finance Report M1]. Per-ICB savings: BSW £73.2m, Dorset £55.3m, Somerset £78.9m — p.50-52, [Cluster Finance Report M1].

[LEAD] In the (Dorset/Somerset) public response, NHS Dorset admits it is the only ICB in the South West not funding a NICE-approved MS treatment (FES) and says the merger will be used to standardise commissioning policy.
[Evidence] "It is not currently funded in Dorset. All other ICB's in the South West provide some element of funding... NHS Dorset is aware that we are an outlier in our funding stance in the South-West... As we form the new Integrated Care Board... we will review our commissioning policies... reduce any unwarranted variation within the new wider ICB area." — p.1-2, [Response to Public Question]

[LEAD] Multiple BSW acute trusts remain segment-downgraded specifically because of financial deficit under NHSE's oversight framework: GWH (segment 3), RUH (segment 4), SFT (segment 3), each flagged "YES" to downgrade-due-to-deficit at Q3.
[Evidence] "Is segment downgraded due to financial deficit? YES YES YES" (across GWH, RUH, SFT) — p.62 region, [BSW M12 System Integrated Performance Report, NOF summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cluster admits its underlying financial position is materially worse than the on-plan headline — a £66.5m underlying deficit is cited at year-end (in the Somerset system report).
[Evidence] "As at month 12, the assessed underlying position at the end of 2025/26 remains at a £66.5m deficit." — p.159 region, [Somerset M12 System Integrated Performance Report]. [INFERENCE] Worth confirming which system(s) this £66.5m underlying figure relates to and how it reconciles with the cluster's "balanced" M1 outturn.

[WORTH WATCHING] System balance in 2025/26 was propped up by year-end deficit-support funding rather than underlying delivery — Dorset's £13.2m "surplus" was entirely a national redistribution of unachieved deficit support.
[Evidence] "Dorset ICS reported a year end position of £13.2m surplus against a breakeven plan... achieved due to a year-end national redistribution of unachieved deficit support funding of £13.2m." — p.97-98, [Dorset M12 IPR]. UHD "£5.0m surplus to plan, due to additional NHSE deficit support funding monies received in M12"; SWASFT, DHC similarly — p.49/49, [Dorset M12 IPR]

[WORTH WATCHING] System-wide workforce-reduction programme (restore to March 2022 size) is significantly behind plan, with non-clinical admin cuts the biggest miss.
[Evidence] "WTE reduction across non-clinical support services – admin and clerical roles, failed to realise with a net reduction of 47 WTE across the three Trusts against a planned 204 WTE reduction (157 adverse variance)." — p.region [Dorset M12 IPR, workforce]; "off-plan for the WTE reduction programme by 664 WTE" at year end — same report.

[WORTH WATCHING] Cancer performance: a named acute is well below the 62-day standard — 61.3% against a 78.9% plan (Mar-26), a -17.6% gap.
[Evidence] "Cancer 62-day standard FDP Mar-26 ... 78.9% 61.3% -17.6%" (trust column) — p.region, [Cluster performance overview scorecard / BSW M12 IPR]. [INFERENCE] Worth identifying which named trust this column represents before use.
