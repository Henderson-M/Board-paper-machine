## Top lines

[LEAD] 1940s St Helier maternity/neonatal block (E Block) is confirmed contaminated with Pseudomonas and Legionella and the Group Board has decided the whole building must be vacated for a full water-infrastructure refit — potentially dispersing inpatient maternity and neonatal births to other units.
[Evidence] "The Board has agreed that... a complete refurbishment of the water infrastructure in the building will be necessary. To carry out that refurbishment, it will be necessary to temporarily vacate the building" and "If services cannot all be decanted from E block and retained on the St Helier site... we may be able to ask our neighbouring units to take on inpatient maternity and neonatal care for the St Helier catchment whilst work on E block is carried out" — p.20, p.26, [Water Safety, E Block, St Helier Hospital, Item 2.2]

[LEAD] Two named external expert reviews are in the pack; one (Dr Susanne Surman-Lee / Leegionella Ltd) concludes the Trust is in statutory breach and "exposed to enforcement action", the other (IPC Partners) explicitly advised AGAINST closing E Block — the Executive overrode that advice, citing asbestos.
[Evidence] "The Water Safety Plan and operational arrangements currently do not demonstrate full compliance with statutory duties under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, COSHH Regulations 2002, and the HSE Approved Code of Practice L8 (ACOP L8), nor with mandatory healthcare guidance including HTM 04-01" and "These omissions collectively represent a failure to evidence compliance with HSE and DHSC-mandated water-safety frameworks, leaving the Trust exposed to enforcement action" — p.45–46, [Appendix 2: Independent Report by Dr Surman-Lee]. IPC Partners: "Overall, there does not appear to be sufficient evidence to recommend the full closure of the E block or neonatal unit" — p.33/40, [Appendix 1: IPC Partners Report]. Trust response: "one (from IPC Partners) argued that a closure of E block would not be necessary. However... the authors would not have been aware of some of the considerations above (e.g. presence of asbestos)" — p.24, [Item 2.2]

[LEAD] NHS England has placed the GESH Group into "enhanced oversight for finance" — a regulatory escalation the CEO confirms in writing; the Month 2 finance report carries only "Limited Assurance" and warns of "material pressure on the year end forecast".
[Evidence] "The decision by NHS England to place the Group into enhanced oversight for finance underlines the seriousness of the challenge facing both our organisation and the wider NHS. While this inevitably increases the level of external scrutiny..." — p.89, [Group CEO's Report, Item 1.5]. Finance committee assurance: "Limited Assurance: The report and discussions did not provide sufficient assurance that the system of internal control is adequate and operating effectively and significant improvements are required" — p.93, [Month 2 Finance Report, Item 3.2]

[LEAD] A neonatal unit MRSA outbreak has been running at St Helier since December 2025 — 12 cases to date — with the action plan awaiting the E Block decant/duct cleaning.
[Evidence] "Neonatal Unit (NNU) MRSA Outbreak: 12 cases to date since December 2025. Last positive case 6th May. Action plan implemented, awaiting decant options/allow duct work cleaning. No clinical infections to date." — p.101, [Integrated Quality & Performance Report, Item 3.3]

[LEAD] An independent Royal College of Physicians invited review into Epsom & St Helier's Interstitial Lung Disease service has found care fell below standard — but the full RCP report is NOT in this public pack despite being flagged for publication.
[Evidence] "One of the most significant issues before the Board at this meeting is the publication of the independent Royal College of Physicians invited review into the care provided to patients within the Interstitial Lung Disease service at Epsom and St Helier... Where care has fallen below the standards that patients rightly expect, we have acknowledged that, apologised and have taken steps to ensure that we learn." — p.80, [Group CEO's Report, Item 1.5]. May minutes: "The expectation was that the RCP report would be published in full as part of the Board papers for the next Board meeting on 9 July 2026" — p.7, [Minutes 08 May 2026]

[LEAD] Near-complete refresh of the Group executive: a new CFO, MD for Epsom & St Helier, and Group Chief Nursing Officer all incoming, while interim MD Alex Shaw departs to UH Sussex mid-August.
[Evidence] "Azara Mukhtar will join the Group in August as our new Group Chief Finance Officer... Chinyama Okunuga will join the Group on 1 October as Managing Director for Epsom and St Helier... In November, Tracy Luckett will join the Group as Group Chief Nursing Officer... Following Alex Shaw's departure in mid-August, Rebecca Suckling... will undertake the role of Interim Managing Director" — p.89–90, [Group CEO's Report, Item 1.5]

[WORTH WATCHING] £57m national capital secured over four years for a St Helier Emergency Department rebuild, after the CQC service inspection cited the current ED estate as a driver of operational pressure.
[Evidence] "confirmation that the Group has secured £57 million of national capital funding over the next four years to support the redevelopment of the Emergency Department at St Helier Hospital" — p.81, [Group CEO's Report, Item 1.5]

[WORTH WATCHING] Epsom & St Helier's 4-hour ED performance has fallen sharply since it implemented a "UTC-First" model and decommissioned ED Same Day Emergency Care in April 2026; a recovery plan needs ~£2m.
[Evidence] "ESTH 4-hour ED performance declined to 61.1% in May 2026 compared to 71.1% in April 2026. 12-hour waits also deteriorated to 20.2% in May 2026 compared to 15.3% the previous month. The deterioration has been driven by the GIRFT UEC recommendation and implementation of UTC First and the decommissioning of ED SDEC on 22nd April 2026" — p.102, [IQPR, Item 3.3]; "Delivery of the plan requires targeted investment, with a significant proportion of the £2m identified through business planning" — p.145, [UEC Recovery Plan, Item 3.4]

[WORTH WATCHING] Maternity safety concerns at St George's: MNSI escalated a thematic concern over CTG interpretation (5-day action plan required); a neonatal-death cluster in Jan/Feb 2026 has prompted a thematic review; and an ESH maternity patient death is under investigation.
[Evidence] "Escalation of a thematic concern from MNSI investigations in relation to CTG interpretation at SGUH. Immediate review of preceding cases and actins coproduced with local, regional and MatNeoIST teams within the 5 day timescale given by MNSI" — p.160, [PQOM Report, Item 3.5.1]; "an increase in the Neonatal death rate partly linked to a cluster of deaths in Jan and Feb 2026... a thematic review of learning has been requested" — p.159; ESH: "The second incident resulted in a patient death, the patient was admitted via the emergency department with gastric obstruction complicated by aspiration post nasogastric tube insertion" — p.101, [IQPR, Item 3.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] St Helier is carrying 32 "super-stranded" patients (LOS >75 days) accounting for 3,381 bed days, and an ESH "never event" (wrong-site local anaesthetic block) was declared in May.
[Evidence] "the discharge of 32 complex patients from the STH site with lengths of stay exceeding 75 days, accounting for 3,381 bed days" — p.102; "Never Events: A Never Event relating to a wrong site local anaesthetic block injection was declared in May 2026" — p.101, [IQPR, Item 3.3]

[FOI] The full RCP invited-review report into the ILD service — flagged for publication in the 9 July pack but not included — should be requested, along with the recommendations and action plan.
[Evidence] Report "had now been received and that all recommendations had been accepted"; "The expectation was that the RCP report would be published in full as part of the Board papers" — p.7, [Minutes 08 May 2026]. *Request from Epsom & St Helier / GESH: the full RCP invited review report into the Interstitial Lung Disease service, the terms of reference, and the accepted recommendations/action plan.*

[FOI] The CQC Well-Led inspection report for Epsom & St Helier (inspected 12–14 May 2026) has been received but not published; the St George's Well-Led report was completed last year.
[Evidence] "Between 12 and 14 May 2026, the Care Quality Commission (CQC) undertook its Well-Led inspection of Epsom and St Helier... We have not yet received the inspection report and expect this in the coming months" — p.82, [Group CEO's Report, Item 1.5]. *Request from CQC/Trust: the final Well-Led inspection reports for both ESH and SGUH once published, and any draft findings/factual-accuracy correspondence.*

[FOI] The long-running Optum/PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System) contract has been referred to the NHS Fraud Agency by a member of the public, with go-live now targeted November 2027 — six years post-signature on a contract expiring 2031; it sits on the corporate risk register at score 16.
[Evidence] Public question: "I have referred the Optum (formerly known as Change Healthcare) PACS contract to the NHS Fraud Agency... the former GESH Director of Finance announced at the 6th November 2025 Board that the go live was now targeted for November 2027, a potential go live date some SIX years post contract signature" — p.17, [Minutes 08 May 2026]; corporate risk "Picture Archiving and Communication System" score 16, Datix 2700 — p.390, [BAF Item 5.4]. *Request from GESH: the Optum/PACS contract (value, term, deliverables, variations), the SWL Imaging Network Board minutes, and the £900k funding spend to date (£34,000 expended per the May response).*
