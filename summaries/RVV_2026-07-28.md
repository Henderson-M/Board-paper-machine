# East Kent Hospitals University Foundation Trust board — 28 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.ekhuft.nhs.uk/about-us/board-of-directors/
- **Pack:** 1 file (combined open meeting pack, 446 pages)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-27T15:00:00+01:00

> Context for the correspondent: East Kent is one of the most heavily scrutinised trusts in the country (post-Kirkup maternity). This pack confirms it is under NHSE enforcement and in the Intensive Recovery Programme, with an interim chair, an acting CEO (the substantive CEO on extended leave) and an acting CMO. Two commissioned external reviews into anaesthetics/critical care and the trust's speak-up culture are being taken **in public**, and an independent stillbirth review is live. This is a high-signal pack — multiple standalone leads.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Two external reviews commissioned after consultants wrote to the chair are being taken in public — Carnall Farrar on critical care/anaesthetics and Helen Buckingham on the trust's speak-up culture — both landing damning findings.
[Evidence] "A number of concerns were raised by staff in the anaesthetics service relating to behaviours, management of patient safety concerns and the impact of medical rota gaps. One investigation and two reviews were commissioned" — p.73, [26/55 Learning from Investigations front sheet]; Carnall Farrar: "services are under significant strain due to consultant vacancies, rota gaps and uneven skill mix… Harm was identified in 16% of incidents raised, which were predominantly from the WHH" — p.74, [26/55 front sheet]

[LEAD] Buckingham review lays out extraordinary leadership instability in black and white — three CEOs in ten years, the sitting CEO on extended leave, the CMO acting up, and a "third Chief Nurse and third Chief Medical Officer".
[Evidence] "The Trust has experienced significant executive churn, with the Quality Committee chair noting they are on their third Chief Nurse and third Chief Medical Officer. There have been three Chief Executive Officers in ten years, and the current Chief Executive is on an extended leave of absence, with the Chief Medical Officer acting up into the role." — p.136, [26/55 App 2 Raising Concerns review]. [Corroborated by register/agenda: Interim Chair Dr Olu Olasode; Acting CEO Des Holden; Acting CMO Dr Helen Mackie; substantive CEO named as Tracey Fletcher in the interests register, p.6]

[LEAD] The trust is under NHSE Enforcement Undertakings and additional licence conditions, sits in Segment 4, and is one of just five trusts nationally in the Intensive Recovery Programme.
[Evidence] "The Trust remains subject to additional licence conditions under Section 111 of the Health and Social Care Act 2012 and to Enforcement Undertakings accepted under Section 106 of the same Act, and is one of a small number of trusts nationally within NHS England's Intensive Recovery Programme (IRP)… The Undertakings reflect serious and sustained concerns about the quality and safety of care, the experience of our staff, and the effectiveness of Board leadership" — p.44; "The Trust remains in Segment 4 and is one of five organisations receiving support through the Intensive Recovery Programme" — p.48, [26/51 Interim Chair's Report]

[LEAD] The trust is forecast to incur the full £19.8m RTT income penalty this year after its 18-week position is projected to fall to 59.7% against a 67% target.
[Evidence] "The contract includes penalties of £2.8m per percentage point below target, capped at £19.8m… The current year end Incomplete RTT performance forecast is that the Trust will fall significantly short at 59.7%. This will result in the Trust incurring the full £19.8m income penalty." — p.158, [26/56.1 Integrated Performance Report]

[LEAD] The reported financial position (broadly on plan) is being held up entirely by deficit support funding — the underlying group position is an £11.3m deficit at month 3.
[Evidence] "The month 3 position for the Group was £0.1m surplus… This includes £2.9m of Deficit Support Funding (DSF). Excluding DSF, the position is £2.8m deficit. YTD the position for the Group is £2.5m deficit… This includes £8.7m of DSF. Excluding DSF, the position is £11.3m deficit." — p.198, [26/56.2 Month 2 Finance Report]

[LEAD] An independent review of a cluster of full-term stillbirths is under way — 11 cases at 34+ weeks over a nine-month window — with findings not due to the public board until October.
[Evidence] "An increased incidence of stillbirths occurring around full-term was identified through the Trust's internal review systems… The scope included all stillbirths at 34 weeks gestation or later, occurring at EKHUFT between the timeframe of 1 June 2025 and 20 December 2025. This timeframe was subsequently extended to the end of March 2026, in order to include 3 further stillbirths… correlate the 11 cases" — p.275, [26/60 Maternity and Neonatal Board Chair's Report]

[LEAD] The Datix analysis inside the anaesthetics review is a patient-safety story in its own right — repeated out-of-hours staffing incidents, slow escalation, and documented delays to emergency surgery and maternity epidurals.
[Evidence] "The first incident took 127 days to close, during which 17 further incidents were reported"; "Risk register escalation occurred only after 29 incidents had taken place"; "68% of incidents documented specific delays or potential compromises to patient care, including emergency surgery delays, cancelled epidurals, and postponed critical procedures"; "39% showed harm ratings downgraded during investigation despite clinical descriptions suggesting greater severity" — p.126, [26/55 App 1 Carnall Farrar review]

[WORTH WATCHING] Buckingham describes a "nepotistic" culture and a fear of retribution in which consultants who raised concerns found themselves investigated.
[Evidence] "Several interviewees described a 'nepotistic' culture, where access to development opportunities and career progression is dependent on 'fitting in' and on the patronage of individuals rather than on merit" — p.136; "In two separate interviews interviewees gave examples where consultants who had raised concerns found themselves under investigation… described the 'Trust values being used as a threat'" — p.137, [26/55 App 2 Raising Concerns review]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's internal Freedom to Speak Up service collapsed in 2024 — no FTSU reports were logged for the entire year.
[Evidence] "The internal Freedom to Speak Up service effectively collapsed in early 2024… During 2024, the Trust had no FTSU reports at all. An external Guardian service was procured and began operations in March 2025. 81 people from across the organisation have raised issues with the service between March and September 2025" — p.140, [26/55 App 2 Raising Concerns review]

[WORTH WATCHING] Four Never Events are recorded in the reporting period (a wrong-site block, a retained wire, a retained PICC line and a retained vaginal pack), with a further Never Event declared in June.
[Evidence] "Four (4) Never Events a wrong site block, retained wire, retained PICC line, retained vaginal pack"; "There was one (1) declared never event in June" — p.176, [26/56.1 Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] CQC activity is intense — four unannounced inspections this year, a Letter of Intent after the January QEQM emergency-care inspection, and three QEQM inspection reports still awaited; the WHH emergency-care report (published 10 July) stays "requires improvement" over corridor care but upgrades effective and well-led to "good".
[Evidence] "The CQC have completed four unannounced inspections to date this year… Following inspection of the QEQM UEC Core Service on 9 January 2026 the Trust received a Letter of Intent that required submission of an action plan"; "The overall status remains 'requires improvement'… the ratings for 'effective' and 'well-led' were upgraded from 'requires improvement' to 'good'" — p.393–394, [26/62 CQC Update Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] East Kent is one of the 12 hospitals named in the national maternity investigation (the Amos report, published 30 June 2026), with its own individual trust report.
[Evidence] "As part of the release, the findings have been reported in our own report as part of the 12 hospitals reviewed. This is a tough read for us all" — p.277, [26/60 Maternity and Neonatal Board Chair's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Kent and Medway Joint Committee has agreed the current model is "no longer sustainable" and that acute providers must work "significantly more closely together", with commissioning to be used to squeeze provider cost bases.
[Evidence] "The current model has reached its limits and is no longer sustainable"; "acute providers will need to work significantly more closely together"; "this approach may create individual organisational pressures for providers, particularly where existing contracts, cost bases or service configurations are not aligned to the emerging model" — p.68–70, [26/54 App 2 Joint Committee Board Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Hyper Acute Stroke Unit build at William Harvey has come in £3.65m over its approved business case, tripping the 10% approval threshold.
[Evidence] "the final costing information now been received by 2gether with the cost being £3.65m higher than the value included within the business case. As this additional cost exceeded the 10% threshold… additional sum required approval by the Board" — p.30, [26/48 Reconfiguration of Acute Stroke Services – Capital Costs]
