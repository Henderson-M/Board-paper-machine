## Top lines

[LEAD] Mid Yorkshire's 2025/26 deficit came in £13m worse than planned, and on current run-rate this year ends with a £23m deficit despite the biggest savings programme the trust has ever run.
[Evidence] "There was a year-end deficit of £21.2m, which was £13m worse than planned... If spending continued at the current rate, the Trust would end the year with a deficit of £23m" — Item A008/26, Financial Statements / Finance report

[LEAD] The trust says achieving 2026/27 financial balance means cutting more than 6% of its income — "the most significant programme ever undertaken by the organisation" — with a £57.3m savings target and only £16m delivered by end of July.
[Evidence] "To achieve financial balance, the Trust had to make significant savings equivalent to more than 6% of its income... the most significant programme ever undertaken by the organisation" — Item A008/26, Finance report

[LEAD] Two rounds of voluntary redundancy have now cost £8.87m to cut ~120 wte, with 117 appeals still working through the system from the second phase alone.
[Evidence] "In summary across phases 1 and 2, a total of 148 applications have been agreed which will... result in a workforce reduction of circa 120 wte. The associated estimated redundancy costs are £8.87m, with anticipated recurrent savings of £7.68m per annum" — Chief Executive's report, 2.3 Voluntary Redundancy Scheme – Phase 2

[WORTH WATCHING] CQC carried out an unannounced inspection of surgery services across all three main sites in late September — the first full surgery inspection since 2017 — and the trust is still awaiting the draft report.
[Evidence] "The Care Quality Commission (CQC) undertook an unannounced inspection of surgery services at Pinderfields Hospital, Dewsbury and District Hospital and Pontefract Hospital between 29 September and 1 October 2026... Surgery services across the Trust were last inspected and rated in 2017" — Chief Executive's report, 2.1 Update on the CQC Visit

[WORTH WATCHING] External auditors (Forvis Mazars) gave Mid Yorkshire a clean 2025/26 audit opinion, but flagged the same Value for Money weakness on financial sustainability as the year before.
[Evidence] "Opinion on the financial statements... a positive, unqualified audit opinion issued on 25 June 2026... Work on the Trust's VFM arrangements – VFM work had... identified the same weakness reported in 2024/25 regarding financial sustainability arrangements, although improvements had been made" — Item A007/26, External Audit Opinion

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust board recorded itself only "partially assured" on its gap analysis against the Lord Mann Review (the government's independent review on tackling antisemitism).
[Evidence] "The Public Trust Board NOTED and were PARTIALLY ASSURED by the Lord Mann Review" — Item T078/26, Lord Mann Review – Gap Analysis

[WORTH WATCHING] The Director of Workforce and Organisational Development is leaving the trust at the end of the year; an Associate Director has been named as board deputy in the interim. [INFERENCE] The departing director is not named in this paper.
[Evidence] "one of the four Associate Directors of Workforce Operations has been identified as a Trust Board deputy for the Director of Workforce and Organisational Development until his departure from the Trust on 31 December 2026" — Company Secretary's report, 1.2 Board Changes
