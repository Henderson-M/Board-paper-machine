## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO Toby Lewis tells the board the trust's commissioning contracts with its ICBs remain unsigned four months into the financial year, with the finance committee escalating the matter to the board.
[Evidence] "An oral update will be offered on unsigned ICB contracts, albeit for the second time we appear to have agreement, but it is not yet in written/signed form." — p.181, [Chief Executive's Report, Paper P]. Also: "progress to create contracts has been glacial, and yet has consumed a significant amount of senior management time. HNY-ICB have sought to add significant revenues... SY-ICB have had multiple personnel changes... The difficulty the ICB is having implementing their CIP programme seems to have bled into their contract offer to us which is slightly lower than that guaranteed in mid-March 2026." — p.184, [Paper P]. FDE Committee escalation: "(At the time, discussions with commissioners regarding contract agreement / signature were ongoing; subsequently they have not progressed to agreement and hence this matter is raised to Board.)" — p.44, [Finance, Digital and Estates Committee report, Paper I]

[LEAD] Trust plans to clear its £6.3m underlying deficit by April 2027 but the CEO says the plan depends on recruitment succeeding — and that it is spending far more than planned on agency doctors.
[Evidence] "We remain on track to remove our underlying deficit by April 2027 if we can recruit to those roles: if not, further service changes may be needed... despite the need to spend far more than planned on agency doctors." — p.181, [Paper P]. "The Trust exited 2025/26 with an underlying deficit of £6.3m. This has been reduced to £3.8m in Q1 and forecast to be at breakeven by the end of the financial year." — p.44, [Paper I]. Reported financial position: "The Trust has a year-to-date surplus of £52k and maintains a breakeven forecast... Key variances include a £2.7m substantive pay underspend, offset in part by agency and bank overspends." — p.48, [Integrated Quality Performance Report, Paper K]

[LEAD] Adult neurodiversity waits at the trust have lengthened, with the longest adult ADHD wait reaching 231 weeks and 6,373 people waiting, and the longest adult autism wait over four years.
[Evidence] Adult ADHD waiting list: "June — Longest wait (weeks) 231 — Total waiting 6,373" (up from 194 weeks / 6,232 in May). Adult autism: "Roth 4 years 24 weeks – 1 patient (next waiting 3 years 43 weeks / Average - 2 years 30 weeks)"; total autism waiting 2,231. — p.201, [Paper P, Annex 5: Neurodiversity waiting time scorecard]

[WORTH WATCHING] External firm The Value Circle has completed a commissioned "well-led" developmental review, and the CEO says the trust's weakest areas under the NHS Oversight Framework relate to its culture, prompting a two-to-four-year culture reset with outside advisers.
[Evidence] "Our weakest parts of the National Oversight Framework - and ... the Provider Capability Assessment - will relate to our culture: or rather our self-assessment of it... The intention remains to make commitments to a 2 – 4-year change process by the time of our leaders' conference in September... We have begun to engage outside advisors to support us with this work." — p.184, [Paper P]. Board response to the 14 TVC recommendations is at Annex 9, pp.213–220. Original review recorded in May minutes: "The Board received and noted the Well led externally commissioned developmental review report from The Value Circle (TVC)." — p.25, [Minutes of 28 May 2026 meeting]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has declined to meet one of four "plan conditions" set by NHS England's regional team when its three-year plan was approved — the out-of-area placement condition.
[Evidence] "We have now had written confirmation that the cost improvement measure has been discharged, as have the workforce planning and sickness planning conditions. We have not sought to meet the Out of Area placement condition, albeit we are achieving well above our planned trajectory, but are mindful of some confusion externally between inappropriate, appropriate, and complex placements." — p.188, [Paper P]

[WORTH WATCHING] Concurrent medical staff departures and rising sickness in the Rotherham care group have forced unplanned agency locum cover outside the trust's financial plan.
[Evidence] "We discussed, in some detail last time the Board met, the Rotherham staffing issues that the Trust is facing, with concurrent medical staff departures, and a surge in mid-term sickness too. We have agreed to a series of agency locums outside our plan." — pp.186–187, [Paper P]. Board asked to "EXPLORE the patient, people and population issues described (including stability in RCG)". — p.181, [Paper P]. Trust-wide sickness absence has risen to 7.04% against a 5.1% target. — p.48, [Paper K]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board asked to approve the full business case for a c.2026–2030 estate redesign centred on Tickhill Road, Doncaster — land disposal plus a new frailty and education centre — with £20m of the trust's £27m backlog-maintenance total sitting on that one site.
[Evidence] "the Tickhill Road ... accounts for £20m of the Trust's backlog maintenance total of £27m." — p.66, [Estate Plan Full Business Case, Pack B]. Funding via "a combination of Public Dividend Capital (PDC) and land sales"; ambient-loop ground source heat pumps to cut emissions "by 1,237 tonnes each year". — p.282, [Estate Plan FBC cover, Paper T] and p.44, [Paper I]. Trust "holds material cash balances of c. £35m". — p.131, [FBC, Pack B]
