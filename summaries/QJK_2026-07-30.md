# South West Peninsula Board (NHS Cornwall & Isles of Scilly / NHS Devon cluster) — 30 July 2026

- **Source:** https://swpboard.nhs.uk/board-meetings/board-meeting-papers-and-minutes/
- **Pack:** 2 files (Agenda & Papers, 202pp; Appendices, 45pp)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-27T12:51:08Z
- **Note:** This is the joint "South West Peninsula Board" for the clustered NHS Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB and NHS Devon ICB (ods QJK). Interim chief executive is Mark Hackett; the CEO report is authored/approved by Susan Bracefield, "Acting Interim Chief Executive". Interim CFO is Gary Heneage.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Trade unions have told the two ICBs' boards that their restructure creates an "unsafe" staffing structure that puts vulnerable people and staff professional registrations at risk — the ICBs' written reply is in the public papers.
[Evidence] "It is our view that the draft structure is unsafe and that remains our view following a number of discussions at the Joint Partnership Committee and with the ICB Chief Officers. There is insufficient capacity to meet statutory requirements such as safeguarding and CHC and financial constraints have been given as the reason that more posts cannot be added." / "Some of the most vulnerable people that the ICBs serve will be put at risk by this unbalanced and unsafe structure. Furthermore, our members will put their professional registrations at risk and suffer the moral injury of being unable to meet the requirements of their roles..." — Appendices p.33–35, [ICB response to TU letter of 30 June, dated 24 July 2026]

[LEAD] Trade unions allege the cluster funded "excessive numbers and bandings" of senior posts in Phase 1 while cutting frontline capacity; the ICBs reject it, saying leadership costs fell 24%.
[Evidence] TU: "The Peninsula Cluster has 70% more Phase 1 posts than the other South West clusters (adjusted for population) and has excessive bandings (e.g. the company secretary equivalents are 8c and 8d)." ICB: "We have 29 roles in our Phase 1 senior structure (excluding the Chief Executive role)... the costs of our leadership structure have decreased by 24%... representing 12.9% of the ICBs' total staff costs, reducing from 16% in 2025." — Appendices p.34, [ICB response to TU letter]

[LEAD] Cornwall Council and Cornwall/Isles of Scilly MPs are formally opposing the planned April 2027 merger of the two clustered ICBs into a single Devon, Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB.
[Evidence] "Cornwall Council is objecting to the formal merger of CIOS ICB with Devon ICB." (Agenda & Papers p.62) + "In Cornwall, where the independence voice is strong, Cornwall Council, expressed opposition to the merger and favoured continuation of a separate ICB for Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly. Cornwall Health and Adult Social Care Overview and Scrutiny Committee also set out their wish to retain a sovereign Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB... Cornwall and Isles of Scilly MPs have taken a similar stance... MPs have sent a separate letter to the Minister of State for Health on these issues" — Appendices p.2, [Merger footprint engagement report]

[LEAD] Cornwall Partnership FT mental health services have received a CQC Section 29A warning notice after an inspection found significant patient safety issues.
[Evidence] "a recent CQC inspection of CFT mental health services identified significant patient safety issues, resulting in a Section 29A Warning Notice and increased scrutiny of improvement actions." — Agenda & Papers p.190 (also flagged p.181), [Performance Exception Report]

[LEAD] University Hospitals Plymouth is carrying two undeveloped cost-improvement schemes worth c£20m combined, flagged high-risk, with £22.06m of its CIP still unidentified at month 2.
[Evidence] "YTD CIP delivery is off plan with the most material variance at UHP. This relates to two schemes at c£20m that are still being developed and remain high risk." + table row "CIP unidentified ... UHP 22.06" — Agenda & Papers p.191, [Performance Exception Report] (cross-ref p.182: "two material schemes at £10m each")

[WORTH WATCHING] The cluster ICBs forecast a £5.9m mental health overspend driven by "right to choose" ADHD and autism assessments sought from independent providers.
[Evidence] "Mental Health forecast overspend of £5.9m is driven by the increase in the cost of individual packages of care and by the impact of 'right to choose' in relation to ADHD and autism assessments and related costs as individuals seek diagnosis from independent providers. Based on early trends there is a significant risk of overspend in this area." — Agenda & Papers p.195, [Appendix 2 — Cluster ICB financial position M2]

[WORTH WATCHING] Royal Cornwall has received a letter from the NHSE regional leadership team seeking assurance on delivery of its 2026/27 plan; a system-wide urgent-and-emergency-care review is being progressed "as a priority" given RCHT's distance from plan.
[Evidence] "All of the above actions are being shared with NHSE in light of the Trust having received a letter from the Regional leadership team seeking assurance on the oversight and delivery of the 206/27 [sic] plan. The response is due to be submitted on 23 July 2026." (Agenda & Papers p.69–70) + "Given the distance from plan at RCHT, a full system-wide Urgent and Emergency Care (UEC) review is now being progressed as a priority." — p.187, [Performance Exception Report / Trusts partner update]

[WORTH WATCHING] All four acute providers are worse than the national average on the newly published corridor-care metric; RCHT averaged 33 patients in corridor care at snapshot in June.
[Evidence] "the newly published corridor care metrics shows RCHT had an average of 33 patients receiving corridor care in the emergency department at the snapshot times during June 2026. UHP had 27, RDUH had 22 and TSD with 31. All providers are worse than the national average of 20." — Agenda & Papers p.187, [Performance Exception Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Private provider Ramsay Mount Stewart Hospital escalated to "enhanced concern" by NHS Devon after a patient transferred to an acute trust subsequently died and three VTE-related incidents.
[Evidence] "Ramsay Health Care UK's Mount Stewart Hospital has been escalated as an enhanced concern... NHS Devon has arranged a second quality meeting with the provider to review limited visibility of patient safety reporting, three VTE-related incidents, and concerns surrounding the transfer of a patient to an acute trust who subsequently died." — Agenda & Papers p.190, [Performance Exception Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Government has confirmed four unitary councils for Devon; the ICB flags this as significant for its place footprints and says it adds a sixth council authority to the cluster from April 2028.
[Evidence] "the Government has announced its decision on the future structure of local government in Devon, confirming that four unitary authorities will be created" (Agenda & Papers p.56) + "as part of the LGR announced last week will have an extra sixth council authority from April 2028, increasing the complexity of partnerships we have to support." — Appendices p.34, [CEO report p.56–57 / ICB response to TU letter]

The pack quotes fragments of the TU letter in the reply but neither the original letter nor the capacity review is published (Appendices p.33, 36). Core to the "unsafe structure" story.

[FOI] "Appendix 4 — NHSE Correspondence" is listed in the Performance Exception Report (Agenda & Papers p.197) but carries no readable text in the pack. Request the full NHSE correspondence tabled at this meeting from the ICBs.
