## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England accepted the trust's 2026/27–2028/29 medium-term plan only as "Compliant with Conditions", warning that an underdeveloped savings programme and unsigned commissioner contract are "material financial risk" that could trigger "enhanced oversight arrangements".
[Evidence] "Your submitted plan has been reviewed against the expectations set out in the national guidance and has been assessed as: Compliant with Conditions" and "The underdevelopment of CIP and unsigned contracts represent material financial risk … failure to make the required progress may result in enhanced oversight arrangements." — p.27 and p.30, [5.1 NHSE medium term plan sign off — letter from Chris Adcock, Regional Director of Finance (North West), 16 April 2026]

[LEAD] At end-March only 7% of the trust's £9.1m savings programme was developed or implemented, despite NHSE requiring all 2026/27 schemes to be fully developed by 31 March; the trust also had no signed contract with its main commissioner by the 31 March deadline.
[Evidence] "As at the end of March 2026 your weekly Cost Improvement Programme (CIP) submission showed 7% of total CIP £9.1m is fully developed or implemented … we required that all 2026/27 CIP schemes were fully developed by 31 March." and "As at 31 March the Trust did not have a signed contract with your main commissioner … The Trust must now move urgently to reach signature on all contracts." — p.29, [5.1 NHSE medium term plan sign off]

[LEAD] New chief medical officer Mark Kellett started on 1 June 2026, replacing Andy Nicolson, who left in May after 20 years and has been appointed medical director of the University Hospitals of Liverpool Group (UHLG).
[Evidence] "Mark Kellett will start as Chief Medical Officer and Responsible Officer at the Trust on Monday 1 June replacing Andy Nicolson who left in May following 20 years as a consultant at the Trust." — p.25, [CEO Report]; "AN informed that he had been appointed as the Medical Director of the University Hospitals of Liverpool of Liverpool Group (UHLG) to begin in post in June" — p.9, [Unconfirmed Minutes 02-04-26]

[LEAD] The trust closed its board assurance framework risk on the Liverpool group/shared-model integration after NHS England stopped the wider Liverpool Adult Acute and Specialist Providers (LAASP) integration programme.
[Evidence] "four risks were sitting outside of the risk tolerance … it was being proposed to close BAF004: Shared Models as it related to the integration into UHLG which had now been stopped by NHS England (NHS)." — p.10, [Unconfirmed Minutes 02-04-26]; "an email had been received from NHSE about the outstanding risk for the Trust … Further feedback was received from NHSE that the action had now been closed." — p.11 (Item 9, LAASP closure report)

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust remained in segment 1 of NHSE's National Oversight Framework but fell to 13th in the league table; it disputed the underlying data and expects its ranking to improve.
[Evidence] "a letter had been received from NHSE informing the Trust that it remained in segment 1 for the National Oversight Framework (NOF) but had dropped to 13th position on the league table, the Trust had carried out a data analysis on the information submitted for accuracy." — p.17, [Unconfirmed Minutes 02-04-26]

[WORTH WATCHING] Resident-doctor mandatory training compliance is the lowest in the region at 65.58%, against a backdrop of 13 open disciplinaries and 11 open grievances at the trust.
[Evidence] "Lead Employer Resident Doctor mandatory training compliance remains low at 65.58%, the Trust ranking lowest for compliance in the region" and "There are currently 13 open disciplinaries and 11 open grievances" — p.45, [Board IPR June 2026, Workforce key points]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cheshire & Merseyside ICS reported a £34.9m deficit at month 1, £7.8m adverse to plan, driven mainly by savings under-delivery — system context for the trust's own conditional plan.
[Evidence] "Cheshire & Merseyside ICS reported a £34.946 deficit in M1, excluding deficit support funding – £7.797m adverse to plan. This was mainly driven by underachievement of CIP." — p.25, [CEO Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2026 Health Bill paper sets out direct local consequences of NHSE abolition, including removal of the Council of Governors' statutory powers and "increased risk of direct intervention in Trust governance and leadership".
[Evidence] "Removal of Council of Governors statutory powers resulting in changes to governance processes" and (Appendix 1) "Increased risk of direct intervention in Trust governance and leadership." — p.34 and p.35, [6. Health Bill, author Katharine Dowson, 18 May 2026]
