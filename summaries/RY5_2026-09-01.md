# Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group board — 1 September 2026

- **Source:** https://lincolnshirecommunityhealthservices.nhs.uk/download_file/3849/201 ("Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals Group board papers September 2026")
- **Pack:** 1 combined file
- **Analysed:** 2026-09-01
- **Note:** This is the group board covering **Lincolnshire Community Health Services (RY5)** and **United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals (RWD)**. Much of the material below relates to ULTH.

## Top lines

[LEAD] The group's own audit committee found the Lincoln community diagnostic centre was not delivering value for money — and that the usual options appraisal was never properly done.
[Evidence] "[the Committee received] assurance in relation to the development of the **Lincoln CDC which though was achieving benefits realisations, was not providing financial value for money. Due to regional and national pressures to develop the CDC after COVID, the usually routine options evaluation was not systematically under[taken]. This resulted in the failure to recognise the benefits gained by improving accessibility to a wider population in the consideration of value for money.**" — [Audit Committee report]
[Note] The pack presents the missed options appraisal as the reason the wider access benefits were not captured; it does not dispute that the centre falls short on financial value for money.

[LEAD] The board is being warned that a patient could die waiting for cardiac intervention, with an explicit Regulation 28 risk on the register.
[Evidence] Risk entry: "[risk of increased] admissions and inpatient length of stay, service destabilisation, and **serious regulatory exposure for the Trust, including the potential for a Regulation 28 'Action to Prevent Future Deaths' notice should a patient die while awaiting PCI or device implantation. Some PCI patients are being medically managed but the delay in waiting for procedures can affect their condition and can result in admissi[on]**" — [Corporate risk register, cardiology]

[LEAD] A string of named consultant specialties are recorded as fragile or failing, including a stroke service down to one substantive consultant and respiratory running at 9 against a GIRFT recommendation of 15.
[Evidence] Stroke: "**Significant deficit in stroke consultant staffing.** As a result of a significant deficit in stroke consultants levels due to staff leaving/retiring and stroke service struggling to recruit substantive consultants there is a risk of not being able to maintain effective stroke provision across ULTH. **From April 2025 the stroke service will have only one Substantive Consultant, upcoming retirement could result in no substantive Consultan[ts]**" — [Corporate risk register]
[Evidence] Respiratory: "Currently, there are **only 9 substantive consultants and 3 locum/agency staff against the GIRFT recommendation of 15**, leaving a significant workforce gap... Currently: x 5 Consultant Gaps in Resp[iratory]" — [same]
[Evidence] Orthodontics: "As a result of the **resignation of the two current consultants** and a national shortage of orthodontists making recruitment difficult, there is a risk that the Trust will be unable to provide a timely and clinically effective Orthodontic service to current patients who are in the middle of the treatment pathway" — [same]
[Evidence] Gastroenterology: "As a result of a **reduction in Gastroenterology Consultant workforce capacity due to resignations, retirements, or reduced job plans**, there is a risk that the service will be unable to maintain adequate inpatient and on-call coverage which would lead to an inability to sustain two-site Gastroenterology inpatient cover, outpatient and cancer performance, and the Upper GI Bleed On-Call service." — [same]
[Evidence] Radiotherapy: "Lack of radiotherapy physics staff for on-call run-ups; Reduced radiographer numbers; **Key staff members leaving the Trust**... There are risks of delivering the necessary patient workload and not always being able to start treatment on time, Increased cancellations" — [same]

[LEAD] ULTH's performance is a long way off plan across the board — RTT 61.2% against 84.1%, diagnostics 43.4% against 99%, 751 patients waiting over a year.
[Evidence] "compliance (85.3% vs 95% target), **RTT performance (61.2% vs 84.1% target), diagnostics (43.4% vs 99% target) and 751 patients waiting over 52 weeks.** Ambulance handover delays and patient flow pressures also remain significant." — [Integrated performance report / committee escalation]
[Evidence] By contrast the community side is holding up: "**adult community services had no patients waiting over 52 weeks**, while overall performance against the adult 18-week standard was in June 2026 at **91.49% (87.93% in May) vs target of 78%**." — [Community performance report]

[LEAD] The group is proposing a formal quality and safety framework for corridor care — "Seated and Corridor Care Areas".
[Evidence] "**Proposed Seated and Corridor Care Areas (SCCA) Quality & Safety Framework: recommendation put forward for introduction of SCCA to bring together operational, nursing, governance, patient safety, IPC, patient experience and estates assurance into one structured process.**" — [Quality report]
[Note] This formalises corridor care as a managed care setting rather than treating it purely as an incident to eliminate — a live national argument. NHSE's 2026/27 winter expectations treat the elimination of corridor care as a patient safety priority.

[WORTH WATCHING] Women are being wheeled through public corridors to theatre, and the board is told this may push up the caesarean rate.
[Evidence] "as well as **poor patient experience due to a lack of privacy and dignity for women when transferring to ground floor theatres through public corridors. There is also a potential for an increase in Caesarean Section rate as clinicians attempt to avoid late labour transfers.**" — [Maternity estates risk]

[WORTH WATCHING] A paediatric audiology room has been condemned, and the service risks losing the Lincolnshire contract.
[Evidence] "**the Paediatric Audiology Service not meeting the Paediatrics Quality Standards** and the consequence of the requirement to attain UKAS IQIPS accreditation, due to insufficient staffing, capacity and lack of soundproofing rooms (**the room at Boston has recently been condemned after IQUIPS benchmarking visit which has reduced capacity further**) there is a risk of 1) an increase in waiting times... and 2) the risk of the Trust **not attaining accreditation which could lead to Audiology losing the contract for Paediatric Services in Lincolnshire.**" — [Corporate risk register]

[WORTH WATCHING] The group has approved an overcommitment of up to £5m on its capital plan while running a £17.7m capital underspend.
[Evidence] "**To ensure best value for money, the Group have approved an overcommitment on the capital plan of up to £5m**, this will be monitored and managed by the CFO and Estates Director to ensure the Group remains within our capital limit for the year." — [Month 4 Finance Briefing]
[Evidence] "2026/27 Capital position M4 YTD: The Group **delivered capital expenditure of £14.7m (£17.7m lower than plan).**" — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The group is on plan overall but its savings programme stepped up in month 4 and delivery did not follow.
[Evidence] "The Group reported a **£7.4m year-to-date deficit at Month 4, which is £0.2m favourable to plan.**" — [Month 4 Finance Briefing, Executive Summary]
[Evidence] "**Our efficiency programme has seen a step up in delivery requirement in month 4 and this has not been matched by delivery of our schemes, resulting in an under delivery of £0.8m.** The maturity of our plans continues to improve with **77% of our schemes having plans fully developed**" — [same]
[Evidence] "2026/27 CIP position M4 YTD: The Group delivered savings of **£25.7m (£1.1m lower than plan)**. M4 Cash position: The Group ended the month with a **cash balance of £33.6m (£13.6m lower than plan).**" — [same]
[Evidence] Principal financial risks: "**Activity shortfall £2.9m below plan** — COO recovery plan and elective productivity actions. **CIP delivery £1.1m adverse variance** — September deep-dives and enhanced PMO ov[ersight]" — [same]
[Evidence] "there is a risk that the Trust may **not deliver the £8.9m efficiency requirement set out in the LCHS financial plan**, which would lead to reduced financial sustainability" — [Board Assurance Framework]
[Evidence] Emergency activity is above plan but barely paid for: "our emergency care activity; however **this is only paid at a 20% marginal payment**, in line with national tariff guidelines." — [Month 4 Finance Briefing]

[WORTH WATCHING] Six NHS England letters land in this pack, including the post-Nottingham mortuary letter and 1,000 extra postgraduate medical training places.
[Evidence] "**Duncan Burton, Chief Nursing Officer, and Sarah-Jane Marsh, Chief Operating Officer, NHS England, wrote to NHS organisations outlining key actions following the findings of the Nottingham Maternity Review and the Fuller Inquiry, with a renewed focus on mortuary oversight, post-death care, and the safeguarding of deceased patients.** The letter requested that Trust Boards review their local arrangements and submit a formal [return]" — [Chief Executive's report]
[Evidence] "**Sir Glen Burley, Deputy Chief Executive of NHS England, wrote to NHS trust Chief Executives in July regarding the national expansion of postgraduate medical training places**, which forms part of the agreement reached following the resolution of the resident doctors' dispute. **The programme will deliver an additional 1,000 training places in 2027**, with further expansion planned" — [same]
[Evidence] "**Sir Jim Mackey, Chief Executive NHS England, wrote to all NHS organisations during July highlighting the national focus on preventing unlawful access to patient records**" — [same]
[Evidence] "NHS England has written to organisations who provide planned care to introduce the **new Minimum Standards for Planned Patient Care, establishing for the first time a nationally consistent baseline for patient experience across elective care pathways**" — [same]
[Evidence] "correspondence received on 4 June 2026 from the Chief Executive and Director General for People at NHS England, which highlighted the opportunity for NHS organisations to move from commitment to a more evidence-based approach in delivering improvements in EDI" — [same, on the Lord Mann Review]
[Evidence] The maternity 10 point plan requires "a Board-approved national assurance template, with a **baseline assessment by 25 September 2026**" — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust risks losing its CNST rebate over maternity training compliance.
[Evidence] "there is a risk that the Trust will not achieve the **90% minimum target across all relevant staff groups by July 2026. This would lead to a failure to attain the MIS Core Standards, loss of the associated financial rebate on CNST contributions**, reduced assurance regarding staff competency and patient safety, and reputational damage." — [Corporate risk register]

## FOI leads

[FOI] **The Lincoln community diagnostic centre value for money assessment.** *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group and NHS Lincolnshire ICB:* the internal audit or assurance report finding the Lincoln CDC "was not providing financial value for money", the original business case, the options evaluation that was not systematically undertaken, the capital and revenue cost to date, and actual against planned activity. *Why it matters:* CDCs were a flagship national programme; a trust's own audit committee concluding one does not deliver financial value for money, and that the options appraisal was skipped under national pressure, goes wider than Lincolnshire.

[FOI] **The cardiology waiting list and the Regulation 28 risk.** *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group:* the number of patients waiting for PCI and device implantation by wait length, the number who have died while waiting in the last three years, the full risk register entry and mitigation plan, and any correspondence with the coroner.

[FOI] **The consultant workforce gaps.** *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group:* the current substantive, locum and agency consultant establishment against funded establishment and GIRFT recommendations for stroke, respiratory, gastroenterology, orthodontics and radiotherapy physics; and any service-continuity or suspension plans for each.

[FOI] **The Seated and Corridor Care Areas framework.** *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group:* the proposed SCCA Quality & Safety Framework, the number of patients cared for in seated and corridor areas by month, and the equality and quality impact assessment.

[FOI] **The condemned audiology room at Boston** and the IQIPS benchmarking visit report. *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group and UKAS:* the visit findings, the remediation plan and cost, current paediatric audiology waiting times, and any ICB correspondence about the Lincolnshire paediatric audiology contract.

[FOI] **The maternity theatre transfer route.** *Request from Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals NHS Group:* the estates risk assessment covering transfers to ground-floor theatres through public corridors, the number of such transfers, and any analysis of the effect on the caesarean section rate.
