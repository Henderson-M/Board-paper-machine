[Evidence] "26-27 plan is breakeven, encompassing £74m of CIP requirement. [Evidence] "£24.7m of schemes developed, with £11m more expected, still far from £70m+ target. Some savings came from prior-year schemes, not new initiatives. [Evidence] "Cash flow is a major concern, with worsening creditor payments and risk of supplier delays." / "Cash balance of £6m, but creditor pressures increasing. [Evidence] "Following discussions with the Integrated Care Board (ICB) in month 11, the Trust had achieved a break-even position for the year... became eligible for £4.9m in deficit support funding, leading to a reported year-end surplus of £4.9m." / "this was the first time since 2011/12 that ESHT had reported a surplus" — p.17–18, [Trust Board Minutes 21.04.26, Item 30/26]

[LEAD] Finance committee assurance puts the acute bed shortfall at an estimated 42 beds, alongside declining A&E performance.
[Evidence] "Bed capacity is insufficient — estimated shortage of 42 beds." / "A&E performance declining (currently ~70% vs 78% target)." — p.27, [Finance & Performance Committee report, Item 7.1]

[LEAD] The nursing establishment review concludes current templated staffing does not meet patients' planned care needs, with 225 safe-staffing red flags in one autumn audit; the board is asked to back a phased £660,515 investment of 15.5 WTE.
[Evidence] "The 2025 NER data analysis demonstrates that our current templated available nurse staffing does not meet the planned care needs for patients. The areas of highest risk... are our elderly care wards." — p.96; "225 red flags raised during the Sept/Oct 2025 audit – 180 related to shortfall in staffing and skill mix concerns" — p.100; "an increase in workforce is required to support safer staffing levels of 15.5 WTE (2.5 RN and 13 CSW) at a cost of £660,515" — p.96, [Nursing Establishment Review 2025, Item 12]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust states an ambition to become a foundation trust and to take on an "integrated healthcare organisation (IHO)" role, subject to national policy.
[Evidence] "We are also ambitious to become a foundation trust and to be ready, subject to national policy and assurance, to take on an integrated healthcare organisation (IHO) role, strengthening our autonomy and accountability" — p.83, [ESHT Five Year Integrated Delivery Plan: 2026/27 Summary, Item 11]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's £36.7m capital plan depends on two asset sales worth £8.2m that have slipped from Q1 to Q2, with the plan to be re-prioritised if either fails to complete in-year.
[Evidence] "The draft capital plan assumes the sale of two Trust assets, will generate £8.2m of additional capital funding in-year... latest information suggests that neither sale will complete until Q2. Should either sale not be complete within the financial year, the capital plan will need to be re-prioritised... to ensure no breach of allocation." — p.71, [IPR Finance – Capital, Item 9(v)]

[WORTH WATCHING] The September go-live of the trust's Nervecentre EPR (EmPoweR programme) faces an NHSE "Stage 4.5" readiness review in early July; the programme notes other trusts have had to pause go-lives after similar reviews.
[Evidence] "in early July NHSE will undertake a Stage 4.5 Review to assess the Trust's readiness to go live... The programme team is aware of certain other Trusts having had to pause planned go lives following similar reviews" — p.120–121, [EmPoweR Programme Update, Item 14]

[WORTH WATCHING] Freedom to Speak Up data shows staff increasingly declining to pursue or disengaging from concerns, citing futility and fear of being seen as a "troublemaker" during ongoing consultations.
[Evidence] "In Quarter 3 10 staff declined to take concerns further and 2 disengaged. 3 staff resigned from roles.
