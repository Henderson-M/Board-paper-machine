[LEAD] External auditors have issued a modified value-for-money opinion on Frimley Health, flagging a "significant weakness regarding procurement".
[Evidence] "The VSM opinion is modified owing to the external Auditors highlighting a significant weakness regarding procurement. Although the underlying risk is likely unchanged from prior years, we are now reporting more breaches (i.e. getting on top of identifying failures) and the Auditors view each as outside our SFIs. They want minimal tender waivers in future." — p.197-198, [14 FHFT_Chair Assurance Report - Audit 22 June 2026]

[LEAD] Frimley has announced Pine Ridge Golf Club land (owned by Frimley Fuel Allotment) as its preferred site for the new Frimley Park Hospital, launching a 13-week public engagement amid local concern over woodland loss and traffic.
[Evidence] "We announced the preferred site for the new Frimley Park Hospital on 16 June as being part of the land owned by Frimley Fuel Allotment and currently occupied by Pine Ridge Golf Club... The initial engagement events have been well attended, with the understandable concerns raised by members of the local population focussed on the potential loss of woodland and the potential impact of additional traffic on the current road infrastructure." — p.42, [6. CEO Board report Part1 July 2026]

[LEAD] Spanish contractor Sacyr has been allocated to Frimley as its New Hospital Programme construction partner; the trust remains targeting a 2028-2029 construction start.
[Evidence] "The central-government-led allocation process has concluded and the Frimley scheme has been allocated Sacyr as our construction partner... We remain on target to start construction in the period allotted by the Secretary of State (DHSC) of 2028-2029, although on schemes of this scale and complexity there is always a significant risk of delay." — p.178, [12. FHFT NHP Update v0.2]

[LEAD] Frimley is set to take on functions and staff from Thames Valley ICB from October under the ICB operating-model redesign, having been named preferred provider in February.
[Evidence] "As part of the redesign of the operating model of Thames Valley ICB we were identified in February as the preferred provider of some of their functions, subject to ongoing due diligence and formal Board approval... we would expect the appropriate transfer of functions and colleagues to happen in October." — p.44, [6. CEO Board report Part1 July 2026]

[LEAD] The trust has set a breakeven plan for 2026-27 that removes the £20.6m of deficit support funding it received in 2025/26, against a £68.6m CIP target (a 2% cut to each division plus recovery of prior-year overspends).
[Evidence] "The Trust has agreed a breakeven plan for 2026-27 which does not include any deficit support funding (£20.6m received in 2025/26)... The Trust has a CIP plan of £68.6m for 2026-27." — p.200, [15. M02 Finance Report]; "This figure comprises a 2% CIP applied to each division, as well as recovery from forecasted 2025/26 budget overspends." — p.205, [Performance Report May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Corridor-care numbers rose in May, with 625 patients treated in ED corridors and a further 76 in ward temporary escalation spaces; the metric now carries a CRR risk scored 20.
[Evidence] "**Patients experiencing Corridor Care in ED** ... May 26 Actual 625" and "Patients experiencing Corridor Care in Ward spaces (previously named Temporary Escalation Spaces) ... 76" — p.211, [16. Source of Risk: Emergency Department" (score 20) — p.51, [7a. Master BAF 2026 June]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's loss of NOF financial override means that from 2026/27 its segmentation will no longer be held down by deficit funding — the Q4 metric score of 1.92 would have placed it in segment 1 without the override.
[Evidence] "We were placed in segment 3 because of the application of the financial override due to our receipt of deficit support funding during the year. Our overall metric score was 1.92... Without the financial override, this would have placed us in segment 1... We are not in receipt of deficit support funding in 2026/27 so will not have the automatic financial override applied." — p.44, [6. CEO Board report Part1 July 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cancer 62-day performance fell to 75.5% in May against a 79% trajectory, driven by breast radiologist recruitment difficulties and a national bowel-prep medication shortage.
[Evidence] "62-day performance was 75.5% in May against a trajectory of 79%... Ongoing radiology capacity issues created by difficulty in recruiting Breast Radiologists is resulting in delays in diagnosis... LGI performance was impacted by a national shortage in bowel preparation medication for CT Colon diagnostics." — p.214, [16. Performance and Productivity Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Freedom to Speak Up concerns rose 43% year-on-year to 222 in 2025/26; staff-survey confidence gaps are widest for staff who prefer not to state gender (-33 points vs benchmark) and White & Black Caribbean staff.
[Evidence] "2024/25: 193 concerns. 2025/26: 222 concerns (increase of 67 concerns; 43%)." — p.172; "targeted FTSU work should support staff who do not feel confident enough to share their gender, and staff whose heritage is White & Black Caribbean" (q25f difference from benchmark -33.90%) — p.173, [11a.
