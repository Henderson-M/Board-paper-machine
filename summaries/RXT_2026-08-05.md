## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust reports a £1.9m consolidated Group deficit at Q1 (month 3) against a break-even plan, with large forecast risks building on bank staff, non-Trust beds and medication.
[Evidence] "The quarter 1 2026/27 consolidated Group position is a deficit of £1.9m. This is £1.9m adverse to the year to date break-even plan. Shortfall on savings delivery, overspend on bank, non-trust beds, and medication…" — p.423, [Month 3 Finance Report coversheet]; bank "YTD overspend £1.8m, forecast risk of circa £7m", non-Trust beds "YTD overspend £1.8m, forecast risk circa £7m", medication "YTD overspend £1m, forecast risk £4m" — p.431/433/434, [Month 3 Finance Report]

[LEAD] Race and disability workforce disparities widened sharply in the Trust's 2026 WRES/WDES data (staff-survey and ESR based), with several indicators moving the wrong way year on year.
[Evidence] Shortlisting: "White colleagues are 1.48 times more likely to be appointed from shortlisting… we have increased the gap on the 0.70 reported in 2025 (-ive)"; disciplinary: "Global Majority colleagues are 1.93 times more likely to enter formal disciplinary process… In 2025 it was reported at 0.82 (-ive)"; capability: "Colleagues with disabilities are 5.74 times likely to enter the capability process then those without (Last year 3.28) (-ive)" — p.403–407, [WRES/WDES Annual Report 2025/26]

[LEAD] The West Midlands Mental Health Provider Collaborative has a live performance notice against the Trust's Talking Therapies (Birmingham Healthy Minds) service over underperformance, with reliable recovery still below the national target.
[Evidence] "The MH Provider Collaborative issued a performance notice in September 2025 relating to underperformance in activity levels and reliable recovery and reliable improvement rates." Reliable Recovery rate "remained at 48.5% and remains below the 51% national target" — p.91, [Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Medication overspend is running £1m over YTD (forecast risk £4m), concentrated in community services, and the finance committee has asked for a "deep dive" into the drivers.
[Evidence] "69% of the overspend is in the ICCR service area"; aripiprazole spend £411k YTD vs £72k plan (-£339k) — p.434, [Month 3 Finance Report]; "Concern was raised regarding increasing medicines expenditure and requested a future deep dive to better understand underlying drivers and risks" — p.418, [FPPC Assurance Report July 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Trust was issued a Prevention of Future Deaths notice covering Wi-Fi signal on inpatient wards and physical-health-monitoring care plans; the Trust responded to the coroner on 9 June.
[Evidence] "The Trust was issued with one PFD notice related to improving Wi-Fi signal on some inpatient wards and improvement in physical health monitoring care plans. A response was sent to the Coroner on June 9th…" — p.30, [CEO & Director of Operations Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] CEO/DoO report flags rising illicit-substance misuse destabilising HMP Birmingham, with HMIP and CQC returning over an October 2025 inspection where the "Red Route" and 28-day mental-health-transfer compliance remained concerns.
[Evidence] "There has been a notable increase in illicit substance misuse across the estate which is impacting on stability in the prison." "HMIP and CQC returned to review progress following the inspection in October 2025… these were the Red Route, which remained a concern for the inspectors as well as the compliance with the 28-day transfer to mental health hospital." — p.27, [CEO & Director of Operations Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Two national access standards slipped in June: First Episode of Psychosis and CYP eating disorders (routine).
[Evidence] "First Episode of Psychosis… June 2026 has seen performance reduce to 56%, below the 60% national target. This relates to 11 of 25 service users not starting their treatment within 2 weeks"; CYP Eating Disorders routine "performance reduce to 72.7% below the 95% national standard" — p.92, [Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Trust intends to bid to become the lead provider for CAMHS Tier 4.
[Evidence] "The Trust would bid to become lead provider for CAMHS Tier 4." — p.421, [FPPC Assurance Report June 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Junior-doctor rota gaps jumped, with a rising trend of "off rota" requests that medical leadership warns could hit out-of-hours cover.
[Evidence] "The number of vacant shifts was 1542… In previous years it was 1101 in 2024-2025, and it was 1583 in 2023-2024." "Senior medical leadership in the trust are aware that 'off rota' requests… has increased and this is being closely monitored. If they were to continue/grow, it would impinge on our ability to provide out of hours medical rotas." 20% of doctors in training were LTFT (16.2% in 2024-25). — p.338/344, [Guardian of Safe Working Hours Annual Report 2025/26]

[WORTH WATCHING] Out-of-area placements in private (Cygnet) hospitals have been reclassified from "inappropriate" to "appropriate" following a Trust SOP approval, changing how the Trust reports against its zero-inappropriate-OOA trajectory.
[Evidence] "From 29th September a new framework arrangement with private providers commenced, placing out of area placements in Cygnet hospitals. From 30th March 2026 these beds are now classified as 'appropriate' following review and approval of the Trust's Standard Operating Protocol (SOP)…" — p.89, [Integrated Performance Report]
