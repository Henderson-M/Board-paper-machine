## Top lines

[LEAD] Group is formally asking its board to send forward a shortlist that includes closing the maternity delivery suites at both QEH and James Paget and concentrating all births at NNUH — the only scenario that closes the group's structural deficit; clinicians "did not reach a consensus to rule out" any of the four maternity scenarios.
[Evidence] "Only NNUH's delivery suite is projected to be of a sustainable size by 2034/35. QEH and JPUH would each perform fewer than four deliveries a day, while still needing to provide cover around the clock." — p.7, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "Overall, the CAG did not reach a consensus to rule out any of the reconfiguration scenarios at this stage. Therefore, the recommendation is to take all four scenarios forward for continued modelling" — p.51, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "With births falling, the Group cannot support three delivery suites of a sustainable size in any scenario." — p.52, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Table 2, "Remaining structural deficit (from £34m)": Scenario A (three sites) "~£16m"; B1 (NNUH+QEH) and B2 (NNUH+JPUH) "~£8m–£10m"; Scenario C (NNUH only) "Gap closed (~£2m surplus" — p.9, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Actual 2025/26 deliveries: NNUH 4,693; QEH 1,626; JPUH 1,546; projected 2034/35 (trust trend): QEH 1,347, JPUH 1,105 — Table 10, p.46, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Scenario C "Around 2,450 women change birth site each year, with around 350 leaving the Group" — p.7; "In 2024/25, QEH and JPUH were two of only 12 hospitals in England with fewer than 2,000 annual births" — p.24, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]
[Evidence] Amos investigation context: QEH "adjusted neonatal mortality rate of 0.8 per 1,000 live births in the 12 months ending October 2024, higher than comparable trusts"; "In 2024/25, our three trusts' maternity contributions to the Clinical Negligence Scheme for Trusts totalled £14.7 million." — pp.24–25, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]
[Note] Part 1 foreword also floats "accommodation for women who need to be near the delivery unit" and Part 2 proposes "a patient and family / carer hotel at the Norfolk and Norwich site" for women in early labour (p.62, Part 2).

[LEAD] Group deficit before deficit support was £51m in 2025/26 (up from £31m) and the group projects a £53m recurrent structural deficit by 2034/35 even if it delivers a £263m three-year CIP — and says in that case it is "unlikely" the QEH/JPUH new hospital business cases would be approved.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26 the Group's deficit, before deficit support funding, was £51m – up from £31m of in 2024/25." — p.16, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "delivering £263m of cost improvement programmes (CIPs) over three years ... This is equivalent to an average CIP rate of 5.0% of gross expenditure each year, compared with a historic average of 3.9%. The plan also assumes that 85% (£223m) of savings will be recurrent, compared with 56% historically." — p.16, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "It leaves the Group with a large recurrent structural deficit of £53m. This means that it is unlikely any business case submitted for the New Hospitals at Queen Elizabeth and James Paget will be approved." — p.16, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "Our current arrangements also leave us unable to operate within our allocated resources without additional financial support." — p.10, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]
[Evidence] NOF table: NNUH "Financial deficit: No"; JPUH and QEH "Financial deficit: Yes" — pp.20–21, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]

[LEAD] Any public consultation would push the New Hospital Programme outline business cases for QEH and James Paget back by at least six months (from October 2027 to April–September 2028), with openings slipping to 2034/2035, inflation of "several million pounds per month" per scheme, and £140m–£354m of additional capital depending on how many birth suites survive.
[Evidence] "If these changes result in a public consultation, there will be a delay of a minimum of 6 months to the submission of the Outline Business Cases for the new hospital builds at Queen Elizabeth and James Paget Hospitals. This would mean that they open in 2034." — p.8, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "The current timetable envisages submission of both business cases in October 2027. A later submission would add construction-cost inflation, at several million pounds per month each hospital scheme is delayed." — p.56, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "We have modelled three possible dates for submission of revised Outline Business Cases: April 2028 in the best case, July 2028 in the middle case and September 2028 in the worst case ... potential hospital opening dates include Q3 2033, Q3 2034 and 2035." — p.56, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "Working with the national New Hospital Programme team, we estimate an additional capital requirement of £140 million to £225 million for scenarios retaining two birth suites, and £263 million to £354 million for scenarios with one birth suite." — p.56, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]; foreword gives "approximately £200 million in additional capital" as the provisional figure — p.3, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]

[LEAD] Under the recommended "unselected medical take" model QEH and James Paget would lose overnight emergency surgery and complex elective inpatient surgery to NNUH, keep smaller ITUs, and transfer around 22 patients a day — with about eight QEH patients a day going to hospitals outside the group (mainly Peterborough) unless EEAST agrees to redirect blue-light conveyances to Norwich.
[Evidence] Table 9, Scenario 2 (unselected take) — Emergency surgery: "SDEC only, with no emergency operating overnight; one surgical registrar on site overnight"; Critical care: "Retained, with fewer ITU beds and more HDU beds" — p.41, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "Unselected take, 11 patients a day would transfer from each of QEH and JPUH – 22 patients in total. Almost all JPUH transfers would go to NNUH; of the QEH transfers, around three would go to NNUH and eight to hospitals outside the Group (largely Peterborough)." — p.42, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] "If conveyances from these LSOAs were redirected to Norwich rather than Peterborough, it would increase the share of QEH patients going to NNUH from around 29% to 63%, and it would reduce the impact on Peterborough to less than 5 patients a day. This mitigation would need to be fully assessed by East of England Ambulance Service." — p.44, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Planned care: "QEH and JPUH elective inpatient activity is being consolidated at NNUH, but JPUH retains inpatient joint replacement surgery (T&O)" — p.37, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Fixed point for every scenario: "NNUH will provide complex care and treat patients with the highest-acuity conditions: stroke, cardiac arrest, complex trauma (including fractured neck of femur and GI bleeds) and those requiring interventional radiology" — p.6, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] The "selected medical take" (no ED at QEH/JPUH, critical care "Removed") "would close almost all of the financial deficit on its own, saving around £32m against a £34m structural deficit" but was rejected by the CAG — p.55 and Table 9 p.41, [Clinical Strategy Part 2 — Scenario Appraisal]
[Evidence] Travel: "92 would be assigned to Peterborough, compared with 37 to NNUH" of 135 QEH-catchment LSOAs; public transport median to best alternative "would increase from 56 to 98 minutes" — pp.51–53, [Clinical Strategy Part 1 — Case for Change]
