[LEAD] Leicester and Northamptonshire are abandoning their formal Group model; the group chair has returned to Northamptonshire and Leicester's vice-chair has become interim chair.
[Evidence] "Last month our board announced the decision that UHL and University Hospitals of Northamptonshire will move away from formal Group model arrangements... Andrew Moore has returned to University Hospitals of Northamptonshire NHS Trust as Chair, and I would like to thank Andrew for his leadership over the last two years." – p.1, [E - CEO Report]

[LEAD] Leicester was one of 12 trusts investigated by the Baroness Amos-led National Maternity and Neonatal Investigation; its own baseline self-assessment finds Martha's Rule still not consistently embedded and only 18 of 26 national 10-Point Plan deliverables fully compliant.
[Evidence] "UHL was one of the 12 trusts included in the AMOS investigation" (p.3) and "Martha's Rule is not consistently visible or embedded" (p.10) – [I - Maternity and Neonatal 10 Point Plan]

[LEAD] Leicester, a level 1 organ donation unit that used to facilitate up to 12 donations a year, has facilitated zero organ donations since April 2026; the Quality Committee gave the update only limited assurance.
[Evidence] "UHL, as a level 1 donation unit having previously maintained up to 12 donations a year, had facilitated zero donations since April 2026... The implication of this situation continuing is that the service will potentially receive reduced funding in the future. (Limited Assurance)" – [G3 - Quality Committee Escalation Report, 27 August 2026]

[LEAD] Leicester's paediatric scoliosis service has transferred the majority of affected patients to Nottingham University Hospitals after "service sustainability, patient delay and workforce challenges"; internal and external reviews are under way.
[Evidence] "the Paediatric Scoliosis Service, which had experienced problems with service sustainability, patient delay and workforce challenges had transferred the majority of affected patients to Nottingham University Hospitals. Potentially affected patients have been identified and internal and external reviews still being undertaken (Moderate Assurance)" – [G3 - Quality Committee Escalation Report, 27 August 2026]

[LEAD] Leicester's estates backlog has jumped from £125m to £160m, identified via a desktop review substituted for the nationally procured condition survey; the Finance and Investment Committee gave only moderate assurance.
[Evidence] "Estates - received quarterly estates report and energy procurement report. Noted increase in ERIC return reported backlog from £125m to £160m following a desktop review in lieu of the nationally procured condition survey. FIC noted opportunities for savings through targeted investment in energy – Moderate assurance." – [G4 - Finance and Investment Committee Escalation Report, 24 June 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Leicester's month 4 deficit is £8.9m (£0.3m ahead of plan) but its cost-improvement target doubles from £9.5m to £20.2m in Q2; losing deficit support would cost the trust £4.7m a month in cash.
[Evidence] "The month 4 position for the Trust is a deficit of £8.9m which is £0.3m better than plan... If the current run rate continues and does not improve then the Trust will be significantly adverse to plan over Q2... If the Trust's financial position becomes adverse to plan then deficit support will be lost, which amounts to £4.7m per month." – p.45-46, [F - Integrated Performance Report M4]

[WORTH WATCHING] Two Never Events were reported in July (six year-to-date) and falls exceeded the upper control limit for the first time in 19 months, with seven incidents of moderate-or-greater harm.
[Evidence] "Two Never Events were reported during the month, bringing the year-to-date total to six, and falls have increased above the upper control limit for the first time in 19 months, with seven incidents resulting in moderate or greater harm." – p.4, [F - Integrated Performance Report M4]

[WORTH WATCHING] Leicester's substantive workforce fell by around 190 WTE year-on-year but pay spend still rose from £88.3m to £91.2m over the same period; finance and people leads say they are "exploring reasons for this."
[Evidence] "Despite substantive WTE figure having decreased from 17,486 in Jul 2025 to 17,296 in Jul 26, spend has risen from £88.3m to £91.2m in same period – people & finance leads are exploring reasons for this (likely in part due to pay awards, overtime etc)" – [J - UHL's Deliverables for 26/27 Q1 Progress]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Operations and Performance Committee gave only limited assurance to a deep dive on elective orthopaedics – the service driving most of the trust's 65-week breaches – saying the report gave "insufficient detail" on mitigation plans.
[Evidence] "The service remains one of the most operationally challenged in the Trust... Whilst the report was detailed and informative it provided insufficient detail on the plans being managed to positively impact on the challenges and as such the committee request a follow up covering the detail on actual mitigations in play. Overall limited assurance was taken." – [G5 - Operations and Performance Committee Escalation Report, 25 June 2026]

[FOI] Request the scope, terms of reference and any findings to date of the internal and external reviews into University Hospitals of Leicester's paediatric scoliosis service.
[Evidence] "Potentially affected patients have been identified and internal and external reviews still being undertaken" – [G3 - Quality Committee Escalation Report, 27 August 2026]

[FOI] Request University Hospitals of Leicester's Preliminary Antibiotic Resistance Report, which the Quality Committee rated limited assurance after finding no progress since the previous update.
[Evidence] "This report provided an overview of antimicrobial stewardship and antimicrobial resistance across UHL. It highlights shortfalls in workforce capacity and data analytics capability.
