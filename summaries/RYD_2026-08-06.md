## Top lines

[LEAD] Independent culture review at SECAmb found staff in positions of authority had misused their power, professional boundaries had not been maintained, and students and junior colleagues faced safeguarding risk — the trust published only a seven-page summary.
[Evidence] "The Review identified examples where professional boundaries had not always been maintained appropriately and where positions of authority had, at times, been used in ways that undermined trust." Reviewer named as "Jacqui Marshall-Dibble, an experienced independent consultant and Fellow of the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (FCIPD)", commissioned January 2026, focused on "professional boundaries, the use of positional power, student experience and safeguarding, leadership behaviours" — p.185–186, [Building on our Culture Improvement journey, Item 53-26]

[LEAD] The same review found pockets of "command-and-control" sub-culture and informal power structures operating against the trust's stated culture, and identified safeguarding risk where there are "inherent power imbalances, especially in relation to students".
[Evidence] "These environments were characterised by informal power structures, longstanding behaviours and, in some instances, command-and-control leadership approaches that differed from the culture the Trust is seeking to embed." … "The Review highlighted particular risks where there are inherent power imbalances, especially in relation to students and individuals early in their careers." — p.186–187, [Building on our Culture Improvement journey, Item 53-26]

[LEAD] SECAmb has scrapped a completed Make Ready Centre procurement after the outcome had already been agreed by its board and the Cabinet Office, and restarted the tender — expecting a 4–6 week delay to implementation.
[Evidence] "Make Ready Centre provider tender was completed with recommended outcomes agreed by Board and Cabinet Office. However, the current procurement has been discontinued and a new tender process commenced for these services. A review identified a need to simplify the tendering process and revisit the specification." … "Make Ready re-procurement will impact mobilisation period, but this can be partly mitigated through contingency days in the original plan. Overall, a 4-6 week delay in implementation is expected." — p.132, [Integrated Quality Report — Sustainable Partner, Item 47-26b]

[LEAD] SECAmb has a fallback plan for a 10 per cent corporate cost restructuring from Q1 2027/28, and says further failure on savings would mean "accelerating corporate function consolidation" across its new group with South Central Ambulance Service.
[Evidence] BAF R4 mitigating action: "If CIP & productivity insufficient then plan to undertake corporate cost restructuring (10% for Q1 27/28)" and "Contingency in place around corporate cost restructuring for Q1 27/28. Evaluated at month three." — p.67, [Board Assurance Framework, Item 47-26a]. Paired with: "If not, further additive cost reduction actions will be worked up, likely to involve accelerating corporate function consolidation across the Group." — p.132, [Integrated Quality Report]

[LEAD] An additional £5m of NHSE performance funding built into SECAmb's 2026/27 plan is now being "de-risked" with the regulator because response times are behind plan.
[Evidence] "Current C2 delivery in the context of activity above plan and increased handover delays will be reviewed with NHSE with a view to de-risking the additional £5million performance funding expected (and in plan) in October." … "Planned plan delivery reviews in August and September will assess confidence in plan delivery, additional recovery action impacts, and NHSE disposition regarding the £5m H2 performance fund and will determine next steps." — p.132, [Integrated Quality Report]. Separately: "The Trust has been awarded an additional £10.7m ambulance growth funding … to enable the Trust to deliver a revised trajectory improvement in C2 mean to 25 minutes for 2026/27." — p.138

[LEAD] Not recruiting newly qualified paramedics is listed as one of SECAmb's agreed cost improvement actions — two months after the board told a public questioner the NQP recruitment reduction reflected a national over-supply.
[Evidence] "The Trust has agreed action plans to deliver CIP that include reduced cost of employment, vacancy freeze, tighter control of overtime, not recruiting newly qualified paramedics, accelerating progress with increasing call handling and other operational KPIs. The Board has agreed to fill any remaining gap with non-recurrent budget underspends and balance sheet provisions." — p.139, [Integrated Quality Report — finance slides]. Compare public question answer at the June board: "JL explained that there is a national shortage of NQP roles due to over-supply and so some graduates are unable to secure employment … Currently, we have no paramedic vacancies." — p.9, [Minutes 4 June 2026, item 34/26]

[LEAD] SECAmb delivered its planned month-three surplus only by not delivering operational standards, its own finance report says — with 68 per cent of savings non-recurrent against a planned 22 per cent and a risk-adjusted full-year efficiency gap of £2.6m.
[Evidence] "In summary, whilst the Trust has delivered the planned (£167k) surplus in Month 3 it did not maintain operational performance standards. The position is largely supported by non-recurrent factors, including vacancies and non-pay timing benefits." — p.154. "This heavily relied on the non-recurrent savings (68% versus the planned 22%), such as recruitment freeze and leaner, non-pay purchasing" — p.152. "The year-to-date under delivery is (£796k) and the risk adjusted forecast gap for the year is (£2,621k)." — p.155, [M03 (June 2026) Financial Performance, Item 48-26]

[LEAD] SECAmb's board assurance framework states that failure to deliver the cost improvement programme could mean a "potential return to NHSE financial oversight" and an "inability to meet liabilities to staff".
[Evidence] "If the Trust fails to deliver its cost improvement programme (CIP), then it will not achieve financial breakeven for 26/27, resulting in unrealised benefits for patients, in erosion of cash reserves, removal of investment capacity for transformation, inability to meet liabilities to staff, and potential return to NHSE financial oversight." Risk scored 16, "Outside appetite" — p.67, [Board Assurance Framework, Item 47-26a]

[LEAD] Category 2 mean hit 28 minutes 30 seconds in June against a 25-minute target, on the trust's busiest day on record; hospital handover time was running 1 hour 57 minutes above plan.
[Evidence] "June C2 mean was 28:30" against "C2 mean target for 2026/27 is 25min"; "C1 performance in June was 08:18 against an ARP target of 7 minutes" — p.116. "C2 mean to 5th June was 25:50 and behind plan by 3:39. Significant negative impacts from activity above plan at 4.1% (8,266 incidents), handover time at ED 1:57 above plan" — p.132. "Demand for the Trust's 999 service has increased by 8%, significantly above the 4% growth assumption within the 2026/27 Operating Plan … culminating in the Trust's busiest day on record on 26 June, when the Trust escalated to the highest level of the Resource Escalation Action Plan (REAP), an exceptional position for this time of year." — p.146, [Finance & Investment Committee Assurance Report, Item 48-26]

[LEAD] SECAmb's board committee has admitted the trust mishandled communication of its virtual care overhaul to staff, after a backlash that produced a joint executive/trade union statement — and says it has little confidence of hitting this year's hear-and-treat target.
[Evidence] "The committee acknowledged the concerns expressed by some staff groups and there was an honest acceptance that some mistakes have been made in how we have communicated to-date." … "there is much less confidence in the ability to deliver against the H&T target for this year." — p.164–165, [Quality & Patient Safety Committee Assurance Report, Item 50-26]. "There was also a discussion about the recent feedback from some staff group related to virtual care and the committee acknowledged the positive joint statement made in response to this, by the executive and trade unions." — p.181, [People Committee Assurance Report, Item 52-26].
