## Top lines

[LEAD] BHRUT now forecasts a most likely deficit of £75.1m this year (downside £93m) and admits it is unlikely to receive the £41.3m deficit support funding it accepted from NHSE and the ICB in August — the forecast excludes it entirely.

[LEAD] The board is being asked to approve emergency cash applications of up to £25.3m to stay afloat to year end — cash falls below the £3m regulatory minimum in December, and the contingency if NHSE refuses is to suspend payments to other NHS bodies and NHS Resolution. Worst case has cash exhausted by end of October.
[Evidence] "Revenue Support PDC applications will be required for: December 2026: £3.6m; January 2027: £4.9m; February 2027: £8.9m; March 2027: £7.9m. BPPC 70% by value in September; all 12 months below the 95% target — p.218.

[LEAD] The trust's nationally published mortality data has become "artificially low" (SHMI 57.67 vs expected ~100) because its EPR implementation caused under-reporting of patient spells and it decommissioned its data validation software — reinstatement is "recommended as a matter of urgency" but has not been secured "despite funding being available".
[Evidence] "As coded data are no longer routinely validated before submission, patients may appear less clinically complex than they actually are… resulting in artificially low mortality indicators"; "The SHMI is at 57.67 (-4.44)"; "Reinstatement of the validation software is recommended as a matter of urgency. The Director of Business Intelligence has previously escalated this issue through IOLG; however, no positive outcome has yet been secured despite funding being available" — p.140, [Learning from Deaths Q1 2026/27, Item 15a]. Monthly provider spells fell from 18,265 (Oct-25) to 11,328 (Nov-25, EPR go-live month); only 9 of 140 validated deaths had a completed Summary of Care document — p.140–141.

[LEAD] BHRUT has been named a national mortality alert outlier for vascular surgery (lower limb bypass and major lower limb amputation), and the CQC has asked for assurance.
[Evidence] "The Trust has been identified as an alert outlier for the National Vascular Registry (NVR) for procedures performed between Jan 2022 and Dec 2024 for the Mortality (Lower limb bypass and Major lower limb amputation). The service have drafted a response and action plan which was submitted to the CQC following approval by the CMO." — p.119, [Integrated Quality and Safety Report M6, Item 13]

[Evidence] "The CPE outbreak across Sahara A and Sahara B involves 11 colonised patients, with confirmed transmission between 3 cases. ## FOI leads

1. **Revenue Support PDC application and Chair/CEO letter.** Request from BHRUT (and NHS England London region): the December 2026 Revenue Support PDC application, supporting cashflow forecasts, and the joint Chair/Chief Executive assurance letter (Appendix B, p.233, Item 21 — not in the public pack). Why: shows the conditions the trust has accepted and its own account of how it got here; applications of this kind are rarely published.

2. **Redacted flu paper.** The pack file is literally titled "Seasonal Flu Vaccination Programme 2026-27_Redacted.pdf" and several pages (pp.128, 131, 135–137) are blank in the published version, including the action plan. Request the unredacted programme and action plan from BHRUT, including the "Financial investment to provide additional Flu Nurses" (p.130). Why: what was redacted from a routine flu plan, and the cost, at a trust forecasting a £75m deficit.

3. **Maternity report appendices held off-pack.** "The Board is advised to refer to the appendices which can be found on Admin Control" (p.47, Item 11) — 13 appendices including Appendix 9 (the Baroness Amos National Maternity and Neonatal Investigation's individual BHRUT report) and Appendix 10 (NHSE's 30 June 2026 letter). Request from BHRUT. Why: the trust's individual Amos report, cited as finding "a gap between the organisation's perception of the quality of care being delivered and the experiences reported by some women and families" (p.63), is not published with the pack; all 10 Amos immediate priority actions are only "partially compliant" (p.63).

4. **The decision to decommission the mortality data validation software.** Request from BHRUT: the decision record/business case for not renewing the data validation software, the IOLG escalation papers from the Director of Business Intelligence, and correspondence with Healthcare Evaluation Data about the SHMI distortion (pp.139–141, Item 15a). Why: the trust says the issue was escalated, funding was available, and "no positive outcome has yet been secured" — someone decided not to fix a known national data integrity problem.
