# Wirral University Teaching Hospital NHS FT board — 3 June 2026

- **Source:** https://www.wuth.nhs.uk/about-us/who-we-are/board-meetings/
- **Pack:** 1 file (296pp combined pack)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-05T00:00:00Z
- **Note:** This is a JOINT "Group Board" covering two foundation trusts — Wirral University Teaching Hospital FT (WUTH, ods RBL) and Wirral Community Health & Care FT (WCHC). They share a single board, joint executives and a joint integrated performance report, and are pursuing a statutory transaction (merger). Lines below are WUTH unless flagged WCHC.

## Top lines

[LEAD] WUTH confirmed to NHSE a revised 2025/26 forecast deficit of £49.5m — a £27.4m adverse swing on plan — and is in NHSE Tier 1 with financial undertakings in place.
[Evidence] "as approved by the Board, the Trust has confirmed to NHSE a revised forecast outturn for 2025/26 of a £49.5m deficit, representing a £27.4m adverse variance against the plan" — p.10, [Minutes of 1 April 2026 meeting, Item 9]. BAF lists external assurance as "Full engagement in Tier 1 meetings" and a gap as "Exit criteria from financial undertakings re: UEC performance," with "Regional oversight from June 2026" — pp.96, 98, [Joint BAF 26-27 Item 8, risks ID01/ID02].

[LEAD] The £15.2m M11 deficit overspend was driven in part by a "Sterile Services critical incident" that caused a loss of income.
[Evidence] "the Trust reported a deficit of £32.3m to date which excluding Deficit Support Funding (DSF) which is £13.6m adverse variance to plan with the most significant change compared to month 9 being the recognition of the loss of income due to the impact of the Sterile Services critical incident" — p.13, [Minutes 1 April, Item 15, WUTH Finance Business Performance Committee]. (M11 minute elsewhere cites £36m deficit excl DSF / £15.2m adverse — p.9.)

[LEAD] NHSE and UKHSA used WUTH's Arrowe Park site to house UK nationals repatriated from a cruise ship after Hantavirus cases — the same site used in 2020 for Wuhan/Diamond Princess COVID repatriations.
[Evidence] "We were asked by NHS England and the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) to house UK citizens repatriated from the MV Hondius cruise ship in the accommodation block on the Arrowe Park Hospital site to provide them with a safe place for their isolation period, following cases of Hantavirus… We welcomed the guests on Sunday 10 May 2026 and nobody showing any symptoms was transferred to the site." — p.23, [Joint CEO Report Item 7].

[LEAD] WUTH's 2026/27 CIP target is £30.7m (6%, 305 WTE) but the risk-adjusted forecast delivery is only £23.1m, and the trust has a non-clinical vacancy freeze in force.
[Evidence] "the primary risk to achieving the 2026/27 financial position is delivery of our CIP target of £30.7m. The current risk adjusted forecast for delivery is £23.1m"; "a non-clinical vacancy freeze remaining in effect" — p.70, [Finance / CFO Summary, IPR Item 7].

[LEAD] WUTH's 2026/27 financial plan has still not been agreed with NHSE and "further measures are required to meet the mandated control total."
[Evidence] "The Trust's financial plan is yet to be agreed with NHSE and therefore remains subject to revision"; Board asked to "Note that the plan has not been approved by NHSE and that further measures are required to meet the mandated control total." — pp.70-71, [Finance, IPR Item 7].

[LEAD] WUTH is running on the minimum cash balance permitted by NHSE, depends on monthly emergency revenue support applications, and warns this is hitting supplier payments.
[Evidence] "The cash balance at the end of M1 was £2.7m, in line with NHSE's stipulated minimum cash balance… The revenue support applications for April and May were approved, and the application for June has been submitted… until a sustainable financial position is achieved, this remains a significant issue." Cash support is "driven by having a non-cash backed deficit" and has "a direct impact on the Better Payment Practice Code (BPPC) target by volume and value." — pp.70, 73, [Finance, IPR Item 7].

[WORTH WATCHING] WUTH sits in NHSE Oversight Framework segment 4 (the worst quartile) across access, patient-safety, workforce and finance domains.
[Evidence] NOF Q3 25/26 dashboard shows WUTH segment quartile 4 for Access to Services, Patient Safety, People & Workforce and Finance & Productivity; combined finance score 4 — p.37, [Joint IPR, NOF Dashboard]. WCHC by contrast scores segment 1 on most domains but segment 4 on People & Workforce — p.40.

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff survey results fell at both trusts, with WUTH's response rate dropping to 42% and the chief people officer warning the merger is creating "an uncertain environment."
[Evidence] "WUTH response rate decreased to 42%, below both its 2024 position and the national average (47%)… of the 9 People Promise elements, 5 were significantly lower for WUTH"; "DS commented that organisational pressures and the merger was creating an uncertain environment for staff… and the need to deliver the merger at pace." Members noted "the 2026 staff survey results were likely to be challenging and may deteriorate further." — p.19, [Minutes 1 April, Item 28]. BAF workforce risk ID03 scored 4x4 (16); culture risk ID04 12 — p.95.

[WORTH WATCHING] WUTH breached its annual C.difficile trajectory; April recorded 14 cases against a target of ≤8, including a ward outbreak.
[Evidence] "the Trust had breached its agreed annual trajectory for Clostridioides difficile (C diff) infections" — p.14, [Minutes 1 April, Item 16]. "In April there were 14 cases of C.diff… Areas of Concern: Ward outbreak detected from sentinel surveillance on ward 25; Delay in obtaining stool samples; Delay in isolation due to high levels of occupancy" — p.43, [CNO Quality & Safety Summary, IPR].

[WORTH WATCHING] WUTH's mortality indicator (SHMI) is showing special-cause concerning variation and remains above the expected range.
[Evidence] SHMI 1.060 at Dec-25, "Special cause concerning variation," above the ≥0.95 threshold; "SHIM remains in as expected range, although there has been an upward trend due to coding backlog issues" — pp.41-42, [CMO Quality & Safety Summary, IPR]. Note: coding backlog "now cleared" per same paper.

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the financial undertakings / exit criteria agreed between WUTH and NHSE on UEC performance. The BAF refers to "Exit criteria from financial undertakings re: UEC performance" and "New engagement arrangements with NHSE" plus "Regional oversight from June 2026" but the documents are not in the pack. Request from WUTH and NHSE North West the undertakings, correspondence and the terms of the new oversight arrangement — pp.96, 98, [Joint BAF 26-27, risks ID01/ID02].

[FOI] Request the Sterile Services critical incident report. The pack attributes a material loss of income to a "Sterile Services critical incident" but gives no detail on cause, scope, duration or cost. Request from WUTH the incident report, root-cause analysis and the quantified income/financial impact — p.13, [Minutes 1 April, Item 15].

[FOI] Request the medium-term financial plan (MTFP, 2026/27–2028/29) and the revised plan submission to NHSE. Referred to repeatedly as the route to sustainability but not published in the pack; BAF risk ID05 (failure to deliver the three-year financial plan) scored 5x3 (15) — pp.70, 95, [Finance / BAF ID05].

[FOI] Request the WCHC/WUTH statutory transaction (merger) business case and integration plan. BAF risk ID09 is "Failure to effectively deliver the 2-year integration plan including delivery of the transaction between WCHC and WUTH"; the CEO report says "the statutory transaction remained ongoing." Request the transaction case, NHSE approval correspondence and integration timeline — p.93, [BAF ID09]; p.6, [Minutes Item 7].
