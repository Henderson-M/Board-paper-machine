## Top lines

[LEAD] SWYPFT has published a paper drawing "system-wide learning" from a recent run of CQC inspections at St Andrew's Healthcare (Northampton) that found failures to protect patients from harm, abuse allegations, and "closed ward cultures" — the trust places West Yorkshire secure patients there and is repatriating two.
[Evidence] "The Care Quality Commission (CQC) has published a series of inspection reports relating to St Andrew's Healthcare (Northampton)... These reports identify failures to protect patients from harm, unsafe staffing levels, poor risk management, evidence of closed ward cultures, and a lack of effective organisational oversight. Importantly, inspectors noted that many of these issues had been raised previously but had not been resolved... The most recent report was published on 13 March 2026 and can be found here: https://www.cqc.org.uk/location/1-121538260" — p.513, [Assurance and Learning from St Andrew's Healthcare, item 11.7]. Repatriation: "Active work is underway to support the repatriation of two West Yorkshire service users back into the region" — p.18 [April minutes / CEO report assurance]

[LEAD] The trust's own St Andrew's "assurance" paper concedes material gaps in SWYPFT's secure services — 18 wards over a 20% unregistered vacancy rate, 217 cancelled staff breaks in a single month, and a "limited assurance" risk-assessment audit.
[Evidence] "unregistered vacancies remain high, with 18 wards exceeding a total vacancy rate of 20% for unregistered posts" and "During May 2026 there were 217 cancelled staff breaks across all services... Missed breaks were highest in working age adult wards (59), and forensic low secure (56)"; "a recent 360 audit delivered limited assurance regarding risk assessment" — p.518–520, [item 11.7]

[LEAD] The trust is forecasting a return to deficit risk this year, recording a year-to-date deficit of £1.2m at month 2 against a breakeven target, and has launched a Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme open until end of July alongside workforce-reduction plans being submitted to NHS England.
[Evidence] "At the end of month 2 the Trust has recorded a deficit in excess of £1m... This month's Trust Board papers include our plans for workforce reduction which are being submitted to NHS England. In addition, we have recently launched a Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme (MARS) that will be open for applications until the end of July" — p.34, [Chief Executive's Report, item 8]. IPR: "Our year-to-date deficit is £1.2m. This remains marginally ahead of plan, but action is required to change the run rate direction and ensure that the breakeven target can be delivered" — p.140, [Integrated Performance Report, item 10.1]

[LEAD] Following the deaths of two female inpatients, the chief executive has commissioned an external review of the women's pathway of care; an interim action plan with extra psychological therapy is in place pending the findings.
[Evidence] "Following the tragic deaths of two female inpatients, the chief executive asked that an external review of the women's pathway of care be commissioned. The findings will provide independent assurance and inform the ongoing redesign of the women's pathway. Pending the outcome of the review, an interim action plan has been put in place which includes increased psychological therapy resource on affected wards" — p.15, [April minutes]; also "Development of the Women's Pathway which will be informed by an external peer review commissioned by the Chief Operating Officer" — p.271, [Patient Safety Oversight Report, item 11.1]

[LEAD] Racist abuse of staff is a sustained problem on the trust's mental health wards, with 27 incidents of race against staff in May alone — 13 of them at Cheswold Park Hospital across five wards.
[Evidence] "The Trust had 27 incidents involving race against staff on mental health wards during May. Thirteen of these incidents occurred at Cheswold Park Hospital across 5 wards/teams... a robust process of personal and clinical support has been created to safeguard staff who experience racist abuse during their work in a clinical role" — p.134, [Integrated Performance Report, item 10.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Three forensic service users are on exceptional packages of care costing roughly £200k a month each — about £7m a year — with the financial risk shared across West Yorkshire.
[Evidence] "Currently there are three service users each receiving packages of care costing approximately £200k per month, equating to an annualised cost of approximately £7m" — p.18, [April minutes]; risk 1511 assurance score reduced from 16 to 12 — p.10, [April minutes]

[WORTH WATCHING] The pack flags an NMC error affecting trusts nationally — a 12-year failure to manage disclosures from staff convicted of offences, with 421 individuals due letters — and the trust says it is not yet clear if any of its staff are affected.
[Evidence] "The Nursing & Midwifery Council (NMC) recently wrote to all trusts highlighting an historical issue... over a 12-year period, meaning they have had to review 18,000 cases. They have now narrowed it down to 421 individuals who should have received a letter from the NMC on 27 May 2026... We are not aware if any of our staff are affected at this point" — p.36, [Chief Executive's Report, item 8]

[WORTH WATCHING] Pressure ulcers developing or deteriorating under SWYPFT care show "special cause variation" — an increase concentrated in Barnsley physical health services.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, the Trust identified a period of special cause variation, reflecting an increase in the number of Category 2, 3 and 4 pressure ulcers that developed or deteriorated under SWYPFT care. Most incidents (98%) were reported within Barnsley physical health services" — p.276, [Patient Safety Oversight Report, item 11.1]
