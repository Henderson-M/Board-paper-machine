# Wirral University Teaching Hospital NHS Foundation Trust board — 4 November 2026

- **Source:** https://www.wuth.nhs.uk/about-us/who-we-are/board-meetings/
- **Pack:** 2 files (joint WUTH / Wirral Community Health & Care "Group Board" pack, 533 pp; maternity appendices, ~40 slides)
- **Analysed:** 2026-11-05T10:30:00Z

Context: WUTH and Wirral Community Health & Care (WCHC) share a joint board and executive team ("Group Board") and are working towards a statutory transaction. WUTH is under NHSE legal undertakings, was in NOF segment 4 until Q1 2026/27 and is in the NHSE North West Finance Performance and Recovery Meeting (FPRM) process.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Formal complaints caseload at WUTH rose 150% in 2025/26 with overdue cases up more than fourfold, re-opened complaints up 57% and more Ombudsman referrals — the annual report calls it "the single most significant deterioration in the year".
[Evidence] "WUTH began 2025/26 with an active caseload of 40 complaints, 11 of which had exceeded the 40-working-day timescale. By the end of the year, this had risen to 100 active complaints, with 47 breaching the timescale – representing a 150% increase in overall caseload and a more than fourfold increase in overdue cases. This is the single most significant deterioration in the year and reverses the improvement achieved in 2024/25." — p.267, [WUTH Complaints Annual Report 2025/26, Item 20]. "against a 31% rise in formal complaints ... the average lengthened to 56 working days ... Re-opened complaints rose by 57%, reversing last year's improvement, and referrals to the Ombudsman also increased." — p.283. In-month: "17% of formal complaints were responded to within 40 working days, compared with 31% in August" and Level 1 concerns "50% above the preceding 12-month average" — p.50, [IPR, CNO summary]. QSC chair alert: "the rise in numbers outpaced the growth in completed responses, with a substantially larger open and overdue caseload at year end" — p.137.

[LEAD] A&E four-hour performance fell to 53.6% in September and the trust's own operations summary blames deterioration on its new "Single Front Door"; ambulance handovers over 45 minutes hit 28% and 923 patients were in corridor care in the month — at a trust whose undertakings are about UEC.
[Evidence] "4-hour performance remains a significant concern, with deterioration following implementation of the Single Front Door." and "Ambulance handover performance is deteriorating, with increasing handover times and 45-minute breaches requiring urgent operational focus." — p.63, [IPR, EMD Operations Summary WUTH].
