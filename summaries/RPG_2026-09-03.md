# Oxleas NHS Foundation Trust board — 3 September 2026

- **Source:** https://oxleas.nhs.uk/download/trust-board-papers-september-2026.pdf
- **Pack:** 1 combined file, "Trust Board papers - September 2026"
- **Analysed:** 2026-09-01

## Top lines

[LEAD] Oxleas' month 4 breakeven has been bought with £8.6m of one-off support — nearly £1m a month.
[Evidence] "We remain on plan at Month 4, but this has required £8.6m of one-off financial support, including a further £2.4m in July. Of the £8.6m used to date, £4.9m relates to commitments previously approved by the Board, including the Healios contract with the remaining £3.7m supporting the delivery of the breakeven position (this equates to an average spend of just under a million pounds each month)" — p.105, Appendix 1 Financial Performance, [Enclosure 11 — Business and Infrastructure Committee report]

[LEAD] The audit committee has formally alerted the board that employment tribunal cases and legal fees are rising.
[Evidence] "The Audit and Risk Assurance Committee wish to alert the Board of Directors that: The increase in employment tribunal cases and associated legal fees continues to be a pressure for the trust. This will be discussed further by the People Committee." — p.119, [Audit and Risk Assurance Committee key issues report, meeting of 20 July 2026]
[Evidence] The committee's own agenda for that meeting carries a standing item: "Overview of employment tribunal claims — To note — Rachel Evans, Chief People and Strategy Officer, Enc 12" — p.122, [ARAC agenda, 20 July 2026]

[LEAD] The trust has churned through three chief finance officers in a matter of months.
[Evidence] "Our Chief Finance Officer Azara Mukhtar left Oxleas in August and we wish her well in her new role as Group Chief Finance Officer for St George's, Epsom and St Helier University Hospitals and Health Group. We look forward to welcoming Damien O'Brien when he joins us as Chief Finance Officer from Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust. In the meantime, Alex Owoo is our Interim Chief Finance Officer." — [Chief Executive's report, Board membership changes]
[Evidence] The July minutes record: "The Board of Directors noted that this is the last board meeting for AM as she will leave Oxleas in August to take up the post of Group Chief Finance Officer for the St Georges, Epsom and St Helier Hospital Group. The board thanked AM for her commitment over the past six years." — [Minutes of 2 July 2026 board]
[Note] The month 4 finance report is signed by "Alexander Owoo, Acting Chief Finance Officer" with "Christine Caton, Interim Associate Director of Financial Management & Planning" — three of the senior finance posts named in this pack are interim or acting.

[LEAD] Nearly 2,700 children are waiting more than a year for a first neurodevelopmental appointment, with the average ADHD wait at 75 weeks.
[Evidence] "In July 2026, 1,460 children and young people on the ADHD diagnostic pathway, 842 on the ASD pathway and 415 on joint pathways had waited more than 52 weeks for a first appointment. Average waits were 75 weeks for ADHD, 48 weeks for ASD and 46 weeks for joint pathways. Additional Healios capacity is supporting throughput" — [Operational/Directorate report, Specialist Services]
[Evidence] Adult autism: "At 31 July 2026, six people had waited more than 52 weeks for a first autism diagnostic appointment, and the average wait for those awaiting a first appointment was 18 weeks." — [same, Adult Learning Disability]

[LEAD] The forensic and PICU directorate is £5m short on a £7.2m savings target, with demand said to have outgrown funded capacity.
[Evidence] "Demand for male and female PICU is above comparable system trends, prompting further analysis, peer review with South London and Maudsley colleagues and discussion with commissioners about whether demand has outgrown funded capacity. The Directorate must also close a substantial cost improvement gap: against a budget of approximately £55 million and a £7.2 million target, £2.85 million of recurrent savings has been identified, leaving around £5 million still to secure." — [Directorate report]
[Evidence] The same report records that "concerns regarding competency, culture, leadership and communication persist following the revised clinical model and reduced bed base; phase two of the improvement programme will therefore strengthen clinical and leadership assurance, address estate issues, consult staff and incorporate the actions arising from the July 2026 CQC Mental Health Act visit." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The CQC has lifted its Regulation 12 warning notice at HMP Rochester, but raised new concerns at HMP Leyhill.
[Evidence] "At HMP Rochester, a focused inspection found significant improvement since the concerns identified in 2025, particularly in governance, incident reporting, staffing, medicines management and access to care. The replacement of the Regulation 12 Warning Notice with an Action Plan Request reflects a marked reduction in risk" — [Directorate report, offender healthcare]
[Evidence] "Significant progress was recognised at HMP Rochester, where CQC confirmed that existing Warning Notices will be removed and no further Warning Notices will be issued... The Committee also considered inspection findings at HMP Leyhill, including concerns relating to [DNACPR documentation and subcontractor governance arrangements]" — [Quality Committee key issues report]
[Evidence] The same programme has been "shortlisted in the HSJ Awards 2026... in the Performance Recovery Award category" — [Chief Executive's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board is arguing about whether a flu jab target belongs on its assurance framework because of the oversight score attached to it.
[Evidence] "The committee considered whether the requirement to achieve a flu vaccination uptake of 60% in 2026/27 should be recorded on the Board Assurance Framework (BAF), as this will impact on our National Oversight Framework (NOF) score. It was clarified that if the target is not achieved, the trust would achieve a NOF score of 4 for that metric only, not segment 4 overall. The committee noted that there were other compelling reasons for staff to have the flu vaccine, and the risk should not be based on the impact on our NOF score." — p.119, [Audit and Risk Assurance Committee key issues report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Every trust board in England must sign an NHSE provider capability self-assessment by 2 October — Oxleas is doing it now.
[Evidence] "NHS England requires every NHS trust and foundation trust board to complete an annual board self-assessment. We undertook this last year and need to complete this year's process by 2 October 2026." — [Chief Executive's report]
[Evidence] "The template will be signed by Chair and CEO before submission on 2 October 2026." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England has set three national mental health sector priority metrics for 2026/27, with formal performance management from April 2027 — and Oxleas questions whether one of them is measurable for a trust like it.
[Evidence] "NHS England has now confirmed the three national Mental Health Sector Priority metrics for 2026/27: Adult Inappropriate Out-of-Area Placements, A&E 12-hour waits for patients known to local mental health services, and Children and Young People's services four-week clock stops. Trusts are expected to support national definition work during Q1-Q2, participate in shadow reporting during Q3-Q4, and move into formal performance management from April 2027." — [Chief Executive's report]
[Evidence] "'95% reduction' target, which depends entirely on the baseline they intend to use; for an organisation with near-zero usage, the applicability of this reduction metric needs further discussion. For A&E liaison referrals, the national definition is still evolving." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust missed the national supplier payment target in July.
[Evidence] "The national target is for 95% of valid invoices to be paid within 30 days. In July, we paid 92% of invoices within 30 days by number and 90% by value" — p.108, [Enclosure 11 — Business and Infrastructure Committee report]
[Evidence] Cash remains comfortable: "At the end of July, we held £56.4m in cash, compared with £63.2m in June... sufficient funds to cover around 40 days of operating expenditure and is line with our forecasted position of £53.1m for March 2027." — [same]

## FOI leads

[FOI] **The employment tribunal caseload.** The audit committee has escalated rising tribunal cases and legal costs to the board but the pack gives no numbers. *Request from Oxleas:* the "Overview of employment tribunal claims" paper (Enc 12) taken to the Audit and Risk Assurance Committee on 20 July 2026, the number of live and concluded tribunal claims by year and by jurisdiction (discrimination, whistleblowing, unfair dismissal), and total external legal spend on employment matters for the last three financial years.

[FOI] **The £8.6m of one-off financial support.** *Request from Oxleas:* a scheme-by-scheme breakdown of the £8.6m of non-recurrent support used to month 4, the £3.7m specifically applied to hold the breakeven position, and the finance papers setting out how long the trust can sustain it.

[FOI] **The Healios contract.** Named in the pack as a board-approved commitment inside the £8.6m, and again as the source of "additional Healios capacity" on the children's neurodevelopmental pathway. *Request from Oxleas:* the contract value, term, activity volumes and unit price, and the procurement route used.

[FOI] **The July 2026 CQC Mental Health Act visit** to the forensic/PICU services. *Request from Oxleas and the CQC:* the visit findings letter and the trust's action plan, and the "phase two" improvement programme documentation addressing the "concerns regarding competency, culture, leadership and communication".

[FOI] **HMP Leyhill.** *Request from Oxleas and the CQC:* the inspection findings including the DNACPR documentation and subcontractor governance concerns, and the subcontractor's identity and contract.
