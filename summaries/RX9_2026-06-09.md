## Top lines

[LEAD] EMAS declared a Business Continuity Incident in late May after one of its busiest non-winter days, handling 4,500+ 999 calls, and is operating in "enhanced incident response mode" into the end of June.
[Evidence] "On 26 May 2026 we experienced one of the busiest days outside winter, handling over 4,500 999 calls. At 19:15 on that day the Trust declared a Business Continuity Incident… We ended the Business Continuity Incident at 13:00 on 28 May 2026… It is our intention to continue this approach until the end of June 2026 to support recovery." — p.3, [Chief Executive's Report PB/26/103]

[LEAD] Category 2 response in May ran well behind the trust's own recovery trajectory — 36min 33sec against a planned 30min 4sec, with the year-to-date mean (33min 11sec) above the 29min 22sec annual plan.
[Evidence] "The planned position for May was 30 minutes 4 seconds and at the end of May the Trust's position was 36 minutes and 33 seconds." — p.3, [Chief Executive's Report PB/26/103]

[LEAD] Emergency Operations Centre clinical staffing is running roughly 30% below plan — 111 WTE against a planned 159 — a shortfall the trust links to falling Hear and Treat rates and patient-safety risk.
[Evidence] "The service recorded 111 WTE against a planned 159 WTE, representing a 30.08% shortfall." Also: Hear and Treat at 17.86% vs 22.50% plan, "20.64% under plan." — IBR slides, [Integrated Board Report PB/26/104]

[LEAD] Only one of seven operational targets was achieved at year-end and no efficiency targets were delivered, with a NED noting only a quarter of patient transport targets were met.
[Evidence] "Oliver Newbould commented that the year-end position involved demand exceeding plan and handover delays significantly higher than anticipated. Only one of the seven operational targets had been achieved, with no efficiency targets delivered and only a quarter of Patient Transport Service targets met." — p.7, [Minutes 5 May 2026 PB/26/100]

[LEAD] The trust is committing to add 40 new double-crewed ambulances over three years under a "Right Sizing" programme funded by ICB Clusters, with crews arriving from Q3 2026/27 and full delivery by March 2028.
[Evidence] "increasing our operational capacity by adding 40 new double crewed ambulances into our service… we will start to see them entering the organisation during quarter 3… with the programme being fully completed by March 2028." — p.4, [Chief Executive's Report PB/26/103]; capital funding of £39.872m for 2026/27 confirmed, "of those schemes being funded by Urgent and Emergency Care capital only the 40 Double Crewed Ambulances has been rated green." — p.7, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/106]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 4% / £14.45m cost improvement programme delivered nothing in-month against its own schemes and the trust scores only a 31% probability of full delivery, while WTE is 240 below plan (red-rated).
[Evidence] CIP risk: "4% Efficiency required for 2026/27 — Value £14.5m — Probability 31%." WTE rated red, variance "(240)". The £1.204m of efficiencies booked at M1 came from a single A&E PAS scheme, not the planned programme. — p.8 and Appendices 1 & 6, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/106]

[WORTH WATCHING] A new, cheaper Private Ambulance Services contract has been delayed and the old contract extended for two months, driving a £0.702m non-pay overspend at month one; the "right sizing" income is not in the financial plan and first arrives only in Q4.
[Evidence] "the new contract is not yet in place, and the previous contract has been extended for two months… Central Private Ambulance Services are showing an overspend due to EMAS not moving to the new contract." — p.1/p.5, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/106]

[WORTH WATCHING] NEPTS discharge performance is far below the 90% standard — 45% of patients discharged within two hours of booked collection in April — and the trust is building a "financial recovery plan" for the overspending Lincolnshire contract.
[Evidence] "Timely discharges / No Excessive waits - two hours after booked collection time… 45%" (target 90%). "The Division are developing a financial recovery plan for Lincolnshire NEPTS." — IBR NEPTS slide, [PB/26/104]; p.6, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/106]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board admitted its own performance dashboard had been mis-rated — a status "should have been reported as red rather than green" — and a NED flagged red-rated sickness absence being "celebrated."
[Evidence] "It was acknowledged that the status should have been reported as red rather than green." Also: "Jackie expressed concern regarding the use of amber and green ratings and noted that sickness absence rates were being celebrated despite being rated as red." — p.8, [Minutes 5 May 2026 PB/26/100]

[FOI] EMAS Prevention of Future Deaths reports — the Quality Account removed individual case detail in favour of "themes and learning." Request the full PFD reports and the trust's responses for 2025/26 from EMAS.
[Evidence] "Prevention of Futures Deaths — Replacement of details of individual cases with an emphasis on themes and learning." — p.2 (cover paper), [Quality Account PB/26/108]; delayed responses remain a top-three theme in patient-safety investigations — IBR Quality slide, [PB/26/104]

[FOI] Confidential session (PB/26/115) excludes press/public for unspecified business. Request the redacted Part 2 agenda and minutes to identify what was discussed privately.
[Evidence] "The Board will resolve that representatives of the press and other members of the public be excluded from the remainder of the meeting." — p.3, [Agenda]
