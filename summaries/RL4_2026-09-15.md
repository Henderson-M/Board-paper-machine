## Top lines

[LEAD] GROUP: Group chief executive Joe Chadwick-Bell confirms she is cutting the executive team to a single set of posts working across both trusts, with RWT managing director Gwen Nuttall retiring in December and four named executive changes between June and December.
[Evidence] "I have already moved to a single group Chief Medical Officer and will be following suit with a single Chief Nursing Officer in October and a single Chief Delivery Officer in December when Gwen (RWT Managing Director) retires." Also: "I have reduced the executive team already but there will be some further changes leading to a smaller team with all posts working across the two trusts." Named: Dr Brian McKaig group chief medical officer (in post June); Lisa Carroll (WHT CNO) to become group chief nurse; Amelia Godson (WHT managing director) to become group chief delivery officer; Debra Hickman (RWT CNO) to move to a new Chief Perinatal Officer role. A new maternity and neonatal building at New Cross is also flagged. – p.37-38, [Group Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 6]

[LEAD] WHT: The trust expects its NHS Oversight Framework position to deteriorate under the 2026/27 framework, and the group says the financial override will cap both trusts' segmentation regardless of other performance.
[Evidence] "Based on our assessment we are expecting to have a neutral overall impact on The Royal Wolverhampton NHS Trust's score, with positive and negative metric changes balancing each other. However, Walsall Healthcare NHS Trust's position was predicted to deteriorate due to a greater number of negatively affected metrics. In addition the financial override would continue to limit segmentation performance for both organisations." – p.34, [Group Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 6]. WHT's own IPR records: "Whilst remaining in segment 3, WHT has seen a slight improvement in its score from Q3 2.32 to 2.21 in Q4. The unadjusted segment was 2, with financial override scoring segment 3." – p.141, [WHT Integrated Performance Report, Enclosure 10.3]

[LEAD] RWT: Ophthalmology now accounts for 62 per cent of the trust's entire RTT backlog, with more than 34,000 patients waiting for follow-up care and over 9,400 on the glaucoma backlog list.
[Evidence] "Ophthalmology now accounts for 62% of the Trust's RTT backlog, with over 6,000 patients awaiting a first appointment and more than 34,000 awaiting follow-up care." And: "Glaucoma Services, where more than 9,400 patients are on the backlog waiting list and over 5,300 patients are overdue for a follow-up appointment. Patients need to be seen face to face and have a diagnostic test in order to manage this condition and prevent sight loss." Urgent eye care attendances have risen "180% in the last 6 years". Referrals up 23.5 per cent since 2021/22; 61.3 per cent of patients treated within 18 weeks (June 2026). – p.27-30, [Ophthalmology Briefing Paper, Enclosure 4.2]. In the July minutes, non-executive Prof Toner noted ophthalmology "had been identified as a longstanding risk area, having been included on the Trust's risk register since 2018". – p.14, [Minutes of 21 July 2026, Enclosure 3]

[LEAD] GROUP: Non-executive committee chairs tell the board the group cannot find enough recurrent savings and that it is likely to miss future CIP targets, against an £81.3m efficiency challenge this year.
[Evidence] "Future financial resilience is being undermined by the Group's inability to identify and deliver a supply of recurrent efficiency savings. It is likely that in future periods CIP targets will be missed." – p.44, [Integrated Committee Chairs' Report, Enclosure 7]. The finance paper states: "The total efficiency challenge in 2026/27 for the group is £81.3m; RWT £53.8m, WHT £27.5m." Group year to date deficit is £14.9m, £0.1m better than plan, with "Both Trust ... recognising a higher proportion of non recurrent savings compared to plan due to slippage in recurrent schemes". – p.101, p.106, [Group Finance and Workforce Report, Enclosure 10.2]

[LEAD] GROUP: The group tells its board it will not produce action plans in response to its WRES and WDES results, which show widening race disparities in staff experience.
[Evidence] "The Group will be avoiding developing action plans in response to Trust WRES and WDES data, opting instead to include key actions in the strategic design and delivery of the cultural improvement programme aligned to Transforming Care Together." – p.249, [WRES/WDES Combined Report 2026, Enclosure 11.3]. The same paper says: "BME colleagues remain more likely than White colleagues to report bullying, harassment, abuse and discrimination at work, and confidence in equal opportunity for career progression has declined," and warns: "The Board should not interpret improvement in individual metrics as evidence that the underlying inequalities have been resolved." The committee chairs' report records "widening disparities for ethnic minority colleagues". – p.246, p.45

[LEAD] GROUP: The two trusts are being lined up to take delegated commissioning and a population budget from the ICB under an "Anchor" model, with a shadow year proposed and general practice joining the steering group this month.
[Evidence] "The Anchor model would give the Trusts a greater role in planning, coordinating and delivering care for the populations of Walsall and Wolverhampton ... The intended future state is more coordinated care, stronger local decision-making and a population budget that supports investment in interventions expected to improve outcomes for local people." And: "A shadow year would be used to confirm the current commissioning and contractual scope and to test the proposed governance and operating arrangements. Formal delegation of defined commissioning responsibilities, including accountability for expenditure and outcomes, would follow only if the necessary approvals and assurances were secured." The paper's Alert line: "The model remains under development. Its contractual form, scope of services, financial envelope, risk sharing, governance and implementation arrangements will be finalised over the coming months." Named risks include "Responsibility without sufficient authority" and "Underfunding, commissioning gaps and population need". – p.39-42, [Progress Report on Anchor Development, Enclosure 6.1]. A separate committee note records: "The committee note concerns regarding Executive capacity in relation to transformation programme and potential NHS Anchor. It was noted this was also raised in Sandwell & Dudley committees and reference made to alternative models employed in those trusts." – p.49
