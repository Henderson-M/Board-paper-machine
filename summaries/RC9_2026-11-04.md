## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has put Bedfordshire Hospitals under enhanced financial oversight after a mid-year review, requiring monthly "Provider Assurance Model" submissions with CEO sign-off, as the trust forecasts up to a £19.4m deficit against a planned breakeven-adjacent position
[Evidence] "Following a mid-year financial review with NHS England regional colleagues, the Trust has been asked to strengthen its financial recovery plans... the Trust has been asked to complete the 'In Year Provider Assurance Model (PAM)'... The model must be submitted monthly and supported by a Chief Executive sign-off letter" — p.1-2, [Provider Assurance Model, Item 2.4.2]; "NHS England would be increasing the level and frequency of its oversight of the Trust's finances from November 2026"; "The Trust reported a £12.2m deficit [at Month 6, against a planned deficit of £0.7m]... it could record a £16.8m deficit on current projections, with a downside of up to £19.4m if winter or UEC pressures worsen" — p.1, [FIP Committee Report, Item 4.2.2b]

[LEAD] The trust's Financial Recovery Plan was judged not fit for approval by its own finance committee, with CIP delivery forecast at £49.5m against a £62m target
[Evidence] "The Committee concluded that the FRP under scrutiny remained incomplete and there was still some work to do before it was in an approvable form"; "the Trust is forecasting that around £49.5m cost savings will be achieved against a target of £62m" — p.1-2, [FIP Committee Report, Item 4.2.2b]

[LEAD] The trust has been placed in tier 1 national performance oversight for cancer and elective care, with fortnightly regional/national meetings, and its access-to-services score is in the lowest quartile nationally
[Evidence] "The Trust was placed into tier 1 performance oversight for cancer and elective care and is meeting fortnightly with regional and national leads"; "The Access to Services domain position was quartile 4 – the lowest scoring 25% of trusts" — p.1, [Operational Performance Report, Item 4.4.1]. Supporting figures: 58.4% RTT within 18 weeks, 1,389 52-week waiters (112 over 65 weeks), diagnostics 33.6% over six weeks vs 19.5% plan, 911 twelve-hour ED waits in September, bed occupancy above 97% — p.1, [Matters for the Attention of the Trust Board, Item 4.2.1]

[LEAD] An HTA-mandated retrospective mortuary review found nine historical incidents never previously reported, including three involving damage or potential damage to deceased people and several lost or uncertainly handled early pregnancy remains
[Evidence] "[The review] identified nine historical incidents that had not previously been reported, including three involving damage or potential damage to the deceased and several cases of lost or uncertain handling of very early pregnancy remains/non-viable foetuses... the Trust has deliberately erred on the side of over-reporting to the HTA" — Mortuary Update section, [Quality Committee Report, Item 4.2.2a]. The paper says there is "no evidence of systemic disrespect or poor conditions of the kind seen in Nottingham".

[LEAD] Executive churn: new chief medical officer Dr Jon Baker and new COO Anjali Joshi both recruited from London North West University Healthcare; medical director Paul Tisi retires 6 November with Dr Tammy Angel acting as interim; substantive chair recruitment has resumed after being paused since December 2025
[Evidence] "Dr Jon Baker will be joining the Trust as our Chief Medical Officer in the new year, following the retirement of current Medical Director Paul Tisi on 6 November... Anjali Joshi will be joining us in January as our new Chief Operating Officer... from London North West University Healthcare NHS Trust" — p.1, [Chief Executive Report, Item 2.3]; "The recruitment process for the substantive Trust Chair position, which was paused in December 2025, resumed in October 2026" — p.4, [FT Corporate Governance Report, Item 4.3.2]. New NED (Finance) Catherine Bland appointed 30 September.

[LEAD] The trust regards a £42.7m cost improvement programme — 4.7% of expenditure — as its most likely planning assumption for 2027/28, on a zero-growth funding scenario
[Evidence] "These produce an indicative Cost Improvement Programme requirement ranging from £26.9m, or 3.0% of expenditure, under a 2% funding-growth scenario, to £42.7m, or 4.7%, if no growth funding is received. The Trust currently regards the 0% growth scenario, with a £42.7m CIP requirement, as the most likely planning assumption" — p.1, [Trust 2027/28 Budgeting Process and Timetable, Item 3.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is reviewing a move from its ~35 service lines to a divisional operating model — a restructure that would go to formal consultation
[Evidence] "The Board is asked to note the proposed review of the Trust's operating model, which will consider a move from the current service line management structure to a divisional model... The current model comprises approximately 35 service lines" — p.2, [Chief Executive Report, Item 2.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] £67m Bedford Elective Care Hub outline business case approved at an extraordinary board on 21 October and now with NHSE — but only £25m of PDC is confirmed; the paper says BLMK is one of only two systems in the country without a dedicated elective hub
[Evidence] "the OBC, which sought £67m investment... £25m of public dividend capital had been made available to support the investment case... An Extraordinary Trust Board held on 21 October approved the Outline Business Case"; "BLMK... is one of two systems in the country without access to a dedicated Elective Hub" — p.1-2, [Elective Hub Investment at Bedford Hospital, Item 2.4.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] Central East ICB's 2027/28-2029/30 commissioning intentions signal hardball contracting — acute block deconstruction with a 2.5% clawback from "overfunded" providers, no payment for activity outside mandated datasets, and GIRFT-only funding for some specialties; the trust says the financial effect "cannot yet be quantified"
[Evidence] "The ICB will remove the maximum 2.5% of the total contract value from the block for all acute providers overfunded"; "The ICB will not pay for activity not reported through mandated datasets"; "The ICB will only fund activity in line with the GIRFT outpatient follow up protocols" — ICB letter of 30 September 2026, sections 1.2, 1.3, 1.7, [Central East Commissioning Intentions, Item 3.2]; "The full financial effect cannot yet be quantified" — p.1, trust cover paper

[WORTH WATCHING] Industrial relations: the theatre staff dispute offer is out to union ballot, the trust has answered an RCN pre-action letter, and BMA consultants strike for 48 hours on 25-26 November
[Evidence] "the Trust's offer to theatre staff was being put to a union ballot... The Trust has also responded to an RCN pre-action letter" — p.1, [Workforce Committee Assurance Report, Item 4.2.2c]; "The BMA have given formal notice of planned industrial action for consultants on the 25 and 26th of November for a 48-hour period. The expectation is that only Christmas day activity remains" — p.2, [Chief Executive Report, Item 2.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] Luton & Dunstable hip fracture mortality is above the 3 standard deviation upper control limit and may trigger a national alert notification
[Evidence] "For LDH, Q1 data is an outlier with a crude mortality of 7.8% and a casemix-adjusted mortality of 8.5% which is above the 3 SD upper control limit (8.4%) and may trigger an alert notification" — p.7, [Learning from Deaths Report, Item 4.4.3]. Quality Committee notes a rise "from 3.8% to around 7.8% over three quarters" with no preventable deaths identified by reviews to date — [Quality Committee Report, Item 4.2.2a]

[WORTH WATCHING] Inquests are pending into three maternal deaths at Luton & Dunstable, all referred to the Maternity and Newborn Safety Investigations programme
[Evidence] "Inquest dates to be confirmed for three maternal deaths (LDH, referred for Maternity and Newborn Safety Investigations, MNSI)" — p.9, [Learning from Deaths Report, Item 4.4.3]

[FOI] Request from Bedfordshire Hospitals: (a) the total value and contract documentation for insourced theatre lists engaged during the theatre staff industrial action in March — the acting CEO told the July board the fees were "confidential"; (b) the RCN pre-action letter and the trust's response
[Evidence] "CJ confirmed that the commercial fees were confidential but assured the Board that the costs were within 15% of what it would have cost to pay Trust staff at the relevant rates" — p.4, [Minutes of 29 July 2026, Item 1.3]; "The Trust has also responded to an RCN pre-action letter" — p.1, [Workforce Committee Assurance Report, Item 4.2.2c]. Why it matters: cost of covering strike action with private insourcing, and a potential legal claim by a royal college, are both standalone stories.

[Evidence] "Counter Fraud reported a recurring theme relating to staff working while on sickness absence. Several investigations remain ongoing, with a number of confirmed or suspected cases resulting in financial losses to the Trust" — p.1, [Audit & Risk Committee Report, Item 4.2.2f]
