# Norfolk and Norwich University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (RM1) board — 3 June 2026

> **Joint / group board.** This is the public meeting of the **Norfolk and Waveney University Hospitals Group** "Group Board (General Purpose Joint Committee)", which is the shared board for three foundation trusts: **Norfolk and Norwich University Hospitals (NNUH, RM1)**, **James Paget University Hospitals (JPUH, RGP)** and **The Queen Elizabeth Hospital King's Lynn (QEH, RCX)**. Items below are tagged by trust where they relate to a single site. The summary is filed under RM1 per the pack record.

## Top lines

[LEAD] QEH (King's Lynn) is in NHS England's National Provider Improvement Programme and has been hit with at least four separate Section 29A CQC enforcement notices across two unannounced inspections in spring 2026.
[Evidence] On the 17 March surgery inspection: "Formal correspondence confirmed the implementation of two Section 29A enforcement notices." On the 28/29 April ED and Medical wards inspection: "Formal correspondence has confirmed the implementation of further Section 29A enforcement notices." — p.27, [7.1 QEH EMD Report]. CEO report confirms "QEH had been included within the National Provider Improvement Programme (NPIP)" — p.5, [Minutes 1 April]; p.26, [7.1 QEH EMD Report].

[LEAD] QEH faces a £4m NHS England funding cliff-edge: NHSE could withhold circa £4m of Deficit Support Funding in Q2 if the trust does not deliver its cost improvement programme and 90% of activity, and the trust is forecast to hit a cash deficit by August.
[Evidence] "The delivery of CIP is directly linked to the release of Deficit Support Funding (DSF) by NHS England in Q2 and failure to improve our CIP delivery in the next two months could result in NHS England withholding DSF. The quantum of DSF in Q2 is circa £4m… The most recent cash forecast shows that the Trust will incur a cash deficit by August, if cash releasing CIP is not achieved and our activity levels do not deliver 90% of plan." — p.29, [7.1 QEH EMD Report].

[LEAD] The group's £102.1m full-year cost improvement programme is £44.3m short of identified schemes one month into the year, the single biggest financial risk to its breakeven plan.
[Evidence] "As at 6th May 2026, the programme consists of £57.8m of Gateway 2 approved schemes. This is £44.3m adverse to the planned £102.1m full year CIP requirement. This has arisen as a result of insufficient recurrent schemes being identified and progressed through Gateways for implementation and delivery at NNUH & QEH. JPUH have identified 100% of planned CIP for 26/27." — p.124, [9.2.2 Group Finance Report M1]. NNUH must find £52.2m of efficiencies (c.5.0% of cost base) — p.40, [7.3 NNUH EMD Report].

[LEAD] QEH has appointed a turnaround director after its CIP was under-delivered and the trust is carrying £2.7m of unidentified savings schemes.
[Evidence] "CIP was under delivered by £350k against a plan of £580k at M1. The 2026/27 plan includes £2.7m of unidentified schemes and circa £13m of identified opportunity but without detailed schemes behind it… The Trust has appointed a Director of Improvement and Recovery (turnaround)." — p.29, [7.1 QEH EMD Report]. Named as "Amanda Ramsay-Dunn… our Director of Improvement and Recovery" — p.26, [7.1 QEH EMD Report].

[LEAD] QEH general surgery is the subject of a Royal College of Surgeons review and a regional rapid quality review, with the RCS team warning of unresolved "internal dysfunction" in the department.
[Evidence] RCS "review team requested that one further update is provided in four months, 14th September 2026. There was also concern that the internal dysfunction within the department had not yet been resolved." A separate "NHS East of England rapid quality review took place on 21 May chaired by the Regional Medical Director with a focus on general surgery." — p.27, [7.1 QEH EMD Report]. Some QEH elective cases have been moved to NNUH to relieve pressure — p.6, [Minutes 1 April].

[WORTH WATCHING] Bullying, harassment and discrimination of BAME staff has been raised at JPUH and is described in the group performance report as "visible Group-wide", with the group warning it carries statutory legal risk.
[Evidence] "Bullying, harassment and discrimination of BAME staff raised at JPUH, visible Group-wide, carrying statutory risk under the Equality Act, Public Sector Equality Duty and Workforce Race Equality Scheme (WRES)." — p.53, [9.1.1 Group IPR cover sheet].

[WORTH WATCHING] The group's board assurance framework has deteriorated, with 10 of 14 principal risks now rated "Significant" — up from nine two months earlier — and digital, EPR and transformation-capacity risks on "Very limited" assurance.
[Evidence] "10 of the 14 risks are currently rated as 'Significant' (12 and above) – up from 9 two months ago." — p.129, [10 BAF report]. BAF summary table shows PR6 (digital), PR7 (EPR) and PR11 (transformation capacity) at "Very limited" assurance — p.132, [10.2 NWUHG BAF May 2026].

[WORTH WATCHING] A QEH counter-fraud investigation has been running for more than a year unresolved, prompting the group audit committee to ask the group CEO to personally review it.
[Evidence] "There was concern that one fraud investigation at Queen Elizabeth Hospital was still ongoing after a year. It was agreed that the Group Chief Executive would review the case and ensure all appropriate actions are being and will be taken." — p.44, [8.2 Group Audit Committees in Common Chair's report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The group's capital programme is forecast to overspend by £14.6m, all on nationally funded schemes including £9.8m on the NNUH electronic patient record, with assumptions yet to be agreed with NHSE.
[Evidence] "Month 1 forecast outturn for total CDEL is a £14.6m overspend compared to the plan, all of which relates to nationally funded programmes… On EPR programme NNUH and JPUH are forecasting to spend £9.8m and £1.2m more than original plan, respectively." — p.127, [9.2.2 Group Finance Report M1].

[WORTH WATCHING] QEH received an Information Commissioner's Office Enforcement Notice in March 2026 over its backlog of Freedom of Information responses.
[Evidence] "The Trust received an Information Commissioners Enforcement Notice on 23 March 2026. The Enforcement Notice relates to the Trust's backlog of responses to Freedom Information requests and ability to respond within the required statutory timeframes." — p.28, [7.1 QEH EMD Report].
