## Top lines

[LEAD] Capsticks investigation into RCHT's FOI staff data breach published in full: hidden sickness-absence and health data on 7,715 trust staff and 288 non-trust workers sat publicly on the trust website for 33 months before a member of the public spotted it.
[Evidence] "An Excel spreadsheet published in February 2023 exposed hidden sickness absence data, including sensitive health information, relating to 7,715 Trust staff and 288 non-Trust workers... The information remained publicly accessible until November 2025." — p.4, [Staff Data Breach Public Board Report, Item 8]. The 91-page PSII records the file was accessed 340 times in the 12 months before removal, with no logs for the first 21 months (p.27, [PSII 255478 Data Breach Investigation Report]); the ICO closed its case on 25 November 2025 (p.60); the trust's own rule requiring conversion to PDF before publication "was not followed" (p.4, board report). The PSII also notes the incident landed during restructuring "triggered in part by the Trust's need to deliver a cost improvement plan of £49.3m" (p.59).

[LEAD] Management told the finance committee that full assurance cannot be given on delivering break-even, with a ~£21.4m full-year risk at month 6 and both the central and worst-case cash scenarios dropping below the four-operating-day cash threshold later this year.
[Evidence] "Management confirmed that full assurance could not currently be given on delivery of break-even, and that further controls, including potential divisional control totals, were being considered." — p.8, [FPC Assurance Report Sept and Oct 2026]. M6: £7.69m YTD deficit, £2.23m adverse to plan; forecast risk £21.4m; FSP shortfall £10.5m against the £42.5m requirement; only 16% of YTD savings recurrent vs 72.5% planned — p.3–4, [M6 Revenue Position]. Cash: "Both the central mitigated and worst-case financial scenarios would result in the Trust falling below the four operating-day cash threshold later in the financial year... an application [for external cash support] cannot be made until the relevant cash threshold is reached." — p.8, [FPC Assurance Report].

[LEAD] RCHT is at South West Finance Intervention Level 3 — "delivery at risk" — and has dropped to NHS Oversight Framework Segment 3, with enhanced financial controls and "quality guardrails" now in force.
[Evidence] "Members heard that, although it had initially been reported that the Trust had moved from a Regional Finance Assessment rating of Level 2 to Level 4, subsequent clarification from the regional team confirmed that the correct position was Level 3. This represented a move from 'moderate confidence of delivery' to 'delivery at risk'." — p.27, [Minutes of 3 September board]. "Trust segmentation has reduced to Segment 3, with financial recovery, UEC performance and staff survey improvement identified as the key recovery priorities." — p.2, [FPC Assurance Report]. QAC approved monthly (up from quarterly) EQIA reporting "during the period of enhanced financial control" — p.10, [QAC Assurance Report].

[LEAD] The trust has missed its own end-of-October deadline to eliminate corridor care and is behind every UEC recovery trajectory; ED overcrowding remains its top-scored risk (25) and escalation spaces cost ~£300k a month.
[Evidence] "The Trust is therefore not currently meeting the agreed UEC recovery trajectories... Risk 9048, ED Overcrowding, remains the Trust's highest-scored risk at 25." Type 1 four-hour 51.3% vs 62.54% trajectory; 17.15% of patients over 12 hours in ED vs 10.5% trajectory; "corridor care has not been fully eliminated, two relevant patient-safety incidents were reported" — p.4, [UEC Performance and Corridor Care, Item 9]. GIRFT plan appendix records "£300K per month in escalation costs" and ED corridor care of "regular 16 patients in these areas" (update 30.09.26) — p.1, [GIRFT 11 Point Action Plan]. CRR: the risk of "accommodating acutely unwell patients in the SDSA corridor increased from high to extreme following identified control deficiencies" — p.2–3, [Corporate Risk Register, Item 20].

[LEAD] RCHT has been warned it may be heading for national mortality-outlier status on fractured neck of femur, with mortality at 6.4% against a 5% national average and a formal improvement plan in place.
[Evidence] "Fractured neck of femur mortality was reported at 6.4% against a national average of 5%. The Trust had received correspondence indicating that it might be moving towards outlier status." — p.4, [QAC Assurance Report]. Surgery within 36 hours "improved to 56% but is subject to a formal improvement plan" — p.9, [CEO Report, Item 7].

[LEAD] The capital funding risk on Cornwall's New Hospital Programme Women and Children's Hospital has moved from amber to red, weeks after construction partner Willmott Dixon was appointed; the trust's wider capital risk is scored 20 (extreme) with no contingency left.
[Evidence] "Movement of the Women's and Children's Hospital capital funding risk from Amber to Red due to potential risks associated with enabling schemes." — p.2, [FPC Assurance Report]. "Our main construction partner has been appointed. It is Willmott Dixon... Our aim is to get building in the second half of 2028, and complete in 2032." — p.10, [CEO Report]. "There is currently no contingency within the capital plan... Capital risk (risk register item 8521) has been reviewed and is 20 (Extreme)" — p.3, [M6 Capital Position Update]. New CRR risks 10373/10261 cover OBC affordability and "the risk that the building is not affordable from a revenue perspective" — p.4, [Corporate Risk Register].

[WORTH WATCHING] Speak-up concerns have more than doubled year on year (up 106%), nearly half are anonymous (45.6% vs 12% nationally), and staff are asking Guardians not to escalate for fear of detriment during restructuring.
[Evidence] "68 concerns were raised through the FTSU service compared with 33 cases in the same period last year, an increase of 106%" — p.7; "In many cases, colleagues have requested that concerns are not escalated due to fears of detriment." — p.4; "Employee Relations formal cases have risen by 27% while the team is reduced." — p.6, [FTSU Bi-Annual Report, Item 12]. Guardians have also heard "concerns about senior leaders' behaviour" — p.6.

[WORTH WATCHING] Leadership remains heavily interim — acting chief executive, acting chief nursing officer, COO doubling as acting deputy CEO — and the remuneration committee has just "considered the Chief Executive Officer arrangements"; the board also loses its audit chair this month.
[Evidence] "Considered the Chief Executive Officer arrangements" — p.1, [Remuneration and Appointments Committee Assurance Report, Item 17]. Kim O'Keeffe is Acting Chief Executive and Robin Jones "Chief Operating Officer and Acting Deputy Chief Executive Officer" — p.1, [IPR cover sheet, Item 18]. Adam Broome leaves 15 November after six years; Niamh Lamond becomes NED and Audit and Risk Assurance Committee chair from 16 November — p.1, [Chair's Report, Item 6].

[WORTH WATCHING] A second-quarter spike in stillbirths has reversed the previous 12-month improvement, and an August 2026 maternal death cannot be investigated by MNSI because next-of-kin consent was not obtained — a joint PSII with another trust is planned instead.
[Evidence] "The Quarter 2 PMRT report (Appendix 1) shows increased stillbirths, reversing the previous 12-month trend, particularly among families visiting Cornwall... RCHT recorded a Quarter 2 increase, considered a spike in adverse events" — p.4–5; "The August 2026 maternal death referral was declined by MNSI in September because next-of-kin consent was not obtained... RCHT and the external antenatal Trust will commission a joint PSII." — p.6, [Perinatal Safety Report, Item 13.1]. QAC recorded "recent perinatal deaths, including a maternal death and stillbirth" — p.6, [QAC Assurance Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust says it has no money or staff to implement NHS England's new single-sex staff-facilities guidance — an audit of 160 staff locations is required but unfunded, and it may de-prioritise other capital schemes to pay for it.
[Evidence] "We require an audit of our 160 staff locations across the main Treliske campus... we have no resource currently to carry out an audit and with this year's significantly constrained capital programme there is no capital currently for outsourced resource... a submission is being made to Capital Approvals Group (CAG) to request i) any slippage funding that may arise later in year or ii) for CAG to consider de-prioritising current planned work" — p.15, [CEO Report]. [INFERENCE] A concrete early example of the October 2026 NHSE single-sex guidance creating unfunded cost pressure at trust level.

[FOI] Request the correspondence warning RCHT of potential fractured-neck-of-femur mortality outlier status — from RCHT (and NHSE/national audit body if named) — the pack confirms the letter exists but does not quote or attach it.
[Evidence] "The Trust had received correspondence indicating that it might be moving towards outlier status. ... Committee concluded the relevant fractured neck of femur correspondence and improvement plan should return to the next meeting." — p.4–5, [QAC Assurance Report].

[FOI] Request from RCHT the total cost of the data breach incident response and the Capsticks engagement (contract value, external communications advisers' fees), plus the number of legal claims received from affected staff — the report concedes "significant direct and indirect costs" and "potential future costs arising from legal claims" but gives no figures.
[Evidence] "The incident generated significant direct and indirect costs associated with incident management, specialist advice, staff communications, regulatory engagement... alongside potential future costs arising from legal claims or remedial actions." — p.7, [Staff Data Breach Public Board Report]. The PSII notes the trust "briefed its specialist external communications advisors" over the weekend of 22–23 November 2025 — p.60, [PSII 255478].
