## Top lines

[LEAD] CNWL is the highest-ranked mental health trust in England on the NHS Oversight Framework and 2nd of 61 MH/community trusts, finishing 0.02 behind the top trust.
[Evidence] "CNWL strengthened its national performance position in Q4, improving its overall NOF score from 1.72 to 1.55, while retaining its ranking of 2nd out of 61 Mental Health and Community Trusts and highest ranked Mental Health provider nationally. The Trust's Q4 NOF score of 1.55 was narrowly behind the top-performing Trust, Kent Community Health NHS Foundation Trust (1.53)." — p.240, [Q4 2025/26 NHS Oversight Framework Segmentation, paper 18b]

[LEAD] The NHS Oversight Framework more than doubles the metrics it monitors trusts against in 2026/27, from 13 to 28 — a material change to national oversight that this top-ranked trust flags as a delivery risk.
[Evidence] "The 2026/27 NOF introduces a significantly expanded oversight framework, increasing the number of metrics monitored from 13 to 28." — p.238, [Q4 2025/26 NHS Oversight Framework Segmentation, paper 18b]

[LEAD] CNWL is carrying a £31.6m efficiency target for 2026/27 and is running a voluntary redundancy programme, having delivered a £38.6m savings target the year before to hit break-even.
[Evidence] "The Trust has an efficiency target of £31.6m for the current financial year, with recurrent and non-recurrent schemes and central support identified to achieve break even at year end." — p.222, [Consolidated Finance Report at Month 2, paper 17]. On the prior year: "The Trust had an efficiency target of £38.6m, comprising a £37.6m budgetary savings target and a £1.1m run-rate reduction target." — p.224, [paper 17]. VR confirmed in March minutes: "There was a voluntary redundancy programme in progress." — p.6, [Part 1 Board Meeting Minutes 25.03.26]

[LEAD] NHS England reallocated £4.9m of deficit support funding to CNWL in March, taken from providers that missed their financial targets, and an additional £6m of regional funding has flowed to its bottom line in 2026/27 on the same basis.
[Evidence] "NHS England instructed the Trust in March to recognise £4.9m of Deficit Support Funding which had been re-allocated from providers which had failed to meet their financial targets." — p.224, [Consolidated Finance Report at Month 2, paper 17]. And in March: "an additional £6m of regional funding for the bottom-line to be received. This had been redistributed from other systems which had not met targets." — p.11, [Part 1 Board Meeting Minutes 25.03.26]

[LEAD] Minority ethnic staff at CNWL are more than four times as likely as white colleagues to enter formal disciplinary proceedings — the highest disciplinary disproportionality ratio of any mental health/community trust in the former North West London ICB.
[Evidence] "The disciplinary likelihood ratio of 4.24 means Minoritised Ethnic staff are over four times more likely to enter formal disciplinary proceedings than White colleagues. This is the highest ratio among mental health and community trusts in the original NWL ICB, and sits above every trust in the London mental health peer group, with the exception of East London NHS Foundation Trust at 5.02." — p.191/192, [WRES and WDES Annual Reports, paper 14]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's two highest-rated board risks are both staff/service-user equality risks scored 20, and the board records zero disability declaration among its own directors.
[Evidence] "(2504b) Equalities (staff) ... 20" and "(2504a) Equalities (service users) ... 20" — p.254, [Board Assurance Framework, paper 21]. "the Board records a 0% disability declaration, a position shared only with West London NHS Trust among regional mental health peers." — p.191, [WRES and WDES Annual Reports, paper 14]

[WORTH WATCHING] The looming transfer of properties from NHS Property Services to providers is flagged as a live risk because multiple providers are expected to contest the same buildings.
[Evidence] "A number of properties are expected to be contested in terms of registrations of interest from multiple providers, which creates a potential risk for the organisation. The Finance and Performance Committee will monitor this as the picture emerges." — p.221, [Finance & Performance Committee Chair's Report, paper 16]

[WORTH WATCHING] CNWL is positioning itself for Advanced Foundation Trust status and a future Integrated Health Organisation role under the Ten Year Plan, with CEO Claire Murdoch set to sit on the new merged ICB board.
[Evidence] "The Trust continues to actively pursue opportunities in relation to gaining Advanced Foundation Trust status as a precursor to pursing future IHO arrangements." — p.267, [Board Assurance Framework, paper 21]. On the ICB: "There were major changes upcoming to the Integrated Care Boards (ICBs), including a significant reduction in headcount. She would be a member of the new ICB Board, representing the Trust." — p.6, [Part 1 Board Meeting Minutes 25.03.26]

[WORTH WATCHING] CNWL's eating disorders inpatient service at Vincent Square drew "significant actions" from a CQC Mental Health Act reviewer, the only listed MHA visit this period with substantive concerns, including consent and feeding-practice issues.
[Evidence] "However, significant actions were identified to improve patient experience and safeguards, including: addressing longstanding maintenance issues ... ensuring informal patients are genuinely consenting and fully informed of their rights ... delivering nasogastric feeding in a way that upholds privacy and dignity." — p.157, [CQC Compliance Report, paper 10]

[FOI] The WRES/WDES benchmarking tables comparing CNWL against named London peers are withheld from the public pack as "Not for publication" — request the full benchmarking appendices.
[Evidence] "Appendix C — Table C1: WRES Mental Health and Community Trusts Only (NWL ICB and London) 2025- Not for publication"; same for Appendices D, E and F. — p.190/contents, [WRES and WDES Annual Reports, paper 14]. *What to request, from CNWL:* the unredacted WRES/WDES Appendices C–F (peer benchmarking against NWL ICB and London MH/acute trusts). *Why it matters:* the pack quotes selective peer comparisons (e.g. ELFT, Oxleas) but withholds the full named-trust tables that would let HSJ rank disproportionality and culture metrics across London.
