[LEAD] The ICB needs £214.6m of cost mitigations this year to hit its control total, and its own risk-adjusted modelling puts it £30m to £40m short if schemes slip.
[Evidence] "The ICB's 26/27 plan requires a minimum delivery of c.£214.6m cost mitigations 26/27 to achieve the required financial position (resulting in a £34.9m deficit as per NHSE control total which would attract deficit support funding)"; and "a proportionate reduction has been applied to each area based on current scheme level classification as per the table below (excluding the additional WRP+ outside of AACC) and results in a risk adjusted position of circa £30m - £40m compared to plan should these schemes not deliver". Scheme risk table shows "£80m low risk, £45m medium risk, £26m high risk", with £10.7m of stretch savings still unidentified at month 4 – p.1 and p.10, [Item 11 Finance Performance Report M4 2026/27]

[LEAD] The system only met its 2025/26 control total after receiving £47m of extra funding from the North West region, the finance committee chair told the July board.

[LEAD] The board is asked to approve an eight-week public consultation on removing inpatient arterial vascular surgery from East Lancashire Hospitals Trust, with roughly 650 inpatient cases a year moving to Royal Preston. The pre-consultation business case states both existing centres have been below national minimum procedure volumes for years.
[Evidence] "≥60 AAA repairs/year: one service completed 26; the other 56 (still below the national minimum)"; "≥35 CEAs/year: one service completed 30"; "One of our current services does not have a hybrid theatre, failing a fundamental specification requirement" – p.11, [Item 14ii LSC Vascular PCBC]. Consultation to launch October 2026 with "a budget of £50,000"; "approximately 650 inpatient vascular cases per year would transfer to the arterial centre"; projected "£1.8 million annual system-wide financial improvement" and circa £2m capital from provider plans – p.1 and p.2, [Item 14i Vascular Reconfiguration Programme PCBC v9]

[LEAD] Blackpool Teaching Hospitals FT has received correspondence from NHS England over deterioration against its agreed plan, and the ICB is working with the trust on recovery.
[Evidence] "Since publication of the report, Blackpool Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust had received correspondence from NHS England regarding deterioration against elements of its agreed plan. [LEAD] The ICB's own impact assessment of its restructure found disabled, minority ethnic and LGBTQ+ staff came off worse in the mapping of who gets which job.
[Evidence] "A People Impact Assessment (PIA) was undertaken which indicated that whilst overall workforce representation remained broadly stable, some disproportionate impacts were identified within mapped outcomes for disabled colleagues, BME colleagues and, to a lesser extent, LGBTQ+ colleagues. This will be actively monitored with continued Executive oversight through the implementation stage" – p.6, [Item 8i Board Assurance Framework, BAF-003]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB has settled a long-running funding dispute with University Hospitals of Morecambe Bay FT at about £40.7m for 2026/27, creating an unplanned pressure, and has capped high-cost drugs and devices spend with the four main acute providers under a risk and gainshare deal.
[Evidence] "The ICB has been working with University Hospitals of Morecambe Bay NHS Foundation Trust in respect of historic issues with the combination of Local Price Modification (LPM), Sparsity and related Segment 0/1 funding streams. An agreement reached on the value for 26/27 (circa £40.7m) ... This has created an additional pressure for the ICB to manage compared to plan assumptions"; and "An approach has now been agreed with the 4 main L&SC acute providers, with expenditure to be capped at a maximum level and a risk/gainshare agreement to be varied into the contracts once finalised" – p.15, [Item 11 Finance Performance Report M4 2026/27]
