## Top lines

[LEAD] CQC has issued Isle of Wight NHS Trust a Section 29A warning notice requiring improvements in Urgent Care; the notice and its content are not described in the public pack.
[Evidence] "The CQC has issued a Section 29A warning notice requiring improvements in Urgent Care, and the Trust is actively addressing these. The quality of care provided by the Trust is rated 'Good' overall... fortnightly briefing calls, and quarterly engagement events." — p.208, [Draft Quality Account 2025/26 – IWT]; also listed as a standalone Q&P Committee agenda item "b) IWT Section 29a Warning Notice" — p.146-147, [Quality and Performance Committee agenda, 24 April 2026]

[LEAD] Non-criteria-to-reside patients are occupying over 35% of IWT's bed base, forcing a two-week suspension of elective inpatient orthopaedics during April.
[Evidence] "The primary driver remains the excessive number nCTR, currently occupying over 35% of the bed base. This is preventing admissions from happening in a prompt manner... This congestion caused the suspension of elective inpatient orthopaedics over a two-week period during the reporting period." — p.168-169, [Integrated Performance Report, IWT Oversight]

[LEAD] CEO names bed occupancy as the single highest risk to the trusts' Medium Term Plan, with both trusts starting 2026/27 above planned occupancy and unable to stand down escalation capacity.
[Evidence] "The occupancy challenge poses the single highest risk to delivery of our Medium Term Plan. Unless we de-escalate the position as we move into the summer, it will impact on our financial and workforce plans for the year." — p.26, [CEO Report]

[LEAD] Resident-doctor strike over the Easter period (45–60% participation) is expected to cost more than previous rounds, with no central reimbursement confirmed.
[Evidence] "The financial impact is expected to exceed previous periods, with no confirmed central reimbursement at this stage." — p.144, [Quality and Performance Committee feedback]; CEO: "industrial action by resident doctors resumed in April, with the most recent strike falling directly after the Easter bank holiday weekend... we incurred additional cover costs and saw a reduction in activity across the strike period." — p.24, [CEO Report]

[LEAD] Both trusts start the year reporting on-plan deficits — IWT £1.0m and PHU £4.4m at Month 1 (excluding deficit support funding) — but the board is told the underlying run-rate must improve and the MTP relies on non-recurrent deficit support that ends after Year 1.
[Evidence] IWT: "Year to date position of £1.0m deficit versus £1.0m deficit plan, excluding deficit support funding (DSF)... CIP delivery risk remains significant." — p.169; PHU: "£4.4m deficit versus £4.4m deficit plan, excluding deficit support funding (DSF)" — p.180; plan "assume[s] receipt of non-recurrent deficit support funding in Year 1, which will not be available in future years. Underpinning this is an additional CIP requirement of approximately 4% for the IWT and 5% for PHU." — p.10, [March minutes, Medium Term Plan 26/TB/172]

[WORTH WATCHING] PHU is a national outlier for endoscopy waits over six months, holding back both cancer and diagnostic recovery.
[Evidence] "the trust remaining a significant outlier nationally for the number of patients waiting over 6 months for an endoscopy." — p.181, [IPR, PHU Oversight]

[WORTH WATCHING] IWT's healthcare-associated infection rates run roughly double the national average for both C. difficile and E. coli.
[Evidence] C. diff "Rolling 12-month rate remains higher than the national rate... (58.6 cases per 100,000 bed days, compared to 29.1 nationally)"; E. coli "(63.6 cases per 100,000 bed days, compared to 41.6 nationally)." — p.168 & p.173, [IPR, IWT]

[WORTH WATCHING] Unannounced CQC inspection of PHU maternity inpatient services has flagged early concerns on leadership culture, triage compliance and fetal monitoring.
[Evidence] "initial feedback focusing on aspects of culture between junior and senior leadership, compliance with triage standards, and fetal monitoring practices. Workforce resilience and staffing fragility were also highlighted." — p.144, [Q&P Committee feedback]

[WORTH WATCHING] Operational pressure/overcapacity is the most over-appetite strategic risk on the refreshed BAF: scored 16 against a target of 6 and a "Minimal" appetite — "outside of appetite and tolerance"; Workforce and Infrastructure also sit at 16.
[Evidence] Risk 6 Operational Pressure and Overcapacity: current "16 (4x4)", target "6", "Minimal", "Current score outside of appetite and tolerance"; Risk 1 Workforce and Risk 2 Infrastructure both current "16". — p.39, [Board Assurance Framework Report, Section 1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Service fragility at IWT: Dermatology requires a contract extension to maintain business continuity, and the IWT 62-day cancer standard has deteriorated on Urology capacity.
[Evidence] "service fragility within Dermatology on the IWT was discussed, including the need for an extension to the existing contract to ensure business continuity while a sustainable solution is developed." — p.17, [March minutes, FIC feedback]; "deterioration in the 62 day cancer standard due to Urology capacity constraints, with recruitment underway." — p.144, [Q&P Committee feedback]

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the IWT Section 29A warning notice and the trust's improvement action plan. The pack confirms a notice exists for Urgent Care and references an "IWT Section 29a Warning Notice" Q&P Committee paper, but discloses neither the breaches found, the date issued, nor the plan content. Request from Isle of Wight NHS Trust (and/or CQC): the warning notice, the date of issue, the inspection report behind it, and the Urgent Care improvement plan with current RAG status. — basis: p.208 and p.146-147.

[FOI] Request the cost of the April resident-doctors' strike and any reimbursement correspondence. Pack states the financial impact is "expected to exceed previous periods, with no confirmed central reimbursement." Request from IWT/PHU the total additional cover cost per strike round in 2025/26 and 2026/27 and any correspondence with NHSE on reimbursement. — basis: p.144, p.24.

[FOI] Request the PHU maternity CQC inspection feedback / letter. An unannounced inspection of PHU maternity inpatient services has reported initial concerns (leadership culture, triage, fetal monitoring) but the report is awaited. Request the CQC factual-accuracy letter and the trust's maternity staffing/sustainability plan. — basis: p.144.

[FOI] Request the NHSE 8 April 2026 cash-support / Cash Committee condition letter and the Cash Recovery Plan. The pack records that NHSE wrote on 8 April requiring a Cash Committee and Cash Recovery Plan by 30 April, and that from June 2026 revenue PDC cash draws will be refused without them; c.£7m cash support expected this quarter. Request the NHSE letter, the Cash Recovery Plan and the value/profile of cash support drawn. — basis: p.110 [Revised ToR – FIC] and p.17 [March minutes].

[FOI] Request the two rejected PHU business cases. The Finance & Infrastructure Committee "rejected" the MRI Scanner MR4 conversion (PHU/BCRC) and the Expansion of Chemotherapy Provision (PHU/BCRC) cases "in current form." Request both business cases, their values and the affordability concerns. — basis: p.102 [FIC Part One feedback].
