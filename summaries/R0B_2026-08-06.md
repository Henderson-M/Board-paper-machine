## Top lines

[LEAD] South Tyneside and Sunderland was named an MBRRACE-UK outlier for stillbirth, neonatal and extended perinatal mortality for 2024 births, with 17 stillbirths and nine neonatal deaths in the year — and the outlier finding is now feeding directly into the trust's own maternity thematic review.
[Evidence] "She noted that the Trust had been identified as an outlier for stillbirth, neonatal mortality and extended perinatal mortality rates, with adjusted rates more than 5% higher than those of comparable organisations. She advised the Board that 17 stillbirths and nine neonatal deaths were recorded during 2024, with a significant concentration of stillbirths occurring during May and June 2024 before rates returned to expected variation from July onwards." — p.9, [Minutes of 4 June 2026 board, Item 13 MBRRACE-UK Perinatal Mortality Surveillance Report]. Repeated in the August pack: "to note, the Trust was identified as an outlier by MBRRACE-UK for losses in 2024 and therefore this data has also been included within this report" — p.89, [Maternity and Neonatal Thematic Review of Learning from Incident Investigations]

[LEAD] The trust has dropped out of NHS Oversight Framework segment 1 into segment 2, and its own paper lists five metrics — including cancer, diagnostics, ED and mortality — putting the Q1 position at further risk.
[Evidence] "The most recent published national segmentation was for Q4 25/26, whereby the Trust was in segment 2 (upper middle quartile), with a delivery score of 2.12. The Trust moved from segment 1 with a reduction in average score of 0.06 from Q3." Areas to note include "Indicative Cancer 62-day performance is below national standard for Q1 (72.0% v 80 National Standard)" and "Summary Hospital Mortality Indicator is higher than expected" — p.260, [Performance Report, Item 12]

[LEAD] The trust is £7.9m short on recurrent savings three months into the year, and says the shortfall would leave it carrying a £27.2m underlying deficit into 2027/28 — £7.9m worse than the £19.3m it planned.
[Evidence] "The CIP target was set at £44,095k for the year, equating to 5.4% of operating expenditure which is yet again higher than the level of efficiency requirement in prior years… Based on current plans, the forecast recurrent delivery is expected to be £16,861k, which is £7,934k below the original planned recurrent delivery of £24,795k. Should recurrent delivery not improve upon the above figures, then there would be a carry forward of £27,234k and this would make up most of the Groups underlying position and starting position for 2027/28 financial planning." — p.229, [Financial Position as at 30 June 2026, Item 15]. Divisional run-rate forecast: "would result in a year end overspend of £7,974k if nothing changed" with Medicine forecast £10,062k adverse — p.233, same paper. Headline reported position is a £714k surplus, £8k ahead of plan — p.222.

[LEAD] The trust's mortality index has returned to the "higher than expected" band, its South Tyneside site has now moved into that band too, and 12 patients were wrongly recorded as having died.
[Evidence] "The latest organisational SHMI value for South Tyneside and Sunderland NHS Foundation Trust (STSFT) is 1.1789 (published 11 June 2026 for the period February 2025 – January 2026) returning to Band 1 'Higher than expected'. At site level, there was an increase in the SHMI for South Tyneside District Hospital (STDH) and is now considered 'Band 1 "Higher than expected"'" — p.52, [Mortality Report Q4 2025/26, Item 7c]. "The index remains higher than the mean (1.1326) and remains above the upper process limit (1.1634) with a trend of eleven consecutive publications above the mean, this continues to represent special cause concerning high variation… Since publication of the latest SHMI it has been identified that twelve patient spells were classified with an incorrect discharge method which transposed as a patient death upon processing." — p.53, same paper

[LEAD] The trust's own annual nurse staffing review puts a £4.07m price on fixing high-priority safe-staffing gaps, and says the recommendations have been prioritised because of the financial position — while one surgical ward cut by a previous savings scheme is now running two nurses to 24 patients at night.
[Evidence] "Full implementation of all high-priority recommendations across acute and community services would require a total investment of £4,069,063." And: "Recognising the current financial challenges, the recommendations from the nursing workforce reviews have been carefully considered, with priorities set on a risk-based approach." — p.183, [Annual Nursing Safe Staffing and Establishment Review, Item 7j]. On the surgical ward: "The Surgical Centre Inpatients (SCIP) at South Tyneside is a 24-bedded general surgical ward currently staffed with only two RNs overnight, resulting in an RN-to-patient ratio of 1:12… In May 2025, the SCIP nursing establishment was reduced in line with the bed reduction. A total of 7.99 WTE RN and 2.29 WTE HCA budget was surrendered as part of the cost improvement plan." — p.180, same paper

[LEAD] The trust missed every single healthcare-associated infection threshold in 2025/26, with Klebsiella bloodstream infections up 26.5% and running at 81 cases against a threshold of 53.
[Evidence] "The Trust has not achieved the 25/26 thresholds for any of the HCAIs." — p.189, [Infection Prevention and Control Annual Report 2025/2026, Item 7i]. "The Trust has breached its threshold for Klebsiella BSI, reporting 81 healthcare associated cases against a threshold of 53, unfortunately this shows an increase of 26.5% when comparing to last year." — p.201, same paper. The report also discloses a carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales outbreak: "An outbreak of carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales (CPE) was identified in June 2025 on Ward B28 (Haematology / Renal) at SRH, affecting 12 patients in total." — p.190

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's director of operations has been appointed substantive chief executive at Gateshead Health FT after a two-year secondment, and STSFT's own CEO is now reviewing the executive structure — the paper names neither the individual nor a replacement plan.
[Evidence] "Update 04 06 26 – Director of Operations appointed as substantive CEO at Gateshead Health NHSFT. CEO to reflect on STSFT structure." Action opened 07 08 25; earlier updates record the secondment being extended twice — p.18, [Action Log, Item 5]

[WORTH WATCHING] Disposal of the Sunderland Eye Infirmary site and a possible vacancy freeze are both on the table as ways of holding the financial plan together.
[Evidence] "Further mitigations and opportunities that would help the Trust to deliver its financial plan in the year would be the full delivery of CIP, the disposal of Sunderland Eye Infirmary site, any further technical adjustments and the implementation of transformational schemes to both improve productivity and reduce costs. The Trust could also consider putting in further measures such as vacancy freezes or additional controls and processes for non-pay expenditure." — p.232, [Financial Position as at 30 June 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust fell 28 places in the national 62-day cancer ranking in a single month and says there is currently no mitigation, with the cause attributed to consultant gaps in urology.
[Evidence] "The Trust ranked 61st out of 119 Trusts in the national dashboard for May (appendix 2), which is 28 places lower than April… This is predominantly due to challenges in Urology due to consultant gaps. Although there is a reduction in patients approaching their breach date, this remains an ongoing unmitigated risk." — p.258, [Performance Report]. Also flagged by the finance and performance committee: "Cancer performance under pressure mainly due to staffing in Urology" — p.220

[WORTH WATCHING] Two workforce internal audits landed limited assurance, and the board is being asked to raise its recruitment risk score by 50% for 2026/27.
[Evidence] HR & OD audit table: "Management of volunteers (2024/25)" — Limited assurance, 17 findings (2 high, 13 medium, 2 low); "Attendance Management" — Limited assurance, 11 findings (3 high, 8 medium) — p.287, [Workforce Committee Annual Report 2025/2026, Item 15a]. BAF risk 4 "Inability to recruit high calibre staff to optimum staffing levels": risk rating 2025/26 of 6, recommended risk rating 2026/27 of 9 — p.288, same paper

[WORTH WATCHING] Agency and bank spend are both tracking well above the national reduction targets, and an unquantified Agenda for Change band 5 nurse regrade could add up to £5.1m of unfunded cost.
[Evidence] "Should spend continue at this rate, the Trust would significantly exceed the target and spend (pro-rated to £5,760k) would also be above last year. There are a number of measures in place, however, to reduce this spend with a current forecast outturn of £5,002k, which would still be £1,350k above the target." — p.227, [Financial Position as at 30 June 2026]. On band 5: "the current high-level estimation is that the impact could range from £856k to £5,089k dependent upon the point of transition and any back dating of arrangements… Should the impact exceed the income available from the CUF uplift the Trust will have a residual financial pressure which is as yet unknown and therefore unmitigated." — p.227
