# Herefordshire & Worcestershire + Coventry and Warwickshire Joint ICB Board in Common — 15 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.hwics.org.uk/meetings/board-papers
- **Pack:** 12 files (6 high-signal PDFs analysed: CEO report, Q&P, Finance, BAF, 2x Annual Reports)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-09
- **Note:** Board in Common — findings apply to both statutory ICBs (H&W = QGH; C&W = QWU), which remain legally independent but operate as a single "cluster".

## Top lines

[LEAD] The two ICBs will recommend a full merger into a single statutory body of ~1.9 million residents from April 2027, choosing to keep the existing cluster footprint rather than realign to mayoral boundaries.
[Evidence] "the proposed recommendation is that NHS Coventry and Warwickshire ICB and NHS Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB should merge on their existing clustered footprint from April 2027. This would create a single statutory ICB that serves approximately 1.9 million residents." — p.1, [Agenda 09 CEO report]

[LEAD] The cluster is cutting £22m in management costs this year, with a 45-day Wave 3 consultation affecting 432 staff still live — completing on the meeting date itself (15 July) — and a further ~£1m of savings pencilled in if the merger proceeds.
[Evidence] "the ICBs delivering a planned reduction of £22 million in management costs during 2026/27… commencement of the Wave 3 consultation, which covers the majority of substantive staff… due to be completed on 15 July" — p.2, [Agenda 09 CEO report]; "Launch of wave 3 consultation affecting 432 staff in post… will impact on productivity" (risk raised 12→16) — p.3, [Agenda 14 BAF]

[LEAD] Worcestershire Acute Hospitals Trust remains under refreshed NHS England enforcement for urgent and emergency care, with NHSE concluding sustainable improvement has not been achieved and the trust "remains below the expected performance trajectory".
[Evidence] "NHS England has refreshed the enforcement undertakings relating to urgent and emergency care performance at Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust… the Trust remains below the expected performance trajectory. Consequently, the Trust remains subject to formal enforcement arrangements" — p.4, [Agenda 09 CEO report]

[LEAD] A spike in adult ADHD/autism "right to choose" spending is the ICBs' main emerging financial risk: H&W monthly spend rose from an average £0.1m in 2024/25 to £1.8m in April 2026.
[Evidence] "the average monthly expenditure in 2024/25 of £0.1m to rising to £1.0m in 2025/26 (noting that the last six months of 2025 saw an average of £1.2m a month)… for H&W ICB the expenditure in April 2026 was £1.8m" — p.3, [Agenda 13 Financial Report]; also BAF risk "Increase in right to choose activity in respect of adult ADHD and Autism" — p.7, [Agenda 14 BAF]

[LEAD] George Eliot Hospital ophthalmology has a live clinical harm review: nine harm incidents confirmed (six moderate) among insourced-provider patients, with a further outsourced-provider cohort still being sized.
[Evidence] "nine incidents of harm have been confirmed, of which six have been categorised as moderate harm. Additional concerns have also been identified within a cohort of patients managed by an outsourcing provider, with the full number of affected patients still being established… this remains a significant patient safety risk." — p.5, [Agenda 12 Quality & Performance]

[LEAD] Wye Valley Trust is re-auditing 50 fractured-neck-of-femur deaths after an external audit found mortality-coding errors, with the outcome expected to change its reported SHMI mortality position.
[Evidence] "50 cases in which surgery occurred more than 30 days prior to death are being urgently re-audited. The outcome of the re-audit is expected to change the reported mortality position and improve the Standardised Hospital Mortality Index (SHMI)." — p.4, [Agenda 12 Quality & Performance]

[WORTH WATCHING] H&W was rated lowest-quartile ("red") nationally on the patient-safety domain in NHSE's 2025/26 annual assessment, driven by perinatal mortality (neonatal deaths and stillbirths).
[Evidence] "HW Headline performance data – 25/26 … Safety: Lowest performing quartile"; "There is a clear understanding of the data and drivers that underpin the red patient safety metric, which is largely driven by perinatal mortality (both neonatal deaths and stillbirths)." — p.3, [Agenda 09 CEO report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Worcestershire is running a measles outbreak — 24 confirmed cases since April 2026 — with an unusual pattern including infected healthcare workers and a UKHSA-led incident response.
[Evidence] "Since April 2026, 24 confirmed cases have been reported… a mixed pattern of transmission, with a combination of hospital-associated and community-acquired infections… A coordinated system-wide incident response has been established through UKHSA." — p.5-6, [Agenda 12 Quality & Performance]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICBs' 2026/27 efficiency plans lean heavily on non-recurrent (one-off) savings — c.£24m of H&W's £44.0m target and c.£32m of C&W's £48.4m — flagging an underlying-deficit question for future years.
[Evidence] "The ICB's 2026/27 efficiency target of £44.023m includes approximately £24m of non-recurrent savings" (H&W) — p.[Appendix A], [Agenda 13 Financial Report]; "£48.415m includes approximately £32m of non-recurrent savings" (C&W) — p.[Appendix B], [Agenda 13 Financial Report]

[FOI] Two Prevention of Future Deaths (Regulation 28) coroner reports were issued in the period, including a joint report to CWPT + UHCW over a mental-health-ward death and one to WAHT where a death was "contributed to by neglect".
[Evidence] "Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust (WHAT) received a Regulation 28 report following a finding that a patient's death was contributed to by neglect… Staffing levels and skill mix were identified as contributory factors." — p.5, [Agenda 12 Quality & Performance] — request the two Reg 28 reports and trust responses from the relevant coroners / trusts.
