# Leicester, Leicestershire & Rutland ICB and Northamptonshire ICB — Board Meeting in Common — 18 June 2026

- **Source:** https://www.icnorthamptonshire.org.uk/download/llr-nicb-board-meeting-in-common-papers-18062026pdf.pdf?ver=67670&doc=docm93jijm4n27105.pdf
- **Pack:** 1 combined Board-in-Common pack (73 pages, 4.97MB; identical pack also published on LLR ICB site)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-11

> Note on pack: this is a joint LNR cluster (Leicester, Leicestershire, Rutland + Northamptonshire) Board-in-Common. Papers cover joint CEO update, LNR-wide quality and performance, SEND reforms, M01 finance and two transition papers — including a freshly arrived NHS England letter (2 June 2026) instructing the cluster to merge into a single ICB by 1 April 2027. The most material item is the merger instruction, attached as Appendix 1 to the Transition Report.

## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has formally instructed the LLR/Northamptonshire cluster to merge into a single ICB by 1 April 2027, with a response due to NHSE by 14 July 2026.
[Evidence] "it is proposed that on 1 April 2027, ICB clusters will be replaced by merged ICBs serving populations of at least 1.5 million" / "You are expected to engage local partners before responding to your Regional Director by 14 July 2026" — p.69–70, [Transition Report – ICB Merger, Appendix 1, letter from Glen Burley, Deputy Chief Executive NHS England, 2 June 2026, to Anu Singh and Toby Sanders, cc Dale Bywater].

[LEAD] The cluster has shifted its transition programme from restructuring towards exploring a full ICB merger, with a programme director appointed for 2026/27.
[Evidence] "In April 2026 the focus of the Transition programme shifted from implementing changes to structures and functions to operating as a Strategic Commissioner and considering future changes to the organisational form, including whether a Merger of the organisations is likely. … A Programme Director was appointed for 2026/27 to oversee this change" — p.61, [Joint Transition Assurance Report, June 2026]. Board paper recommends: "Note the instruction from NHSE proposal for clustered ICB to become merged ICBs from 1 April 2027" — p.67.

[LEAD] NHS England's national repatriation of all 287 patients from St Andrew's Healthcare in Northampton continues under 24-hour onsite Regional Support Programme oversight, with a newly appointed StAH chief executive.
[Evidence] "St Andrew's Healthcare: NHSE-led repatriation of all patients from the Northamptonshire site continues under national and Regional Support Programme (RSP) oversight (24-hour on-site presence). … Recent leadership changes include a newly appointed CEO. Significant quality, safety and system implications remain." — p.21, [Quality Assurance LNR ICB Cluster]. CEO report adds: "placed into special measures following serious quality concerns, with admissions previously suspended. These concerns have been compounded by significant governance failings, leadership instability and ongoing investigations into alleged abuse and neglect." — p.16, [Chief Executive Report]. "Northamptonshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust (NHFT) is also currently undertaking due diligence to explore the potential for providing services at the hospital site" — p.16.

[LEAD] Kettering General Hospital home birthing is paused for three months from June 2026 after a maternity review identified "significant safety, training and governance risks."
[Evidence] "KGH Home Birthing: A three-month temporary pause is in place following identification of significant safety, training and governance risks. A safe, standardised UHN-wide homebirth model is being developed ahead of planned reopening in July 2026." — p.21, [Quality Assurance LNR ICB Cluster]. Detail: "The MatNeo SLT review (Feb 2026), following the Regulation 28 PFD and NHSE directive, identified significant safety, training and governance risks in the KGH homebirth model, echoed in the 2026 national interim investigation." — p.28, [Quality Report – LNR Executive Summary].

[LEAD] CQC has formally withdrawn its Section 29A Warning Notice on Kettering General Hospital maternity, with a further CQC inspection expected; no report will be issued because the notice "did not pass CQC internal QA".
[Evidence] "Section 29A Warning Notice has been formally withdrawn, and no report will be issued as it did not pass CQC internal QA; a further inspection is expected, but no date confirmed." — p.28, [Quality Report – LNR Executive Summary]; mirrored at p.21, [Quality Assurance].

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire ran a five-month clinical correspondence outage to GPs after a December 2025 ICE digital upgrade — undetected because the fault generated no alerts — and a harm review is underway.
[Evidence] "A technical fault following the December 2025 ICE upgrade meant clinical correspondence did not transmit to some GP practices. The issue generated no alerts and was not immediately visible. The cause has been identified and resolved. … A harm review is underway with ICB oversight." — p.21, [Quality Assurance]. Resolved with Clinisys; "historic letters are being reviewed and re-issued where clinically needed" — p.27, [Quality Report].

[LEAD] More than 7,000 children aged 0–6 across the East Midlands — including in LLR and Northamptonshire — had vaccination call-and-recall suspensions applied after repeated non-attendance, a practice the cluster says is "not consistent with best practice."
[Evidence] "Over 7,000 children aged 0–6 across the East Midlands had call and recall suspensions applied after repeated non-attendance, including within LLR and Northamptonshire. This is not consistent with best practice. … Suspensions are being lifted; safeguards have been implemented; and re-invitations have been issued. As a result, approximately 800 children have already been vaccinated following reinstatement." — p.16, [Chief Executive Report].

[LEAD] The cluster reports a net £3.8m contract risk at month 1, with four major contracts still unsigned and being adjudicated by NHSE — including a dispute with George Eliot, the UHL specialised services urgent care contract, and Oundle boundary disputes with NWAFT and Cambridge University Hospitals.
[Evidence] "Contract update – net risk of £3.8m … there are four major contracts yet to be signed, that are with NHSE to resolve as follows: UHL – delegated Specialised Services urgent care contract value not agreed … George Eliot – LLR ICB baseline difference and underlying growth issue. Escalated to CFO level discussion and joint dispute papers developed; NWAFT – underlying growth difference … Oundle boundary issue from 2022 … CUH – Oundle boundary issue for N ICB meaning that CUH are paid more than the value of the activity." — p.57, [Finance Report Month 01].

[LEAD] Transition Director Alice McGee is leaving LLR ICB — flagged in the April minutes as leaving "a big gap" — with Louise Young redeployed to pick up transition work alongside her existing portfolio.
[Evidence] "Alice McGee will be leaving LLR ICB later this month, following working her notice as the Transition Director. Formal thanks were recorded for the work Alice McGee has undertook over the past nine months. She will leave a big gap in the organisation. Louise Young will be redeployed to pick up transition work as part of her portfolio." — p.11, [Minutes of 16 April 2026, Item ICBIC-26-27].

[WORTH WATCHING] LNR is forecasting M01 breakeven on £5.16bn planned spend — but only after £5.1m of non-recurrent deficit support to LLR ICB; Northamptonshire receives £0m support.
[Evidence] "The full year reported forecast is a breakeven position across LNR ICBs of £0.0m after £5.1m non-recurrent support (£5.1m LLR and £0m Northamptonshire), which is in line with planned spend of £5.2 billion." — p.56, [Finance Report Month 01]. Efficiency plans: "£88.9m" LLR, "£53.2m" Northants.

[WORTH WATCHING] A&E attendances and admissions at UHL and UHN are running above plan against a "0% growth assumption" — exposing the system to in-year financial risk.
[Evidence] "The latest April evidence indicates that A&E attendances at UHL and UHN show growth year on year against a plan built on a 0% growth assumption. Further detail and validation of the latest activity information together with an assessment of the consequential financial risk is in progress." — p.56, [Finance Report Month 01].

[WORTH WATCHING] The LNR quality report flags eight Prevention of Future Deaths reports across mental health, emergency care, severe infection, records, nutrition and hydration as a "persistent" amber risk.
[Evidence] "Eight Prevention of Future Deaths (PFDs) across mental health, emergency care, severe infection, records, nutrition and hydration; UHN GP letter incident under review" — p.25, [Quality Report – LNR Executive Summary, risk trajectory table].

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire jumped from NHSE Oversight Framework segment 3 to segment 4 in Q4 2025/26 — a deterioration; Kettering General Hospital and UHN both sit at segment 4.
[Evidence] "NOF Scores: UHL 3 | NGH 4 | KGH 4 (UP FROM 3) | LPT 2 | NHFT 2" Q4 25/26 — p.25, [Quality Report – LNR Executive Summary]. (Note: NGH and KGH are the two sites of UHN; KGH worsened in-year.)

[WORTH WATCHING] The cluster is pursuing "supra-cluster" working across the Midlands — including with Derbyshire/Nottinghamshire/Lincolnshire (DNL) cluster on Pharmacy/Optometry/Dentistry, regional procurement, and Midlands-wide 111/999 commissioning.
[Evidence] "a decision was made to work at scale across the Midlands for 111/999 commissioning. Pharmacy, Ophthalmic and Dental (PODs). An approach for the PODs Service has been reached with Derbyshire, Nottinghamshire and Lincolnshire (DNL) cluster ICB, and arrangements progressing. Regional Procurement Service for goods and services has been agreed in principle" — p.62, [Joint Transition Assurance Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] Cancer performance is behind plan across all three LNR acute providers (UHL, UHN, NGH), with a risk of missing the year-end target.
[Evidence] "Cancer performance across all 3 acute providers is behind plan with a risk to the delivery of the planned target." — p.24, [Performance Report LNR Executive Summary].

[WORTH WATCHING] Adult Learning Disability and Autism inpatient numbers in LLR remain above plan, with end-of-year target delivery at risk because of Ministry of Justice restrictions on patients.
[Evidence] "In LLR the number of Learning Disability & Autism Adults inpatients remains above plan. This is linked to the number of LDA inpatients that are under Ministry of Justice (MoJ) restrictions that risks meeting the end of year target." — p.24, [Performance Report LNR Executive Summary].

[WORTH WATCHING] "The Squirrels" short-breaks service has "persistent clinical risks" in care planning, medication, training and leadership; ICB and Northampton Children's Trust maintaining intensive oversight of the jointly-commissioned provision.
[Evidence] "Short Breaks – The Squirrels: Persistent clinical risks continue in relation to care planning, medication management, staff training and leadership. ICB and Northampton Children Trust are maintaining intensive oversight of this jointly commissioned provision." — p.21, [Quality Assurance]; expanded at p.28.

[WORTH WATCHING] CEO Toby Sanders signals tougher decisions ahead, including "honest conversations over decommissioning services, alongside attitudes to risk."
[Evidence] "There is a further meeting next week with national colleagues to look at the 10-year plan and ensuring a operational re-set takes place. Part of this will be a culture change within the cluster. Some hard decisions need to be made, with honest conversations over decommissioning services, alongside attitudes to risk." — p.5, [Minutes of 16 April 2026, Item ICBIC-26-20].

[WORTH WATCHING] Nottingham and Nottinghamshire ICB's three-year delegated oversight of Pharmacy, Optometry and Dentistry (PODS) for Northamptonshire ICB ends March 2026, with continuity arrangements still being worked through.
[Evidence] "Pharmacy, Optometry and Dentistry (PODS): Delegated oversight of Pharmacy, Optometry and Dentistry (responsibility held by Notts ICB) is ending. NICB is working to ensure continuity of oversight, statutory clarity and system-level assurance through the transition." — p.21, [Quality Assurance]; expanded p.27, [Quality Report].

## FOI leads

[FOI] Obtain Glen Burley's full 2 June 2026 letter and any cross-cluster correspondence on the proposed merger of LLR and Northamptonshire ICBs from 1 April 2027.
[Evidence] Most of the letter is in the pack (Appendix 1, pp.69–71) but it cross-refers to "our guidance on implementing ICB mergers and boundary changes for April 2026 and 2027" (p.71) and "further details of this process, including on engagement." Request from NHS England Midlands: the full guidance document, any equivalent letters sent to the other clusters, and the Midlands regional director's covering correspondence. Worth pursuing the response when it goes in by 14 July 2026.

[Evidence] "A technical fault following the December 2025 ICE upgrade meant clinical correspondence did not transmit to some GP practices … A harm review is underway with ICB oversight." (p.21). Request from University Hospitals of Northamptonshire / LNR ICB cluster: terms of reference and findings of the harm review; the number of letters affected; the number of patients identified at potential harm; the Clinisys post-incident report.

[FOI] Request the MatNeo SLT review (February 2026) into Kettering General Hospital home birthing, the Regulation 28 PFD that triggered it, and the NHSE directive that followed.
[Evidence] "The MatNeo SLT review (Feb 2026), following the Regulation 28 PFD and NHSE directive, identified significant safety, training and governance risks in the KGH homebirth model" (p.28). Request from University Hospitals of Northamptonshire (KGH site) / NHSE Midlands: the MatNeo SLT review report, the coroner's PFD, and the NHSE directive.

[FOI] Obtain the withdrawn CQC Section 29A Warning Notice on KGH maternity and the internal CQC QA correspondence on why it "did not pass CQC internal QA."
