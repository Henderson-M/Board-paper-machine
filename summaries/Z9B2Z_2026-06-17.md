# West and North London ICB — Board (in public) — 17 June 2026

**Pack scope:** Agenda + 2026/27 W&NL ICB annual budget (paper 2.3). Annual report papers (2.1, 2.2) for 2025/26 are listed as "to follow". Meeting is short (9.00–9.45). First budget paper after the merger of NW London and NC London ICBs into West & North London ICB.

**Source files:** `p1.pdf` (agenda, 1p), `p2.pdf` (2026/27 budget paper, 9pp).

## 1. Top lines

**LEAD — W&NL ICB ringfences £120m (1% of recurrent spend) for "Neighbourhood" investment in first budget after merger, but holds £60m of it back as a "trading reserve" against acute over-performance.**
[Evidence] "Within the plan 1% has been earmarked for Neighbourhood investment (£60m in-year with a £120m full-year impact)" and "The remaining £60m is not expected to be committed in 2026/27 and will be held to fund in-year pressures against acute services as a trading reserve." — p.2–3, [2026/27 NHS W&NL ICB annual budget].

**LEAD — £71m management-cost cut baked into the efficiency plan, matching the allocation reduction to hit NHSE's £19-per-head running-cost target.**
[Evidence] "Management Costs (£71m target) — The value of the target matches the reduction in allocation the ICB has had in respect of reducing management costs to the £19 per head of population target." — p.5, [budget paper]. [INFERENCE] At ~£71m, this looks like one of the largest single-ICB running-cost reductions in the country; would need cross-check against other ICB budgets.

**LEAD — £23m Continuing Healthcare efficiency target premised on ICB "spending more on All Age Continuing Care than the population requires".**
[Evidence] "Complex Care (£23m target) — the modelling undertaken shows that the ICB spends more on All Age Continuing Care (which makes up most of the complex care budget) than the population requires. The ICB will look to reduce spend in line with that expected by for the population." — p.5, [budget paper].

**LEAD — Underlying financial position worsens by £10.7m in year one despite breakeven plan; deficit not eliminated until 2028/29.**
[Evidence] "The growth required in these areas will result in a worsening of the ICB's underlying position in 2026/27 of £10.7m." and "Improved underlying position by end of the plan period with the deficit eliminated by 2028/29." — p.2–3, [budget paper].

**LEAD — Acute contracts in W&NL to be aligned to NWL prices and methodologies — a material change for former NCL providers (UCLH, Royal Free, North Mid, Whittington).**
[Evidence] "Align acute contracts in W&NL to NWL prices and methodologies." and "Ensure as a result of these changes providers have not been financially destabilised." — p.3, [budget paper].

**LEAD — Total ICB budget £11.93bn for 2026/27; acute £5.39bn, mental health £1.22bn, community £902m, CHC £522m.**
[Evidence] Budget table — p.4, [budget paper].

**WORTH WATCHING — ICB warns end of system control total "could signal the end of financial system working", flags risk of provider over-trading.**
[Evidence] "There is no longer a system control total, which could signal the end of financial system working increasing the risk of over-trading by providers, under-commissioning and increase in counting and coding." — p.5, [budget paper]. Quotable line on national policy shift, from a large London ICB.

**WORTH WATCHING — £16m medicines management efficiency, £15m INT pilot for top 5% highest-risk adults across 25% of patch.**
[Evidence] "£15m to assess Integrated Neighbourhood Teams (INTs) for the top 5% of highest-risk adults across 25% of patch, with the aim to rapidly scale the model across W&NL in 27/28 if successful." — p.3, [budget paper].

**WORTH WATCHING — Performance committee approved budget on 5 May 2026; budget paper note says NHSE deadline for upload was 1 June 2026 — board approval comes 16 days after submission deadline.**
[Evidence] "The ICB Budget was presented to the ICB Performance Committee on the 5 May 2026 and approved by the Committee" and "The deadline for uploading budgets is 1 June 2026." — p.1–2, [budget paper]. Governance question on board-vs-committee approval sequencing.

## 4. Pack note

Slim two-paper pack but editorially rich: the 2026/27 budget is the first post-merger financial blueprint for one of the largest ICBs in England (£11.93bn). The neighbourhood ringfence (with half held back), the £71m management-cost cut, the £23m CHC overspend admission, and the open warning about the end of system working are all individually publishable. Annual report papers were not in the published pack ("to follow") — worth a follow-up download closer to the meeting.
