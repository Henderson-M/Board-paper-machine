# Rotherham Doncaster and South Humber NHS Foundation Trust (RDaSH) board — 28 May 2026

- **Source:** https://www.rdash.nhs.uk/documents/board-of-directors-packs-may-2026/
- **Pack:** 2 files (packA.pdf — 350pp main pack; packB.pdf — 35pp supplementary)
- **Analysed:** 2026-05-28

## Top lines

[LEAD] RDaSH staff survey has fallen for a third straight year and now sits below its mental-health/community peer-group average on all but two of nine People Promise measures, with the "raising concerns" sub-score at a multi-year low.
[Evidence] "Overall, the Trust has seen a decrease in results against each of the people promise themes when compared against our 2024 scores… we are now below our peer group average scores for all bar 2 promises". "Nationally the raising concerns sub-score is at a five year low… a sizeable drop from 6.77 to 6.47". "16 Directorates require targeted improvement to achieve Trust average benchmarks" and the Children's Care Group free-text heat map is rated "very high". — pp.284–288, [Staff Survey – Areas of Focus, Paper V]

[LEAD] Sickness absence has jumped to 7.25% in April 2026 from 5.95% a year earlier — described by the trust as an "outlier" position regionally — as the trust runs a large organisational-change/redundancy programme.
[Evidence] "The Trust's current sickness absence rate for April 2026 is 7.25%, as a comparator the position for April 2025 was 5.95%." "our sickness absence levels remain an outlier (Annex One)." Top reason: "Anxiety/Stress/Depression". — pp.188–191, [Sickness Absence, Paper R]; CEO calls it "our outlier sickness position", p.148, [CEO Report, Paper P]

[LEAD] CEO Toby Lewis says NHS North-East and Yorkshire approved the trust's 2026–2029 plan but "perplexingly" attached four conditions — including demands for a workforce-reduction plan and details of unidentified CIP — which the trust says are unnecessary or sent in error and will challenge chair-to-chair.
[Evidence] "Somewhat perplexingly four conditions were appended to this approval, and when we meet with the regional team on June 1st we will seek further clarity on these conditions before a chair-to-chair reply will be issued." Lists: workforce reduction plan ("given that the submission itself contains this… we are seeking clarity"); unidentified CIP ("we suspect this is a standard clause sent to us in error"); further OOAP work ("we will not be further amending the plan"); a board-approved sickness implementation plan. — p.151, [CEO Report, Paper P]

[LEAD] The trust has commissioned an independent Well-Led developmental review from external firm The Value Circle (TVC); its final report (8 May 2026) is broadly positive but flags that staff confidence to raise concerns "may have decreased in some areas".
[Evidence] "The Value Circle (TVC) were appointed, following an open procurement process, to provide the Trust with an independent Well Led Developmental Review." Finding: "triangulation across interviews, staff feedback, and Trust papers indicate that confidence to raise concerns may have decreased in some areas of the organisation with some staff perceiving that raising concerns may lead to negative consequences." Trust self-assessment is "amber" on Freedom to Speak Up, EDI and Learning/Improvement; aim is to move Well-Led to "green". — pp.214–235, [Well led externally commissioned developmental review, Paper T]

[WORTH WATCHING] RDaSH has received a new Prevention of Future Deaths (Reg 28) notice over the death of a patient named only as "Delwyn"; CEO notes it follows unrelated 2023 and 2024 coronial notices to the trust.
[Evidence] "We have received a Prevention of Future Deaths (PFD, Reg 28) notice in respect of the death of our patient, Delwyn. The Board is familiar with unrelated 2023 and 2024 notices issued by the coronial office." Response actions: end of long-day/end-of-shift recording and alignment of Section 17 leave policies with practice; audit to board September. — pp.148–149, [CEO Report, Paper P]

[WORTH WATCHING] CEO warns the absence of clear national NOF metrics for 2026/27 makes the trust's July segmentation "something of a lottery"; on current metrics it would move from segment 3 to segment 2, but novel measures lack data definitions and peer comparison.
[Evidence] "Initial publication in July will, therefore, be something of a lottery". "The Trust had spent 2025-26 at segment 3 (of four), owing to the finance override applied because we received deficit support funding. With the same metrics on April 1 we would, therefore, be rated at 2." Flags "perverse incentives" in proposed restrictive-intervention/incident-reporting measures "that we will wish to resist." — p.148, [CEO Report, Paper P]

[WORTH WATCHING] Underlying deficit cut to a projected £6.3m at year-end (31 March 2026) from £16m, with underlying breakeven projected for 2026/27; trust says it declined to submit a plan with unidentified savings "unlike neighbouring organisations" amid South Yorkshire ICB's £130m challenge.
[Evidence] "the underlying deficit was expected to be £6.3m at year end… a significant improvement from £16m in 2023, with an underlying break even position projected for the following year". "the Trust had declined to submit a plan with unidentified savings, unlike neighbouring organisations." Month 11 surplus £654k; M1 (April 2026) £21k better than plan, breakeven forecast, all 24 directorates within budget. — pp.13–14, [Minutes 26 Mar 2026]; p.82, [IQPR, Paper M]

[WORTH WATCHING] A NED-style challenge embedded in the patient-safety paper questions whether the board has the data to back executives' assurance claims, given the similarity of 2024/25 and 2025/26 serious-incident themes.
[Evidence] "Given similarities between 24/5 PSIIs and 25/6 PSIIs the Board should consider whether we have sight of, and there exist, suitable data measures to match the assurances of change being offered by clinical executive Board members." 6 PSIIs in 2025/26, dominant category suspected suicide. — pp.194–197, [2025-26 serious patient safety incidents – learning update, Paper S]

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the full Value Circle (TVC) Well-Led developmental review report and the trust's management response. The pack reproduces the report's executive summary and selected positives, but the recommendations (pages 33–36 of the TVC report) and the private-session discussion are not quoted; a management response goes to the June timeout and July board. Request from RDaSH (and/or TVC contract value/scope, given an open procurement was run).
[Evidence] "The TVC report has not been discussed elsewhere prior to today's Board of Directors meeting"; "a management response report to be presented to the Board of Directors in July 2026" — pp.214–215, [Paper T]

[FOI] Request the Regulation 28 PFD notice concerning patient "Delwyn" and the trust's statutory 56-day response, plus the two earlier (2023, 2024) PFD notices the CEO references. None are quoted in the public pack. Request from RDaSH / the relevant coroner.
[Evidence] "We have received a Prevention of Future Deaths (PFD, Reg 28) notice in respect of the death of our patient, Delwyn… unrelated 2023 and 2024 notices" — pp.148–149, [Paper P]

[FOI] Request the four conditions attached by NHS NEY to RDaSH's 2026–2029 plan approval, and the trust's chair-to-chair reply. The CEO summarises but the regional correspondence itself is not in the public pack. Request from NHS England (NEY region) / RDaSH.
[Evidence] "four conditions were appended to this approval… a chair-to-chair reply will be issued" — p.151, [Paper P]

[FOI] Request the staff-survey free-text comments and the directorate-level heat map. 342 free-text comments and the risk heat map (Children's "very high", Rotherham "significant") were shared only in the private board papers. Request from RDaSH.
