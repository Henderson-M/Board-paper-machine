## Top lines

[LEAD] Royal Cornwall has been downgraded from NHSE oversight segment 1 to segment 2, which the trust attributes to a deteriorated position across access, finance, productivity, effectiveness and experience.
[Evidence] "The Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust has moved from segment 1 into segment 2 which is a deteriorated position due to access to services, finance and productivity, effectiveness and experience... Our executive team are working on how we can take action to recover our position." — p.45, [CEO Report to Board in Public, July 2026]

[LEAD] Urgent care performance deteriorated sharply in May, with Type 1 four-hour performance falling to 42.4% and average ambulance handover rising to 82.4 minutes — the trust declared an internal critical incident on 27 May.
[Evidence] "Type 1 ED 4-hour performance falling to 42.4%, all-type 4-hour performance reducing to 75.7%, patients spending more than 12 hours in ED increasing to 18.4%, and average ambulance handover time rising to 82.4 minutes... ED crowding remains the Trust's highest scored risk, with risk 9048 continuing to score 25." — p.50, [Urgent Care Update and Ending Corridor Care, Item 8]; internal critical incident "On the 27 May, the Trust declared an internal critical incident due to pressures within our emergency department" — p.46, [CEO Report]

[LEAD] The trust does not have an affordable capital programme and is at risk of breaching its statutory Capital Resource Limit — the associated risk has been escalated from High (16) to Extreme (20).
[Evidence] "At the date of this report, the Trust does not have an affordable capital programme and is therefore at risk of operating outside the funding available"; "the Trust has statutory requirements to operate within its Capital Resource Limit (the CDEL funding)... The Trust has not previously breached these requirements." — p.213–214, [Month 2 Capital Position Update, Item 11.2]. Risk 8521 "increase... from High (16) to Extreme (20) linked to how an overspend could result in the breach of a statutory financial duty" — p.9 agenda, [Item 11.2]

[LEAD] The trust is carrying a £43.49m underlying deficit and has delivered only a fraction of its £42.5m savings requirement two months into the year, with recurrent savings at 9.9% of plan.
[Evidence] "The underlying deficit has remained at the month 1 position of £43.49m"; "The 2026/27 financial plan set a total savings requirement of £42.5m. In May, the FSP programme delivered £2.07m against a target of £2.31m"; "Recurrent delivery also remains low, at 9.9% against plan and 11% of total delivery, which increases the risk to sustainable achievement of the programme." — p.204–205, [Month 2 Revenue Position, Item 11.1]

[LEAD] The 2025 NHS Staff Survey shows a decline in overall staff experience, with only 23.1% of pulse-survey respondents now saying they would recommend the trust as a place to work.
[Evidence] "The initial results of the 2025 NHS Staff Survey indicate a decline in overall staff experience... most indicators show reduced advocacy, concerns about safety climate, and lower confidence in raising concerns"; "internal pulse survey data from Q1 2026/27 shows RCHT is on that 18-24 month journey with the Pulse survey identifying only 23.1% of total number of respondents (281) would now recommend us as a place to work." — p.61, [Staff Survey 2025 Action Plan, Item 9]. Three priorities: "Appraisals (largest gap compared to comparator organisations), Raising Concerns, Health and Safety Climate."

[LEAD] The trust's eCare (electronic patient record) go-live has slipped again — the finance committee concluded a November 2026 go-live is "not viable" and endorsed a February–March 2027 option.
[Evidence] CEO report: "The Trust is now in the latter stages of agreeing a new go live date which is anticipated, but not yet confirmed to be, Spring 2027" — p.39, [CEO Report]. FPC: "The Committee supported the conclusion that November go live is not viable and endorsed further work to develop a February–March 2027 option, subject to affordability and assurance." — p.191, [FPC Assurance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's governance improvement plan — developed in response to an external well-led review in autumn 2025 — has zero of eight actions complete, one overdue, and the wider review of its executive governance structure has been "paused".
[Evidence] "The Governance Improvement Plan 2026/2027 comprises eight actions... A summary progress table: Complete 0, Proposed complete 1, In progress 6, Overdue 1"; "The principal area requiring revised timescales is the wider review of the Executive Governance Structure, which has been paused to allow the new organisational arrangements to embed before completion later in 2026." — p.40–41, [CEO Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Concerns about racism and discrimination raised within the trust's perinatal service have been escalated to the senior leadership team, prompting a new steering group.
[Evidence] "We have seen concerns raised in our Perinatal Service about racism and discrimination. These concerns were escalated to the Senior Leadership Team"; safety champion feedback: "There has been a concern raised regarding discriminatory language used by members of staff whilst at work." — p.135, p.137, [Perinatal Safety Report, Item 10.2]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's own risk analysis benchmarks it as having a more complex, higher-rated risk profile than peer trusts, and it is holding two extreme (score 20) corporate risks including a Subject Access Request backlog with potential ICO regulatory action.
[Evidence] "Analysis of six NHS Trusts' CRRs shows RCHT has a more complex, higher-rated profile than peers"; risk 9573 "remains at 20 (extreme) while the Trust continues to hold a backlog of Subject Access Requests... it has the potential for regulatory action by the ICO." — p.377, p.379, [Corporate Risk Register, Item 17]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust says it now intends to become an Advanced Foundation Trust but is currently ineligible under NHSE's changed criteria — which require remaining in NOF segment 1 or 2 (it is now in segment 2).
[Evidence] "Trust still has a strategic intent to become an AFT however... the Trust is currently not eligible due to the change in criteria." — p.192, [FPC Assurance Report]; AFT eligibility "including remaining in NOF segment 1 or 2 and achieving a score of 3 or higher in each NOF quality domain for the previous two consecutive quarters" — p.46, [CEO Report]

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the additional UEC assessment and bed-capacity modelling produced by the acting CEO and COO. The paper repeatedly refers to a further assessment "finalised to be shared with the Vice Chair on 30 June" and "updated modelling of the impact of existing improvement initiatives and an assessment of bed capacity" — none of which is in the public pack. Request from RCHT the completed assessment, the bed modelling gap analysis (scheduled 29 June) and any output shared with the Quality Assurance Committee. — p.48, p.52, [Item 8]

[FOI] Request the External Well-Led Development Review (autumn 2025) and the full Governance Improvement Plan. The CEO report references the review and an eight-action plan but does not publish either the review findings or the plan detail. Request from RCHT the well-led review report/terms of reference and the current governance improvement plan tracker (including the single overdue action). — p.40, [CEO Report]

[FOI] Request the terms of reference and emerging themes of the perinatal racism/discrimination steering group. The perinatal report confirms concerns about racism and discrimination and discriminatory language were escalated to the senior leadership team, but describes only that a steering group is being formed. Request from RCHT any records of the concerns raised, the steering group scope, and the "themes emerging from the concerns raised." — p.135–137, [Item 10.2]
