## Top lines

[LEAD] External auditor BDO issued the trust a formal "significant weakness" in value-for-money over financial sustainability, after finding it had no detailed plan for £16.6m (44%) of its £37.9m 2026/27 savings target as at May.
[Evidence] "The Trust has identified a £37.9 million savings target for 2026/27. As at May 2026, the Trust had not identified detailed plans for how it will make £16.6 million (44%) of these savings. This matter is evidence of a significant weakness in the Trust's arrangements for financial sustainability, in that the Trust did not have adequate arrangements in place to bridge its funding gaps and identify achievable savings" — p.186–187, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, BDO LLP, Item 15.1]; and "we have identified a significant weakness in respect of the Trust's arrangements for securing economy, efficiency and effectiveness in its use of resources. This significant weakness was in respect of financial sustainability and the Trust's ability to manage their medium-term budget gaps." — p.168.

[LEAD] BDO's report discloses that an MHRA inspection found a "critical failure" at the Lister Hospital Quality Control Laboratory, meaning test results could not be reliably assured; the inspection was closed on 19 March 2026 after remediation.
[Evidence] "the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) inspection identified serious deficiencies within the Lister Hospital Quality Control Laboratory, including a critical failure to ensure analytical methods were properly validated, meaning test results cannot be reliably assured. MHRA raised a number of other matters in relation to investigating deviations, incomplete equipment validation, data integrity, ineffective implementation of previously agreed corrective actions, as well as gaps in the self-inspection programme… resulting in MHRA closing the inspection process as at 19 March 2026 and concluding that the Trust's arrangements were in line with the GMP requirements." — p.180–181, [Auditor's Annual Report, Item 15.1].

[LEAD] The trust is £6.1m in deficit at Month 2 (against a planned £5.3m deficit), with £3.1m of pay CIP still unidentified, and its top strategic risk — "system and internal financial constraints" — is scored at the maximum-tier 20.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a £6.1m YTD deficit at Month 2 YTD against a plan of £5.3m. The primary drivers of this position are industrial action (£0.5m), sustained workforce pressures within nursing and premium capacity solutions within radiology and an adverse variance against the external CIP plan phasing (£1m)… Unidentified pay CIP (£3.1m)" — p.152–153, [Integrated Performance Report, Month 02, Item 14]; BAF Risk 3 "System and internal financial constraints… Current 4 5 20" — p.42, p.53, [Board Assurance Framework, Item 11].

[LEAD] The trust's 2025 staff survey deteriorated across wellbeing measures — burnout scored 4.99 and appraisals 4.89 (its lowest score) — the response rate fell about 10 points, and 391 of 632 free-text comments were negative, citing a perceived "recruitment freeze."
[Evidence] "Burnout (4.99) - A clear signal of fatigue, workload pressure, and emotional strain. Appraisals (4.89) - This is the lowest score" — p.25; "391/632 of the comments were negative." — p.26; "The response rate has fallen by around 10 percentage points." — p.24; "This theme highlights the impact of (perceived) recruitment freezes on colleague wellbeing and morale." — p.27, [Staff Survey – review of free text comments, Item 9].

[LEAD] BDO discloses that KPMG was commissioned to review control failures at trust subsidiary ENH Pharma Ltd, where staff manually adjusted drug pricing to reimbursable contract prices rather than actual costs; the trust has not reconciled its own drugs spend to the pharmacy stock system.
[Evidence] "The Trust commissioned KPMG to review the design of controls and processes… The KPMG report was finalised close to year end on 24 March 2026, and an action plan was drafted by the Trust… The Trust has not, as noted in our Audit Completion Report, undertaken a similarly detailed piece of work to reconcile its own drugs expenditure to the pharmacy stock system." — p.181, [Auditor's Annual Report, Item 15.1].

[WORTH WATCHING] Leadership churn at the top: newly appointed substantive CEO Adam Sewell-Jones — who at the previous meeting had announced his departure — says the trust has "become too comfortable being regarded as a good organisation" and that performance is "below the standards our patients, staff and Board rightly expect"; Barry Jenkins is interim CFO and chief nurse Theresa Murphy takes on deputy CEO.
[Evidence] "I am incredibly honoured to have been appointed as chief executive… Whilst there is much to be proud of, I believe we have become too comfortable being regarded as a good organisation… Performance across a number of important areas remains below the standards we aspire to achieve and, in some cases, below the standards our patients, staff and Board rightly expect." / "Following my appointment, Barry Jenkins has been appointed as interim chief finance officer… Theresa Murphy, our chief nurse, has agreed to undertake the additional responsibilities of deputy chief executive." — p.16, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 8].
