# George Eliot Hospital NHS Trust board — 3 June 2026

> **Joint board.** This pack covers a single Joint Board held in public by **George Eliot Hospital NHS Trust (GEH, ods RLT)** and **South Warwickshire University NHS Foundation Trust (SWFT, ods RJC)**, which share a joint leadership model, joint management board and combined papers. ## Top lines

[LEAD] Shared Cerner/Oracle Health electronic patient record go-live slips a year — to August 2027 — after the trusts missed the March 2026 build milestone, with the GEH objective rated RED and citing clinical safety concerns.
[Evidence] "A threshold of 75% was established by Oracle Health for build completion... M3 gateway was not achieved in March 2026. There are a number of areas of high risk that have come to fruition in the plan... The reluctant Critical Change Requests cannot be completed by oracle within the timescales allocated in the original programme plan thus creating significant clinical safety concerns for the system if it is to be launch on the intended go-live date. These key issues have been discussed at the EPR programme Board and trust Board who have now supported a go-live date of August 2027." — p.8 (objective 5), [Agenda 8.3 End of Year Assessment of GEH and SWFT 2025/26 Trust Objectives]; SWFT version of same objective (no.13) also RED — p.6, same paper

[LEAD] Both trusts opened 2026/27 already adrift of their breakeven plans, reporting combined month-one deficits driven by industrial action, staff sickness and undelivered savings.
[Evidence] "At Month 1, SWFT reported a YTD deficit of £3.4m, which is £1.9m adverse to plan, and GEH reported a YTD deficit of £2.2m, which is £1.1m adverse to plan. For both Trust's, the adverse variance is primarily driven by expenditure on temporary staffing required to cover high levels of staff sickness, Industrial action costs and CIP slippage." — p.28, [Agenda 10.1 Integrated Performance Report, Finance Executive Summary]

[LEAD] CIP/savings shortfall is trust-wide at month one, attributed to plans not being developed in time for the start of the year.
[Evidence] "In terms of cost improvement and delivery, the under delivery across all divisions relates to insufficient plan development at the start of the financial year." — p.28, [Agenda 10.1 IPR Finance Summary]; SWFT divisional table shows SWFT position −£1,911k adverse to plan, GEH −£1,077k adverse — p.29, same paper

[LEAD] Chief executive Glen Burley has returned from his NHS England national role only part-time, while former chair Russell Hardy has gone to NHSE as Midlands regional chair.
[Evidence] "she welcomed back the Chief Executive from his national role. Continuing to support the national team in a reduced capacity, it was noted that he would therefore not be returning on a full-time basis just yet" and thanks to Hardy "for his phenomenal impact across the Foundation Group throughout his tenure. She wished him all the best in his new role as Chair of NHS England (NHSE) for the Midlands region." — p.2, [Agenda 4 Draft Minutes of Joint Board 01.04.2026, BOD 26.045]

[WORTH WATCHING] April 2026 junior-doctor industrial action carried a "significant (and costly)" financial and activity hit across both trusts.
[Evidence] "it is difficult to draw conclusions from this, particularly noting the significant (and costly) period of Industrial Action in April 2026" — p.4, [Agenda 10.1 IPR, Managing Director Executive Summary]; "The impact of industrial action in April 2026 through lost activity and increased backfill arrangements has impacted the financial position" — p.29, same paper

[WORTH WATCHING] Rising violence and aggression against staff — including a confirmed racially-motivated component — has been escalated to local MPs and the Secretary of State.
[Evidence] "particular attention was drawn to the increase in incidents of violence and aggression across services... a significant proportion of the reported incidents had been racially motivated... The issue had also been raised with local Members of Parliament who had escalated it to the Secretary of State." — p.4, [Agenda 4 Draft Minutes 01.04.2026, BOD 26.052]

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts submitted 2026/27 plans that no longer rely on deficit support funding, a position the chair flagged as the product of years of effort at GEH.
[Evidence] "the Trust Chair commended the significant work undertaken to enable GEH to submit a plan that does not rely on deficit support funding." — p.6, [Agenda 4 Draft Minutes 01.04.2026, BOD 26.054]; "SWFT and GEH submitted compliant net breakeven financial plans for each of the three financial years 2026/27-2028/29." — p.28, [Agenda 10.1 IPR Finance Summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWFT crude mortality (SHMI) sits above the national average and remains the higher of the two trusts, though trending down.
