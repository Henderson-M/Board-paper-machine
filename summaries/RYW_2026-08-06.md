## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England's Midlands regional chair and chief executive called BCHC's chair and CEO to a bilateral over the trust's segment 4 placement and told them to "work harder and faster" — and will escalate the discussion to the ICB.
[Evidence] "CEO and I met with the NHS Regional Chair and CEO at their request to discuss our placing in the National Oversight Framework (segment 4). The meeting was a sharp nudge to remind us of our responsibilities for improving our placement… While they agreed there are some reasons for not progressing as intended, they also stressed the importance of acting now on some of the issues within our sphere of action and that we should work harder and faster to resolve them. They will escalate our discussions to the ICB, accordingly." — p.46, [Trust Chair's Report August 2026, ENC 4]

[LEAD] BCHC has 10,377 children and young people waiting more than 52 weeks for a neurodevelopmental assessment, of whom 4,703 have waited more than two years.
[Evidence] "All of the patients waiting over 52 weeks in CHS pathways are in neurodevelopmental services where we have 10,377 children and young people waiting over 52 weeks, 4,703 of whom have waited over 104 weeks. These numbers were relatively stable in June compared to May." — p.53, [Chief Executive Officer's Report July 2026, ENC 5]; corroborated at p.296 and p.301 (10,399 of the trust's 10,587 52-week waits sit in the Children & Families division), [Quality and Performance Report, ENC 19]

[LEAD] Sickness absence hit 7.4% in June — the trust's highest monthly rate since the pandemic — and the trust is spending c.£300k on temporary HR capacity to chase long-term cases.
[Evidence] "High rates of sickness absence remain our most significant workforce concern. Unfortunately, sickness absence rose again in June to 7.4% - the highest monthly rate since the COVID19 pandemic. Since last month, we have agreed a refreshed set of actions and an Executive-led taskforce approach to addressing this issue. This includes committing c. £300k to temporary HR capacity to support divisions and ensure we can focus on the longest periods of absence and sickness associated with formal HR processes." — p.52, [CEO Report, ENC 5]. The People and Culture Committee "were concerned that sickness absence deteriorated again in June to 7.4% with all Clinical Divisions except the Dental Division off track against the improvement trajectories agreed in May 2026" — p.197, [People and Culture Committee Escalation Report 30 July 2026, ENC 11]

[LEAD] The trust's own performance report states its sickness and long-wait positions are what put it in the bottom quartile of NHSE's oversight framework, and benchmarks it well outside the community-trust average on sickness and vacancies.
[Evidence] "The Trust's high levels of sickness compared to other Providers is one of the key measures triggering our fourth quartile position in the NHSE Performance Oversight Framework. In May the NHS Benchmarking Network reported an average sickness for community trusts of 5.18% compared to the 6.58% we reported." — p.295; "NHS Benchmarking shows a mean average vacancy rate for community Trusts of 4.86% in May compared to the 7.64% we reported that month." — p.291; "High levels of patients waiting to commence treatment, in particular on Neuro Developmental pathways is one of the key measures in the NHS Performance Oversight Framework and our high waits relative to other Trusts is one of the reasons for our fourth quartile rating." — p.299, [Quality and Performance Report, ENC 19]

[LEAD] BCHC was assessed as fully "Non-Compliant" with the NHS core standards for emergency preparedness for 2025/26 — 28 of 58 standards only partially met — and expects to reach no better than "partially compliant" at this month's resubmission.
[Evidence] "Following submission of the 2025/26 NHS Core Standards self-assessment by BCHC, the ICB and regional EPRR team reviewed the organisation's submission and evidence provided. This provided an overall compliance of 'Non-Compliant' with the Core Standards… It is anticipated that BCHC will be 'Partially Compliant' with this year's submission, which shows an improvement on last year's submission." Table: 2025-26 — Total Standards 58, Fully Compliant 30, Partially Compliant 28, Overall status Non Compliant. — p.240 and p.242, [NHS Core Standards for Business Continuity and Emergency Preparedness, ENC 17]

[LEAD] The Community Care Collaborative has been stood down and its work programmes handed to a Neighbourhood Health Board led by University Hospitals Birmingham under a new system "Anchor contract".
[Evidence] "As part of the transition to the Anchor contract arrangements, we have stood down the Community Care Collaborative at its July Steering Group meeting as its work programmes transition to the Neighbourhood Health Board led by University Hospitals Birmingham (UHB)." — p.54, [CEO Report, ENC 5]. The CCC Committee "proposed to formally stand down the Collaborative structures and work programmes" and be "reconstituted as an Integrated Care Committee" — but "As the meeting was not quorate, no formal Committee decisions were taken." — p.217–218, [Community Care Collaborative Committee Escalation Report 9 July 2026, ENC 13]. A new BAF risk 26-15 for the Anchor contract has been created with the CEO named as owner — p.42, [Action Log, ENC 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is at Month 3 £336k in deficit against a breakeven plan, with a £14.1m savings target that still carries a £1m recurrent gap and a forecast the finance director calls "finely balanced".
[Evidence] "At Month 3 we have reported a deficit of £336k against a planned breakeven position… We have completed a year end forecast for the first time during Month 3. Whilst we are forecasting to achieve a breakeven position at the year end, it is clear the remainder of the year will be challenging and finely balanced." — p.232; "In total, recurrent CIPs of £13.1m have been identified against a target of £14.1m… Of the recurrent CIPs, there is an in-year impact of £11.0m – leaving an in-year shortfall of £3.1m. However, non-recurrent schemes have been identified to mitigate the resulting gap." — p.233, [Month 3 Finance Report, ENC 16]. Finance and Performance Committee: "this position is finely balanced and relies upon full delivery of the CIP target" — p.229, [FPC Escalation Report 28 July 2026, ENC 15b]

[WORTH WATCHING] BCHC expects c.£5m from a Birmingham and Solihull ICB investment in autism and ADHD pathways, and is asking the board to sign off outsourcing assessments — £815k of activity under a framework worth up to £5m.
[Evidence] "We understand that the Integrated Care Board approved a significant system-wide investment in Autism and ADHD pathways at their July Board meeting. [WORTH WATCHING] The trust's £-significant 0-19 children's contract (health visiting, school nursing, Birmingham Forward Steps) is out to competitive tender and expires on 28 February 2027 — rated 15 on the corporate risk register.
[Evidence] "There is a risk that the Trust may not be awarded the 0-19 contract (Health Visiting, School Nursing, BFS partnership) beyond the current contract which expires on 28.02.27. This would result in significant loss of income into the C&F Division, instability for the Division and it's workforce, and the reputational impact of losing a large community based service for children, young people and their families across Birmingham." — initial rating 20, current 15 — p.6, [Corporate Risk Register June 2026, risk 4211]. "We expect to hear the outcome of this tender process at some stage in the autumn." — p.53, [CEO Report, ENC 5]. [WORTH WATCHING] Cyber and digital infrastructure is the highest-rated risk on the trust's board assurance framework at 16, sitting above the board's agreed appetite, with legacy hardware out of support and untested disaster recovery listed as control gaps.
[Evidence] "BAF 26-12 - Absence of the required skills, knowledge, infrastructure and cyber protection as we increase our reliance on digital technology which may impact our ability to deliver services throughout the Trust (L4xC4 = 16)." Gaps in controls: "Insufficient cyber workforce capacity and specialist capability. Legacy infrastructure and hardware outside support lifecycle. Business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements not fully mature or tested. Privileged access and user permissions not fully optimised or regularly reviewed. Increasing external cyber threat landscape exceeding current resilience capability." — p.328 and p.355, [Board Assurance Framework 2026/27, ENC 21]
