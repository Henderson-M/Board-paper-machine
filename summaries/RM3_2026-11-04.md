## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has withheld NCA's Q3 deficit support funding, the trust's risk-assessed mid-case forecast is a £56.9m deficit, and it expects to need emergency cash support by December.
[Evidence] "Deficit Support Funding for Quarter 3 had been withheld. Release is reliant on delivering the agreed financial position for Month 7,8 and 9" and "The risk-assessed mid-case forecast stands £56.9m deficit. This requires a further £30.5m of Cost Improvement to be delivered in the remaining months of the year" — p.2, [Report from Finance and Performance Committee (26 October 2026)]. "Based on the current expenditure and capital forecast, additional cash support is expected to be required by December 2026, and an application has been prepared" — p.11, [Month 6 Finance Report]. The reported YTD position (£24.9m deficit, on plan) is "supported by £27.5m of non-recurrent mitigations"; "£52.4m — Deficit if mitigations excluded" — p.2, [Month 6 Finance Report]. The committee twice formally reported it "was not assured that the financial plan was deliverable" (28 Sept and 26 Oct reports).

[LEAD] Kevin McGee is interim chief executive from 1 October following Owen Williams' departure, and the substantive CEO recruitment has been paused — the latest in heavy board churn (acting CEO Suzanne Robinson has returned to CFO/deputy CEO, turnaround director Joe Gibson has left, NED Rachel Jones left 30 September, Bill Hall appointed NED).
[Evidence] "The Council received an update on the appointment of Kevin McGee OBE as Interim Chief Executive from 1 October 2026, following the departure of Owen Williams" and "the pausing of the Chief Executive recruitment to allow for further engagement and market testing" — pp.3 and 2, [Report from the Council of Governors]. McGee: "I am pleased to introduce my first Horizon Scan as Interim Chief Executive at NCA. These are early days" — p.2, [Chief Executive Horizon Scan]. The draft IIP states: "NCA has experienced significant Executive and senior leadership instability, affecting continuity, organisational alignment and the pace of delivery" — p.34, [Trust Integrated Improvement Plan draft, Appendix 1 to IIP paper].

[LEAD] NCA has been escalated into NHS England's elective recovery Tiering regime and has abandoned its 67% March RTT plan — forecasting 62%, a waiting list ~9,900 above plan and 3,932 over-52-week waiters against a plan of 3,131.
[Evidence] "Acute RTT is off plan: list size, 18-week performance and long waits. The NCA has entered Tiering for elective recovery" and "March: 62% (5 pp below plan) and PTL 129,991 (9,882 above plan). The original 67% March plan is not forecast to be achieved" — summary and trajectory slides, [Elective Recovery and RTT Performance Update]. Aug-26 actuals: 136,973 PTL; 55.8% within 18 weeks; 3,932 over 52 weeks (+801 vs plan); 286 over 65 weeks. "Six October industrial-action days are outside the forecast."

[LEAD] A baby died from Serratia marcescens during a declared infection outbreak on Royal Oldham's neonatal unit in Q2, in which seven babies tested positive for Serratia and six for Pseudomonas aeruginosa; the same report records a maternal death (thromboembolism, age 36).
[Evidence] "there was the declaration of an infection outbreak in the neonatal unit at the Royal Oldham Hospital as it was noted that seven baby's in total had been identified as having Serratia Marcescens and six reported as having positive laboratory results of pseudomonas aeruginosa. In total there was one baby who died as a result of Serratia Marcescens" — MOSS section, [MIS Year 8 Safety Action C — Learning from reviews and investigations, Q2 report, appendix to Perinatal Quality Surveillance Report]. Maternal death: "she passed away as a result of a Thromboembolism", aged 36, 8–10 weeks pregnant — same section.

[LEAD] External auditors Mazars found Value for Money "weaknesses relating to governance, service delivery and financial sustainability" in the 2025/26 accounts, signed off late (October) with an unqualified opinion on the financial statements.
[Evidence] "The External Auditor's (Mazars) confirmed that the Value for Money assessment identified weaknesses relating to governance, service delivery and financial sustainability. The Committee noted that whilst the report was challenging, it was a fair reflection of the Trust's ongoing organisational pressures" — p.2, [Report from the Audit and Risk Committee (extraordinary meeting) (23 October 2026)]. The revised Annual Governance Statement "explicitly recognises the significant operational control weaknesses in certain areas, particularly in: Quality governance, Risk management and Internal control arrangements" — p.2, same paper.

[LEAD] NCA is required to cut 2,416 whole-time equivalents over three years — while its workforce is currently 510 WTE ABOVE plan and the reduction is "off track and moving in the wrong direction".
[Evidence] "the workforce reduction of 2416 WTE's required of the Trust is further intensifying capacity pressures" with milestones "1008 wte – Mar 27; 811 wte - Mar 28; 597 wte - Mar 29" — Principal Risk 4, [Board Assurance Framework Q2 Position, Appendix One]. "Workforce remains 510 WTE above the M6 planned trajectory" — p.8, [Month 6 Finance Report]. People Committee: "the total reduction of Whole Time Equivalents (WTEs) required over the next 3 years was showing as in progress, however, this was off track and moving in the wrong direction" — p.3, [Report from People and Education Committee (October)].

[LEAD] The GMC and NHS England are to visit general surgery at Royal Oldham Hospital after training-survey results deteriorated, placing it in level 2 of the GMC's Intensive Support Framework.
[Evidence] "It was confirmed that NHS England and the GMC will be visiting General Surgery at Royal Oldham Hospital as its results had shown a deterioration and it was now in level 2 of the Intensive Support Framework" — p.3, [Report from People and Education Committee (October)].

[LEAD] The theatres pay dispute triggered a critical incident and the trust puts its cost at £1.2m direct plus up to £8.2m lost income; UNISON is recommending members reject the trust's "best offer" (12 months of enhanced NHS Professionals rates) and gave four further late-October strike dates. (Dispute already covered by HSJ — the critical incident declaration and £ figures are the new lift.)
[Evidence] "The recent theatres industrial action and subsequent harmonisation of rates of pay, which resulted in a critical incident, have significantly impacted elective activity and performance" — Principal Risk 10 rationale, [BAF Q2 Position]. "£1.2m direct cost; up to £8.2m lost income — Industrial action" — p.14 Key Risks table, [Month 6 Finance Report]. "We have made our best offer: enhanced NHS Professionals rates for eligible additional voluntary theatre shifts for a fixed 12-month period" — p.4, [Chief Executive Horizon Scan]. "Members are being asked to vote on the offer, and they are recommending members to reject the offer" — p.4, [Industrial Action Position Update]. 12 maternity "delay in treatment" incidents in Q2 were "directly attributable to the industrial action"; 5 more were cancelled elective caesareans — [MIS Safety Action C Q2 report].

[WORTH WATCHING] In private session on 2 September the board decided NOT to seek Real Living Wage Foundation accreditation and to stay on national Agenda for Change rates — during a live pay dispute; the F&P Committee recorded the recommendation was "not unanimously supported".
[Evidence] "Decision not to seek Real Living Wage Foundation accreditation" and "Decision to stay aligned to nationally agreed Agenda for Change rates in relation to the Real Living Wage" — p.1, [Board of Directors' Closed Session Information Report]. "The recommendation concerning continuation of payment above nationally agreed Agenda for Change rates was not unanimously supported" — p.3, [Report from Finance & Performance Committee (1 September 2026)].

[WORTH WATCHING] The electronic prescribing system (Medchart EPMA) failed in Q2 — there is currently no EPMA at Bury, Rochdale or Oldham — while the trust's single-EPR business case has been knocked back regionally on financial viability.
[Evidence] "the Trust experienced failure of the Electronic Prescribing and Medicines Administration (EPMA) system, highlighting the fragility of a number of critical clinical systems" — p.5, [BAF Q2 Position]; "there is currently no available EPMA across Bury, Rochdale or Oldham sites, which in addition to greater patient safety risk, is increasing the time taken to complete discharges" — Principal Risk 9 rationale, same paper. "The Committee noted that the EPR Outline Business Case had not progressed to national submission following regional feedback regarding financial viability" — p.3, [Report from Finance & Performance Committee (1 September 2026)]. Twelve of the 14 BAF principal risks are scored 20.

[WORTH WATCHING] Category 1 emergency caesarean 30-minute compliance fell to 45% in August, and the maternity governance team twice learned of deaths via MBRRACE-UK rather than internal reporting.
[Evidence] "August: 31 Category 1 caesarean sections were performed, with 14 completed within 30 minutes (45% compliance)" — p.8, [MOSS Critical Safety Check (drill version), appendix to Perinatal Quality Surveillance Report]. "On two occasions, the Maternity Governance Team became aware of deaths through MBRRACE-UK rather than via internal reporting processes. These cases involved a neonatal death following antenatal care at The Royal Oldham Hospital and a maternal death" — [MIS Safety Action C Q2 report]. BAF risk 7 rationale: "There has been increase in still birth rate and there is variation in this across the service" — [BAF Q2 Position].

[WORTH WATCHING] Service reconfiguration moves: adult social care services to transfer to Salford City Council (business transfer agreement recommended to board); breast services planned to transfer to MFT; consultation under way with MFT on GM-wide vascular services.
[Evidence] "The Committee supported the Adult Social Care Business Transfer Agreement and recommended it to Board for approval" — p.4, [Report from Finance & Performance Committee (1 September 2026)]. "the planned transfer of Breast Services to MFT is expected to release estate capacity" and "consultation underway with MFT on delivery of Vascular services across GM with the potential to release ward estate on the Oldham site" — Principal Risk 9 mitigations, [BAF Q2 Position].

[FOI] "Drivers of Deficit" diagnostic — request from Northern Care Alliance FT: the full Drivers of Deficit diagnostic report (and any external support/consultancy engaged to produce it), referenced as underpinning the 2026/27 financial position but never published.
[Evidence] "The Trust entered 2026/27 with a materially challenged underlying financial position, consistent with findings from the Drivers of Deficit diagnostic" — Principal Risk 13 rationale, [BAF Q2 Position].

[FOI] Spinal services external review — request from NCA: terms of reference, scope and final report of the external review of spinal services, plus the mediation arrangements. The pack confirms the review has started but gives no findings.
[Evidence] "commencement of an external review. Mediation and engagement activity was underway to address cultural concerns" — p.3, [Report from Quality Committee (September)]; committee recorded only "limited assurance... regarding cultural improvement, staff engagement, capacity pressures", with "psychological safety, ongoing disengagement within the service, deteriorating waiting times and increasing patient concerns" — p.2, same paper.

[FOI] NHSE paperwork approved/received in private — request from NCA: (a) the Provider Capability self-assessment approved in private on 29 September; (b) NHSE's formal regional feedback on the EPR outline business case; (c) the joint stakeholder readout of the 5 October System Improvement Board.
[Evidence] "Provider Capability self-assessment" approved at the 29 September private board — p.1, [Closed Session Information Report]; "Formal feedback is awaited" on the EPR OBC — p.3, [Report from Finance & Performance Committee (1 September 2026)]; "The joint stakeholder readout following the System Improvement Board (SIB) on 5 October highlights the need for stronger alignment between the IIP, evidence requirements and criteria for demonstrating sustainable improvement" — p.4, [Chief Executive Horizon Scan].
