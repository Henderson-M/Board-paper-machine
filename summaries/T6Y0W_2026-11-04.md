# Norfolk and Suffolk ICB board (special meeting) — 4 November 2026

- **Source:** https://www.norfolkandsuffolk.icb.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261104-Agenda-and-Reports.pdf (combined pack); public Q&A at https://www.norfolkandsuffolk.icb.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Public-questions-received-for-the-4-November-ICB-Board.pdf
- **Pack:** 3 files (agenda .docx, combined agenda-and-reports PDF, public questions PDF)
- **Analysed:** 2026-11-05T15:15:12+00:00

Single-item special meeting, held online at 4.30pm, chaired by Will Pope. The only substantive item is the Norfolk and Waveney University Hospitals Group (NWUHG — NNUH, QEH King's Lynn and James Paget) "Case for Change" for acute reconfiguration, presented by deputy CEO Richard Watson and Amanda Lyes. The ICB is asked to agree the case is "sufficiently compelling" and task its Executive Committee with commissioning an ICB-led Pre-Consultation Business Case (PCBC). The pack carries the Group's two documents in full (Part 1 Case for Change, 29 Oct 2026, ~85pp; Part 2 Scenario Appraisal, ~77pp) plus a Healthwatch Norfolk interim slide deck. The Group Board considered the same papers on 2 November. No attendance/apologies list in the pack; no board-level appointment or departure signals found.

Page references below are the internal page numbers of each appendix document, not the combined-PDF page.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Norfolk's hospital group is asking its ICB to open the door to a reconfiguration whose shortlisted scenarios include closing the delivery suites, neonatal units and inpatient paediatrics at both QEH King's Lynn and James Paget, leaving NNUH as the sole maternity site.
[Evidence] "Scenario C: one maternity site (NNUH) — Delivery suites, neonatal units, inpatient paediatrics: NNUH … 24/7 emergency surgery: NNUH only … Remaining structural deficit (from £34m): Gap closed (~£2m surplus)" — p.9, Table 2 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. "The CAG did not reach a consensus to rule out any scenario, so the recommendation is to take all four forward for continued modelling. The one site scenario would deliver the highest standard of care for women with complex births, providing mitigations to access can be developed." — p.7 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. Driver: "The CAG agreed that a sustainable delivery suite requires around 3,000 deliveries a year … Only NNUH's delivery suite is projected to be of a sustainable size by 2034/35. QEH and JPUH would each perform fewer than four deliveries a day" — p.7; projected 2034/35 deliveries QEH 1,347, JPUH 1,105 — p.46, Table 10. ICB recommendation wording: "Agree that the Case for Change provides sufficient evidence to justify the ICB commissioning work to develop and assess options for acute hospital service changes … This does not approve a preferred service configuration or authorise formal public consultation" — p.1 [ICB cover paper, Item 3].

[LEAD] The Group says it will still carry a £53m recurrent structural deficit in 2034/35 even if it delivers a £263m three-year savings plan in full — and that on that basis the New Hospital Programme business cases for QEH and James Paget are unlikely to be approved.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, our deficit before deficit support funding was £51m, compared with £31m in 2024/25. The MTFP projects that we will reduce the deficit to £5m by 2028/29. This depends on delivering £263m of cost improvements over three years, equivalent to an average of 5.0% of gross expenditure each year against our historical average of 3.9%. It also assumes that 85% (£223m) of the savings will be recurrent, compared with 56% historically." — p.36 [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. "It leaves the Group with a large recurrent structural deficit of £53m. This means that it is unlikely any business case submitted for the New Hospitals at Queen Elizabeth and James Paget will be approved." — p.16 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. "Reconfiguration of both unplanned care and women's and children's services is needed to address the structural deficit." — p.8 [Appendix 2].

[LEAD] The £2.8bn QEH and James Paget rebuilds face a delay of at least six months and a provisional £140m–£354m extra capital requirement if the reconfiguration goes to consultation; the Group's modelled opening dates now run from Q3 2033 to 2035.
[Evidence] "Our current plan is to submit Outline Business Cases for the two new hospitals in October 2027. If a service proposal requires public consultation, the business cases for the new hospitals will have to follow the decision made after that process. On the present timetable, our earliest planning assumption would move submission to around March 2028" — p.3, Foreword [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. "We have modelled three possible dates for submission of revised Outline Business Cases: April 2028 in the best case, July 2028 in the middle case and September 2028 in the worst case … potential hospital opening dates include Q3 2033, Q3 2034 and 2035." — p.56 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. "A later submission would add construction-cost inflation, at several million pounds per month each hospital scheme is delayed." — p.56. "Working with the national New Hospital Programme team, we estimate an additional capital requirement of £140 million to £225 million for scenarios retaining two birth suites, and £263 million to £354 million for scenarios with one birth suite." — p.56. "If these changes result in a public consultation, there will be a delay of a minimum of 6 months to the submission of the Outline Business Cases … This would mean that they open in 2034." — p.8. [Note: the foreword says "around March 2028"; the appraisal's best case is April 2028 — minor internal inconsistency.]

[LEAD] The Group's own paper lays out how badly QEH and James Paget score under NHSE's oversight framework: QEH is 132nd of 134 trusts, bottom of the country on staff raising concerns and NHS staff standards, with a Red NHSE capability rating; QEH staff were least likely of 121 acute organisations to recommend their own hospital's care.
[Evidence] "James Paget University Hospitals and The Queen Elizabeth Hospital King's Lynn are in segment 4 of the NHS National Oversight Framework, ranked 125 and 132 respectively out of 134 trusts." — p.19 [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. QEH: "Staff advocacy: 133rd of 134 trusts … Staff raising concerns: 134th of 134 trusts … NHS staff standards: 134th of 134 trusts … Staff engagement: 133rd of 134 trusts … NHS England capability rating: Red"; JPUH: "NHS England capability rating: Amber/Red" — p.20, Table 1. "In the 2025 NHS Staff Survey, only 34.7% of QEH respondents, 50.4% of NNUH respondents, and 60.6% of JPUH respondents said they would be happy with the standard of care if a friend or relative needed treatment. On this metric, QEH recorded the lowest result among the 121 organisations in the acute and acute-and-community benchmarking group." — p.22. Also: "At JPUH, Board reporting recorded 399 nursing 'red flags', the majority associated with staffing shortfalls" — p.22. (Rankings are the Group's statements of NHSE data; the reporter holds the comparative dataset.)

[LEAD] The recommended "unselected medical take" model would strip stroke, cardiac arrest, GI bleed, complex trauma and interventional radiology from QEH and James Paget and move ~22 patients a day to NNUH — but around two-thirds of diverted QEH patients would by default go to Peterborough, outside the Group, so the Group is proposing to change ambulance conveyance rules to keep them in Norfolk.
[Evidence] "Unselected take, 11 patients a day would transfer from each of QEH and JPUH – 22 patients in total … of the QEH transfers, around three would go to NNUH and eight to hospitals outside the Group (largely Peterborough)." — p.42 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. "QEH: ~29% of QEH patients remain within the Group and transfer to NNUH, ~67% to Peterborough, ~3% go to Addenbrookes Hospital, and ~1% to West Suffolk Hospital" — p.41. "54 of 92 LSOAs that are closer to Peterborough than Norwich, are less than 10 minutes further from Norwich than Peterborough … If conveyances from these LSOAs were redirected to Norwich rather than Peterborough, it would increase the share of QEH patients going to NNUH from around 29% to 63%, and it would reduce the impact on Peterborough to less than 5 patients a day. This mitigation would need to be fully assessed by East of England Ambulance Service." — p.44. The "selected medical take" (no ED at QEH/JPUH, 47 transfers/day, saving £32m of the £34m gap) was rejected by clinicians: "its benefits do not outweigh the negative impact on patient access, neighbouring systems and the ambulance service" — p.6. Savings: unselected take ~£18m/yr; two-site maternity £6m–£8m; single site £18m — p.8.

[LEAD] Eleven services across the three hospitals are formally classed as "fragile": paediatric audiology at QEH has been paused with a five-year patient recall under way after children were given "inappropriate amplification", and the Group's paediatric cystic fibrosis service has lost both specialist consultants and no longer meets its national service specification.
[Evidence] "Paediatric audiology is provided at all three sites. It is considered fragile because of capability and clinical-quality concerns at QEH, where the service was paused, and a five-year patient recall is under way after some children were found to have received inappropriate amplification … NHSE is already conducting an NHSE regional-review" — p.60–61 [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. "Paediatric cystic fibrosis … is considered fragile because both specialist consultants have left and the service no longer meets the relevant specialist consultant requirement. The service has to meet a nationally mandated service specification from Specialised Commissioning; as it does not currently meet this specification, urgent work to find a solution in combination with commissioners is required." — p.60. "Paediatric respiratory medicine … consultant capacity has fallen from three posts to one" — p.62. Oncology: "a substantial consultant establishment gap, which is most acute at QEH where cover has been arranged on a short-term basis" — p.62. Spinal surgery JPUH waiting list "rising from 82 to 187 and the median wait from 9 to 22 weeks"; clinical haematology QEH list "rising from 50 to 192 and the median wait from 5.5 to 17 weeks" — p.61–62. "We have identified 11 services that are deemed fragile and in need of immediate further action." — p.59.

[WORTH WATCHING] NNUH's PFI cost £94.1m in 2025/26 and the Group is openly pointing to "precedents elsewhere" for taking parts of a PFI estate back before the 2037 break clause; QEH spent £29.3m on RAAC failsafes in a single year.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, the unitary payment to the PFI operator was £94.1 million … The PFI agreement makes provision for the Trust to exercise a break clause to end the agreement in 2037. A programme is underway to improve the condition of the site ahead of the contract ending. There are precedents elsewhere in the NHS for negotiating with PFI providers to return parts of an estate to NHS control before the full contract expires, enabling NHS capital to be invested in replacement or improved facilities. Any similar approach would require detailed legal, commercial and value-for-money assessment." — p.38 [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. "In 2025/26 alone, we spent £29.3 million on the RAAC failsafe programme." — p.37. PFI operator named as Octagon Healthcare — p.38. [INFERENCE] The single-maternity-site scenario requires an NNUH extension on PFI land ("effects on parking and the PFI estate, require further feasibility work" — p.56, Appendix 2), so the PFI question is live to the reconfiguration.

[WORTH WATCHING] The new QEH and James Paget could each be built two 32-bed wards smaller — "one floor" shorter — on the strength of "neighbourhood max" modelling the Group itself says is "the top of the evidence" and rests 88% on four interventions.
[Evidence] "Under the 'neighbourhood max' model, the projected reduction in inpatient demand could remove two 32-bed wards from each new hospital. That is broadly equivalent to reducing the height of one wing of each hospital by one floor." — p.55 [Appendix 2: Scenario Appraisal]. "neighbourhood health could take out about 93,900 bed days a year, roughly 280 beds at 92% occupancy … The figures are the top of the evidence, assume full delivery by 2034/35, and rest on four interventions that provide 88% of the figure." — p.4. "We have not modelled the workforce, funding, estate, digital or social care capacity needed outside hospital." — p.75, caveat 21. "None of the 43 places selected for the first wave of the National Neighbourhood Health Implementation Programme (NNHIP) are in Norfolk and Waveney." — p.25 [Appendix 1]. The Group's net "Neighbourhood Max" saving is £25m: £118m cost out, £93m income forgone — p.19, Table 4 [Appendix 2].

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB's own cover paper admits it may not have the capacity or expertise to run the PCBC, and flags predetermination and legal-challenge risks; campaign group "Joan's Army" and a King's Lynn borough councillor have already lodged public questions.
[Evidence] "Capacity and affordability: There is a risk that the ICB do not have sufficient capacity or specialist capability to deliver an assurance-ready PCBC. A costed programme, clear accountabilities and dedicated resource arrangements will be required before commencement of substantive development of the PCBC." — p.4 [ICB cover paper, Item 3]. "Predetermination: There is a risk that progression is interpreted as support for a particular configuration." "Statutory and legal challenge: There is a risk of challenge if involvement, equality, scrutiny or decision-making duties are not discharged at the correct stages." — p.4. "Questions 1–4 from Joan's Army"; "Questions 5–9 from Cllr Tom Ryves, Borough Council of King's Lynn & West Norfolk" — p.1–2 [Public questions received for the 4 November ICB Board]. The ICB confirms "The timetable for subsequent stages is still being developed" — Q5 answer. [INFERENCE] "Costed programme … dedicated resource arrangements" implies external PCBC support will be procured — see FOI below.

[FOI] Healthwatch Norfolk's engagement report — the ICB is deciding before it exists, and the final version will only be published after the Group has issued a "formal response". Request from NWUHG and the ICB: the interim findings "already shared with the Hospital Group", the final report (due January 2027) and the Group's formal response.
[Evidence] "Although the final Healthwatch report will not be available before the Boards consider the Case for Change, early findings have already been shared with the Hospital Group." — Q4 answer [Public questions]. "The report will be shared with the Hospital Group in January 2027. Once a formal response has been provided, the report will be shared with the wider public." — final slide [Appendix 3: Healthwatch Norfolk Interim Report 29/10/2026]. Survey n=4,454; "67% of respondents were reporting on the Queen Elizabeth Hospital, Kings Lynn" — demographics slide. Emerging theme 4 is "Lack of trust in decision makers".

[FOI] The Royal College of Surgeons' 2025 review of general surgery at QEH and NHS England's Rapid Quality Review of the same service are cited but not published. Request from QEH (and NHSE East of England): the RCS invited review report, its terms of reference and action plan; the RQR summary and outcome letter.
[Evidence] "The Royal College of Surgeons' 2025 review of General Surgery at QEH found that the service had received no Deanery surgical trainees during the preceding year because of the reported quality of the training experience. The review heard that trainees had insufficient access to operative experience and training … In some cases, trainee involvement had been reduced because of concern about how complications would be judged." — p.35 [Appendix 1: Case for Change]. "NHS England has also previously convened a Rapid Quality Review focused on general surgery. This reflects the seriousness of the concerns about that service and the need for further scrutiny of its quality, safety and governance." — p.22. Also: NHSE regional review of paediatric audiology (p.61) — request its scope and findings, plus the number of children recalled.

[FOI] NHS England's "strategic sense check" of the Case for Change and the ICB's PCBC commissioning arrangements. Request from the ICB: the submission to NHSE and NHSE's response; the "costed mobilisation plan" (scope, timetable, resource model, funding source) going to the next board; and any contract, tender or engagement letter for external advisers supporting the PCBC, with value and firm.
[Evidence] "Subject to Board approval, the Case for Change will be shared with NHS England for a strategic sense check." — p.2 [ICB cover paper]. "develop and approve a costed mobilisation plan setting out the detailed scope, methodology, timetable, resource model, funding source and impact on other ICB priorities before substantive options appraisal begins" — p.4. "The Executive Committee will bring an update to the next Board meeting on the PCBC mobilisation plan and commissioning arrangements, including more detailed timescales." — p.3. Next public board: 16 December 2026 (online) — agenda. No external firm is named anywhere in the pack for either the Case for Change or the PCBC. [INFERENCE] The scale of the modelling (30 interventions, 175 sources, top-down financial model "developed with the NWUHG finance team") and the ICB's stated capability gap make external support likely; worth asking who did the work.

## Other datapoints worth having to hand

- Maternity: "In 2024/25, the Queen Elizabeth and James Paget were two of only 12 hospitals in England with fewer than 2,000 annual births." — p.3 [Appendix 1]. "the number of non-assisted vaginal births would fall to an average of around 1.3 a day at QEH and 0.7 a day at JPUH" — p.34. Amos investigation: QEH "adjusted neonatal mortality rate of 0.8 per 1,000 live births in the 12 months ending October 2024, higher than comparable trusts" — p.24. Three trusts' maternity CNST contributions "totalled £14.7 million" in 2024/25 — p.25.
- Scenario B2 (NNUH + JPUH, closing QEH maternity) moves ~3,250 women a year, including ~350 QEH births leaving the Group — p.7 [Appendix 2]. Scenario C moves ~2,450 — p.7.
- Travel if QEH services unavailable: median public transport journey 56→98 minutes; "Twenty-six of the 135 LSOAs would have journeys exceeding two hours, and Addenbrooke's would be the best available public-transport destination for 71 areas" — p.53 [Appendix 1]. Ambulance: "92 would be assigned to Peterborough, compared with 37 to NNUH" — p.51.
- Elective: "QEH and JPUH elective inpatient activity is being consolidated at NNUH, but JPUH retains inpatient joint replacement surgery (T&O)" — p.6 [Appendix 2]; "The planned care model does not deliver any savings relative to the 'Do Minimum'." — p.6.
- Flow, August 2026: ~191 patients/day no longer meeting criteria to reside (72 JPUH, 66 NNUH, 53 QEH); 662 twelve-hour trolley waits after DTA (373 JPUH, 151 QEH, 138 NNUH) — p.25 [Appendix 1].
- "Do Minimum" requires ~15 additional ED consultants, 3 surgical registrars, ~10 WTE obstetric residents, ~18 WTE midwives/nurses, ~10 WTE paeds/neonatal nurses — p.17 [Appendix 2].
- Group's earlier work "in early 2026 … identified five broad scenarios … ranging from retaining all core acute services at all three sites to consolidating them at a single site" but "did not provide … the necessary clinical engagement" — p.32 [Appendix 2].
- ICB's own future dates: 16 Dec 2026 (online), 27 Jan 2027 (Dereham), 24 Mar 2027 (King's Lynn Town Hall) — agenda.
