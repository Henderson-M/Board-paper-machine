## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust ranked sixth-worst provider in England for community waits over 52 weeks, at 51% against a national median of 0.3% — mostly children waiting for paediatric autism/ADHD assessment.
[Evidence] "In the latest NOF ratings (Q4) the Trust ranked sixth worst NHS provider in the country for waits over 52 weeks, at 51%. The national median was just 0.3%. From internal data the May position has deteriorated to 51%… The majority of the long waits are for Community Paediatric ASD assessment and/or attention deficit hyperactive disorder (ADHD) assessment. Demand over the past six years has been as high as 450% above capacity and remains to date at 250% above." — p.50, [Integrated Performance Report, Operational Performance]

[LEAD] CYP mental health waits over 104 weeks jumped to 1,072 at end of May against an NHSE-mandated trajectory of 104, after neurodevelopmental waits were transferred in.
[Evidence] "At the end of May 2026, the number of CYP waiting over 104 weeks was 1,072, above the operational plan trajectory of 104. The trajectory is particularly challenging as it was set based on a baseline mandated by NHS England prior to the ASD and ADHD waits being transferred over from the CSDS." — p.59, [IPR, Transformation and Improvement] (internal CYP MH figure excluding ND is 50 children — same page; p.37 cites 1,300 once ND counted)

[LEAD] Trust has commissioned Advancing Quality Alliance (AQUA) as a "critical friend" external review against CQC Well-Led standards — its first Well-Led assessment since 2020; findings will set the trigger points for escalating risks to the board.
[Evidence] "Commissioning of AQUA as a critical friend review, providing independent challenge and supporting the Trust… Given that the Trust's last Well-Led inspection was undertaken in 2020, there is recognition that a further assessment may be due." — p.106, [Fundamental Standards of Care]. Action tracker: board will review escalation parameters "in conjunction with the Well-Led review work", due 30 Sep 2026 — p.22, [Action Matrix]. AQUA director Cath Hill attended the 19 May board — p.8, [Draft Minutes].

[LEAD] Derby and Derbyshire ICB has withdrawn funding for the trust's Suicide Prevention Lead post at a mental health trust; the post-holder is also departing and the trust is scrambling to sustain the function.
[Evidence] "The ICB's decision to withdraw funding for the Suicide Prevention Lead post, with the Trust working to sustain the essential functions." — p.76, [Quality and Safeguarding Committee assurance summary]. Also "the planned departure of the suicide prevention lead" — p.43, [IPR Quality]; "The loss of the Suicide Prevention Lead was noted, acknowledging a review being undertaken to establish how actions from the Suicide Prevention Plan will be sustained" — p.78, [Q&S Committee summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] Adult acute out-of-area placements overspent by £8.3m in 2025/26 — £4.4m above plan — and remain the single biggest risk to this year's finances.
[Evidence] "An overspend in Adult Acute OoA placements was reported, totalling £8.3m, which is £4.4m above plan. This was identified as a key area for review." — p.14, [Draft Minutes, Finance]. Current year: adjusted deficit of £0.9m at Month 2, £0.3m behind plan, "principally driven by higher than planned Adult Acute out of area (OoA) placement expenditure" — p.87, [Strategy Deep Dive – Productive]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust told NHSE its own three-year length-of-stay target is undeliverable — board formally recorded the Year 3 submission as "known and agreed as unrealistic at the time".
[Evidence] Medium-Term Plan "had been accepted, it was conditional upon the Trust meeting the specified target for LoS. James acknowledged that further improvement would be required by Year 3, characterising the targets as 'very ambitious'." — p.15, [Draft Minutes]; RESOLVED "Noted the non-compliance regarding Year 3 LoS submission, which was known and agreed as unrealistic at the time" — p.15

[WORTH WATCHING] CEO voiced "continued discomfort" that the trust runs no standalone Assertive Outreach service, citing a ~£2m funding gap — a risk tied to CQC's Section 48 findings and being re-escalated to the ICB.
[Evidence] "Mark expressed continued discomfort about the current service model… the AO service does not operate as a standalone outreach model, which he considered a significant risk due to lack of funding (estimated at around £2m)." — p.17, [Draft Minutes]; self-assessment against the Midlands Intensive and AO tool linked to "CQC's findings under Section 48 of the Health and Social Care Act" — p.17

[WORTH WATCHING] Assaults on staff by patients deteriorated across the quarter to 43 in May (target 24), with racist abuse of staff flagged as a persistent theme; prone restraint remains above the zero target.
[Evidence] "Assaults on staff by patients were 29 in March; 33 in April and 43 in May against the 10% reduction ambition of 24.2… All three months were above threshold, with a deteriorating trajectory across the period." — p.43; "given the continued issue of racist abuse in patient-to-staff incidents" — p.43; prone restraint "three incidents in March; 11 in April and 10 in May" against a target of zero — p.42, [IPR Quality]

[WORTH WATCHING] Some patient-safety learning responses have been outstanding for up to 700 days, and the trust is carrying 37 overdue risks.
[Evidence] "challenges remain around overdue learning responses, with some cases outstanding for 700 days, particularly in Learning Disability (LD) and Substance Misuse services." — p.77; "the 37 overdue risks are being actively followed up by the triumvirate" — p.77, [Q&S Committee assurance summary]
