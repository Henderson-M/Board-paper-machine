# Lancashire and South Cumbria ICB (QE1) — Board pack, 9 July 2026

Analysis for HSJ (correspondent: Zoe). Public Part 1 pack, ~21 itemised PDFs. Papers reviewed in full: Finance Performance Report M2 (Item 12), Finance & Contracting Committee Escalation (Item 11), CEO report (Item 7), Quality & Outcomes Escalation (Item 8), Integrated Performance Report (Item 10), Matters in Private (Item 20), IUC Case for Change (Item 14), Chair's report (Item 6). Note: this is a strategic-commissioner ICB, so system finance, running-cost/commissioning-cost cuts, redundancies, provider oversight and commissioning change are the core signals.

## 1. Top lines

**LEAD — LSC ICB needs to strip out c.£215m of costs in 2026/27 to hold a £34.9m planned deficit, with £25m of the savings rated high-risk at month 2.**
"The ICB's 26/27 plan requires a minimum delivery of c.£215m cost mitigations 2026/27 to achieve the required financial position (resulting in a £34.9m deficit as per NHSE control total which would attract deficit support funding)... The mitigation plan comprises three primary components:(WRP c.£127m / Organisational Change (c.£36m) and Other Mitigations (c£52m)". Of the residual programme "£78m low risk, £68m medium risk, £25m high risk". — p.1 and p.7, [Finance Performance Report – Month 2, Item 12]

**LEAD — LSC system closed 2025/26 only by deploying £164m of deficit support funding, plus a further £11.5m one-off DSF at year end.**
"The LSCICB system has delivered break even to plan (£164m deficit) with a £9.7m surplus in the ICB, offsetting a £9.7m deficit in provider trusts. This is after deploying £164m of deficit support funding (DSF) agreed at plan. There was a further receipt of DSF amounting to £11.5m as a one off adjustment at year end." — p.4, [Finance & Contracting Committee Escalation, Item 11]

**LEAD — Second voluntary redundancy scheme: 121 applications, ~50 staff leaving the ICB by end of July, as ICB restructures to cut commissioning costs; £15.8m of VR savings already banked from the first-round exits.**
"In May, we launched a second voluntary redundancy scheme and received 121 applications... We expect approximately 50 colleagues will be leaving the ICB on this second scheme by the end of July. This has remained an important step in reducing the need for compulsory redundancy." Organisational-change programme "targeted to deliver the resource allocation adjustment of £36m with £24.24m currently identified... voluntary redundancy savings realized in M1 (£15.80m)". — CEO p.4, [Item 7]; and p.7–8, [Finance Performance Report M2, Item 12]

**LEAD — LSC ICB confirmed as host of the North West Office for Pan-ICB Commissioning (OPIC); will take on specialised commissioning, health & justice, and vaccination/screening functions transferring from NHSE from April 2027.**
"In the North West, NHS Lancashire and South Cumbria (LSC) ICB has been collectively agreed as the host organisation for OPIC, working in full partnership with NHS Greater Manchester (GM) ICB and NHS Cheshire and Merseyside (CM) ICB, which was formalised in May this year... A 'shadow' OPIC is planned to be in place by autumn 2026." — CEO p.9, [Item 7]. F&C committee "requested further details as to the financial implications of these decisions and any associated impact on the costs or running the Lancashire and South Cumbria ICB." — p.5, [Item 11]

**LEAD — ICB is operating under NHSE enforcement undertakings; private board is tracking a "dashboard of compliance against the specific enforcement undertakings."**
Private board on 14 May 2026 "Noted a report which provided Board with an update on progress on two pieces of assurance work; the completion of the System Delivery Meeting / Improvement and Assurance Group actions from the March and April 2026 cycles of meetings and a dashboard of compliance against the specific enforcement undertakings." Further "Noted an update on the progress against the ICB's enforcement undertakings" (4 June 2026). — p.3–4, [Matters in Private, Item 20]

**LEAD — Continuing healthcare (AACC) in trouble: overdue reviews up 31%, fast-track backlog growing, financial risk despite headcount cuts — escalated to the board twice; a formal "AACC turnaround plan" is being written into the Board Assurance Framework.**
"There has been a notable deterioration in continuing healthcare review timeliness with overdue reviews increasing by 31%... early signs of financial risk despite headcount reductions." Board Assurance Framework action: "Financial sustainability risk within the BAF to be strengthened, ensuring clearer alignment to the AACC turnaround plan." The ICB "is a national outlier in both monthly CHC eligibility rates and eligibility per 50k population, with almost double the rate seen nationally." — p.3–7, [Finance & Contracting Escalation, Item 11]; IPR p.2 and p.12, [Item 10]

**WORTH WATCHING — Board-level system finance risk still flagged as "high risk" at this early stage; a Q1 re-forecast at end of July could move the position off plan.**
"The delivery of all of these cost savings at this early stage should be considered high risk and will be considered alongside a further year end re-forecast at the next meeting." "The system has agreed to undertake a quarter 1 risk review... at the end of July." — p.4, [Item 11]; p.9, [Item 12]

**WORTH WATCHING — Two non-executives (deputy chair / NED) departed in June; replacements imminent.**
"A robust process has supported the recruitment of a deputy chair and non-executive member for the ICB following Sheena Cumiskey and Roy Fisher leaving the ICB last month... I expect to be able to announce those who have been successful in the coming days." — Chair p.4, [Item 6]

**WORTH WATCHING — Major service change: IUC (integrated urgent care) redesign — ICB seeking board approval that a "like-for-like re-procurement is not viable", replacing 12 legacy contracts from 8 providers.**
"Current IUC services are fragmented, inequitable and no longer fit for purpose. Twelve legacy contracts delivered by eight providers have resulted in significant variation in access, quality, digital maturity, workforce models and cost." Stage one reprocures West Lancashire IUC (excl GP out of hours); existing providers direct-awarded to 31 March 2027. — p.1–2, [IUC Case for Change, Item 14]

**WORTH WATCHING — Elective/diagnostics deterioration: 229,313 on the waiting list; diagnostic 6-week performance fell to 77.3% (target 99%); 62-day cancer 63% (target 85%) with no provider hitting the 62-day target.**
"229,313 patients were waiting for treatment across the ICB"; diagnostics deteriorated to 77.3%, "58,018 patients waiting... an increase of... 12.96% from the beginning of the financial year"; cancer 62-day "64.4%, below the planning trajectory of 75.3%. None of the four main providers achieved the target." — IPR p.1, p.6, p.8, [Item 10]

## 2. FOI leads

- **NHSE enforcement undertakings.** The pack confirms LSC ICB is subject to "specific enforcement undertakings" with a compliance dashboard, but does not disclose their content. Request from LSC ICB (and/or NHSE North West): the enforcement undertakings served on the ICB, the letter/notice imposing them, the scope, and the latest compliance dashboard. This is the single strongest withheld item in the pack. (Ref: Matters in Private, Item 20, p.3–4.)
- **OPIC financial impact on the ICB.** The F&C committee has explicitly asked for "the financial implications" and "impact on the costs or running the Lancashire and South Cumbria ICB" of hosting the North West OPIC — "Detail to be presented to a future committee meeting." Request the OPIC business case / financial modelling and the North West OPIC Programme Development Board papers. (Ref: Item 11, p.5.)
- **VR settlement costs.** The private board on 4 June 2026 "approved the cost of approved voluntary redundancy applications" — figure not disclosed. Request the total cost of the two 2026 VR schemes, number of exits by band, and any senior exit packages. (Ref: Item 20, p.4; ~50 leavers on scheme two, £15.8m banked from scheme one.)
- **NED departures.** Sheena Cumiskey and Roy Fisher left the ICB in June with no reason given. Request any correspondence relating to their departures and whether either was linked to the enforcement/turnaround position. (Ref: Chair, Item 6, p.4.)
- **£25m high-risk / £20.5m high-risk "other mitigations".** Request the scheme-level detail behind the high-risk savings lines (TFIG papers) — these are the schemes most likely to slip and tip the system off plan. (Ref: Item 12, p.7–9.)
- **Preston Davey safeguarding.** CEO notes two Lancashire MPs (Chris Webb, Andrew Snowden) have called for a public inquiry into possible safeguarding failures linked to the death of 13-month-old Preston Davey; statutory safeguarding procedures initiated. Request the safeguarding practice review / any published findings via the Safeguarding Children Arrangements. (Ref: CEO, Item 7, p.10 — handle as accountability, not patient-story.)

## 5. Pack assessment

**Not routine — a strong, story-rich pack.** Multiple publishable leads: the £215m cost-mitigation ask and £34.9m planned deficit for 26/27; the £164m + £11.5m deficit support funding that held 25/26; a second round of voluntary redundancies (~50 more exits, £15.8m already banked); confirmation LSC is the North West OPIC host taking on NHSE direct-commissioning functions from April 2027; and — most notably — confirmation the ICB is under NHSE enforcement undertakings with a compliance dashboard (withheld, strong FOI). Supporting operational deterioration (CHC/AACC outlier and 31% review backlog growth, diagnostics and 62-day cancer misses) and a major IUC service-change case for change. NED churn (Cumiskey, Fisher) adds a leadership angle. The enforcement undertakings and OPIC financial-impact FOIs are the highest-value follow-ups.
