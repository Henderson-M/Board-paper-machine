# Doncaster and Bassetlaw Teaching Hospitals NHS FT — Board (public), 7 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.dbth.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Public-Board-of-Directors-7-July-2026-v2.pdf
- **Pack:** Public Board of Directors, 7 July 2026 (v2) — 397 pages
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-06 (ODS RP5)

Context: DBTH remains under interim leadership (Interim Chair Mark Bailey, Acting CEO Zara Jones) following the well-led / culture review that HSJ reported in January 2026 ("'Disconnected' staff report bullying and blame culture"). This pack confirms the retirement of substantive CEO Richard Parker OBE, sets out a conditional NHSE deficit-support regime, and shows post-review culture metrics still moving the wrong way.

## Top lines

**[LEAD]** Richard Parker OBE has retired as chief executive after ~44 years in the NHS and almost a decade leading DBTH, leaving the trust with an acting CEO and no announced recruitment timeline.
[Evidence] "In June we announced the retirement of Richard Parker OBE, the Trust's Chief Executive… We will set out our next steps in terms of Chief Executive recruitment in due course, and in the meantime, I want to place on record my thanks to Zara, for her stewardship of the organisation as Acting Chief Executive." — p.17–18, [B1 Chair's Report]; "44 years of NHS service and almost a decade as Chief Executive of DBTH" — p.24, [B2 Chief Executive's Report]

**[LEAD]** NHS England has tied DBTH's £15.7m deficit-support funding for 2026/27 to a quarterly "£1-for-£1" clawback — for every £1 the trust under-delivers against plan, £1 of DSF is lost, up to the full allocation.
[Evidence] "Your organisation has been confirmed as eligible for £15,700k DSF in 2026/27… If the financial plan is not achieved, for every £1 an organisation under-delivers against its plan, a further £1 of DSF will be lost, up to the total DSF available for the year." — letter from Tarryn Lake, NHSE Regional Director of Finance, North East & Yorkshire, 22 May 2026, p.253–254, [G2 Appendix 1 NHSE Guidance Letter]; "receipt of the £15.7m DSF allocation is conditional on sustained delivery of the approved financial recovery plan" — p.252, [G2 Deficit Support Funding]

**[LEAD]** The trust is carrying an underlying deficit estimated at £52.1m at month 2, and has committed to NHSE to cut its recurrent exit run-rate to £10.1m this year and reach financial balance by 2028/29 on ~£90m of recurrent savings.
[Evidence] "At month 2 the current underlying deficit is estimated at £52.1m and the 26/27 exit rate is estimated at £10.1m in line with plan." — p.34, [C1 Board Integrated Performance Report]; "reducing the in-year deficit and reaching financial balance by 2028/29, supported by approximately £90m of recurrent savings over the period" — p.29, [B3 Reset Programme Update]

**[LEAD]** DBTH's £46.29m in-year efficiency programme is risk-adjusted down to just £23.7m under NHSE's own methodology — a ~£22.6m credibility gap on the savings underpinning the DSF deal.
[Evidence] "The full-year in-year forecast remains £46.290m, although £23.733m is the risk adjusted value derived using the NHSE risk and programme maturity methodology." — p.27, [B3 Reset Programme Update]; net financial risk stated as £21.7m at M2 (down from planned £38.9m) — p.34, [C1 IPR] / p.78, [C1 Appendix 2 Finance]

**[WORTH WATCHING]** One year on from the culture/well-led review, the trust's Freedom to Speak Up annual report claims "significant assurance" while conceding its own reported fall in bullying and harassment concerns contradicts wider organisational feedback — with a Bullying & Harassment Deep Dive not reporting until Q3 2026/27.
[Evidence] "Data also shows a reduction in Inappropriate Behaviour (18%) and Bullying and Harassment (11%). However, this contrasts with wider organisational feedback from reports and is currently subject to further analysis through the planned Bullying & Harassment Deep Dive review with feedback due in Quarter 3 - 2026-2027." — p.178, [E2 Speaking Up 2025/26 Annual Report]; anonymous reporting up to 15% "rising above the national average… This may indicate reduced psychological safety"; medical colleagues "not feeling safe to speak up" — p.178–179

**[WORTH WATCHING]** The 2025 staff survey (reported to the May board) shows advocacy scores falling sharply after the culture review: all three "would recommend as a place to work / to receive care / patients are top priority" questions "significantly declined" and are "significantly worse than the sector average", and People Promise 3 "We each have a voice that counts" recorded a significant decline.
[Evidence] "a significant decline in People Promise 3 'We each have a voice that counts'… all three questions… had significantly declined from 2024 and were significantly worse than the sector average." — p.378, [J1 Minutes 5 May 2026]

**[WORTH WATCHING]** WRES/WDES data shows equality outcomes going backwards: disabled staff are "almost four times more likely to enter formal capability processes", white applicants are 2.92x more likely to be appointed than BME applicants (a "marked deterioration"), and there is no BME or disabled representation on the board.
[Evidence] "disabled employees are almost four times more likely to enter formal capability processes than non-disabled colleagues"; "White applicants more likely than BME applicants to be appointed from shortlisting, representing a marked deterioration from the previous year"; "The absence of both BME and disabled representation at Board level in the reporting year highlights a critical leadership gap." — p.184–186, [E3 Workforce Race & Disability Equality Standards Report]

**[WORTH WATCHING]** ED four-hour performance deteriorated to 71.1% in May (against a 76% plan, ranked 86th of 150 acute providers), and sepsis antibiotic reliability is well below the 90% standard at 53.2% in A&E.
[Evidence] "71.1% of patients were in ED > 4 hours… against the plan of 76%. The Trust ranked 86th out of 150 acute providers." — p.39, [C1 Appendix 1 IPR]; "sepsis antibiotic administration remains materially below the 90% trajectory, at 53.2% in A&E and 70.0% for inpatients" — p.34, [C1 IPR]

**[WORTH WATCHING]** The trust is progressing a business case for ~£32m of capital to build a new Urgent Treatment Centre and co-located SDEC at Doncaster Royal Infirmary, against a backdrop of ED being "subject to increased external scrutiny and public attention".
[Evidence] "we continue to progress a business case for approximately £32 million of capital investment to deliver a new purpose-built Urgent Treatment Centre, co-located Same Day Emergency Care facilities and wider improvements to the Emergency Department" — p.22, [B2 Chief Executive's Report]

## FOI leads

- **NHSE DSF letter and the trust's quarterly Board Assurance Statements.** The pack summarises but the full assurance statements (signed by Chair and CEO, covering pay retraction, CIP delivery, contract position, exit run-rate) are the substance. Request from DBTH: the signed Q1 Board Assurance Statement submitted w/c 29 June 2026 (approved by Finance & Performance Committee 25 June), and any subsequent quarterly statements, plus NHSE's decisions on each DSF payment (first cash expected 15 July 2026). — p.251–254, [G2].
- **CQC unannounced inspection of Children & Young Persons' Services (22–23 April 2026).** May minutes note "written correspondence… received from the Care Quality Commission" and a draft report pending, with "In excess of 130 data submissions… to be verified." Request from DBTH/CQC: the CQC feedback letter and, when published, the draft/final inspection report. — p.386, [J1 Minutes 5 May 2026].
- **Bullying & Harassment Deep Dive.** Commissioned as part of the Culture and Leadership programme, reporting Q3 2026/27 — its existence is disclosed but not its scope or interim findings. Request: terms of reference, commissioning body/supplier, and interim outputs. — p.178, [E2].
- **CEO recruitment / Richard Parker departure terms.** Retirement announced but no timeline; request any exit or settlement arrangements and the CEO recruitment plan/timeline. — p.17–18/24, [B1/B2].

## Pack assessment

- **Routine flag:** Not routine. This is a substantive finance-and-governance pack for a trust HSJ is already covering.
- **Tier counts:** LEAD 4 · WORTH WATCHING 5 · FOI 4
- Strongest single line: the NHSE £1-for-£1 DSF clawback letter (quoted verbatim, named RD of Finance) paired with the £46.29m→£23.7m risk-adjusted savings gap and the £52.1m underlying deficit — a clean "distressed trust on conditional NHSE support" story. The CEO retirement is the ready-to-run leadership line; the culture metrics (FTSU + staff survey + WRES/WDES) provide the follow-on to HSJ's January 2026 well-led story.
