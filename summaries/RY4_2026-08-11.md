## Top lines

[LEAD] A £27m local neighbourhood health allocation collapsed to around £0.5m once NHS England confirmed only £50m was available nationally, which the board was told has "effectively removed" the investment and undermined local planning.
[Evidence] Asked "about the previously identified £27m allocation for neighbourhood health… for further assurance on how it had been addressed or absorbed and its implications", the executive "replied that an initial expectation of the £27m funding for ENH was later revised as national announcement clarified that only £50m was available nationally, resulting in the local allocation reducing significantly to around £0.5m, effectively removing the anticipated investment and undermining local planning assumptions." — p.~9, [Minutes / Board discussion]

[LEAD] The trust's 2025/26 accounts show a £761,000 surplus against a prior-year deficit of £747,000, with a draft unqualified opinion, no value for money weaknesses and no significant weaknesses identified by auditors Azets.
[Evidence] "The Trust recorded a surplus for the year of £761k compared to a prior year deficit of £747k. The accounts were prepared on a going concern basis with no Value for Money weaknesses identified." "A draft unqualified opinion was noted by the committee as anticipated to be finalised as such in the finalised audit." Following "an update to the committee from the Azets team the committee were pleased to note only a small number of recommendations and that no significant weaknesses were identified." — p.83–84, [Audit Committee report, Attachment B7]

[LEAD] The audit committee recorded that the accounts were delivered during a change of leadership in the director of finance post.
[Evidence] "External Audit - a very positive audit process and an unqualified opinion. Recognition to the finance team who managed the process during a change of leadership on the DoF position." — p.86, [Audit Committee report, Summary of Committee governance issues]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's cost improvement programme is its main flagged risk, with much of last year's delivery non-recurrent and a gap to target on this year's plans.
[Evidence] "The main risk flagged is the Cost Improvement Plan (CIP) gap, moving from green to amber/green, with sustainability concerns for improvements achieved through non-recurring resources." "CIP delivery exceeded the plan, but much of the savings were non-[recurrent]". "For Month 1, the Trust submitted a break-even plan with a 5% CIP target". The BAF financial risk is scored 12 now, rising to 16 later in the year, with the note: "Position remains unchanged whilst continue to see gap to target in CIPs plans." — p.21–22 and p.39, [Finance report and Board Assurance Framework]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust closed 2025/26 with a £1.6m surplus and reported cash of £13.5m at month one.
[Evidence] "The Trust ended the year with a surplus of £1.6 million, supported by late [income]… a strong cash position." "Cash at Month 1 is £13.5 million, reflecting a more typical position". A non-executive "sought clarification on whether the reported £6.5m CIP savings reflected [recurrent delivery]". — p.22, [Finance report]. [INFERENCE] The £1.6m figure in the finance report and the £761k statutory surplus in the audit committee report are different measures; the pack does not reconcile them.

[WORTH WATCHING] Board papers cite the Roger Kline and Resendis reports on disparities in the treatment of ethnic minority staff, and link them to gaps in management capability.
[Evidence] "The reports referenced the Roger Kline and Resendis reports, noting disparities in treatment of ethnic minority staff and the need for improved management capacity and capability." Concerns remain "about appraisal quality and fundamental management skills, especially around communication and early intervention in formal processes." — p.21, [Committee assurance report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The audit committee says its risk management team is under-resourced.
[Evidence] "Risk management - the committee noted that the risk management team could do with more resources to support strengthening the risk management process." — p.86, [Audit Committee report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Nurses are recording care outside contemporaneous records in breach of NMC guidance because of a systems access gap.
[Evidence] staff are keeping "contemporaneous records (in breach of NMC guidelines) as a result of the lack of access to CSAIS units on SystmOne, which may lead to…" — p.~46, [Risk register]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is absorbing the operationalisation of the new Central East ICB, with implications for providers still unclear.
[Evidence] "The Central East (CE) ICB continues to be operationalised, with the implications for Health and Care Partnerships (HCPs) and providers expected to become clearer." — p.~72, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 5.3]
