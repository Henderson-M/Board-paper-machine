[LEAD] The trust has suspended its 2026 governor elections in anticipation of the Health Bill abolishing councils of governors, and admits the decision was late and communication with affected governors "sub-par".
[Evidence] "Due to the expected removal of the statutory requirement for Foundation Trusts to have a Council of Governors, the Chair of Governors took the decision to suspend the planned election process for 2026, with the Lead Governor informed of the process." Reasons include: "Holding the elections may give a false pretence to any candidate that may put themselves forwards for election about the role" and "Suspending the election avoids the immediate cost of the election process of c.£6,000" – p.118, [NHS Foundation Trust Governance Reform and the Proposed Abolition of Councils of Governors, Item 21.3]
[Evidence] "The decision to suspend the election was unfortunately delayed until early August and communication with the affected governors sub-par. The decision to delay was in part due to awaiting additional guidance provided by NHS Alliance, through planned briefing sessions with Trust Chairs and Governors, and personnel changes at the Trust. The timeline was also impacted due to the need to bring forward the date of the Annual Members Meeting to 30 September." – p.119, [same paper]
[Evidence] Legislative timetable as the trust understands it at 24 September 2026: "Commons stages Completed; House of Lords First Reading Completed (September 2026); House of Lords Second Reading Scheduled for 13 October 2026; Royal Assent Anticipated in Early 2027; Earliest anticipated implementation 1 April 2027" – p.117. "There is currently no nationally agreed replacement model for Councils of Governors" and "there has been a mixed picture, between those Trusts that have continued with planned elections, those that have suspended elections (with several having suspended elections in 2025), and some seeking to extend the terms of office of sitting governors." – p.118
[Evidence] Three of 26 seats affected (two public, one staff); the three governors "have been invited to continue to attend future meetings of the Council of Governors, albeit as non-voting attendees" – p.118.
