## Top lines

[LEAD] STH was £15m behind its financial plan at month 4 with an £18.3m forecast gap to close, and is launching a further round of mutually agreed resignations; month 6 showed no improvement on the trajectory.
[Evidence] "the concerning financial performance at month four being £15.01m behind plan" ... "The Month 4 version of this forecast has a £18.33m gap to close" ... "Further plans include a further round of the mutually agreed resignation scheme (MARS)" — 14 September report, [Paper F — Finance and Performance Committee Assurance Report]. Month 6: "whilst performance had not got worse it remained on the same trajectory as month's four and five meaning further work will be needed to deliver the financial position for the year" — 12 October report, [Paper F]. Context: the trust needs £65m of productivity and efficiency savings; recurrent schemes identified so far total £47.4m. September minutes put the plan at "a £20m deficit in the context of a £1.4 billion budget" — p.4, [Paper A — Minutes, 8 September 2026].

[LEAD] Chief Medical Officer (Development) Nick Lyons is leaving STH for an executive role in Bermuda, and Assistant Chief Executive Sandi Carman is retiring with her post abolished and re-advertised as Corporate Governance Director.
[Evidence] "Dr Nick Lyons, Chief Medical Officer (Development), will leave the Trust on 5 February 2027 to take up an executive leadership role in Bermuda." ... "The revised post of Corporate Governance Director is currently being advertised, with interviews planned by the end of November 2026." — p.4, [Paper C — Chief Executive's Matters]. [INFERENCE] Lyons jointly leads the PROUDFuture efficiency programme and the directorate P&E conversations with the CFO, so his departure lands mid-recovery.

[LEAD] STH's capital programme is £14.1m overcommitted for 2027/28 after a national change to the allocation formula cut its funding by roughly £6m a year — and the Northern General vascular angiography scheme has slipped again (spend down from £7.5m to £1m this year) while the related vascular services risk sits at 20 on the risk register.
[Evidence] "The current over commitment of £14.1m for the 2027/28 programme is concerning" ... "the change to the OCA calculation has resulted to a reduction in an OCA allocation of circa £6m less than last year" ... "This has resulted in the forecast value of spend this year to reduce from £7.5m in the quarter 1 update to £1m in this update." — pp.4–5, [Paper E — Quarter Two Capital Plan]. Quality Committee exceptionally called in the vascular risk "in light of the length of time this had been on the risk register and its score (20)" — 19 October report, [Paper I — Quality Committee Assurance Report].

[LEAD] STH says it is carrying more risk into this winter than ever, will not open the surge wards used in previous winters even at extreme demand — and has taken over the ICB's role convening Sheffield's system-wide winter response "given recent changes to the ICB".
[Evidence] "it is anticipated there will be greater system wide and organisational risk carried into this winter than previously" — p.1, [Paper G — Winter Plan]. "Options to address 'extreme' levels of demand this year will not include the levels of further surge wards as used in previous winters." — p.14, [Paper G]. "Given recent changes to the ICB and following discussion at UEC Board it has been agreed that STH will act as the system convenor when a system-wide response is required to minimise system-wide risk." — p.16, [Paper G]. [INFERENCE] A trust formally assuming a convening function previously held by the ICB is an early concrete example of ICB-cut consequences at place level.

[LEAD] A Maternity Outcomes Signal System warning was triggered at STH in August by a cluster of four baby deaths; the critical safety check was rated green/amber.
[Evidence] "The trigger was linked to a cluster of deaths - three stillborn deaths and one neonatal death - two of which were associated with significant foetal abnormalities." — p.11, [Paper A — Minutes, 8 September 2026]. The check "reflecting no new unmitigated safety concerns were identified" was signed off by the chief nurse and CMO (Operations). [INFERENCE] MOSS is the new national early-warning system; a named trust publicly reporting a Level 1 trigger and its outcome is still a relatively rare, trackable datapoint.

[WORTH WATCHING] F&P Committee remains "only partially assured" the trust will hit year-end performance trajectories: 52-week waiters at 1,744 and worsening, 62-day cancer at 54.1%, and the operational delivery risk rated Limited assurance.
[Evidence] "members remain only partially assured regarding the sustainability of improvements and achievement of yearend trajectories" — 12 October report, [Paper F]. "RTT 52-weeks at 1,744 (off trajectory and worsening)" and "62-day at 54.1% which, whilst a 5% in month improvement, is still well adrift of trajectory/plan" — 14 September report, [Paper F]. BAF deep dive on operational delivery "showed an aggregated assurance rating of Limited with all three cause statements now limited" — 12 October report, [Paper F]. Also: STH has "the fifth highest number of shared pathways for cancer treatment centres in the UK" and the national team is now interested in leading work with the top nine trusts with 30%+ inter-provider-transfer workload — action 133, [Paper V — Action Log].

[WORTH WATCHING] STH stays in NHS Oversight Framework segment 3 for Q1 2026/27 but its national ranking among acute providers jumped from 86th to 64th of 134.
[Evidence] "We remain in segment 3, unchanged from the previous quarter. It is positive to see that our national ranking amongst acute NHS providers has improved from 86th to 64th out of 134 organisations, with our average metric score standing at 2.38." — p.3, [Paper C — Chief Executive's Matters].

[WORTH WATCHING] The workforce reduction plan is behind trajectory and "further measures are being implemented", including another MARS round and vacancy control panels.

[WORTH WATCHING] A coroner issued a Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths report to STH over urgent-care access for patients with serious immunotherapy toxicity, and a patient safety investigation concluded a death was more likely than not due to problems in care.
