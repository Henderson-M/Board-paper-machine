## Top lines

[LEAD] [NEW] Dorset HealthCare is forecasting a £7.2m year-end deficit against a planned £0.5m surplus, at month three.
[Evidence] "The table below shows that the current year-end forecast is a potential deficit of £7.2m against the planned £0.5m surplus. Current projections assume no further efficiency savings, only the fully developed schemes where budget has been removed." — p.198, [Trust Finance Report for Month 3, June 2026 (DHC), internal p.16 of 22]

[LEAD] [NEW] The Finance and Performance Committee's assurance report to this board says Dorset HealthCare "remains on plan at Quarter 1, reporting a surplus in line with forecast" — and does not mention the £7.2m forecast deficit that appears in the finance report in the same pack.
[Evidence] "DHC Finance Report. The Committee received the Report for assurance. Members noted that the Trust remains on plan at Quarter 1, reporting a surplus in line with forecast. The Committee noted delivery of £4.6m of efficiency savings, although performance remains behind plan, primarily reflecting temporary staffing expenditure and continued out-of-area placement pressures." — p.146, [Finance and Performance Committee in Common Assurance Report, meeting of 29/07/2026]
[INFERENCE] The two statements are not necessarily contradictory — the committee line describes the Q1 year-to-date position and the £7.2m is a full-year forecast outturn. But the committee's summary to the board omits the forecast entirely. Worth putting to the trust.

[LEAD] [NEW] NHS England's Sir Jim Mackey and Penny Dash wrote to the federation on 26 June about Dorset County Hospital's first-quarter performance. The letter is not published in the pack.
[Evidence] "On 26 June we received a letter from Sir Jim Mackey and Penny Dash in relation to the performance at Dorset County Hospital in the first quarter of the year. In Dorset County Hospital, we have seen very high numbers of attendances in the Emergency Department and our emergency waiting times have not met the expected performance levels. This is also true of our waiting times for planned care (referral to treatment times and some of our cancer performance measures), where we have seen a rise in our waiting lists and longer waits than planned. As a result of receiving this letter the executive team, Board and ICB worked collaboratively to submit a response to NHSE to provide assurance on our governance and oversight and the Recovery Plans in place." — p.47, [Chief Executive Officers Report, section 2.2]
[Evidence, corroborating] "The Committee received an update on the establishment of the Operational Performance Recovery Group, introduced in response to actions arising from the NHS England performance review. Assurance was provided that the Group has been established, terms of reference drafted, and weekly meetings commenced with executive oversight in place. Members noted that ICB representation has been incorporated within the arrangements and that weekly performance briefings are now being circulated to Non-Executive Directors." — p.146, [Finance and Performance Committee in Common Assurance Report]
[Note] The pack names Mackey and Dash without titles.

[LEAD] [NEW] Dorset HealthCare is owed £11.4m of debt more than 90 days old, and 99.7% of it is owed by Dorset County Hospital — its own partner in the Board in Common.
[Evidence] "Overall debtors remain high with £11.40m of LA and NHS debt which is over 90 days old. DCH represents 99.7% of this balance." — p.199, [Trust Finance Report for Month 3 (DHC), internal p.17 of 22]
[Evidence, background] From the June minutes: "The Trust had £41m cash. This was lower than planned by £5.1m due to the timing of NHP spend and NHS debt which was being addressed via a payment plan." — p.26, [Minutes of the Board in Common, 17 June 2026]

[LEAD] [ALREADY SENT 10 AUG] Dorset County Hospital is still using off-framework agency staff two years after NHS England said the practice must stop, and the pack says usage is rising.
[Evidence] "NHSE mandated that all off-framework agency spend ceased from July 2024. In Month 3, off-framework agency is being increasingly utilised across ED, Purbeck and SCBU with a YTD spend of £142k. This remains an area of high focus going in to 2026/27. Internal controls through the High Cost Agency Reduction Group remain in place." — p.274, [DCH Finance Report – Month 3 2026/27]
[Evidence] "Month three Off framework usage of £135k has been incurred noting break glass limited usage in place, and the highest spend incurred in this area this month with the quarter now totaling £277k." — p.269, [DCH Finance Report – Month 3 2026/27, cover]
[INFERENCE] The two figures in the same paper do not obviously reconcile — £142k year-to-date on p.274 against a quarter total of £277k on p.269. Worth asking which is right.

[WORTH WATCHING] [NEW] Dorset County Hospital has missed NHS England's end-of-Q1 conditions on its efficiency plan, with £3.4m of savings still unidentified and £3.9m rated high risk.

At month three, the Trust has 15% unidentified (£3.4m) and 17% of high risk schemes (£3.9m). Recognising the high risk that remains in this area, the Trust has provided a detailed update to NHSE confirming the actions being taken with timescales, which was shared with Trust Board in July for information." — p.270, [DCH Finance Report – Month 3 2026/27]
[Evidence] The trust has "an efficiency delivery requirement of £23.3m to achieve the planned full year breakeven position." — p.274, [DCH Finance Report – Month 3 2026/27]
