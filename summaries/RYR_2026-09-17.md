## Top lines

[LEAD] Grant Thornton reported significant value for money weaknesses at the trust in three separate areas for 2025/26, including governance and culture.
[Evidence] "The auditors identified significant weaknesses in arrangements relating to financial sustainability (delivery of savings), governance (culture), and performance (NOF4 rating), and issued key recommendations to address these." The same table records "An unmodified audit opinion was issued" on the financial statements and "No Public Interest Report was issued". p.308, [12.1 Annual Audit Committee Report 2025/26]. The committee adds that Grant Thornton "made recommendations in respect of addressing both of their significant weaknesses" and that the Board "should also be seeking views from the respective Committee Chairs as to the assurance they are receiving", p.309.

[LEAD] The trust applied to NHS England for £20m of cash support in July and is paying many suppliers 90 days beyond terms, with Better Payment Practice Code performance down to 40 per cent against a 95 per cent target.
[Evidence] "Prioritisation of cash to ensure the payroll can be paid means that many suppliers are being paid at 90 days beyond the 30 days payment terms; Better Payment Practice Code (BPPC) performance has further dropped to 40% (target 95%)." And: "At the end of July the Trust submitted an application for £20m cash support to NHSE. This has been given regional approval and is now under consideration from the national cash team. If successful, cash support would be paid to the Trust in September." p.144, [19 Integrated Performance Report]. The finance committee separately records "a deterioration in Better Payment Practice Code performance together with increasing supplier concerns" and cash at £3.2m against a £3.0m plan at month 3, p.100-101, [17 Finance & Performance Assurance Committee Chair's Report].

[LEAD] An independent PwC review found a significant underlying financial challenge and immature efficiency schemes; the trust has separately appointed an unnamed financial improvement consultant.
[Evidence] "The Committee considered the findings of the independent PwC review which identified a significant underlying financial challenge, limited maturity in parts of the efficiency programme and the need for a longer-term recovery trajectory." p.100, [17 Finance & Performance Assurance Committee Chair's Report]. Separately: "The Trust has appointed a financial imporvement consultant to support maturing the efficiency progrmamme and implementing financial recovery measures including enhanced grip and control measures on areas of variable expenditure." p.144, [19 Integrated Performance Report]. Efficiency delivery at quarter 1 was "£3.4m, £2.8m behind plan" against a "£140m Efficiency and Productivity Programme" (p.100-101); the IPR month 3 table shows efficiency actual £12.2m against £17.2m plan, variance £5.0m adverse (p.144).

[LEAD] The trust will ask the board to approve a £280m strategic outline case to replace RAAC at St Richard's Hospital, for submission to NHS England and the Joint Investment Committee.
[Evidence] "The SRH RAAC strategic outline business case, seeking £280 million to replace RAAC at St Richard's will be recommended by FPC to Board this week to enable onward submission to NHSE and Joint Investment Committee." p.147, [19 Integrated Performance Report, Major Projects Portfolio Report Aug 26].

[LEAD] CQC found maternity at Princess Royal Hospital still in breach of the staffing regulation, with whistleblower concerns about staffing and an obstetric theatre frequently closed by equipment and ventilation failures.
[Evidence] "The service continued to have poor staffing levels and remained in breach of regulation 18: Staffing." p.248, [8b CQC Maternity Inspection Report: Princess Royal Hospital]. "Staff told us they felt tired and burnt-out due to workforce pressures and the demands of the service. Shifts felt increasingly overstretched and sickness rates were high with 8% midwifery sickness, we were told this was mostly due to staff stress. ... The service had 1 obstetric theatre which was frequently reported to be closed due to ongoing equipment failures and issues with the heating and ventilation." p.251. "We had received several whistleblower concerns in relation to insufficient staffing. Staff said they were 'tired and burnt out' and it was common for staff not to take their break due to low staffing." p.284. On theatre closure: "all elective cases were delayed, leading to longer waits for women and a significant risk of theatres being unavailable for emergency caesarean section cases", p.264. Both sites were rated requires improvement; well-led at Princess Royal improved from inadequate, p.24, [8 CQC Maternity Inspection Report cover paper].

[LEAD] A workforce consultation affecting nearly 300 staff across 14 professional groups is due to start in mid-October as part of the stroke service reconfiguration.
[Evidence] "Workforce consultation preparation is advanced with mid October 2026 the anticipated start date – affects nearly 300 staff across 14 professional groups". Same table records "Handover of Boxgrove Ward on 24th August for 11 weeks with 27 beds closed". p.148, [19 Integrated Performance Report, Major Projects Portfolio Report Aug 26]. Separately, consultation has also begun on phase two of the Trust Operating Model, with "20 new cross-Trust clinical directors from a field of more than 40 applicants" appointed, p.16, [7 Chief Executive Report].

[LEAD] The annual equality report puts sexual harassment of staff above national levels and links it to the Niche well-led review's finding on sexual safety.
[Evidence] "In the 2025 NHS Staff Survey, 10.7% of staff reported unwanted sexual behaviour from patients, relatives or members of the public, compared with 9.1% nationally, while 4.3% reported sexual harassment from a manager, team leader or colleague, compared with 3.5% nationally. ... These findings are consistent with the Niche developmental Well-Led review, which identified sexual safety and gender-based discrimination as pronounced concerns requiring sustained and visible organisational action." p.527, [16a Annual Equalities Report 2025/26]. The report's conclusion lists deteriorated or inequitable outcomes including "disparities affecting Global Majority staff in disciplinary and capability processes", p.528.

[LEAD] The trust is creating a ninth executive post, Chief Infrastructure and Digital Officer, requiring a constitutional change and a matching increase to at least nine non-executives.
[Evidence] "At the Board's Appointments and Remuneration Committee it agreed to the Chief Executive's proposal to strengthen the Executive Team with the creation of a new executive position, the Chief Infrastructure and Digital Officer. This requires a change to the Trust's constitution to allow up to 9 Executives, the current constitution only allows up to 8, consequently the constitution is required to reflect that the Trust must have at least 9 Non-Executives". p.172, [21 Company Secretary Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] A maternal death at St Richard's Hospital is under both MNSI and internal patient safety investigation.
[Evidence] "Maternal death at SRH under MNSI and PSII investigation." p.706, [18 Perinatal Quality Oversight Model Report, May 2026 data]. "MNSI investigation in progress and has been reported to MBRRACE. Internal PSII investigation recorded but investigation defers to MNSI/MBRRACE", p.712. The report's closing message says focus "remains on learning from the SRH maternal death investigation", p.746.

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England has moved the trust from segment 4 to segment 3 for quarter 1, ranking it 100th of 134 acute trusts, but the performance report in the same pack still shows segment 4 and 121st.
[Evidence] "NHS England has confirmed that UHSussex has moved from Segment 4 to Segment 3 for Quarter 1 of 2026/27 and ranked 100th of 134 acute trusts. This is an improvement on our previous position and reflects progress in several areas. It does not, however, chang e the need for sustained improvement in urgent and emergency care, planned care, productivity and financial performance." p.16, [7 Chief Executive Report]. The IPR, which reports the published quarter 4 2025/26 position, states: "UHSx remains Segment 4 overall rating with a provider capability assessment of red, and the Trust Overall ranking remained static at 121 of 134", p.116 and p.154, [19 Integrated Performance Report]. The trust is also "in Tier 1 for elective support from NHSE", p.114.

[WORTH WATCHING] A follow-up backlog of about 5,800 patients in oral and maxillofacial surgery has triggered a trust-wide review of non-RTT follow-up waiting lists.
[Evidence] "The Committee noted that approximately 5,800 patients were originally identified within the backlog and welcomed progress made in reducing risk and validating patients affected. ... The Committee requested a broader review of follow-up backlog management and associated governance arrangements." p.79, [15 Patient & Quality Assurance Committee Chair's Report]. The committee also records complaints "at their highest recorded level" (p.76), duty of candour timeliness in sharing investigation findings "reduced to 68%" (p.80) and clinical documentation compliance "below 50% against a target of 95% compliance by the end of 2026" (p.80).

[WORTH WATCHING] Twelve months on from the Niche well-led review, NHS England's provider improvement team finds nine of 22 recommendations still outstanding and suggests commissioning a repeat review.
[Evidence] "From the information provided 13 of the 22 recommendations are now considered as complete with the 9 remaining recommendations in progress". p.38, [11 Progress update – Implementation of the Niche developmental well-led review recommendations]. "The Trust may benefit from commissioning a repeat well-led developmental review next financial year to assess the overall progress made following the latest CQC well led inspection." p.35. The stocktake was authored by "Fleur Blakeman, Improvement Director NHSE, Jabir Alam, Project Lead NHSE" (p.35) and the NHSE National Provider Improvement Team is described as "currently providing support to the Trust" (p.37). Outstanding areas include exploring "perceptions of paternalism in the Trust's culture" (p.43) and a Dearden-commissioned HR review whose actions are still being implemented (p.39, p.44).

[WORTH WATCHING] A trust-wide recruitment pause and vacancy controls are blocking maternity and neonatal staffing approved on safety grounds.
[Evidence] "Recruitment activity is currently affected by vacancy controls and the temporary Trust-wide recruitment pause. The BR+ review uplift has been approved by the Board but remains unfunded." p.729, [18 Perinatal Quality Oversight Model Report]. On the maternity incentive scheme: "Element 3- Requires increase in Epilepsy Specialist Nurses which are currently not being approved by Vacancy Control Panel." p.739. Risks to compliance include "Budget setting implications impact ability to continue previous plans towards BAPM compliance with neonatal nursing and medical establishment", p.738.

[WORTH WATCHING] Twelve-hour A&E waits at Worthing Hospital were 190 per cent higher in June than a year earlier, and the trust is procuring external improvement support for urgent care.
[Evidence] "Worthing site had an extremely challenging month with 1048 over 12 hr waits, 190% higher than June-25 (361). SRH also saw an increase to 849 patients compared to 674 Jun-25". Trust-wide 12-hour waits were 3,133 in June, "the highest 12 hour waits since Jan-26", up 23.5 per cent year to date. p.126, [19 Integrated Performance Report]. "The Committee discussed the refresh of the UEC Improvement Plan, the ongoing procurement process for external improvement support", p.102, [17 Finance & Performance Assurance Committee Chair's Report]. The BAF records that for urgent care "five of the seven key action were at risk of not delivering their intended benefit at the pace planned", p.164, [20 Board Assurance Framework].
