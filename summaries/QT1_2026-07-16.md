# DLN Boards in Common (Derby & Derbyshire / Lincolnshire / Nottingham & Nottinghamshire ICBs) board — 16 July 2026

> Note: this is a single *joint* meeting "in common" of three statutory ICBs — NHS Derby and Derbyshire (DDICB), NHS Lincolnshire (LICB) and NHS Nottingham and Nottinghamshire (NNICB). Where an item concerns one ICB specifically, that is flagged. The three are being run as a single "cluster" and are heading toward merger (see LEAD 1).

## Top lines

[LEAD] Three ICBs confirm they are actively planning a merger, with NHS England, and are already running Wave Three redundancies across the cluster.
[Evidence] Chair: "I have continued discussions with partners, local authorities, elected representatives and NHS organisations regarding the future development of the NHS operating model and proposals relating to ICB mergers." — p.34, [Chair's Report ICB CIC 26 029]. Transition Committee received an update "regarding future ICB merger arrangements and the evolving strategic commissioning and provider oversight model"; Wave Three recruitment "remained challenging due to the scale of voluntary redundancy exits." — p.630–631, [Committee Highlight Reports ICB CIC 26 039]. The Health Bill (introduced 14 May 2026) would "remove requirements for certain provider representatives on ICB Boards." — p.38, [Chief Executive's Report ICB CIC 26 030].

[LEAD] Nottinghamshire system already £9.4m adverse to plan at month two, driven by two named trusts; whole-DLN provider position reliant on non-recurrent measures.
[Evidence] "At Month 2, the ICBs' systems are reporting a deficit of £61.1m against a planned deficit of £52.0m… the adverse position driven by Nottinghamshire, which is reporting a £9.4m variance against plan." Primary drivers: "Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust (£6.1m adverse)… and Nottinghamshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust (£2.8m adverse)." "The position remains reliant on non-recurrent measures and therefore continues to present a longer-term sustainability risk." — p.177–178, [Finance Report ICB CIC 26 036].

[LEAD] Efficiency programme running £5.6m behind at month two, with a 24% year-end gap to target — and members openly questioning whether the ICBs' own reorganisation is undermining savings delivery.
[Evidence] "The risk adjusted year end forecast was estimated at £123 million, which was currently a 24% gap to target." "There was a discussion on whether the management of change process was impacting the ICBs' capacity to implement the efficiency schemes and whether this posed a significant risk to the programme. The committee asked for this issue to be raised at the July meeting of the Joint Transition Committee." — p.616, [Committee Highlight Reports — Joint Finance and Performance Committee]. Concerns NNICB most; £163.1m combined efficiency target for the ICBs — p.178, [Finance Report].

[LEAD] NHS Nottingham & Nottinghamshire ICB breached its statutory breakeven duty in 2025/26 because NHS England withheld its Deficit Support Funding — triggering a formal external-auditor referral to the Secretary of State.
[Evidence] CEO report: NNICB "reported achievement of all statutory financial duties except financial breakeven for the year. This deficit was due entirely to the non-receipt of the ICB component of NHS England Deficit Support Funding for the second half of the year, which was withheld due to the financial performance of Nottinghamshire NHS system partners being lower than planned." — p.45, [Chief Executive's Report]. May minutes: "the ICB's External Auditor would be required to write formally to the Secretary of State on the matter. However, the breach was a technical issue." — p.21, [Minutes 21 May 2026].

[LEAD] Boards asked to formally adopt the Government's working definition of anti-Muslim hostility and commit to new anti-racism training, following the Mann Review.
[Evidence] Recommendations: "Adopt the Government's working definition of anti-Muslim hostility" and "Confirm their commitment to completing the new anti-racism training when it becomes available." NHS England "has requested confirmation of the ICBs' adoption of the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's definition of antisemitism and the Government definition of anti-Muslim hostility by 31 July 2026." — p.39, p.41–42, [Chief Executive's Report].

[LEAD] NHS trust boards across the cluster must give NHS England a Board Assurance Statement on mortuary and post-death care by 31 July 2026, following the Ockenden and Fuller findings; the HTA is ordering mortuaries to audit 11 years of incident records.
[Evidence] "The Human Tissue Authority is requiring mortuaries to audit reportable incident records from 2015 to 2026… NHS Trust Boards and senior leaders must regularly review mortuary departments… and provide a Board Assurance Statement to NHS England by 31 July 2026." — p.40, [Chief Executive's Report]. Quality committee flagged "one Trust as the primary area of concern, where external reviews had identified ongoing risks relating to workforce resilience, capacity and operational sustainability" (Fuller Inquiry context). — p.621, [Committee Highlight Reports].

[WORTH WATCHING] Derby & Derbyshire ICB found non-compliant with the Provider Selection Regime (transparency / intention-to-award notices) and an outlier for single-tender waivers — Partial assurance from Audit.
[Evidence] "There had been some instances of non-compliance in the undertaking of responsibilities under PSR rules within NHS Derby and Derbyshire ICB with regard to the publication of transparency notices and intention to award notices." Separately "one area highlighted… as an outlier was the over-reliance of NHS Derby and Derbyshire ICB on single tender waivers." — p.633–634, [Committee Highlight Reports — Audit Committees].

[WORTH WATCHING] 118 patients waiting 65+ weeks at end of April across five named trusts, with year-end RTT gains at risk of unwinding.
[Evidence] "There were 118 patients waiting longer than 65 weeks across the ICBs at the end of April, concentrated within University Hospitals of Derby and Burton NHS Foundation Trust, Chesterfield Royal Hospital NHS Foundation Trust, United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust and Sherwood Forest Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust." 4-hour A&E: Derbyshire 75.5%, Lincolnshire 82.8% (both above plan), Notts under pressure. NHS Oversight Framework Q4 2025/26: "NNICB is the most challenged overall." — p.145, p.152, p.140, [Commissioning Delivery Oversight Report ICB CIC 26 034].

[WORTH WATCHING] NNICB has missed its zero-inappropriate-out-of-area-placement target for the 12th consecutive month; Lincolnshire structurally dependent on out-of-area beds with no female PICU.
[Evidence] NNICB "inappropriate OAPs (Adult Acute) was 10 at the end of the month, which was 10 above plan of 0… failed to achieve the plan for the 12th consecutive period." "Lincolnshire does not currently have a female PICU, resulting in an ongoing requirement for out-of-area placements for this cohort." — p.58, p.60, [Chief Executive's Report Appendix A — Out of Area Placements Update]. All-ICB plan is to eliminate adult OAPs by March 2028 (assurance rated only "adequate").

[WORTH WATCHING] Nottingham University Hospitals and East Midlands Ambulance Service both declared critical incidents during the June heatwave.
[Evidence] "East Midlands Ambulance Services NHS Trust declared an incident due to a significant increase in demand caused by the extreme heat, and Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust declared a critical incident due to severe and sustained pressure on all services. Both incidents were stood down within three days." — p.47, [Chief Executive's Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS Lincolnshire ICB fell short on the cyber Data Security and Protection Toolkit ("Approaching Standards") due to outsourced-IT assurance gaps.
[Evidence] "NHS Lincolnshire ICB submitted an overall position of 'Approaching Standards', due to limitations in the assurance evidence available to the ICB in relation to elements of its outsourced IT support arrangements, rather than evidence of a significant or immediate cyber security control failure." — p.43, [Chief Executive's Report].

[FOI] An unnamed provider has been escalated into "enhanced quality oversight" — identify the trust and the trigger.
[Evidence] Quality committee "supported the escalation of one Trust into enhanced quality oversight and emphasised the importance of ensuring that improvements at providers in escalation were embedded and sustainable." — p.620, [Committee Highlight Reports]. *Request: from the three ICBs / System Quality Group, the name of the escalated provider, the escalation criteria met, and the enhanced-oversight action plan.*

[FOI] Mortuary / post-death care Board Assurance Statements due to NHS England by 31 July 2026.
[Evidence] p.40, [Chief Executive's Report]. *Request: from each DLN provider (esp. Nottingham University Hospitals and Nottinghamshire Healthcare FT), the Board Assurance Statement submitted to NHSE and any HTA audit findings on reportable incident records 2015–2026.*

[FOI] 2026/27 contracts still not agreed with three unnamed providers midway through the year.
[Evidence] Finance & Performance committee "noted that contract agreement discussions remained ongoing for three providers and members were advised of the ongoing actions being taken to progress these." — p.617, [Committee Highlight Reports]. *Request: from the ICBs, which three providers, the values in dispute, and the sticking points.*
