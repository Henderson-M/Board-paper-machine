[LEAD] The reported £2.2m adjusted deficit rests on £6.6m of deficit support funding — £1.6m of it handed over at year-end from money "not provided to other organisations" — without which the trust would have lost £8.8m.
[Evidence] "The Trust's reported performed included the receipt of £5.0m of planned Deficit Support Funding agreed in setting the plan for the year. The Trust received an additional £1.6m (£6.6m in total) allocated at year-end from funding not provided to other organisations which reduced the reported deficit for the year. The Trust's result without Deficit Support Funding would have been a deficit of £8.8m compared to a planned deficit excluding DSF of £11.1m. The receipt of Deficit Support Funding means that the Trust is limited to a maximum NOF rating of 3." — p.350, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, s5.1]. [WORTH WATCHING] Deloitte challenged the trust's year-end use of "vesting certificates" — paying capital suppliers in advance of receiving goods — against Managing Public Money rules on circumventing spending controls.
[Evidence] "The Trust entered into vesting certificates with certain of its capital suppliers ahead of year-end, with £0.9m of capital expenditure recognised (2024/25 £0.7m). These agreements are intended to transfer legal title ahead of receipt of goods, in exchange for payment in advance... Managing Public Money states that public sector organisations should not use payments in advance to circumvent spending controls, such as to avoid underspending the capital budget for the year. We have recommended that the Trust put in place approval processes to check that any use of vesting certificates reflects genuine commercial requirements." — p.355, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, s5.2 Governance]. Also: "The Trust continues to use vesting certificates only at year end." — p.364, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, Appendix 2 Insights]

[WORTH WATCHING] A £3m saving from transferring estates functions to SWFT Clinical Services Ltd did not happen in 2025/26, was plugged with extra ICB money, and has been banked again in the 2026/27 plan.
[Evidence] "The Trust's non-recurrent efficiencies for FY26 included planned savings from the transfer of estates functions to SWFT Clinical Services Limited, which has not taken place in the year. These unachieved efficiencies were covered by additional funding from NHS Coventry and Warwickshire ICB in the current year and are now assumed to take place in 2026/27. If this scheme does not take place this will put further strain on the Trust's ability to hit its planned financial position for 2026/27." — p.352, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, s5.1]. And: "This included an additional £3m of funding from NHS Coventry and Warwickshire ICB for the assumed savings from the delayed transfer of services to SWFT Clinical Services in the year. We note that the 2026/27 CPIP plan also includes £3m of savings on the assumption that the transfer takes place." — p.361, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, Appendix 1]

[WORTH WATCHING] The auditors warn the shared GEH/SWFT executive team is stretching capacity and has been focused primarily on George Eliot, citing waiting lists and corridor care.
[Evidence] "The combined executive structure risks puts additional pressure on executives and reduces focus on individual Trust issues... There is a recognition that the executive teams focus has primally been on George Eliot following the merger, due to identified issues such as waiting lists and corridor care. This is to the benefit of GEHT, though management and those charged with governance are conscious to ensure this does not have a negative impact on SWFT's performance." — p.355, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, s5.2 Governance]

And: "One internal audit review received a limited assurance rating, which was the audit of Waiting List Management... Issues were identified where processes for booking original appointments and for rebooking cancelled patient appointments were not consistently followed, there were also shortcomings in the waiting list data validation processes which might impact on the accuracy of waiting list data." — p.150, [Annual Report 2025/26, Annual Governance Statement].
