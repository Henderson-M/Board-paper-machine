# Humber Health Partnership Boards-in-Common (Northern Lincolnshire & Goole / Hull University Teaching Hospitals) board — 11 June 2026

- **Source:** https://www.nlg.nhs.uk/about/events/?cat=board-meetings
- **Pack:** 1 file (521pp combined Boards-in-Common pack)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-09T17:45:35Z
- **Note:** This is a joint **Boards-in-Common** pack covering BOTH **Northern Lincolnshire and Goole Foundation Trust (RJL)** and **Hull University Teaching Hospitals Trust (RWA)**, which operate together as the Humber Health Partnership (HHP). Both trusts remain in NHSE's Intensive Recovery Programme ("special measures"). Figures are reported at Group (HHP) level and split by trust where the pack does so.

## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO confirms the group model itself is now under formal review by Carnall Farrar, with NHSE backing, expected to conclude this summer — i.e. the partnership's structure may be unwound.
[Evidence] "The Partnership has commenced the next phase of improvement and organisational review work, supported by NHS England (NHSE) through the Intensive Recovery Programme (IRP) and an independent review by Carnall Farrar (CF). This work is expected to conclude over the summer of 2026… The next phase will therefore look at whether the current model provides the right basis for long-term improvement, stronger accountability and sustainable services… This work will consider a range of possible future arrangements." — pp.27–28, [Group Chief Executive Briefing, BIC(26)132]

[LEAD] The Group is forecasting a £63.1m planned deficit for 2026/27 but with a £40.2m unidentified gap, and a straight-line/adjusted run-rate pointing to a £138.7m deficit before mitigations.
[Evidence] "The Group is formally forecasting a plan compliant position. However, an adjusted straight-line projection would give a deficit of (£138.7m). Mitigating actions reduce the deficit leaving an unidentified gap of (£40.2m)." — p.376, [Finance Report Month 1, BIC(26)144]. Forecast bridge gives planned deficit (£63.1m), forecast deficit (£103.3m), unmitigated variance (£40.2m) — p.379.

[LEAD] Month 1 (April) was already £2.2m off plan, £10.0m year-to-date deficit, with the CIP programme £4.9m behind target in month one and forecast to undershoot a £119.1m efficiency target by £46.7m.
[Evidence] "The Group ended month 1 with a year-to-date (YTD) deficit of (£10.0m), (£2.2m) adverse to plan." / "The Group has delivered £2.7m in CIP against a YTD target of £7.6m, (£4.9m) behind target. The Group are forecasting CIP delivery of £72.4m, (£46.7m) adverse to plan." — pp.376, 382, [Finance Report Month 1, BIC(26)144]. Of the £119.1m efficiency target, only £54.17m was risk-adjusted forecast — p.379.

[LEAD] The Group has applied to NHSE for additional external cash support — £6.35m for May and £7.98m for June — and is awaiting approval.
[Evidence] "The Group did not require additional external cash support for April but has requested £6.35m in additional external cash support for May and £7.98m for June." — p.376; "(HUTH) and £7.98m for June (£6.0m HUTH / £1.98m NLAG) and is awaiting NHSE approval." — p.377, [Finance Report Month 1, BIC(26)144]

[LEAD] The recovery plan is built on cutting the workforce: a 633 whole-time-equivalent post reduction and a 30%/£30m cut in variable pay, both transacted into Month 1 budgets.
[Evidence] "The two most significant areas of financial recovery in 2026/27 are linked to workforce and include a requirement for 30% reduction in variable pay and a whole-time equivalent reduction of 633 posts. This latter issue has come about due to a significant rise in the number of staff at HUTH and NLaG over the past five years." — p.227, [Our People, BIC(26)138]; "Agreed a targeted 30% / £30m reduction in variable pay" / "At this stage plans to reduce headcount are the least developed" — p.380, [Finance Report Month 1]

[LEAD] The two trusts are jointly the 12th-highest reporter of never events of all NHS providers in England, per the latest published 2025/26 data, cited in the pack.
[Evidence] "The latest published data for 2025/26 shows that HUTH/NLAG has the joint 12th highest number of Never Events from all NHS providers in England." — p.41, [Monthly Patient Safety Report, BIC(26)134]

[LEAD] CQC Section 31 conditions on HUTH maternity services — imposed after the March 2023 "Inadequate" rating — have now been lifted, with a full CQC inspection anticipated.
[Evidence] "Following the Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspection in March 2023, maternity services at HUTH were rated Inadequate and Section 31 conditions were [imposed]… the conditions of the Section 31 have been removed. A full CQC inspection is anticipated soon." — p.96, [Maternity & Neonatal Assurance, BIC(26)136]; also "the Section 31 Conditions for HUTH have now been removed" — p.57, [Maternity & Neonatal Safety Champion's Report, BIC(26)135]

[LEAD] A new Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths report has been issued over a sepsis death at Hull Royal Infirmary, citing the absence of a single validated triage tool.
[Evidence] "There has been one Regulation 28 Letters issued in respect of the initial triage of a patient in the emergency department at HRI, who presented with an infection and developed sepsis. The concern relates to the need for a single validated triage assessment tool to be universally used and the need for staff to be appropriately trained to use it." — pp.41–42, [Monthly Patient Safety Report, BIC(26)134]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Q&S committee flagged the trusts as negative national outliers on several clinical audits — including NLAG on the National Neonatal Audit and Surgical Site Infection, and HUTH on knee revision rates and end-of-life care.
[Evidence] "Clinical Effectiveness – national outliers and significant variations in performance — NLAG: Surgical Site Infection - negative; National Neonatal Audit - negative. HUTH: National Joint Registry Knee Revision Rate - Negative; National Audit of Care at the End of Life - Negative." Status: "Limited assurance was given" / "Alert to the Board May 2026" — p.49, [Quality & Safety Committees-in-Common Advise/Alert/Assure report, BIC(26)143]

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff engagement is deteriorating across both trusts despite the recovery push, with Q1 pulse scores down at both and minority-ethnic staff reporting more bullying than White colleagues.
[Evidence] "Q1 Pulse Survey results show a deterioration in engagement scores at both HUTH (6.0, down from 6.4) and NLAG (5.9, down from 6.4), with advocacy scores also declining" — p.227, [Our People, BIC(26)138]; from May minutes: "staff from minority groups continue to report fewer opportunities compared to others, and higher levels of bullying and harassment when compared to White staff" — p.9, [Minutes 14 May 2026, Staff Survey item BIC(26)109]

[WORTH WATCHING] The HRI Tower Block fire-safety enforcement notice will not be met by its 16 July 2026 expiry; an extension will be requested, and the Group has asked NHSE to re-profile the Tower Block capital funding into later years.
[Evidence] "the Tower Block enforcement notice was due to expire on 16 July 2026. It was acknowledged that, although a 12-month period had been available to complete the required works, this would not be achieved within the timeframe. An extension would therefore be requested 28 days prior to expiry." — p.11, [Minutes 14 May 2026]; "The Trust has submitted a revised profile to NHSE for the Tower Block funding to reduce the expenditure in 26/27 and is awaiting approval." — p.386, [Finance Report Month 1]

[WORTH WATCHING] NLAG did not achieve full 10/10 compliance on the Maternity Incentive Scheme Year 7, while HUTH did (receiving £813,846 including a share of the surplus redistributed from non-achieving trusts).
[Evidence] "Confirmation of funds received in respect of Maternity Incentive Scheme Year 7 — HUTH £813,846 including £46,041 share of surplus from trusts that did not achieve 10/10. NLAG MIS Discretionary Funding to support our improvement plan £235,245 which must be ring-fenced for maternity services." — p.59, [Maternity & Neonatal Assurance Report, BIC(26)136] [INFERENCE] The discretionary-funding (rather than surplus-share) route for NLAG indicates NLAG did not declare 10/10; the pack does not state NLAG's safety-action score directly.
