## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has classed SWB's 2026/27 financial plan as "non-compliant" — an £8m planned deficit that relies on £14m of national deficit support, i.e. a £22m underlying unsupported deficit.
[Evidence] "The financial plan submitted by Sandwell and West Birmingham is classed as non-compliant by NHS England as it is a plan to deliver an £8m deficit financial position by 31st March 2027, rather than delivering a break-even position" / "an unsupported deficit position of £22m" — p.58–59, [Addressing Financial and Operational Non-Compliance, Enclosure 5]

[LEAD] As a condition of signing off the non-compliant plan (letter dated 5 June 2026), NHSE has imposed detailed monthly financial/workforce/CIP reviews, a mandated independent "Drivers of the Deficit" review, and prior approval for any consultancy spend over £50,000.
[Evidence] "The plan sign off conditions were set out in a letter from NHSE dated 5th June 2026" / "2.3.1 The commissioning of an independent 'Drivers of the Deficit' review" / "2.3.6 Ensuring prior approval for any consultancy expenditure above £50,000 from NHS England regional team" — p.59, [Enclosure 5]

[LEAD] NHSE has downgraded SWB's Provider Capability Assessment from Amber-Green to Amber-Red after its Q1 2026/27 review, citing 2025/26 financial performance and 2026/27 planning.
[Evidence] "NHS England has advised in a recent letter to the Trust that is has revised Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust's Provider Capability Assessment rating from Amber Green to Amber-Red following its Quarter 1 2026/27 review" — p.24, [Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 2]

[LEAD] A trust "lessons learned" review (commissioned after an NHSE letter of 6 February 2026 from Nicola Hollins, Regional Director of Finance) found financial risks were not transparently reported to committees, the board or NHSE — with the reported position materially changing only after a new Director of Finance was appointed.
[Evidence] "whilst financial risks and concerns were identified within detailed financial reports, these were not consistently reflected within high-level reports presented to Board Committees, the Trust Board or NHS England. As a result, the assurances provided through formal governance arrangements did not fully align with the underlying level of financial risk" — p.42, [Board Assurance Report Appendix 1 — Lessons Learned Exercise]

[LEAD] Two Never Events at SWB in successive months; board notes anecdotal link to recently increased theatre list sizes.
[Evidence] "A Never Event occurred on 21 May 2026 in the Birmingham Treatment Centre (BTC) Ophthalmology department. The incorrect lens size (21.5) was implanted when a 12.5 lens was required" / "A further Never Event involving a wrong site fascia iliac block, occurred on 23 June 2026… the 'stop before you block' check was not completed" / "Early anecdotal feedback has suggested that this may be in part due to the increased list sizes recently implemented" — p.69, [Group Chief Nurse & CMO Report, Enclosure 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] Appointment of a Turnaround Director is under active consideration, alongside a financial improvement expert to review the deficit drivers; Finance Committee is "seriously concerned" that the 2026/27 CIP carries only Partial Assurance and held an extraordinary meeting before the board.
[Evidence] "Consideration has also been given to the potential benefits of appointing a Turnaround Director" / "The Finance Committee remains seriously concerned regarding the current development of the Trust's 2026/27 CIP programme, which continues to provide only Partial Assurance… an extraordinary meeting has been scheduled prior to the July Trust Board meeting" — p.35, [Integrated Committee upwards assurance report, Enclosure 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWB reported a £5.52m deficit at Month 2 (May 2026) against a £3.90m planned deficit — £1.62m adverse; full-year plan requires an "ambitious 7% CIP" worth £58.1m, only 68% of savings delivered so far are non-recurrent.
[Evidence] "At month 2, the Trust reported a (£5.52m) deficit, against a deficit plan of (£3.90m), an adverse variance of (£1.62m)" / "Full delivery of the ambitious 7% CIP requirement (£58.1m full year) remains the key risk" — p.45–47, [SWB Finance Report M02, Enclosure 4a]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWB closed 2025/26 with a £12.6m deficit against a revised NHSE plan (adverse to an original break-even plan); only 39% of the year's CIP was recurrent.
[Evidence] "the Trust reported a year-end deficit of £12.6m, in line with the revised plan agreed with NHS England, although adverse to the original breakeven plan" / "CIP… achieved approximately 80% of target; however, only 39% of savings were recurrent" — p.10, [Minutes of 20 May 2026 meeting, Enclosure 1a]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWB one of only 12 trusts examined for the Baroness Amos national maternity investigation (final report published 30 June 2026); trust has formally accepted findings on racism, discrimination and culture. (NB: already widely covered by ITV/Birmingham Mail/Express & Star — the board's response and the earlier 9 Feb 2026 feedback letter are the fresh angle.)
[Evidence] "Initial feedback was received in the form of a letter on the 9th February 2026 with concerns regarding racism, discrimination and behaviours within Perinatal Services" / "SWBH was one of only 12 trusts selected for detailed investigation" — p.84–85, [Baroness Amos Report Briefing, Enclosure 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] Complex Endometriosis Service at SWB remains suspended after losing BSGE accreditation, affecting 13 women; separately, MMUH endoscopy backlog exceeds 1,950 patients with eight cases of harm linked to delayed cancer diagnoses.
[Evidence] "The Complex Endometriosis Service at SWB remains suspended following non-compliance with British Society for Gynaecological Endoscopy (BSGE) accreditation requirements, affecting 13 women" / "The Endoscopy backlog at MMUH remains a significant concern, with over 1,950 patients awaiting treatment and eight cases of harm associated with delayed cancer diagnoses" — p.38, [Integrated Committee upwards assurance report, Enclosure 3]
