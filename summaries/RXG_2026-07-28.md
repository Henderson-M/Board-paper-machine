## Top lines

[LEAD] Bottom-up forecast shows a c.£15.7m–£16m deficit at a segment-1 trust that was required to break even, with modelling not yet showing a return to breakeven.
[Evidence] "the baseline forecast, based on detailed bottom-up submissions, currently indicated a deficit of c.£15.7m–£16m. He noted that this reflected an early-year position with a degree of prudency applied and that, despite identified mitigations and delivery actions, the current modelling did not yet demonstrate a return to breakeven." — p.94, [FIP&P Committee minutes, item 7.2 Financial forecast]

[LEAD] The trust reports having the second-highest level of seclusion on working-age adult wards in England; an external review of its restrictive-practice team and training is under way, with a report imminent.
[Evidence] "RRP – 2nd highest level of seclusion on working age adult wards in the country, external review of restrictive practice team and training underway to be reported imminently. An improvement plan is in development and will be overseen by QSC." — p.52, [Assurance from Quality and Safety Committee, 7 July 2026]

[LEAD] The trust has launched a Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme, with a Board-approved workforce reduction plan imposed by NHSE as a condition of accepting its 2026/27 plan; MARS applications close 31 July and a special committee will sign off decisions.
[Evidence] "There has been notable interest in the Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme (MARS) for which applications come to a close at the end of July." — p.50, [Chief Executive's report]; "the plan had been assessed as compliant with conditions. These included the requirement for a Board-approved workforce reduction plan, a sickness reduction plan, minimal unidentified savings from Month 1, and participation in a regional review of out of area placements." — p.96, [FIP&P Committee minutes, item 9.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] A specific review of women's services has been commissioned, prompted in part by inpatient deaths; QSC says early indications are positive with findings due later in the summer.

[WORTH WATCHING] Around a third of Barnsley GPs are no longer accepting shared-care requests for ADHD medication, blocking the trust's ADHD inpatient pathway and lengthening waits.
[Evidence] "It was noted that Barnsley GP's (about a third) are no longer accepting shared requests for ADHD medication. This is resulting in SWYPFT services not accepting transitions to our services without a shared care agreement. [WORTH WATCHING] The revised cost plan for the trust's Older People's Services (OPS) capital scheme is likely to exceed the original business case, requiring fresh Board approval before signature.
[Evidence] "the intention to issue a stage 3 contract before the end of the month alongside a cost plan to confirm affordability ahead of receipt of a Guaranteed Maximum Price from the contractor for work on the OPS. It is likely that the revised cost plan will exceed the value of the original business case and so Board approval will need to be sought before signature." — p.56, [Assurance from Finance, Investment and Performance Committee, 20 July 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] At month 3 the trust is only £8k ahead of plan (cumulative deficit £1.8m) on the back of one-off benefits, with £5.4m of value-for-money savings rated high-risk; the committee says immediate action is needed.
[Evidence] "At the end of month 3 the Trust had an in month deficit of £0.5m and a cumulative deficit of £1.8m... cumulatively we are still ahead of plan by £8k. The savings from the Value For Money Programme that are categorised with a high level of risk is now £5.4m in respect of workforce savings required." — p.55–56, [Assurance from Finance, Investment and Performance Committee, 20 July 2026]

## Context notes (not for publication as-is)

- The trust retained a segmentation rating of 1 in the NHS Oversight Framework; its own Q1 self-assessment against the new ~25-metric NOF suggests a likely segment 1 or 2 (p.55). Segment-1 status is what makes the £15.7m–£16m forecast deficit newsworthy — a strong performer flagging a material gap early. Do not compute a plan/prior-year comparison the pack does not state.
- The "all systems... deficit of £109.3m which is adverse to plan by £20.8m excluding Deficit Support Funding" figure (p.56) is an NHSE regional/system context figure, NOT the trust's own position — do not attribute to SWYPFT.
- NHSE regional director Fiona Edwards is leaving for personal reasons after a period of ill health; Sam Allen (NENC ICB CEO) covers as interim regional director (p.50, CEO report) — national context, may already be known.
- Christine Brereton (Chief People Officer) declared her spouse will become Interim Finance Director at Nottinghamshire Healthcare Mental Health Trust from 1 July 2026 (p.2, minutes) — declaration of interest only.
- Cheswold Park Hospital (acquired): local collective agreement on the NHS terms-and-conditions implementation date "remains unsigned" and is a condition of the offer (p.54, PRC assurance); higher post-acquisition turnover noted (p.6, minutes).
