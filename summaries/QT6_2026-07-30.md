# Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB (South West Peninsula Board, joint with Devon ICB) — 30 July 2026

- **Source:** https://swpboard.nhs.uk/board-meetings/board-meeting-papers-and-minutes/
- **Pack:** 2 files (Agenda and Papers, 202pp; Appendices, 45pp)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-27T14:05:00Z

> Note: This is the **South West Peninsula Board** — the joint cluster board of Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB (QT6) and Devon ICB, working towards a mandated merger on 1 April 2027. Almost every item is cluster-wide and spans both ICBs and their providers (Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust, University Hospitals Plymouth, Royal Devon University Healthcare FT, Torbay and South Devon FT, Cornwall Partnership FT, Devon Partnership Trust).

## Top lines

[LEAD] Six recognised trade unions wrote collectively to the entire cluster board declaring the ICB restructure "unsafe" and the board in breach of its statutory duties — and have lodged a Freedom to Speak Up concern with NHS England South West.
[Evidence] "We have unfortunately reached the conclusion that the ICB and therefore the Board has failed in its statutory duties in relation to the organisational change process… It is our view that the draft structure is unsafe… There is insufficient capacity to meet statutory requirements such as safeguarding and CHC" — Appendices p.30–31, [Union letter, 30 June 2026, signed by MiP, PDA, RCN, BMA, UNISON and GMB officials]. Escalation confirmed: "a Freedom to Speak Up (FTSU) issue has also been lodged with NHS England SW Region as a result, regarding the Union letter" — p.49, [Chair's Report].

[LEAD] The chair publicly dismissed the union letter as "quite misleading" — a sitting ICB attacking its recognised unions in a public board paper.
[Evidence] "we were surprised to have received a second Union letter – sent direct to all ICB board members… We believe the union letter does not present a full and accurate picture… In fact, we find it quite misleading and it is bound to be distressing for our staff once they know the full facts" — p.49, [Chair's Report].

[LEAD] CQC has served a Section 29A Warning Notice on Cornwall Partnership Foundation Trust after an inspection of its mental health services found "significant patient safety issues".
[Evidence] "a recent CQC inspection of CFT mental health services identified significant patient safety issues, resulting in a Section 29A Warning Notice and increased scrutiny of improvement actions" — p.190, [Performance Exception Report]; also "the recent CQC inspection of Cornwall Partnership Foundation Trust mental health services, which identified patient safety issues and resulted in a Section 29A Warning Notice" — p.182.

[LEAD] NHS England's regional chair and regional director have sent a formal escalation letter to the chairs and CEOs of Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust, University Hospitals Plymouth and the cluster over sustained A&E four-hour and 12-hour breaches.
[Evidence] "Of specific concern is the continued distance from plan for 4-hour ED Performance and the number of over 12 hour waits… at the Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust (RCHT) and United Hospitals Trust Plymouth (UHP). This has prompted escalation by NHS England (NHSE) with a letter from the Regional Chair and Regional Director sent to the Chair and CEO of both Trusts and the Chair and CEO of the Cluster" — p.181, [Performance Exception Report]. (Letters cited as Appendix 4 but not reproduced — see FOI.)

[LEAD] Cornwall Council, Cornwall's scrutiny committee and local MPs are formally opposing the NHSE-mandated merger of Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB into Devon ICB; the chair states outright that staying separate "is not an option".
[Evidence] "Cornwall Council is objecting to the formal merger of CIOS ICB with Devon ICB" — p.62, [Local Authority Partner Member Update — CIOS]. "Cornwall Council, its Health and Adult Social Care Overview and Scrutiny Committee and local MPs opposed the merger and stated a preference to retain the sovereign ICB" — p.47; "remaining as a cluster and not merging is not an option… our new financial envelope for staffing ICBs means we could not safely operate two ICBs going forward" — p.47, [Chair's Report]. Cornwall Council OSC and Healthwatch Cornwall opposition letters at Appendices p.16 and p.21.

[LEAD] The cluster confirms compulsory redundancies are coming, with the final Phase 2 restructure decision due 6 August and the new single-cluster structure rolling out from 17 August.
[Evidence] "We will over the coming weeks/months have many of our current ICB colleagues leaving our employment, mostly through voluntary redundancy, but some through compulsory redundancy too" — p.48, [Chair's Report]. Restructure designed within "the reduced funding envelope of £19 per head of population that ICBs have to operate in"; a Band 9 Director post and the Director of Service Transformation post have been removed from the senior structure — Appendices p.33–36, [CEO response to unions].

[WORTH WATCHING] Interim cluster chief executive Mark Hackett is "away from his role for personal reasons", with chief clinical officer Susan Bracefield acting up as interim cluster CEO.
[Evidence] "Mark Hackett is currently away from his role for personal reasons, with Susan Bracefield, Chief Clinical Officer, undertaking the role of Interim ICB Cluster Chief Executive in his absence" — p.46, [Chair's Report]. Open recruitment for a substantive cluster CEO starts August, final panel late September, via Gatenby Sanderson (contracted "months ago") — p.49.

[WORTH WATCHING] All four acute trusts in the cluster are running corridor care above the national average, with Royal Cornwall Hospitals Trust the worst at an average of 33 patients per snapshot.
[Evidence] "the newly published corridor care metrics shows RCHT had an average of 33 patients receiving corridor care in the emergency department at the snapshot times during June 2026. UHP had 27, RDUH had 22 and TSD with 31. All providers are worse than the national average of 20" — p.187, [Performance Exception Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals Plymouth carries the cluster's biggest unidentified savings gap — two undeveloped CIP schemes worth roughly £20m combined, flagged "high risk".
[Evidence] "the majority of this relates to UHP whereby there are two material schemes at £10m each" — p.182; "YTD CIP delivery is off plan with the most material variance at UHP. This relates to two schemes at c£20m that are still being developed and remain high risk" — p.191; M2 table shows UHP "CIP unidentified" of £22.06m — p.191, [Performance Exception Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] Devon's providers are collectively carrying an £80m savings programme to reach breakeven, and Devon ICB a £281m recurrent CIP target by March 2027.
[Evidence] "a plan that achieves a breakeven position in 2026/27… requires a savings programme of £80m" — p.84, [NHS Trusts and Foundation Trusts Partner Member Update — Devon]. "the importance of achieving recurrent delivery of the £281m Cost Improvement Programme target by March 2027" — p.153, [Chair's Report of the Devon Finance, Performance and Quality Committee, 11 June 2026].

[WORTH WATCHING] A cultural review is under way at University Hospitals Plymouth, referenced only in passing in a committee report.
[Evidence] "The Committee also discussed the University Hospitals Plymouth NHS Trust's (UHP) cultural review and potential links to NHS Devon's diagnostic work" — p.158, [Chair's Report of the Devon Finance, Performance and Quality Committee, 9 July 2026].

[WORTH WATCHING] Independent provider Ramsay Health Care UK's Mount Stewart Hospital has been escalated to "enhanced concern" following a patient death after transfer to an acute trust.
[Evidence] "NHS Devon has arranged a second quality meeting with the provider to review limited visibility of patient safety reporting, three VTE-related incidents, and concerns surrounding the transfer of a patient to an acute trust who subsequently died" — p.190, [Performance Exception Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The cluster ICBs' mental health budgets are forecasting a £5.9m overspend driven partly by "right to choose" ADHD and autism assessments.
[Evidence] "Mental Health forecast overspend of £5.9m is driven by the increase in the cost of individual packages of care and by the impact of 'right to choose' in relation to ADHD and autism assessments and related costs as individuals seek diagnosis from independent providers. Based on early trends there is a significant risk of overspend in this area" — Appendix 2, p.195, [Performance Exception Report].
