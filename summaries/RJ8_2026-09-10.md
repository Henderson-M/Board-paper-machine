## Top lines

[LEAD] Cornwall Partnership is still working to a CQC section 29A warning notice on its community mental health services, with prescribing, medicines management and physical health monitoring risks significant enough that the quality committee says continued oversight is necessary.
[Evidence] "Community Mental Health Services and CQC Section 29A Warning Notice: The Committee received assurance regarding progress against the improvement programme established in response to the CQC Section 29A Warning Notice. However, due to the significance of prescribing, medicines management and physical health monitoring risks, continued oversight remains necessary." — p.60, [Quality Committee report to the Board]

[LEAD] NHS England approved the trust's 2026-27 plan with conditions attached, specifically requiring it to de-risk the cost improvement programme.
[Evidence] "from NHS England, with conditions. These relate to de-risking the plan, including the Cost Improvement Programme." — p.303, [Finance report]

[LEAD] The trust reports a breakeven position but assesses the gross financial risk to that plan at £21m, and every efficiency delivered so far has been non-recurrent.
[Evidence] "The gross financial risk to plan is assessed as £21m at Month 4, reflecting additional income risk not included in the original plan." — p.303, [Finance report]; "Notably, efficiencies delivered to date are entirely non-recurrent, increasing delivery risk for future periods." — p.197, [Finance report]

[LEAD] Cornwall has climbed from segment 4 to segment 2 of the oversight framework and now ranks 27th of 61 trusts.
[Evidence] "The report noted continued organisational improvement, including the Trust's rise to 27th of 61 trusts in the National Oversight Framework, and a move from segment 4 to segment 2." — p.7, [Minutes, Deputy Chief Executive/Chief Finance Officer's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] £14.5m of full-year income underpinning the position is unconfirmed, and the reported position leans on higher staff turnover and investment slippage.
[Evidence] "3m year to date and £14.5m full year. This income remains unconfirmed and represents a risk to the financial position. The reported position is currently supported by favourable pay variances, driven by higher workforce turnover, slippage on planned investments and enhanced workforce controls." — p.197, [Finance report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The efficiency programme requires £22.7m, 6.3 per cent of allocation, of which £10.35m is explicitly planned as non-recurrent.
[Evidence] "The efficiency programme requires delivery of £22.7m (6.3% of allocation), comprising £12.35m recurrent and £10.35m non-recurrent savings." — p.197, [Finance report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Named risks include £4m on minor injury unit income, £5m on the return of non-recurrent funds and £3.5m of income not yet confirmed; temporary staffing is £1.2m above the national cap, driven by medical locums.
[Evidence] "Temporary staffing is £1.2m above national cap, with Medical locums being the main cost pressure ... Risks & Mitigations Income risk around MIUs £4m, efficiency mitigation £5m, return of non-recurrent funds £5m, other income not yet confirmed £3.5m and palliative end of life care." — p.221, [Finance report]
