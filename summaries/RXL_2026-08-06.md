## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England's chief executive and chair wrote personally to Blackpool Teaching Hospitals on 26 June about its performance against the 2026/27 plan; the trust replied on 13 July and must take a full recovery plan to NHSE's regional oversight meeting in August.
[Evidence] "On 26th June the Trust received a letter from NHSEs Chief Executive and Chair (Sir James Mackey and Dr Penny Dash) in respect of the Trusts performance (finance, activity and workforce) against the 2026/27 plan. This letter was responded to on 13th July." — Item 16, [Finance Report for the period ending 30th June 2026], para 2.12. Also: "At the Regional Provider Oversight Meeting (RPOM) on the 22nd June the Trust and NHSE NW agreed that a recovery plan covering performance, finance and workforce should be brought back to the August RPOM. The plan must include the Trusts underlying position and best, worst, most likely forecast scenarios." — same paper, para 2.13.

[LEAD] The trust's rolling 12-month SHMI has risen from 109 to above 130 — the paper attributes this to clinical coding rather than a rise in deaths.
[Evidence] "The rolling 12 – month SHIMI has increased from a satisfactory 109 to above 130." and, under Alert: "Mortality – The latest 12-month SHMI has increased to above 130" — Item 12, [Integrated Performance Report (IPR) – Quality], front sheet Summary/Advise and Implications/Alert. Context in same paper: "The crude mortality trend is stable at 4%, indicating no increased deaths per 1,000 patients admitted. This gives assurance that mortality is not increasing, despite the increased SHMI due to coding."

[LEAD] Blackpool's underlying deficit is running far above its plan: a £34.4m outturn in 2025/26 sat against an underlying exit run rate of £50.3m, and the 2026/27 forecast exit run rate is £25.4m against a £19.9m control limit.
[Evidence] "The 2025/26 outturn was a £34.4m deficit. The underlying exit run rate at the end of 2025/26 was £50.3m deficit which is £15.9m above the outturn position." / "The medium term financial plan gave an underlying exit run rate at the end of 2026/27 as a £23.1m deficit. This is £3.2m above the 2026/27 plan control limit of £19.9m." / "The current forecast as at 30th June 2026 gives an annual exit rate for 2026/27 of £25.4m deficit (was £28.8m deficit at the 31st May 2026)." — Item 16, [Finance Report], paras 2.17–2.19.

[LEAD] The trust is £3.9m behind plan three months into a "breakeven" year that depends entirely on £19.9m of national deficit support funding and £34.9m of savings, with capital spend at 9 per cent of plan and agency spend running at 3.7 per cent against a 2.6 per cent cap.
[Evidence] "The Trust submitted an updated break even plan on the 18th March 2026. Included within this break even plan is deficit support funding (DSF) of £19.9m." / "The Trust remains in deficit and is currently reporting financial performance which is £3.9m worse than the plan at the end of June." / "YTD, WRP delivery is £3.0m against a YTD target of £4.6m, so £1.6m behind plan." / "The YTD actual capital expenditure is £0.6m compared to planned capital expenditure of £6.6m." / "The year to date agency spend is £1.4m which is 3.7% of the total pay bill (the agency cap for 2026/27 equates to 2.6%)." — Item 16, [Finance Report], front sheet and paras 3.2, 4.2. On the fragility of the cash position: "This position is however unsustainable, and it is imperative that the Trust delivers its 2026/27 Financial Plan in order to support the maintenance of a positive cash position going forward."

[LEAD] Blackpool remains under NHS England tiering across all four access standards, and its own non-executives say its harm review process is not picking up harm despite very long waits — an assurance gap they have escalated to board.
[Evidence] "The Trust remained subject to tiering oversight across UEC, RTT, cancer and diagnostics, with NHS England requesting clearer specialty-level trajectories and evidence of the impact of individual interventions." and "Concern was raised that harm reviews were not identifying harms despite significant long waits and complaint volumes. The current position may indicate insufficiently sensitive harm review methodology and could unintentionally worsen inequalities where less complex patients access alternative provision while more complex patients remain waiting locally." — Item 15, [Finance and Performance Committee Escalation Report, 21 July 2026], Page 1 of 4 and Page 2 of 4. Mirrored by the quality committee: "the Committee noted that no elective waiting list harms had been identified through the current harm review process; however, members did not consider the report provided sufficient assurance on the wider impact of long waits on patients... The Committee therefore considered this an assurance gap requiring Board visibility." — Item 11, [Quality Assurance Committee Escalation Report], Page 1 of 4.

[LEAD] The Amos investigation's individual report on Blackpool — one of 12 trusts reviewed — found concerns about culture, psychological safety and staff confidence to raise concerns, and families who felt their concerns were dismissed.
[Evidence] "Concerns were identified regarding culture and behaviours, psychological safety and the confidence of some staff to speak openly and raise concerns." and "some families also reported that they did not feel listened to, that concerns were dismissed or not acted upon and that communication was not always clear, compassionate or consistent." — Item 13a, [Maternity and Neonatal Report / CNST], section 15.0, internal page marker "33". The trust has not yet completed its response: "The Trust will now undertake a structured assessment of the findings to identify areas where current improvement work provides assurance and where further action or strengthening is required." Maternity remains CQC "Requires Improvement" and in the Maternity Safety Support Programme "Yes – Commenced October 2022" (same paper, Appendix 1, internal page marker "37").

[WORTH WATCHING] Sickness absence is running at 6.9 per cent against a 5 per cent NHS Oversight Framework trajectory, with some estates and ancillary cost centres above 19 per cent, and the workforce report records a "continued deterioration" in staff experience.
[Evidence] "The Trust's rolling 12-month sickness absence rate is 6.9%, compared with the 5% NHS Oversight Framework trajectory and the Trust's ambition to reduce absence by at least 1% in-year." / "Nursing, Midwifery and HCA staff have a baseline sickness rate of 8.7%. Some cost centres are above 10–15%, with some estates and ancillary areas above 19%." / "Staff Survey results show a continued deterioration in staff experience, particularly across staffing/workload, morale and wellbeing, and culture/leadership confidence, requiring a coordinated Trust-wide response." — Item 20, [Integrated Performance Report (IPR) – Workforce], pages marked "- 2 -" and "- 3 -". Reported violence/aggression/sexual safety incidents "increased in July to 133" (same paper).

[WORTH WATCHING] A never event (wrong-eye intravitreal injection) and two MRSA bacteraemias breaching the zero-tolerance threshold were reported in May, alongside safe staffing fill rates of 89.4 per cent and continued use of ED and AMU corridors.
[Evidence] "Never Event – there was one never event declared in the month of May - Intra vitrail injection given to right eye instead of left eye" / "2 cases of MRSA bacteraemia were reported in May therefore the Trust has breached the zero-tolerance threshold for this infection based on 2025/2026 thresholds." / "Additional unfunded escalation areas remain open. In addition to these, corridors in ED and the acute medical unit and additional beds in ward areas continue to be used when required." — Item 12, [Integrated Performance Report (IPR) – Quality], front sheet pages marked "- 3 -" and "- 4 -". Open formal complaints stand at 224, "a 26% increase from May 2025 data" for new complaints.

[WORTH WATCHING] Category 2 pressure ulcers rose 22 per cent year on year and the trust's own thematic review says the rate has doubled since summer 2024.
[Evidence] "The 1st chart shows that from summer 2024, the mean has doubled from 1.1 to 2.2." and the table showing Category 2 totals rising from 476 (2024-25) to "579 +22%" (2025-26), with the Tertiary division's category 3 ulcers up "+225%" — Item 14, [Hospital Acquired Pressure Ulcers April 2025 - April 2026], pages marked "- 3 -" and "- 4 -".

[WORTH WATCHING] Blackpool has engaged an unusual number of named external firms on its improvement work — The Value Circle, Deloitte, PA Consulting, AQuA and Lloyds Healthcare — while still working through NHS England provider undertakings that include NHSE involvement in its executive appointments.
[Evidence] "Board and Executive Development Programme in place and embedded supported by The Value Circle, with planned enrolment in cohort one of the NHS Board Development Programme (Deloitte)" / "Progress has been made with regular meetings held between PA consulting, AQuA and Deputy CPO to ensure we have one singular plan to support rapid improvement in this space." / "Trust has agreed to pre-treatment assessment pilot with input from Lloyds Healthcare" — Item 9, [Single Improvement Plan Update], slides 9, 13, 16. Undertakings listed include "2Aa – Co-operate with the NHSE 'Governance and Leadership Review'" and "2Ab – NHSE involvement in Executive appointments" — same paper, slide 7. The trust exited the Recovery Support Programme in April 2026: "The plan has been refreshed following the Recovery Support Programme exit process and IAG in April 2026".

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's Green Plan has stalled because a recommended dedicated sustainability post was never created — and the paper says the risks flagged in 2024 "have materialised".
[Evidence] "This recommendation was not implemented, and responsibility for the Green Plan transferred into existing management arrangements. As a result, the programme has continued without dedicated leadership or operational support capacity." and "These risks have materialised to varying degrees." — Item 18, [Green Plan Progress Report], pages marked "- 1 -" and "- 2 -".

## Pack assessment

- Routine: false
- Top lines: 11 (LEAD: 6, WORTH WATCHING: 5, FOI: 5 — listed in the separate FOI leads section)
- Colour quotes: 7
- Annual accounts / annual report present: no — the 2025/26 Annual Report and Accounts are **not** in this pack. They were received and noted at the previous (2 July 2026) meeting, and that minute is in this pack: "The Board received the final Annual Report and Accounts following external audit review and noted that the documents had been completed and submitted within the required timetable. It was reported that the external auditors had issued their sign-off and that this represented a significant improvement in financial reporting and governance arrangements" — Item 5, [Minutes 02.07.26], minute 19. The minutes do not state the audit opinion type or the outturn figure; the outturn is given elsewhere in this pack as a £34.4m deficit for 2025/26 (Item 16, [Finance Report], para 2.17). A written resolution supporting the ARA was approved on 25 June 2026 (minute 6ii). [INFERENCE] The full audited accounts and the ISA 260 / auditor's report to those charged with governance are not in this pack and would need to be requested or picked up from the trust's website / AGM papers.
