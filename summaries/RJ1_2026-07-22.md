[LEAD] GSTT has identified less than half of its £124.9m savings target three months into the financial year, with £73.1m still unidentified, and is delivering at 21 per cent against an evenly phased plan.
[Evidence] "At the end of June identified CIPs stood at £51.8M, against the Trust target of £124.9M. [LEAD] The trust's Finance, Commercial and Investment Committee raised its financial sustainability risk to the highest score on the board assurance framework in April.
[Evidence] "The Committee reviewed its two Board Assurance Framework risks and agreed to increase Risk 8 (Financial Sustainability) to 16 (Very High) and Risk 9 (Capital Expenditure Investment) to 12 (High), reflecting concerns regarding delivery of future savings and the Trust's ability to fund wider transformation priorities." — p.69, [Finance, Commercial and Investment Committee 22 April 2026, Item 11.3]

[LEAD] GSTT's reported 2025/26 surplus was supported by non-recurrent month 12 funding including deficit support, and its own committee asked for the underlying position stripped of one-offs — a materially different framing from the CEO's account of the same result.
[Evidence] Committee: "an update on the Trust's year-end financial position, noting a surplus of £5m supported by additional Month 12 funding, including deficit support and payments for activity overperformance. Pay expenditure remained above plan, driven largely by medical staffing costs, and CIP delivery was below target. Members requested further analysis of the Trust's underlying financial position excluding non-recurring income." — p.69, [Finance, Commercial and Investment Committee 22 April 2026, Item 11.3]. CEO: "Following our annual external audit the Trust reported a surplus of £5.2 million in 2025/26. This was ahead of our breakeven plan and comes despite the challenging operational and financial environment across the NHS." — p.13, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 5]

[LEAD] GSTT's cash balance fell by £59.5m in the first three months of 2026/27, while capital spend ran at about a third of the year-to-date plan.
[Evidence] "The Trust closed the month with a cash balance of £109.4M; this is a £59.5M reduction from the opening balance on 1st April 2026." and "The Trust was initially allocated a CDEL limit of £166.2M for 2026/27. [LEAD] Amanda Pritchard — the former NHS England chief executive, now GSTT chief executive — tells her board that trusts' positions in NHSE's expanded oversight framework and forthcoming league tables may move for reasons unrelated to actual performance.
[Evidence] "there has been a significant expansion in metrics for acute trusts, which have increased from approximately 23 to around 33. The Framework continues to apply a financial override, limiting the maximum segmentation rating of organisations in deficit, and will be accompanied by publication of comparative performance data and league tables. It is not yet clear what the implications of such a substantial set of changes in the Framework will be for the Trust... whilst the Trust's overall ranking will likely change under the new Framework, that may not be related to changes in actual performance." — p.9, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 5]

[LEAD] GSTT's waiting list is running roughly 4,700 above plan because Q1 demand grew at nearly four times the planned rate — despite the trust delivering elective activity at 114.8 per cent of its operational plan.
[Evidence] "At the end of May, almost 112,000 patients were waiting for treatment, compared with our planned position of 107,248. Much of this is driven by far higher levels of demand than anticipated, with 7.9% growth (vs 2%) seen over Quarter 1 (April to June 2026)." — p.10, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 5]. [Evidence] "The closure of the NGO on 1st July 2026 has removed a central source of training and guidance for FTSU Guardians, and the Trust alone is now responsible for maintaining an effective Freedom to Speak Up Service with 'the right skills and training'.
