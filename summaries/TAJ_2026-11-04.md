## Top lines

[LEAD] NHSE has lifted three of the four groups of enforcement undertakings on Black Country Healthcare — but the culture and organisational development undertakings stay in force because staff survey raising-concerns results have not improved.
[Evidence] "NHS England's Midlands Regional Support Group reviewed the Undertakings on 17 September 2026. It concluded that the Trust had demonstrated sufficient compliance for a Compliance Certificate to be issued in respect of the Improvement Plan, Governance and Board Oversight, and Buddy Organisation requirements" — p.62, [Update on NHSE Undertakings, Item 4.2]
[Evidence] "2025 Staff Survey Raising Concerns results remained below the national and peer average and showed no significant improvement from 2024" — p.65, [Update on NHSE Undertakings, Item 4.2]. Closure now hangs on "a sustained positive trend in the 2026 National Staff Survey" so the trust "can then demonstrate 2 years sustained improvement" (p.65). The undertakings were accepted in January 2025.

[LEAD] The plan for a single "Anchor" lead provider contract covering mental health and learning disability services across the Black Country and Birmingham & Solihull ICB cluster has been paused — and the trust's own audit committee has listed at least 15 risks in the Anchor policy, including detriment to clinical services.
[Evidence] "The plan to establish a single lead provider contract covering mental health and learning disability services across the wider cluster population has been paused whilst both Black Country Healthcare NHS FT and Birmingham & Solihull Mental Health FT further develop collaborative working arrangements and partnership governance" — p.47, [CEO Report, Item 3.2]
[Evidence] "at least 15 key risk topics have been listed including ambiguity of governance /regulation, detriment to clinical services, heightened financial sustainability risk, hindering the 'three shifts' of the NHS 10 year plan and diversion of expertise and leadership away from Trust's strategic goals" — p.140, [Audit Committee Chairs Escalation Report, Item 6.5]. The committee recommended a full risk register "in a short timeframe" with auditors regularly briefed and independent expert advice sourced.

[LEAD] The trust has moved out of NOF Segment 4 into Segment 3 — but the same Q1 2026/27 data ranks it 55th of 58 providers nationally.
[Evidence] "The Trust moved from Segment 4 to Segment 3, with the average metric score improving from 3.09 to 2.73... national position 55th of 58" — pp.85 and 87, [Integrated Quality Performance Report coversheet, Item 5.1]. Finance alone "has improved to Domain 2 in the National Oversight Framework at Q1 2026/27" (p.125, FPP escalation).

[LEAD] Month 5 finance showed a £566k year-to-date deficit (£568k adverse to plan), driven partly by £357k of MARS severance payments; a £13m commissioning pressure has been whittled to about £4m, and the section 117 funding review outcome is delayed.
[Evidence] "At month 5 the Trust is reporting a YTD deficit of £566k. This is £568k adverse to plan. The in-month deficit is driven by a combination of £357k of one off MARS (Mutually Agreed Redundancy Scheme) severance payments... Section 117 remains an additional risk because the review outcome is delayed. Wider commissioning actions have already reduced the starting £13 million pressure to approximately £4 million" — pp.124–125, [FPP Committee Chairs Escalation Report, Item 6.2]. By month 6 the IQPR reports income and expenditure "breakeven on plan" at Q2, with efficiency "£6m with £1m shortfall to planned levels and a higher than planned level of non-recurrent delivery" (pp.85, 97). MARS: "31 of 41 applications were approved, with full-year savings reported as just over £1.2 million... The Equality Impact Assessment remained outstanding" (p.134, People Committee).

[LEAD] Board churn: a substantive chief medical officer (Dr Tonye Sikabofori) started 26 October, ending a near three-year acting arrangement that was itself a gap flagged in the NHSE undertakings; audit committee chair Roger Dunshea retires in November with a replacement NED still being recruited; and the trust has been picked for cohort two of NHSE's Deloitte-run Board Development Programme.
[Evidence] "I would also like to pay tribute to Roger Dunshea, our Non-Executive Director, who will retire in November 2026 after six years with the Trust... We are currently recruiting a Non-Executive Director to serve as the new Chair of the Audit Committee" — p.41, [Chair's Report, Item 3.1]. Dr Rao "will be returning to CAMHS after kindly stepping into the role of Acting Chief Medical Officer for the past three years" (p.41). "Black Country Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust has been selected to participate as part of the second cohort of NHS England's Board Development Programme, delivered by Deloitte" — p.47, [CEO Report, Item 3.2]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's £16.5m capital programme — including £8.4m for 24/7 neighbourhood mental health hubs and £3.4m for mental health emergency departments — is all still in design, with the committee told there is a risk of losing this year's funding, and £8.2m of "Left Shift" capital has been unable to proceed.
[Evidence] "the portfolio position of the five schemes with a combined stated capital value of approximately £16.505m... All schemes are in the design phase, there is a risk of losing identified funding for this year" — p.125, [FPP Committee Chairs Escalation Report, Item 6.2]. On the £3.420m MH EDs: "Analysis is continuing to determine whether these facilities are required or whether demand in the ICS dictates that investments in other areas would be more beneficial" (p.125). "Capital expenditure is behind plan with spend of £1.6m against a plan of £6.3m... Constitutional Standards and Left Shift capital of £8.2m originally planned for 26/27 has been unable to proceed as Community First programme is emergent" — p.97, [IQPR, Item 5.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] GP practices are refusing to prescribe oral antipsychotic medication where no commissioned shared-care pathway exists — escalated to the ICB "on multiple occasions" and sitting on the trust's high-level risk register as a public safety risk.
[Evidence] "a significant number of prescriptions issued by the Trust are not subsequently collected by patients, creating a risk of deterioration, relapse and potential impact on patient and public safety. The issue has been escalated to the ICB on multiple occasions" — p.121, [QSC Chairs Escalation Report, Item 6.1]. The same report challenged "over 500 overdue incidents" in Adult Services (p.121).

[WORTH WATCHING] Patients are choosing to stay in the trust's newer mental health wards as self-funders after being declared fit to leave — two older adults have stayed beyond 200 days and the trust is taking legal advice on serving notice.
[Evidence] "the issue currently related to two older adult patients whose lengths of stay exceeded 200 days... both individuals had been offered alternative placements but had chosen to remain in the Trust's newer facilities and were funding their own care... the Trust was reviewing its choice policy and seeking legal advice regarding the management of such cases... as newer facilities became established, this issue was becoming more common" — p.30, [Draft Public Trust Board Minutes, September 2026, Item 2.4]

[WORTH WATCHING] A Regulation 28 prevention of future deaths report criticised the trust's organisational learning and risk assessment practice after an informal patient died in a fall from height while on leave, and patient safety incident investigations are up 33% on last year.
[Evidence] Coroner: "I have concerns about the Trusts organisational learning from these incidents and timely implementation of the recommendations made by the external investigation report author. In addition, I have concerns about the quality and timely preparation of risk assessments" — p.153, [Patient Safety Oversight Report Q1–Q2, Item 7.2]. "Twelve PSIIs were commissioned, compared with nine in the equivalent period last year (+33%)" (p.149). Nine inquests concluded in H1, six with suicide conclusions (p.149).

[WORTH WATCHING] The charity's annual report and accounts 2025/26 are in the pack for approval: a £107k deficit funded from reserves, unrestricted funds down to £236k, and only £18k uncommitted.
[Evidence] "The Charity made a deficit for the year amounting to £107k which was funded from reserves" — p.193, [Charitable Funds Annual Report and Accounts 2025/26, Item 8.2]; "Limited uncommitted funds, £18k, with £5.7k in general fund, requiring likely tough choices" (p.184). Independent examiner (TAG Accountants Group): "no material matters have come to my attention" (p.195).

[FOI] Request the final report of the joint external review of section 117 aftercare funding commissioned with the Black Country ICB — from both BCHFT and the ICB: terms of reference, final report and the funding gap it identifies. The September minutes say the review "had been completed and that the Trust was awaiting receipt of the final report" (p.26); by the 24 September FPP meeting "the review outcome is delayed" (p.125). Section 117 sits inside the £13m→£4m commissioning pressure, the eligible cohort jumped from 1,176 to 1,393 in a month (p.105), and no data exist for the percentage of reviews done in 12 months.
[Evidence] "the joint external review of Section 117 arrangements undertaken with the Integrated Care Board (ICB) had been completed and... the Trust was awaiting receipt of the final report" — p.26, [Draft Public Trust Board Minutes, September 2026, Item 2.4]

[FOI] Request NHS England's September 2026 letter and the three Compliance Certificates on the enforcement undertakings, plus the agreed "closure tests" — from NHSE and the trust.
