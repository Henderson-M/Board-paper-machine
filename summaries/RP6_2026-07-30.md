[Evidence] "The independent reviews commissioned by the Membership Council following receipt of a letter from the Chair of Consultant Staff Committee, on behalf of consultants, are ongoing. The outcomes of these reviews will be reported to Membership Council and Board." — p.30, [Chief Executive's report]. Carried on the BAF as a CEO-owned strategic risk (entry XB, "External reviews").

[LEAD] The Oriel new-hospital build has slipped, with completion pushed back from December 2026 to as late as March 2027 after the Building Safety Regulator paused notifiable works in May.
[Evidence] "Following the instruction to pause notifiable works on 6 May 2026, the Building Safety Regulator approved the reversionary building control application... The planned completion date has been revised from 15 December 2026 to 5 February 2027. This date has not yet been accepted... BYUK currently forecasts contract completion on 19 March 2027." — p.35, [Oriel Update]. Programme carries £21.0m for known risks and £6.8m optimism bias (£5.3m Moorfields). BAF 3A cites "an ongoing risk relating to the collapse of CAI (building safety inspector for the new centre)", p.22, [BAF].

[WORTH WATCHING] The MoorConnect electronic patient record programme has been delayed, with "red rated risks" and no confirmed safe go-live date.
[Evidence] "The committee noted, at its meeting on 17 July, that there would be a delay in the delivery of the MoorConnect programme. This entry will be reviewed in light of that." — p.22, [BAF entry 3A]; "detailed planning is required to determine the earliest date that the EPR can safely go live."

[WORTH WATCHING] A trust-wide "patient letters incident" disrupted appointment communications in June, driving up missed appointments and hitting waiting-time performance.
[Evidence] "Administrative and management capacity was also affected by the Trust-wide patient letters incident... This reduced the benefit of the additional activity that had been planned to support waiting time improvements." — p.135, [IPR, Access to Services]; "increased patient enquiries following the patient letters incident" — p.133, [IPR exec summary].

[WORTH WATCHING] The migration of Bedford services caused multiple data breaches and a "greater than anticipated impact on performance due to data quality issues".
[Evidence] "the transition of Bedford services has had a greater than anticipated impact on performance due to data quality issues" — p.135, [IPR]; June minutes record "the Bedford data migration issues leading to multiple data breaches", with the ICO taking no further action — p.8-9, [Minutes 4 June 2026].

[WORTH WATCHING] Waiting-time and access metrics are failing plan: 16 patients breaching 52-week RTT in June (target ≤6), theatre utilisation at 64.9% against an 85% target, and an average call-answering wait of 590 seconds against a 120-second target.
[Evidence] "52 Week RTT Incomplete Breaches... Current Period 16... Target ≤6 Breaches" and "Average Call Waiting Time... 590... Target ≤2 Mins" — p.134, [IPR Metric Overview]; "Theatre Utilisation (MEH Definition)... 64.9%... ≥85%" and "Cataract Eyes Per Four Hour Theatre List... 6.3... ≥8" — p.134/136, [IPR].

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2026/27 efficiency programme is running behind: only £11.3m of the £17.6m target identified, with the trust forecasting £11.26m delivery — a £6.37m shortfall — and £9.3m still unidentified or non-recurrent.
[Evidence] "The Trust is forecast to deliver £11.3m of the £17.6m full year target"; "£11.26m forecast, compared to a plan of £17.63m, £6.37m adverse to plan"; "£9.3m of un-identified and non recurrently identified schemes" — p.118/121/127, [Monthly Finance Performance Report M03]. Capital is squeezed: after £16.4m of pre-commitments, current assumptions leave "only £1.3m for all other Trust wide Capital including medical devices" — p.125, [Finance report].

[WORTH WATCHING] A group of 27 intravitreal (IVT) nurses raised concerns collectively through Freedom to Speak Up about a "consultation process" — the single largest driver of the trust's Q4 speak-up spike.
[Evidence] "Q4 2025/26 saw the highest number of cases reported to the FTSU team (60) with 27 of these cases raised by a team of IVT nurses speaking up collectively regarding a consultation process." — p.65, [Freedom to Speak Up annual report]. Leadership & management (25%), employment terms & conditions (20%) and inappropriate attitudes/behaviours (17%) are the top FTSU themes.

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff-survey speaking-up confidence is deteriorating: fewer than half of staff (49.83%, down from 51.85%) are confident a raised concern would be addressed, and the trust has its largest benchmark gap on feeling safe to speak up.
[Evidence] "Q25f... fell from 51.85% to 49.83% (-2.02 percentage points), dropping below 50%... fewer than half of respondents are confident that concerns they raise will be addressed"; "Q25e... MEH remains 6.23 percentage points below the peer average, representing the largest benchmark gap" — p.72, [FTSU annual report, NHS Staff Survey 2025 triangulation]. Morale score 6.04; one-third of staff report burnout.
