# NHS Surrey and Sussex ICB board — 24 June 2026

- **Source:** Board meeting in public, NHS Surrey and Sussex ICB, 24 June 2026 (papers pack, 20 documents)
- **Pack:** 20 files (combined ~330pp)
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-24

Context: This is the new NHS Surrey and Sussex ICB, formally launched 1 April 2026 by merging the former NHS Surrey Heartlands, NHS Sussex and the Surrey Heath and Farnham parts of NHS Frimley (p.3, [Chief Executive's Report]). It covers a population of over 3 million and is reducing to "about half the size" of the two combined predecessor organisations. ods_code S9B9J.

## Top lines

[LEAD] New merged ICB cutting running costs by £79m / 58% to a £59.3m envelope, with compulsory redundancies now going to NHS England for approval.
[Evidence] "The target operating cost envelope is £59.3m, representing a reduction of £79.0m (58%) from baseline costs." — p.7, [2026/27 Financial Plan update]; and "Where this is not possible, we will be making applications to NHS England for compulsory redundancy in due course." — p.4, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] ICB carrying a £166.1m efficiency requirement for 2026/27 to hold breakeven, with £11.6m still unidentified and a £25m unresolved contract gap with providers.
[Evidence] "The plan includes a total efficiency requirement of £166.1m. Of this, £154.5m (93%) has been identified, leaving a residual gap of £11.6m." and "the variance between commissioner and provider positions reducing from £75.0m to £25.0m." — p.1, [2026/27 Financial Plan update]

[LEAD] System breakeven in 2025/26 was propped up by £60.7m of Deficit Support Funding in Sussex alone; underlying Sussex providers ran £44.3m adverse before DSF, led by UHSx at £26.2m.
[Evidence] "Providers ex DSF ... (£44.3m) ... UHSx ... (£26.2m) ... Deficit Support Funding £3.7m £20.1m £16.4m £44.3m £60.7m" — p.29, [Annual Assessment 2025/26 Pack, Balancing Commissioner and Provider Budget – Sussex]

[LEAD] Sussex legacy BAF states the system was "overfunded by circa £186m" and needed deficit support to break even, warning it is "both clinically and financially unsustainable" without transformation.
[Evidence] "Sussex was overfunded by circa £186m and required deficit support funding to deliver a breakeven plan for 2025/26. Unless the system was able to transform services to deliver care within its funding allocation it would be both clinically and financially unsustainable" — p. [Appendix B, NHS Sussex BAF year-end], [Board Assurance Framework]

[LEAD] UHSx has failed to exit Tier 1 oversight for planned care "despite two years of intensive oversight and support" and missed its CIP by £12.1m.
[Evidence] "However, UHSx has failed to exit Tier 1 oversight for planned care despite two years of intensive oversight and support, with ongoing concerns regarding accuracy of forecasting, delivery of planned activity and ability to sustain improvements." — p.7 [Elective Care], [Annual Assessment 2025/26 Pack]; and "UHSx under delivered by £12.1m due delays in key schemes such as the medical rate card" — p.30, same pack

[LEAD] £389.9m system capital plan flags cash constraints in Sussex as a specific delivery risk, with national Estates Safety and Constitutional Standards funding still not signed off by NHSE.
[Evidence] "There is a risk that delays to capital investments within Sussex arise due to constraints on the availability of cash." — p.15; and "Proposals on the use of Estates Safety and Constitutional standards are subject to the agreement by NHSE, which has yet to take place." — p.7, [Joint Capital Resource Use Plans]

[WORTH WATCHING] SASH named as the driver of Surrey's provider overspend, missing its 2025/26 CIP by £7.7m amid ward closures.
[Evidence] "the provider position was £11.3m adverse to plan, reflecting pressures at SASH arising from an efficiency delivery shortfall" and "SASH 33.3 25.5 (7.7)" — p.27-28, [Annual Assessment 2025/26 Pack]; "Estate challenges such as ward closures at SASH placed pressure on the Trust's bed base" — p.7

[WORTH WATCHING] Health Bill (passed 1 June 2026) removes the duty for ICBs to include GP, local authority and trust representatives, reshaping ICB board composition.
[Evidence] "The Health Bill, passed on 1 June 2026 ... contained changes to ICB compositions - removing the duty to have representatives from GPs, local authorities and trusts. ICBs can choose to retain these roles if preferred." — p.4, [Transition Committee]

[WORTH WATCHING] Creation of a single Kent, Medway, Surrey and Sussex Digital, Data and Intelligence directorate, hosted by NHS Surrey and Sussex, with staff consultation launching end June 2026.
[Evidence] "A single Surrey and Sussex-hosted DDI function, has been agreed in principle ... The operating model will serve Surrey and Sussex, Kent and Medway plus some other southeast region ICBs for specific functions within DDI." — p.6, [Transition Committee]

[WORTH WATCHING] Corridor care is a named consequence in the in-common UEC strategic risk, scored 16 (Significant); A&E four-hour performance 72.4% (Surrey) / 76.7% (Sussex), 12-hour waits 12.0% / 11.1%.
[Evidence] "RESULTING IN ambulance handover delays; delayed assessment and treatment of patients; corridor care; negative impact on staff." — p. [ID19 UEC risk], [Board Assurance Framework]; performance figures p.5 [Urgent and Emergency Care], [Annual Assessment 2025/26 Pack]

[FOI] Multiple "tranches" of compulsory redundancy business cases have been approved by the Remuneration Committee and submitted to NHSE — numbers, total severance cost and grade breakdown not disclosed.
[Evidence] "Items for decision have included individual compulsory redundancy cases ... as well as several 'tranches' of compulsory redundancy cases ... Compulsory Redundancy business cases approved by the Committee have been submitted for onward consideration by NHS England (NHSE)". Committee met extraordinarily six times Apr–Jun 2026. — p.1, [Remuneration Committee report]. Request: number of compulsory redundancies, total redundancy/severance spend, and any exit packages over £100k, from NHS Surrey and Sussex ICB.

[FOI] NHSE's 2025/26 annual assessment of the legacy ICBs is due as a letter from the Regional Director to the ICB Chair by end July 2026, following the assessment meeting on 22 June 2026 — contents not in pack.
[Evidence] "The outcome of the annual assessment will take the form of a letter sent from the relevant NHSE Regional Director to the ICB Chair by the end of July 2026." — p.1, [Annual Assessment 2025/26]. Request: the NHSE annual assessment letters for NHS Surrey Heartlands and NHS Sussex 2025/26.

[FOI] CSU "stranded costs" — national funding confirmed but allocation undisclosed; CSUs to be dissolved by March 2027.
[Evidence] "Whilst it has been confirmed that there is national funding to support CSU closure ... detail on how this will be allocated is yet to be confirmed." — p.6, [Transition Committee]. Request: the ICB's estimate of CSU stranded costs and any correspondence with NHSE / Office of the South East ICBs on the funding allocation.
