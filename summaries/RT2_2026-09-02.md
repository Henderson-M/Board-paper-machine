## Top lines

[LEAD] Pennine Care sits in segment 4 — the lowest band — on the national metric for urgent mental health crisis response, and is not forecasting compliance until 2027.
[Evidence] "[The proportion of] people accepted with urgent mental health needs remains below the expected standard, with variation across localities. **PCFT are currently in Segment 4 (lowest performing) against this metric.**" — p.215, [Crisis offer and performance report]
[Evidence] "The current NOF thresholds for this metric are as follows: Segment 1 – Above 80%; Segment 2 – 71%-79%; Segment 3 – 44%-70%; **Segment 4 – Below 44%.** The Trust is targeting delivery of over 73% compliance by September 2026 and over 80% by January 2027." — p.216, [same]
[Evidence] "Performance against the NOF 24-hour face-to-face crisis contact standard remains below expectation, although recovery actions improved the Trust position to **49% by 18 August 2026**; the ambition is to exceed 73% by September 2026 and 80% by January 2027." — [same]

[LEAD] The trust is in segment 3 overall and ranked 39th of 61 non-acute trusts, with the board assurance framework recording weakness across four domains.
[Evidence] "The Trust was placed in **Segment 3 of the NHS Oversight Framework, indicating below-average performance. The trust was ranked 39th out of 61 non-acute trusts**, reflecting low performance across several key domains, including access to services, effectiveness and experience, patient safety, and workforce." — [Board Assurance Framework, risk WL23 Financial sustainability plan]
[Evidence] The same BAF entry records that the financial position "remained a concern due to a **continued reliance on non-recurrent measures**." — [same]

[LEAD] The trust forecasts break-even but its board is being told the risks around that forecast are significant.
[Evidence] "Members noted that the Trust was **forecasting a break-even position for year-end, however a number of risks existed around this forecast.** Target reductions in bank staff and agency spend were highlighted and the Trust's cash position remained above plan" — [Finance and Performance Committee report]
[Evidence] "Members received assurance the Trust remained on plan year-to-date and in forecast terms. A reduction in the forecast deficit position had been achieved, supported by improvements in bank and agency expenditure and improvements within both clinical networks... **Significant risks remained regarding delivery of the Value Improvement P[rogramme]**" — [Month 4 Finance Report, item 6.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] The audit committee has issued an "Alert" asking for a deep dive into disciplinary cases, racism and disproportionality — and into cases running six months or longer.
[Evidence] "cases lasting six months or longer. **Consider a broader deep dive into disciplinary matters, racism and disproportionality. Alert**" — p.489, [Committee key issues report — rated "Alert" on the trust's own Not Assured / Moderate / Assured scale]
[Evidence] The trust's anti-racism action plan records that formal board oversight of racism investigations is not yet established: "Establish formal Board oversight of racism investigations (incl. annual deep dive). Reporting agreed **31/01/2027**... **Reporting currently through PWC**" — [Anti-racism / EDI action plan]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board is being told it has only limited assurance it could survive a cyber attack lasting weeks rather than days.
[Evidence] "Local Business continuity plans are primarily designed to manage short term disruption measured in hours or days. **These arrangements provide limited assurance against prolonged cyber incidents, where systems and data may be unavailable for weeks or longer.** Further work is required to strengthen assurance that critical services can be sustained safely over extended periods of digital disruptio[n]" — [Board Assurance Framework, cyber risk]
[Evidence] "there is limited assurance regarding the scalability and sustainability of specialist cyber, digital and decision making cover during prolonged incidents requiring extended out of hours operation over weeks rather than days." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] Violence and aggression incidents are showing statistically significant deterioration, concentrated in Tameside.
[Evidence] "**Incidents of Violence and Aggression: are showing Special Cause Variation of a Concerning Nature**, Tameside continues to report significa[nt numbers]" — [Integrated Performance Report]
[Evidence] For context the same report notes the trust "continues to benchmark well nationally, remaining in the lowest quartile on the NHS England Restrictive Interventions National Benchmarking Dashboard for physical restraint." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The inquest load is about to more than double.
[Evidence] "**19 inquests were held between 1 June and 31 July 2026. No Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths notices were issued to the Trust**, and the Coroner requested no additional assurance responses during the reporting period. However, **23 further inquests are listed between 1 August and 30 September 2026.**" — [Learning from deaths / inquests report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Deputy chief executive appointed from within, taking the role alongside an existing executive portfolio.
[Evidence] "**Gaynor Mullins, our Chief Strategy and Partnerships Officer, has been appointed as Deputy Chief Executive, alongside her current role.** Gaynor joined Pennine Care seven years ago and brings over 30 years of NHS experience, with a strong track record in leading large-scale, complex transformation programmes." — p.28, [Chief Executive's report, Leadership updates]
[Evidence] "Dr Neil Crossley has been appointed as clinical director for Bury mental health services." — [same]
[Evidence] "Anthony Hassall, our chief executive, has been appointed a Visiting Professor at Manchester Metropolitan University." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] Limited assurance recorded on medicines safety in early intervention services, unresolved since 2024.
[Evidence] "**2057.05-24: If Early Intervention (EI) do not have a funded pharmacy service, then there is limited assurance around safe medicines practices and medicines optimisation as well as limited to no clinical input into patient care.** Pharmacy actively involved in EI transformation, EI Standard Operating Procedure review and business case to progress risk resolution." — [Significant risk profile, risks scoring 15+]
[Evidence] A separate internal audit "initial finding was of limited assurance however all bar one action had been closed down by April 2026. The remaining outstanding action was **the signing of the service level agreement with the NCA** which was due to sickness of key staff" — [Audit report summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board pack records the change of health secretary and a new junior minister who is a local MP.
[Evidence] "**Yvette Cooper MP has been appointed as the new Secretary of State for Health and Social Care**, taking over responsibility for the NHS, social care and wider health policy. **James Frith, Bury North MP, has been appointed as Health and Social Care Under-Secretary.** The Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman will become the public service ombudsman later this year." — [Chief Executive's report, policy update]

## FOI leads

[FOI] **The disciplinary, racism and disproportionality data behind the audit committee's Alert.** *Request from Pennine Care:* the number of formal disciplinary cases opened in each of the last three years broken down by ethnicity and staff group, the number lasting six months or longer, the number of racism investigations, and any completed deep dive. *Why it matters:* the committee has escalated this to the board as an "Alert" but no figures appear in the public papers.

[FOI] **PwC's role in racism investigation reporting.** The anti-racism action plan records that reporting on racism investigations is "currently through PWC" pending board oversight from January 2027. *Request from Pennine Care:* the contract with PwC covering this work — value, term, scope and deliverables — and the reports produced.

[FOI] **The crisis response recovery plan.** *Request from Pennine Care and NHS Greater Manchester ICB:* the locality-level 24-hour crisis contact performance data, the recovery plan and trajectory to 73%/80%, and any NHS England correspondence about the segment 4 rating on this metric.

[FOI] **The Value Improvement Programme.** *Request from Pennine Care:* the scheme register with recurrent/non-recurrent split and RAG ratings, and the schedule of non-recurrent measures relied on to hold the break-even forecast.

[FOI] **The cyber business continuity gap.** *Request from Pennine Care:* the cyber incident response plan approved in 2026, the business continuity assessment underpinning the "limited assurance" finding, and any tabletop exercise reports covering multi-week outages.
