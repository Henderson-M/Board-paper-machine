## Top lines

[LEAD] Medway Community Healthcare, which Kent Community Health Foundation Trust absorbs on 1 October, has nearly 3,500 patients waiting more than 52 weeks and is meeting its 18-week access standard for 42.7 per cent of patients against a 92 per cent target.
[Evidence] Subcontractor performance table for July 2026 records MCH "52+ Week Waits" of 3,489 (down from 3,724 in April) and "Non Consultant-Led Access Waits (18 Weeks)" of 42.7 per cent against a 92.0 per cent target; average length of stay 30.29 days against a 21-day standard; bed occupancy 96.1 per cent against a 90 per cent target; "MCH's No Criteria to Reside rate increased from 5.9% to 10.8%". Narrative text on the same page states "MCH's 52+ week waits reduced to 3,469", which does not match the 3,489 figure in the table on that page. – p.239, [Integrated Performance Report, Community Services sub-contractor performance, Item 15]

[LEAD] A board assurance framework action to renegotiate the funding of Medway Community Healthcare activity is overdue three weeks before the integration completes, with a fallback plan drawn up in case the money is not rebalanced.
[Evidence] Action "Active discussion at CEO Forum to rebalance the financing of activity delivered by MCH", owners "Gordon Flack / Mairead McCormick", target completion "1 September 2026", status "Overdue", update: "Meeting scheduled for 4 September 2026, mitigation plan developed in the event finances not rebalanced, verbal progress can be reported at Board." The same risk lists among its top five causes "MCH deteriorating financial position, increased CIP target, and non-currency of CIP". – p.58, [Board Assurance Framework, risk ID 6960 Financial Sustainability and Strategic Investment, Item 8]

[LEAD] The trust chair has given his own programme of non-executive service visits a "Limited" assurance rating, with two issues escalated immediately, one of them a medicines policy restriction affecting access to anaphylaxis treatment.
[Evidence] "Assurance rating: Limited. There is strong positive frontline evidence, but recurring workforce and change concerns, wider control gaps and incomplete triangulation prevent a higher rating." Ten visits generated 135 findings. "Two separate issues also required Immediate Escalation. The first relates to a gap in the commissioned pathway for children under eight with avoidant or restrictive eating needs. As the ICB owns the commissioning response, the Trust should formally raise the gap and monitor any related risk within its own services. The second relates to a reported medicines-policy issue affecting access to anaphylaxis treatment. This requires prompt review within the Trust, with appropriate interim controls." – p.29 and p.32, [Chair's Report, Thematic review of Trust Chair and NED visits, Item 6]

[LEAD] The number of open employment tribunals at the trust is the highest it has ever recorded, and police involvement is delaying some internal cases.
[Evidence] "The Committee noted that the number of open employment tribunals was at the highest level experienced by the Trust, largely because national listing delays have created a cumulative backlog rather than because of a substantial increase in new claims. Police involvement was also delaying some internal cases." – p.184, [People Committee chair's assurance report, 26 August 2026, Item 13]

[LEAD] The People Committee has escalated to the board a "limited" assurance rating on the national NHS People Portal programme, because its timing collides with the Medway integration.
[Evidence] "The Committee identified a significant delivery risk arising from the scale and complexity of the Future Workforce Solution transformation programme (now renamed the People Portal). The Committee was alerted to uncertainty about the system's functions and the direct overlap of timings for the transformation programme with the Medway Community Healthcare (MCH) integration. The Committee assessed assurance as limited and agreed that programme readiness, capacity pressures and associated risks should be escalated to the Board." Rated "Alert". The CEO's report states KCHFT is an "early adopter organisation" for the portal, which "will eventually replace ESR", with go-live "between February and March 2028". – p.184, [People Committee chair's assurance report, Item 13]; p.42, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 7]

[LEAD] The Quality Committee has told executives that cutting the cyber security risk score was premature and blocked any reduction until a deep dive is completed.
[Evidence] "The Committee was concerned that the reduction in the cybersecurity rating from 16 to 12 was premature, although members recognised that the latest controls had reduced the likelihood of a cyber incident occurring but had not reduced the potential consequence should such an incident take place... members asked for the completion of the cybersecurity deep-dive through the Audit and Risk Committee before any reduction in the current risk rating of 16 is considered." Rated "Advise". – p.190, [Quality Committee chair's assurance report, 3 September 2026, Item 14]

[LEAD] More than a third of patients in the trust's community hospital beds have no criteria to reside, against a 15 per cent target, and the trust has found its own recording of the measure is inconsistent.
[Evidence] "KPI 4.7 Community No Criteria to Reside is in special cause variation (declining) at 35.2%, materially above the 15% target. The position reflects both operational-flow and data-quality issues." And: "a deep dive into the assessment and reporting of NCTR status has identified site level variation and coding inconsistencies and variation." – p.196 and p.226, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 15]

[WORTH WATCHING] BME staff at the trust are now almost twice as likely as white staff to enter a formal disciplinary process, worse than the community trust peer average, and the trust has removed a set of cases from the metric from April 2026.
[Evidence] WRES metric 3 relative likelihood 1.94 in 2025-26, up from 1.10 in 2024-25, against a community trust peer group average of 1.35. "We report this figure to the Board on a monthly basis; in response to the worsening trend, the Board requested further analysis... In these circumstances the Trust had followed a process that would not be regarded as a disciplinary process within the meaning of the technical definition in the WRES guidance. Following this analysis, data from April 2026 does not include these cases." The monthly dashboard reports the same metric at 2.56 and says "several cases which were not appropriate to report per the definitions of this metric... has subsequently been removed. We have also further defined the entry gate for cases being reportable per this metric". The WRES dashboard also records "0.0% of our executive Board members are from BME backgrounds". – p.85, p.97, p.98, p.238 and p.83, [Workforce Race Equality Standard Report 2026, Item 10; Integrated Performance Report Workforce EDI Dashboard, Item 15]

[WORTH WATCHING] The CQC is inspecting three of the trust's community hospital inpatient sites, and at the first of them both patients and staff told inspectors staffing levels were too low.
[Evidence] "The Care Quality Commission (CQC) undertook a comprehensive assessment of West View Rehabilitation Unit on 30 June and 1 July 2026, reviewing 34 quality statements... CQC also conducted this inspection under their new hybrid approach and piloted the draft sector-specific framework, which we consented to... Both patients and staff raised concerns around staffing levels, which are perceived to be too low, including nursing and therapy staff." And: "The CQC announced an inspection of the inpatient ward at Victoria Hospital, Deal, on Tuesday 2 September. We expect a third inpatient site to be confirmed for inspection shortly." The trust's current overall CQC rating is recorded as "Outstanding (July 2019)". – p.37 and p.38, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 7]; p.198, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 15]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Kent and Medway Provider Alliance is recruiting a chief executive and two managing directors, and the ICB has ordered an independent review of all-age mental health, learning disability, autism and ADHD services.
[Evidence] "Recruitment is now underway for a chief executive to lead the Alliance, alongside two Managing Director roles to lead the Acute and Neighbourhood Collaboratives. This marks an important step in moving the Alliance from its development phase into a more formal delivery model". And: "the ICB has announced an independent review of all-age mental health, learning disability, autism and ADHD services across Kent and Medway. The review will examine how services are commissioned and delivered across the four layers of care". – p.36 and p.37, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] Just over half of respondents to the trust's own wellbeing survey reported burnout, and fewer than half felt prepared for the year ahead, at a trust that scored above sector average on every element of the 2025 staff survey.

The staff standards paper states that in "the 2025 NHS Staff Survey, in which KCHFT scored above the sector average across all survey elements". – p.42, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 7]; p.72, [NHS Staff Standards, Item 9]
