## Top lines

[LEAD] Underlying finances have deteriorated to a forecast £7m deficit reliant on non-recurrent savings — despite the public board performance report reporting "breakeven" at month 2.
[Evidence] "Year-to-date position was on plan but supported by non-recurrent items and subject to significant assumptions. Underlying financial position had deteriorated with a forecast £7m deficit and reliance on non-recurrent savings. Savings delivery remained behind plan… The sensitivity analysis indicated a potential risk to the financial position of £6.4m if mitigations are not delivered." — p.786, [Performance and Finance Committee Chair's Assurance Report, 24 June 2026, item 6.1, rated "Not Assured/Limited Assurance"]. Cf. "At month 2 the financial position is breakeven." — p.546, [Integrated Performance Report]

[LEAD] Pennine Care has dropped a segment in NHS England's Oversight Framework, from Segment 3 to Segment 4, with its national ranking among non-acute trusts sliding from 40/61 to 51/61 over the year.
[Evidence] "Following the Quarter 4 data refresh of the NHS Oversight Framework, Pennine Care NHS Foundation Trust has dropped from Segment 3 to Segment 4 and our overall our ranking against 'other Non-Acute Trust' has slipped from 40/61 in Quarter 3 to 51/61. Across the Northwest Providers we ranked 3rd." — p.552, [Integrated Performance Report]. F&P Committee flagged it "Not Assured": "The Trust's NOF score has declined to Segment 4 - driven by crisis performance, CYP access, staff survey and sickness metrics. This is a concern." — p.787, [Performance and Finance Committee Chair's Report, item 7.6]

[LEAD] CQC rated the trust "Good" for well-led — its first "good" leadership rating in 10 years — but the underlying core-service picture is mixed, with the trust having been issued Section 29A warning notices in Nov 2024 (older adults) and July 2025 (acute/PICU).
[Evidence] "This assessment gave the trust an overall rating of good for its leadership, culture and governance. There were no breaches of regulation although there were a few areas where the trust should make some improvements." — p.505, [CQC Well-Led assessment report, published 17 June 2026]. "Acute and Psychiatric Intensive Care Units (PICU) services were rated Requires Improvement, with a Section 29A Warning Notice issued to drive rapid improvement in safety, medicines management and oversight." — p.506, [CQC Well-Led report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Six of the trust's 12 CQC-inspected core services remain rated Requires Improvement overall, including acute/PICU wards, older people's wards and crisis services.
[Evidence] Older people's wards, acute wards/PICU, crisis services and community MH services for adults all rated "Requires Improvement" overall in inspections Nov 2024–Oct 2025. — p.498–499, [CQC inspection activity update, item 7.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] CQC's well-led report records governors reporting they felt excluded from trust decision-making — a governance note landing as the 10 Year Health Plan proposes scrapping FT councils of governors.
[Evidence] "governors described having inconsistent involvement in shaping the trust's strategic direction. They reported feeling excluded from key decision-making forums, unclear on how national priorities were applied locally, and uncertain about their evolving role." — p.512, [CQC Well-Led report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Restraint and violence-and-aggression rates are flagged as "special cause variation of a concerning nature," with restraint concentrated in PICU and CAMHS.
[Evidence] "Use of Restraints per 1000 Occupied Bed Days: is showing Special Cause Variation of a Concerning Nature… the overall rate remains elevated and warrants ongoing focus. Higher levels of restraint continue to be concentrated within PICU and CAMHS services." — p.543, [Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The risk register has grown to 649 risks, with 46 scoring 15+, including three at the top score of 20 (cyber security, CAMHS capacity/demand, and backlog maintenance) plus a Tameside inpatient overspend.
[Evidence] "649 risks are currently on the register (an increase from previous report)… There are 46 risks scoring 15 or above." — p.66, [Board Risk report]; risks scoring 20: "Increased cyber security risk," "CAMHs Capacity & Demand," "Backlog maintenance identified as high risk"; and "Expenditure is currently exceeding allocated budget due to increased spending on the inpatient units (Tameside)" (15) — p.72–74, Appendix 1.

[FOI] The trust self-assessed as "Standards Not Met" against the Data Security and Protection Toolkit — request the full self-assessment, the improvement plan and any NHSE correspondence.
[Evidence] "Trust has self-assessed as 'Standards Not Met.' Significant improvement, with compliance reaching 94%; however, key risks remain in supply chain assurance and information asset register completeness." — p.788, [Performance and Finance Committee Chair's Report, item 9.1, rated red]

[FOI] The trust's Value Improvement Programme (savings) is behind plan with a £6.4m sensitivity risk and a "list of potential contingencies against shortfall" — request the VIP recovery plan, contingency list and the Part 2 (private) risk-assessed scenario analysis going to board.
[Evidence] "The Committee reviewed and commended the list of potential contingencies against shortfall in VIP, albeit further work was required. Commitment to report risk assessed scenario analysis to Board (Part 2)." — p.786, [Performance and Finance Committee Chair's Report, item 6.1]
