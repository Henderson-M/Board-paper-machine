## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust expects to stay in NHSE's lowest oversight tier (Segment 4), blaming sickness rates and neurodevelopmental waits, with a new community-provider framework due that could re-rate it from Q1.
[Evidence] "We expect to remain in Segment 4 because of relatively high sickness rates and neurodevelopmental pathway waiting times but may have improved our average score again. A revised framework for 2026/7 is also expected to be published soon including a wider range of indicator for providers of community services with Quarter 1 ratings expected around the end of August." — p.7, [ENC 5 CEO Report May 2026]

[LEAD] External auditors say BCHC's 2025/26 reported surplus was created solely by two late NHSE cash injections — strip them out and the trust only broke even.
[Evidence] "An operating deficit of £1.9m (2024/25: a deficit of £2.9m); and an adjusted financial performance under the control total of £3.7m surplus... the £3.7m surplus was driven entirely by two non-recurrent year end allocations from NHS England, without these the Trust would have landed at break-even." — VFM section, [ENC 7 Draft NHS Annual Audit Report 25-26, Forvis Mazars]

[LEAD] Auditors flag that £14m of the trust's £17m 2026/27 efficiency requirement is rated "high risk", with every penny of the three-year savings plan high-risk and recurrent.
[Evidence] "2026/27: £17m of which all are recurrent and £14m are deemed to be high risk – 4.3% of operating expenditure; 2027/28: £9m which are all recurrent and high risk... 2028/29: £9m which are all recurrent and high risk." — VFM Financial Sustainability section, [ENC 7 Draft NHS Annual Audit Report 25-26, Forvis Mazars]

[LEAD] The trust's largest community contract — the Birmingham 0-19 children's service — is up for re-tender and BCHC has submitted a bid; loss is logged as a current red risk (rated 15) with "significant loss of income" and workforce instability.
[Evidence] Risk 4211: "There is a risk that the Trust may not be awarded the 0-19 contract (Health Visiting, School Nursing, BFS partnership) beyond the current contract which expires on 28.02.27. This would result in significant loss of income into the C&F Division, instability for the Division and it's workforce, and the reputational impact of losing a large community based service for children, young people and their families across Birmingham." (current rating 15, initial 20) — p.6, [Corporate Risk Register June 2026]; "We have submitted our bid in response to the City Council's tender for an integrated 0 – 19 years Healthy Child Programme." — p.5, [ENC 5 CEO Report May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Month 1 deficit of £174k against a planned breakeven, driven by CIP under-delivery; trust says a £0.3m residual savings gap for the year is still unfound.
[Evidence] "At Month 1 we have reported a deficit of £174k against a planned breakeven position... recurrent CIPs of £12.8m have been identified against a target of £14.1m... non-recurrent schemes of £2.7m have been identified, leaving a residual balance of £0.3m to find. Work is ongoing to ensure that the remaining gap is closed." — p.2, [ENC 17 Month 1 Finance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Very long neurodevelopmental waits: 4,589 children have waited over 104 weeks, out of 10,631 waiting over 52 weeks; plan to move to a "needs-led" model due end of June.
[Evidence] "10,631 children and young people waiting over 52 weeks, 4,589 of whom have waited over 104 weeks." — p.4, [ENC 5 CEO Report May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Auditors note BCHC's NHS Staff Survey results sit below the community-trust average (though improving on prior year and with a record 63% response rate).
[Evidence] "Whilst the results do identify that the Trust is performing lower than other Community Trust's this offers a limited comparable... whilst below the community trust average, is not indicative of a significant weakness." Example metric: "We are compassionate and inclusive 7.30 [2024] 7.47 [2025] 7.70 [average] Below Average." — VFM Improving Economy section, [ENC 7 Draft NHS Annual Audit Report 25-26]

[WORTH WATCHING] WRES/WDES report finds recruitment inequality has worsened and disciplinary inequality is "emerging as a concern", with senior-leadership gaps widening; refreshed action plan not due until September 2026.
[Evidence] "Recruitment inequality has worsened... Disciplinary inequality emerging as a concern... Workforce diversity improving, but senior gaps widening... a revised action plan will be developed and brought to the People and Culture Committee in September 2026." — pp.1-2 of paper, [ENC 14 WRES & WDES Data Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Sickness absence remains a flagged workforce concern at 6.4% (April), above last year's 5.9% and ranging up to 7.9% in adult community services; vacancies up to 8.5% after establishment reset.
[Evidence] "Sickness absence reduced slightly to 6.4% in April but this remains higher than last year (5.9%). Sickness ranged from 7.9% in Adult Community Services to 4.4% in corporate teams... The resetting of establishments for the start of the year has increased vacancies to 8.5% (457 WTE vacancies)." — p.4, [ENC 5 CEO Report May 2026]
