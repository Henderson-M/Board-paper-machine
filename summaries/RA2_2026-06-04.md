## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust's board assurance framework risk has risen 25% since January and now sits 137% above the board's own risk appetite.
[Evidence] "The overall level of risk has increased by 25% since Board review in January. This has been caused by the addition of a new entry (BAF10) and an increase in the score of BAF9." … "The total tolerable risk score is 51. Therefore, the Trust is currently holding 137% more risk than the Board are comfortable with." — p.179 (BAF p.4 of 4), [5.2 Board Assurance Framework / BAF May 2026]

[LEAD] A new strategic risk on neighbourhood health has been added to the BAF at the maximum-tier score of 20, with the trust blaming reduced ICB capacity.
[Evidence] "This new risk has been proposed to cover the impact that community provision has on demand for services at Royal Surrey, especially urgent and emergency care… an ongoing issue that has recently been exacerbated by the recent reduction in the capacity of the Integrated Care Board… this entry is allocated a high risk score of 20 presently." — p.178 (BAF p.3 of 4), [5.2 BAF May 2026]

[LEAD] Finance month-1 carries c.£3.5m income risk from "undercommissioning" of the elective activity plan, plus a CIP gap, against a £31m savings requirement for 2026/27.
[Evidence] "The current risk profile includes c£3.5m income risk linked to undercommisioning of the Trust's Elective Activity Plan, a gap in CNST of c£0.5m and gap on the CIP plan for fully approved schemes." … "The Divisional CIP Target for the year is £22.7m, with a further £8.3m of CIP target held centrally." — p.133, p.132 (Finance Update p.3/3, p.2/3), [3.2 Finance update – Month 1]

[LEAD] Month-1 pay CIPs are already under-delivering and the trust is breaching NHSE's agency and bank spend caps.
[Evidence] "Pay costs for M1 were (£31.17m) (£0.57m) higher than plan, mainly due to pay CIPs assumed in the plan not yet materialising." … "In M1, we were (£0.04m) above the Agency Cap and (£0.27m) above the Bank Cap." Performance slide headed: "Pay – We are not delivering against the NHSE Agency/Bank reduction targets". — p.132 (Finance Update p.2/3); p.142 (Finance Appendix A p.8/12), [3.2 / 3.2a Finance Update M1]

[LEAD] A maternal death has been referred to MNSI, with an immediate review under way.
[Evidence] "One case (a maternal death) was referred to MNSI and immediate review identified key actions being undertaken." — p.20 (Operational Performance cover p.1/2), [1.8 Operational Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Corridor care peaked at its highest recorded level in January 2026 and continues to be used as an escalation response.
[Evidence] Corridor Care chart shows 1,362 patients in corridor care in Jan-26 (highest bar on the Apr-24 to Apr-26 series; Mar-26 889, Apr-26 960). "Corridor Care at RSCH is being utilised as a contingency response to periods of extreme operational pressure… Corridor Care is predominantly used when system pressures are highest (e.g. OPEL 4, >92% occupancy)." — p.88 (Corridor Care p.4/6), [2.4 Actions to virtually eliminate corridor care]

[WORTH WATCHING] A cohort of maxillofacial patients referred by dentists was not triaged correctly, leading to "avoidable long waits".
[Evidence] "A waiting list issue was identified in Maxillofacial Surgery, where a cohort of patients referred by dentists through the Rego referral system had not been triaged correctly, leading to avoidable long waits. All affected patients have been contacted and booked (where required), and the cause has been rectified." — p.20 (Operational Performance cover p.1/2), [1.8 Operational Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is replacing its internal auditors after a tender, with BDO out and TIAA in.
[Evidence] "BDO were to be replaced by TIAA as internal auditors, with the procurement process to be reviewed given feedback from the candidates that had submitted bids… Grant Thornton were to be retained for the counter fraud service." [ACTION] "the Chief People Officer to raise concerns over the internal audit procurement process with Healthcare Partners Limited." — p.11 (Minutes, TB1 55.26), [1.4 Public Board Draft Minutes 26 March 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust expects ~£700k of pay-bill savings in 2026/27 from a Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme, with 26 of 44 applications approved.
[Evidence] "The Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme had closed, with 26 of the 44 applications received being approved which would save approximately £700,000 from the pay bill for 2026 – 27." — p.6 (Minutes, TB1 39.26), [1.4 Public Board Draft Minutes 26 March 2026]
