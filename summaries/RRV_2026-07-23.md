## Top lines

[LEAD] Human Tissue Authority found one critical and seven major shortfalls at UCLH's mortuaries, including post-mortem tissue stored for an indeterminate period on unlicensed premises without the Designated Individual's knowledge — the full inspection report is published in this pack.
[Evidence] "Although the HTA found that UCL Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust ('the establishment') had met the majority of the HTA's standards, one critical, 7 major and 9 minor shortfalls were found against standards for Consent, Governance and quality systems, Traceability and Premises, facilities and equipment." — p.132, [Inspection report on compliance with HTA licensing standards, inspection date 24 and 25 September 2025, HTA licensing number 12054; appendix to Agenda Item 7]
[Evidence] "Post-mortem tissue consisting of blocks and slides retained for a scheduled purpose were discovered to have been stored for an indeterminate period on unlicensed premises. The Designated Individual (DI) had neither oversight of nor awareness that this tissue was being stored on unlicensed premises, which represents a serious failure in governance and regulatory compliance." — p.133, [HTA inspection report, Critical Shortfalls, standard T1g]

[LEAD] The chief executive's report summarises the same mortuary work as positive assurance and does not put the critical shortfall to the board in the report body; the detail sits only in the appendix.
[Evidence] CEO report: "The review found that the Trust has appropriate governance, leadership, safeguarding, security and quality assurance arrangements in place across its mortuary services at UCH, NHNN and the maternity bereavement service." and "Following an HTA inspection in 2025, all identified actions have been completed and the Trust was confirmed as fully compliant in May 2026." — p.103, [Chief Executive and Senior Directors Team report, Agenda Item 7]
[INFERENCE] The contrast between the CEO summary language and the appendix findings is the analyst's characterisation; the pack does not itself draw the comparison.

[LEAD] UCLH has identified only two-thirds of its £92.6m efficiency target for 2026/27, with 15 of 21 clinical areas missing the internal identification milestone and a £39.2m recurrent gap left in the position.
[Evidence] "On a full-year basis, the Trust has identified £61.4m (66%) of the £92.6m efficiencies target for the year. Fifteen out of 21 clinical areas have failed to meet the Financial Improvement and Recovery Board Committee (FIRB) target of 50% identification of annual targets by the end of June." and "The full-year effect of the identified recurrent schemes (i.e. impact across a full 12 months) is £53.4m, which currently represents an ongoing gap of £39.2m in the Trust's financial position." — p.171, [Chief Financial Officer's report — Month 3 2026/27, Agenda Item 8]

[LEAD] The trust's reported month 3 surplus was delivered by pulling non-recurrent benefits forward from quarter four; stripped out, UCLH was £2.2m adverse in month and the underlying clinical board run rate deteriorated.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a £4.8m surplus on a control total basis in June, a £4.4m in-month favourable variance… The month 3 position incorporates £2.2m of non-recurrent benefits in the other corporate budgets and ITDA rephased from Quarter 4 to the current month… Without the reprofiling adjustments, the Trust would have been £2.2m adverse to plan in-month." — p.170, [CFO's report — Month 3 2026/27]
[Evidence] "Excluding the non-recurrent measures, the clinical board budget variances worsened from a net £0.8m adverse variance to plan in month 2 to a £2.2m adverse variance in month 3, suggesting a deterioration in the underlying run rates within the clinical boards." — p.170, [CFO's report — Month 3 2026/27]

[LEAD] UCLH is under enhanced NHS England regional oversight for elective waits after being given a London Provider Risk Rating of 3 out of 4.
[Evidence] "The Trust has been given a London Provider Risk Rating of 3 (1 = best, 4 = worst) for its RTT performance and is therefore subject to enhanced oversight from the NHSE regional team. Based on the most recent weekly waiting-list-minimum-dataset, UCLH is ranked 10th out of 17 Trusts in London for its RTT performance" — p.85, [Performance report, Agenda Item 5]

[LEAD] Internal audit has given UCLH amber/red annual opinions on risk management and data, driven by outstanding high-risk fire safety and complaints actions — and the board's own fire safety training has been pushed to the autumn.
[Evidence] "Ratings for risk management and data capture are at amber/red which is driven by the outstanding high risk actions related to fire safety and patient complaints." — p.203, [Report from the audit committee, Agenda Item 11.1, covering the 23 June 2026 committee]
[Evidence] Action BOD/3/26: "Organise a session in early autumn for board members to undertake fire safety training and review progress on addressing outstanding fire safety concerns." Due "October / November 2026", status Open. — p.9, [Board of Directors Action Tracker — Part 1, public meeting]

[WORTH WATCHING] Diagnostic waits are 20 percentage points below the trust's own planning trajectory, with one Queen Square scanner out of use since December 2024 and its redeployment slipping again.
[Evidence] "In June, 72.1% of patients were waiting under six weeks for a diagnostic test… remains significantly below the Trust's target of 92% submitted in the national planning submission." and "The re-deployment of one of the Queen Square scanners, which has been out-of-use since December 2024 has been delayed until the beginning of Aug-26." — p.87, [Performance report]
[Evidence] "Queen Square neurophysiology 6 week-wait performance remained at 30% in June. Long-term improvements to performance in this service are tied to recruitment efforts as there are currently consultant gaps" — p.87, [Performance report]
