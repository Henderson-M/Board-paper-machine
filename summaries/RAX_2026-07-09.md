# Kingston and Richmond NHS FT (RAX) — Trust Board Part 1, 9 July 2026

**Pack:** "2026-07-09 KRFT Trust Board Part 1 - July 2026" (153pp). Only **Part 1 (public)** was available; a private/Part 2 session exists (Item 19 lists items discussed in private). Recently merged trust (Kingston Hospital + Hounslow & Richmond community/Your Healthcare arrangements) — now Kingston and Richmond NHS FT. CEO Jo Farrar; Chair Phil Hall.

---

## 1. Top lines

**LEAD** — Kingston and Richmond faces an "unprecedented" £40m improvement target (>7% of turnover) and is already tracking behind on its underlying deficit at month two.
> "The submission is not without risk given the implied financial improvement target of £40m, which is more than 7% of our total budget, and unprecedented. Achieving this level of improvement will mean changes to the services we provide and how we work." — CEO report, p.2 (pack p.17/150), [7.00 CEO Board report July 2026]
> "In Month 2, the Trust is reporting an in-month deficit of £3.9m and YTD deficit of £7.7m, in line with plan… The reported position should therefore be viewed alongside the underlying position, which is currently assessed at a £41.5m deficit compared with a planned underlying deficit of £35.2m." — [15.04 M2 Finance Report], p.4 (pack p.104/150)

**LEAD** — Finance & Investment Committee escalated to the Board that the underlying position is "fragile" and that "further difficult decisions may be required" to hit the 2026/27 plan; recurrent savings are already behind.
> "Underlying financial position remains fragile despite reported delivery of the Month 2 plan, with continued reliance on non-recurrent measures and risks associated with recurrent FIP delivery… although FIP identification has improved, a significant delivery challenge remains, and further difficult decisions may be required to achieve the 2026/27 financial plan." — FIC Chair's Assurance Summary (mtg 22/06/26), pack p.98/150, [15.00 FIC Assurance Report]
> "recurrent FIP delivery is £0.6m below plan… This presents a material risk to delivery of the full-year financial plan unless recurrent schemes are identified, approved and delivered at pace." — [15.04 M2 Finance Report], pack p.105/150

**LEAD** — Trust is restricting maternity bookings — now excluding Sutton and Croydon — a move it says could cut bookings by up to 100 per month, agreed with NHSE's Regional Chief Midwife.
> "These discussions have led to the decision to further extend our demand management approach to exclude bookings from Sutton and Croydon. This change has the potential to reduce bookings by up to 100 per month and while this is not a step we take lightly, it is currently essential to maintain patient safety." — CEO report, p.3-4 (pack p.19/150), [7.00 CEO Board report July 2026]

**LEAD** — Trust to launch formal consultation in September on an organisational restructure, moving from divisions to four care groups (workforce reductions expected).
> "a preferred model has been identified, moving from the current divisional structure to four care groups with strengthened clinical, nursing and operational leadership… formal consultation expected to begin in September." — CEO report, p.5 (pack p.21/150). Paired with May minutes: "Balanced discussion on workforce reduction, with recognition that reductions will be required" — pack p.8/150, [WEC report].

**WORTH WATCHING** — "Immediate safety concerns" and guardian fines flagged in General Medicine/AAU over persistent rota gaps, in the Guardian of Safe Working annual report.
> "the Division of Unplanned Care continues to experience the greatest workforce pressures, particularly within General Medicine/Acute Assessment Unit (AAU), where persistent rota gaps remain associated with high levels of exception reporting, guardian fines, and immediate safety concerns." — [18.01 Guardian of Safe Working Annual Report 2025/26], p.1 (pack p.118/150). 443 exception reports in year; General Medicine/AAU = 60% of all reports.

**WORTH WATCHING** — Despite strong operational ranking, the Trust remains in NHSE oversight **segment 3** solely because of its deficit.
> "The Trust is ranked 36th out of 134 trusts in England and rated as 'high performing' or 'above average' across all key domains… our financial deficit means we remain in segment 3, in line with national guidance." — CEO report, p.3 (pack p.18/150), [7.00 CEO Board report July 2026]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Procurement/contract governance weaknesses under review, and the SWL PACS (imaging) programme may head toward contract termination.
> "proposals to strengthen procurement governance and contract management arrangements following concerns regarding procurement support and compliance oversight." … "Commercial and procurement risks associated with the South West London PACS programme and potential contract termination discussions." — FIC Chair's Assurance Summary, pack p.98-99/150, [15.00 FIC Assurance Report]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Trust's first response to the Second Ockenden and Baroness Amos maternity reviews: self-rated **amber on 6 of 10** national 10-point-plan actions.
> "Against the 10 points the Trust has rated itself as 'green' for 3 actions, 'amber' for 6 and has not rated against the remaining 1 action… For the 6 actions rated amber all have processes in place but could be further strengthened based on the review findings." — [11.02 Response to Ockenden and Amos], p.10 (pack p.74/150). Driven by NHSE letters from Duncan Burton (26 June) and Sir Jim Mackey (30 June 2026).

**WORTH WATCHING** — Community district nursing / community matron services "outgrown" their staffing model; a QGC deep dive found outdated models and staff "feeling overwhelmed."
> "Current staffing models are outdated and do not reflect rising demand… contributing to staff feeling overwhelmed… Overall demand, patient complexity and acuity have outgrown the current service model." — QGC Chair's Assurance report, pack p.93/150, [12.01 QGC Chairs report June 2026]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Cyber security internal audit rated amber/red over failure to remove system access for leavers (from May minutes; expected fixed by July).
> "weaknesses in processes for removing system access for staff leaving the organisation had resulted in an amber/red rating." — KRFT Board Minutes 7 May 2026, pack p.6/150, [Audit & Risk Committee report]

## 2. FOI leads

- **Sir Jim Mackey / Duncan Burton maternity letters.** The pack references an NHSE letter from Duncan Burton (26 June) and one from Sir Jim Mackey (30 June 2026) "outlining the urgent actions that Trusts need to take" post-Ockenden/Amos, but does not reproduce them. *Request: copies of both letters, from NHS England, plus the Trust's initial gap analysis rated amber on 6 of 10 actions. Why: reveals nationally-mandated maternity requirements and where a named trust falls short.* (pack p.69/150)

- **Maternity demand-management decision.** Request from Kingston and Richmond FT (and/or SWL ICB) the correspondence/decision paper with the NHSE Regional Chief Midwife agreeing to exclude Sutton and Croydon bookings, plus the capacity/safety risk assessment underpinning the "up to 100 per month" figure. *Why: an acute maternity unit turning away a whole borough's bookings on safety grounds is a substantive story.* (CEO report p.3-4)

- **SWL PACS contract.** Request the contract value, supplier name, term, and any termination correspondence for the South West London PACS (imaging) programme, from Kingston and Richmond FT. Pack refers to "potential contract termination discussions" and "affordability concerns." *Why: contract dispute on a shared regional programme.* (pack p.99/150)

- **Items discussed in private (Item 19).** Request the Part 2 agenda/list of items discussed in private session for 9 July 2026, from Kingston and Richmond FT. *Why: identifies what finance/workforce/restructure detail was kept out of the public pack.* (pack Item 19, [19.00])

- **Restructure business case.** Request the workforce and financial modelling for the "four care groups" restructure and any planned WTE reductions, from Kingston and Richmond FT, ahead of the September consultation. *Why: quantifies job impact of a merger-driven restructure.* (CEO report p.5)

- **Guardian of Safe Working fines.** Request the value and volume of guardian fines and the "immediate safety concerns" logged in General Medicine/AAU during 2025/26, from Kingston and Richmond FT. *Why: puts a number on rota-gap safety risk in a named division.* (pack p.118/150)

## 4. Routine pack note

Not routine. Solid finance lead (£40m "unprecedented" target; underlying deficit £6.3m adverse to plan at M2; FIC escalation) plus a genuinely newsworthy maternity demand-management decision (excluding Sutton and Croydon) and a September restructure with expected workforce cuts. Merger integration itself is now low-key — the Integration Oversight Committee reported "None to escalate" and has formally shifted from "post-merger delivery to ongoing integration and transformation oversight" (pack p.110/150).
