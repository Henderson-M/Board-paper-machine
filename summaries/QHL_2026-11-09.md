[LEAD] The board was asked to approve an urgent care reconfiguration for Birmingham and Solihull under which Summerfield and Washwood Heath urgent treatment centres close as UTCs, Warren Farm UTC moves permanently to Good Hope Hospital, three new hospital UTCs open (QE, Heartlands, Midland Met) and GP out of hours is re-procured in three lots, with full implementation from April 2028. The replacement "locality hubs" at the two closing sites will be mainly referral-only. [INFERENCE] The board cover sheet and CEO report describe this as "add locality hubs" and "enhanced locality urgent care arrangements" and do not use the word "close"; the closure wording is in the business case itself.
[Evidence] "Summerfield and Washwood Heath UTCs will close. Instead, enhanced locality urgent care services will be delivered from the same sites as part of wider health and wellbeing hubs." and "North Birmingham UTC - which was previously Warren Farm UTC - would move permanently to Good Hope Hospital." (DMBC p.10, [Item 10, Decision-Making Business Case, UTC and GP Out of Hours Review]). "Access will primarily be through professional referral and agreed, clinically triaged pathways, rather than unrestricted walk-in attendance." (DMBC p.48, same paper). [LEAD] CEO David Melbourne says the ICB will move money away from providers that miss contracted activity: the CFO is reviewing every major contract by 30 November, with elective performance off plan (1,685 52-week waiters in August against a plan of 1,242) and formal contractual escalation under way on learning disability and autism inpatients.
[Evidence] "18-week performance around 65% against a year-end target of 68%, 1,685 people had waited more than 52 weeks in August against a plan of 1,242, and the year-on-year fall in our waiting list has slowed from 6.4% in April to 1.2% in August. Paul Athey is leading a review of every major contract with provider operational and finance leaders, concluding by 30 November and allowing for the consultant strike announced on 26 October. Where a provider cannot deliver agreed activity, the funding attached to it will move to providers that can. Learning disability and inpatient autism also remain above trajectory, at 95 adults against 80 and 16 children and young people against seven, and formal contractual escalation is under way." ([Item 8, Chief Executive's Report], "Our role as payer")
