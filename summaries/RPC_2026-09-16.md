[LEAD] The trust's chief operating officer left in mid-August with no reason given in the papers, and four executive roles are held on an interim or acting basis including the chief executive.

BAF006 records the risk is "Caused by: Four interim executive roles including Chief executive officer; turnover in Board level roles and organisational memory loss; three Non-executive directors coming to end of terms in June 2026" — p.345, [BAF006 Leadership Capacity]. Board membership list shows interim chair, acting CEO, acting CNO, interim CFO, acting chief strategy officer and interim company secretary — p.5, [Membership].

[LEAD] Nine of the trust's ten strategic risks sit outside its own risk appetite, with estates, finance and leadership capacity all scored 16, and no score has moved since the last board review.
[Evidence] "Nine of the ten strategic risks remain outside their agreed risk appetite. Workforce remains the only strategic risk operating within appetite." and "No strategic risk scores have changed since the previous Board review." — p.325, [77-26 Board Assurance Framework]. "The highest-rated risks continue to be: Estates (16), Finance (16), Leadership Capacity (16)" — p.326.

[LEAD] A culture diagnostic put to the board finds staff belief that the trust prioritises patient care has fallen more than six points in a year, with staff reporting a perceived shift towards cost-saving.
[Evidence] "There is decline in: - perception that patient care is prioritised (q25a: 88.6% to 82.2%)" — p.190, [72-26 Culture Diagnostic Review 2025/26]. "Feedback from listening exercises and the 2026 Wellbeing & Inclusion Survey suggests some staff perceive a shift towards cost-saving and performance objectives over care for patients and staff, creating tension between organisational priorities and the values that motivate many employees." — p.190. Also "increased work-related stress (32.4% to 39.3%)", "reduced confidence that the organisation takes positive action on wellbeing (60.7% to 56.4%)", "declining morale (6.2 to 5.9)" — p.191, and "staff involvement in decisions (q3e: 54.7% to 49.3%)" — p.189.

[LEAD] The same review says uncertainty about the group partnership is itself denting staff confidence in local improvement work.
[Evidence] "There is evidence that organisational and system uncertainty may be affecting staff confidence in improvement activity... some feedback suggests uncertainty regarding partnership arrangements. Organisational developments may reduce confidence in investing time and effort in local change initiatives where future priorities, structures or service models are perceived as uncertain." — p.193, [72-26 Culture Diagnostic Review 2025/26].

[LEAD] The trust's quarter four national oversight framework ranking fell 13 places in a quarter, and the papers state that without deficit support funding it would have been in segment one.
[Evidence] "The Trust's quarter 4 National Oversight Framework (NOF) position has declined to 42 out of 134 acute provider trusts, compared to 29 out of 134 in quarter 3. The drivers for this are primarily the staff survey results and elective care access performance... It should be noted that, excluding the impact of Deficit Support Funding, QVH would have been positioned within Segment 1." — p.15, [Minutes, public board 9 July 2026, item 32-26].

[LEAD] White applicants are 2.99 times more likely than minority ethnic applicants to be appointed from shortlisting, against a national average of 1.77, and the disciplinary disparity ratio has jumped to 2.99 from 1.15.
[Evidence] "There has been a small increase in the relative likelihood of white applicants being appointed from shortlisting compared with BME applicants, from 2.94 in 2024/25 to 2.99 in 2025/26." — p.91, [69-26 WRES and WDES report 2025/26]. "NHS Workforce Race Equality Standard 2025 National Average 1.77" — p.106, [WRES 2025-26 appendix]. "There has been an increase in the relative likelihood of BME staff entering the formal disciplinary process, from 1.15 in 2024/25 to 2.99 in 2025/26" against a stated national average of 1.11 — pp.91 and 107. The paper cautions "12 cases recorded during 2025/26 (6 were BME staff and 6 were white staff)... Given the relatively low numbers, percentage differences should be interpreted with caution" — p.92. "BME staff remain approximately three times more likely to experience discrimination than white staff (13.9% compared with 4.4%)" — p.92.

[LEAD] The trust's estates self-assessment found 28 per cent of requirements non-compliant, and names electrical infrastructure and fire compliance as its two biggest statutory compliance risks.
[Evidence] "65% of assessed requirements were compliant, 28% require improvement, and 7% were not applicable" — p.272, [74-26 Premises Assurance Model 2026]. "The two most significant areas of statutory compliance risk currently identified are electrical infrastructure and fire compliance." — p.280. The report also lists gaps in "authorised person coverage and specialist training compliance, reducing assurance that services can be managed safely and effectively" — pp.273-274.
