## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has formally escalated Plymouth over urgent and emergency care, demanding a board-level assurance response, after telling the trust it is "off plan".
[Evidence] "On 13 July 2026, the Trust received a formal request from the NHS England South-West Regional Chair and Director for a board level response to the delivery of the Urgent and Emergency operational delivery standards, which requires the board to: Provide assurance that the delivery of the UEC plan is sufficiently ambitious and credible… confirm that there is leadership grip and organisational capability to deliver the plan" — p.38, [CEO Report, Appendix 2: UEC Performance Improvement & Recovery plan]. Also: "In response to NHS England's recent correspondence highlighting that the Trust is off plan in relation to urgent and emergency care performance, a paper was presented to provide assurance to Trust Board on the 22nd July" — p.24, [CEO Report].

[LEAD] The trust is in the "most challenged cohort nationally" (Tier 1) for UEC and elective recovery and sits in NHS Oversight Framework segment 4, ranked 105th of 134 acute trusts.
[Evidence] "As a Trust currently identified within Tier 1, representing the most challenged cohort nationally for Urgent and Emergency Care and Elective Recovery" — p.126, [Integrated Performance Report]. Segment 4; "Trust Ranking 105th out of 134"; Finance and Productivity "4 – Low performing"; Access to Services "3 – Below Average" — p.131, [IPR] / p.75, [IDP Q1].

[LEAD] Plymouth plans to apply in August for £23m of emergency revenue cash support, on top of a breakeven plan that only holds because of £35m non-recurrent NHSE/ICB funding and £25.1m deficit support funding; the underlying deficit was assessed at £72m.
[Evidence] "it is recommended the Trust submit a revenue cash support application in August for £23m to be received in September that will be in line with the planned deficit for the first 6 months of the year" — p.160. "The Trust originally submitted a plan for 2026/27 with a deficit of £46.5m… the Board approved a revised breakeven plan… based on non-recurrent NHSE/ICB funding of £35m and £11m of increased CIP savings." "The underlying deficit was assessed as £72m based on the original NHSE 3 year plan submission." — pp.161–163, [2026-27 Financial Plan Delivery – Month 3]. Board asked to take **partial assurance only** — p.160.

[LEAD] The trust's £124m capital programme is underpinned by a proposed sale-and-leaseback of trust property, with the decision due at this July board.
[Evidence] "The capital plan is underpinned by a proposed sale and leaseback property transaction." "A decision on the sale and lease back proposal will be taken at the July Trust Board meeting." — pp.162, 166, [Finance Month 3]. Capital plan £124m offset by a £25m capital disposal contribution — p.162.

[LEAD] A trust-commissioned review found its Urgent Treatment Centre has delivered none of the £2.5m savings and none of the ED-flow benefits set out in its approved business case.
[Evidence] "The £2.5m savings anticipated in the business case have not materialised. No cost release has been achieved to date." "UTC activity has consistently remained below the expected level of 150 attendances per day, with a maximum of 116 achieved. Redirection from ED has not reached the anticipated 70 patients per day… there has been no demonstrable improvement in ED length of stay, ED four-hour performance, or overall ED crowding attributable to the UTC." — pp.172–174, [Urgent Treatment Centre (UTC) Performance and Benefits Review]. Based on "a structured audit of 100 UTC attendances".

[LEAD] Ambulance handover delays at Derriford have sharply deteriorated, with the mean handover time nearly doubling year on year.
[Evidence] "Mean handover time – 76 mins July 2026, from 43 mins July 2025" — p.58, [CEO Report, UEC recovery appendix]. GIRFT rating for ambulance performance: "Red"; "Mean handover time 76 minutes; >2,300 hours lost during July trajectory period" — p.50. "deterioration in handovers in July 26 are of significant concern with mean time lost from 43mins to 76mins" — p.43.

[LEAD] Plymouth has disestablished its equality, diversity and inclusion team because of cost pressures, and has no black/minority-ethnic or disabled representation on its board.
[Evidence] "The cost pressures in the previous financial years has resulted in the EDI team being disestablished which has led to limited progress in delivery of equality activity." — p.108, [Workforce Equality Diversity and Inclusion Annual Update]. "The Trust Board does not reflect the diversity of the workforce, with no representation from some underrepresented groups." — p.103. WRES indicator 9 "BME Board membership" 0% and WDES "Disabled Board membership" 0% every year 2022–2026 (both 6.3%/6.25% in 2021) — pp.104–105. White applicants "2.19" times more likely to be appointed from shortlisting than BME applicants, "the disparity widening in 2026" — pp.103–104.

[WORTH WATCHING] External consultancy has been brought in on the trust's financial recovery, including a deep-dive into two clinical specialties, and an 18-week "Productivity, Strategy and Financial Support Programme" to tackle the "structural financial position".
[Evidence] "External consultancy engaged to identify further opportunities in coding and P&L." / "External consultancy to deep dive into two specialties to validate assumptions and identify further opportunities." — p.149, [IPR counter-measures]. "TMB received a proposal supporting the Trust through an 18-week programme to identify and unlock productivity opportunities, while providing targeted financial support to address the structural financial position. The programme will bring additional capacity, external benchmarking and analytical expertise" — p.64, [Trust Management Board Report]. Firm(s) not named. [INFERENCE] This matches the KPMG/York-and-Scarborough pattern (external firm engaged on a distressed financial position) — worth standing up who the provider is.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust went live with the Epic electronic patient record on 23 July 2026, but its Safety & Quality Committee gave only partial assurance, flagging "outstanding clinical risks associated with Epic implementation".
[Evidence] "On 23 July 2026, UHP implemented the Epic electronic patient record system, joining Torbay and South Devon and Royal Devon" — p.24, [CEO Report]. Committee "concluded that only partial assurance could be taken due to concerns regarding organisational learning… and the significance of outstanding clinical risks associated with Epic implementation" — p.178, [Safety & Quality Committee Assurance Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] A Devon-wide "Pay to Grade" bank-worker pay realignment is being pursued across the One Devon system, requiring a formal 45-day notice period to staff.
[Evidence] "A preferred Devon-wide Pay to Grade model has been identified which will align bank workers more closely with substantive Agenda for Change principles… TMB noted that a formal 45-day notice period was required" — pp.65–66, [Trust Management Board Report].
