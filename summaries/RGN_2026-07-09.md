## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust is in the bottom quartile nationally for corridor care in the first-ever national publication, ranking 123rd of 134 providers for admitted corridor care.
[Evidence] "This places the Trust 81 out of 134 providers for ED corridor care and 123 out of 134 for Admitted corridor care (1 being best / lowest level reported)." — Integrated Performance Report, [3. Area of Focus: Corridor Care], IPR p.14. CEO report adds the Trust is "currently positioned within the lower-performing cohort of providers in the inaugural publication" — [Chief Executive Officer's Report §2.3], p.4. Reported daily averages: 19 in the ED corridor over 45 mins, 17 admitted patients in boarding spaces.

[LEAD] Trust's national ranking on the NHS Oversight Framework fell 21 places to 79th of 134 acute trusts in Q4 2025/26, and its overall segment is being capped at NOF3 because it is in deficit.
[Evidence] "our national ranking has moved from 58 out of 134 NHS Acute Trust for quarter 3 to 79 out of 134 for quarter 4. This shift moves us into the lower mid quartile of Trusts." Rank trajectory across the year: Q1 105 → Q2 81 → Q3 58 → Q4 79. Unadjusted score improved to NOF2 but "The Trust overall position is therefore capped at achievement of NOF3" under the financial override (max domain score of 3 for any provider in deficit). — [4. NHS Oversight Framework: 2025/26 Q4], IPR p.16.

[LEAD] Trust is £1.6m adverse to plan at month 2 with a £4.9m year-to-date deficit, and has delivered only about 40 per cent of its planned savings.
[Evidence] "The Trust is reporting a year-to-date deficit of £4.9m, which is £1.6m behind plan… Delivery of the financial improvement programme is significantly behind plan, with only around 40% of the expected savings achieved to date." — [2. Finance Overview], IPR. Dashboard confirms "an adjusted year-to-date (YTD) deficit of £4.9m against a planned deficit of £3.3m" — i.e. the Trust is planning a full-year deficit, having previously "met its financial plan for the seventh year in a row" (Chair, [Minutes 12 May 2026] p.2). Drivers: industrial action (£0.7m) and under-delivery of variable elective income (£0.9m).

[LEAD] A Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme targeting £9.9m of recurrent savings is running as part of a workforce-reduction drive.
[Evidence] "Joel Harrison clarified that the £9.9 million recurrent savings target for MARS has been based on average salary modelling, with severance costs and phased savings accounted for in the financial plan." — [Minutes 12 May 2026, item 17.1 public questions] p.12. MARS launched 11 May 2026 over three months. HMC flags "workforce reduction requirements" as a current alert (below).

[WORTH WATCHING] The Trust's ability to track mortality is being undermined by a coding backlog, which the quality committee has escalated to the Board as a risk; Peterborough City Hospital's HSMR is "above expected".
[Evidence] "Over the last two months QAC has expressed concern that the Trust's ability to track mortality via the HSMR is being negatively affected by the backlog of coding. QAC has already escalated to the Board the need for action to be taken to rectify this situation which is related to low staffing levels." — [Quality Assurance Committee Assurance Report, 30 June 2026], ALERT. Rolling 12-month HSMR 100.1 trust-wide, PCH 108.3 ("above expected"), Hinchingbrooke 92.1; "individual patient comorbidities were not being recorded properly. Peer trusts appear to be recording this area more efficiently." — [QAC Assurance Report, 25 May 2026], ADVISE.

[WORTH WATCHING] Diagnostics and RTT sit in the bottom quartile nationally, and the incoming 2026/27 oversight framework is expected to score the Trust "Negative" partly because of it.
[Evidence] "Diagnostics DM01 performance at 56.5% remains challenged, a growing waiting list and increasing breaches… particularly by ultrasound demand." NOF 2026/27 estimated impact rated "Negative" for Access due to "Inclusion of DM01 where Trust is bottom quartile nationally", and "Negative" for Effectiveness of Care due to length of stay and 30-day readmissions "where the Trust benchmarks poorly due to the admission approach in Hinchingbrooke ACU." — [2. Operational Performance Overview] and [4. NOF 2026/27: What This Means for North West Anglia], IPR.

[WORTH WATCHING] Maternity is under heightened board scrutiny following Ockenden/Amos, with an NHSE 10-point plan letter received and a fetal-monitoring-interpretation risk escalated.
[Evidence] "On the 30th June 2026 communication was received from Sir James Mackay CEO of NHSE detailing a 10 point plan for maternity and neonatal services based on the publication of the Ockenden and Amos reports." — [Ockenden Report, Amos Report and 10 Urgent Actions] p.2. Maternity risk register top-3 risks include "Fetal monitoring interpretation – risk raised to 15" and community midwifery venues for Hinchingbrooke "planned commencement of services delayed until June 26." — [Perinatal Report, Risk Register] IPR/Perinatal p.35. HMC noted "sensitivities around maternity assurance following the Ockenden Report."

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff sickness is running well above plan and is estimated to cost the Trust nearly £16m a year.
[Evidence] "Staff absence is estimated to cost the Trust £15.81 million annually, including £6.2 million attributable to Nursing and Midwifery staff, with the highest departmental impact in Surgery at £3.7 million." Sickness 5.2% against a 4.1% target; anxiety/stress the single largest cause of days lost (36,602 over 12 months). — [2. Workforce Overview (2 of 2)], IPR.

[WORTH WATCHING] Board asked to back major Hinchingbrooke new-hospital and RAAC capital cases worth tens of millions.
[Evidence] Recommended to Board: "Land Acquisition… Hinchingbrooke New Hospital. Land acquisition costs £6.5m, total £8.1m"; "Service Wing Decant & Demolition… First SFBC £3.6m (second of £5.5m, total £9.1m)"; "RAAC Programme updated Business Case… £9.7m." Contract awards approved: Water Supply £1.3m/yr (3+1 yrs) and PAS £1.2m/yr (3+1+1 yrs). — [Finance and Performance Committee Assurance Report, 29 June 2026], ACTIONS.

[WORTH WATCHING] Chief medical officer departed; acting CMO in post.
[Evidence] "Thanked Dr Callum Gardner for his service to the Trust following his resignation as Chief Medical Officer and welcomed Dr Emilia Wawrzkowicz for taking on the role of Acting Chief Medical Officer." — [Minutes 12 May 2026, CEO update] p.2.
