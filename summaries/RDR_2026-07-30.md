## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust's flagship Brighton General Hospital redevelopment has stalled — the preferred scheme is judged to have no economically viable way forward and the board is being asked to consider next steps.
[Evidence] "significant challenges exist regarding affordability of the scheme to develop a new Community Health Hub with an increasing gap between anticipated build costs and land sales value. FIPC recognised that, despite extensive work over many years, the current preferred scheme did not have an economically viable way forward, and that the Trust Board would need to carefully consider next steps." — p.140, [FIPC Chair's Assurance Report, 23 June 2026, Item 12b]

[WORTH WATCHING] SCFT's total waiting list rose to its highest recorded level in June; 52-week-plus waits up to 1,765, including 16 MSK patients waiting more than 104 weeks (two years).
[Evidence] "SCFT's total waiting list – the number waiting for treatment to start – increased in June to its highest recorded level. As part of that, the number waiting more than 52 weeks increased month-on-month by 27 to 1,765, including 16 MSK waits of more than 104 weeks." — p.119, [IPR Month 3, Operational Performance Exception MT607]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust is carrying a £24.0m cost improvement requirement (a further increase on previous years) that it is meeting increasingly through non-recurrent savings, with the financial-sustainability risk sitting above appetite and no full mitigations yet for the 2026/27 forecast.
[Evidence] "The current plan requires delivery of £24,015k of savings which is a further increase on previous years... this had been achieved through a greater use of non-recurrent savings." — p.141, [FIPC Chair's Report, Item 12b]; "We have do not yet have full mitigations for the current estimation of risk against the forecast out turn for 26/27" (current risk score 15, target 12) — p.73–74, [Financial Sustainability BAF Risk 09.2d]

[WORTH WATCHING] SCFT has decided not to bid to keep its wheelchair service; staff are already looking to leave and further staffing gaps are expected before the contract hands over in April 2027.
[Evidence] "Ongoing workforce pressures in the wheelchair service were noted whilst we await the award of a new contract where SCFT has chosen not to bid. Some staff have indicated intentions to seek alternative NHS roles and further gaps to staffing are anticipated prior to full handover in April 2027." — p.141, [FIPC Chair's Report, Item 12b]

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS Benchmarking flags SCFT as a national outlier for reporting of pressure ulcers and medication incidents, and both metrics are red month and year-to-date.
[Evidence] "NHS Benchmarking shows SCFT as an outlier for reporting pressure ulcers and medication incidents." — p.147, [Quality Committee Chair's Report 14i]; MT267 Pressure ulcers cat 3&4 missed opportunities and MT184 medication incidents both "MONTH RAG (RED) / YTD RAG (RED)" — pp.113–114, [IPR Month 3, Quality Exception Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] New chief people officer appointed as the long-serving incumbent leaves after a decade.
[Evidence] "I am pleased to formally announce the appointment of Rupert Goodman as our new Chief People Officer. Rupert will join the Trust on 7 September and joins us from Hampshire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust." — p.16, [CEO Board Report]; outgoing CPO Caroline Julien noted as attending "the final Board meeting in public" after "ten years of service" — p.4, [Draft Board Minutes 28 May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board is being asked to raise the money thresholds in its financial-risk scoring — doubling the major/catastrophic boundary to £2m — a change presented as reflecting the trust's growth.
[Evidence] "Recommendation to Audit Committee for a technical amendment to the scoring methodology... This increases the boundary between major and catastrophic risk from £1m to £2m and the boundary between minor and moderate from £50k to £75k." / the change "reflects the growing size of the Trust." — pp.142–143, [FIPC Chair's Report, Item 12b]

[WORTH WATCHING] Freedom to Speak Up annual report discloses a case of racist conduct said to have become "normalised" within a large clinical team, now prompting a proposed cultural review.
[Evidence] "One case involved concerns about individual racist conduct and systemic racist behaviours that had become normalised within a large multidisciplinary team. This was immediately escalated and has executive involvement. A cultural review is proposed... HR processes are have also been followed to address the allegations of serious misconduct." — p.204, [FTSU Annual Report 2025-26, Item 15]

[FOI] FTSU cultural review — request the terms of reference and (when complete) the findings of the proposed cultural review into the "normalised" racist conduct in the named multidisciplinary team, from SCFT. The annual report confirms it exists but withholds the team and the substance.
