## Top lines

[LEAD] An internal forecast says the Medway children's long waits the trust inherited on 1 October will push it out of segment 1 of the NHS Oversight Framework and into segment 2 in quarter 3, after it had already slipped from 1st to 6th among community and mental health providers.
[Evidence] "For Q1 2026/27, the Trust remains in Segment 1, with an average metric score of 1.86, ranked 6th of 58 mental health and community providers and 2nd in the South East. The Segment 1 threshold is below 1.94, leaving 0.08 points of headroom. However, an SBAR presented to Executive Team meeting (ETM) has forecast that transferred Medway children's 52+ week waits could raise the score to 2.03 and move the Trust into Segment 2 in Q3, unless other metrics improve." The report summary adds: "the score moved from 1.53 to 1.86 and rank from 1st to 6th — a 0.33 deterioration and five-place fall", with the caveat that "the Q4 comparison is directional rather than like-for-like" because the framework changed. — approx. p.146 and p.151, [Integrated Performance Report front sheet and Executive Summary: National Oversight Framework, Item 15]

[LEAD] Five of the trust's eight strategic risks have been raised since the Medway integration, with the quality and safety risk now scored 20 against a board appetite ceiling of 9.
[Evidence] "The following risk movements have occurred since the last Board meeting, primarily driven by a change in the underlying risk environment following integration with Medway Community Healthcare: Risk ID6962 'Quality and Safety' – increased from 15 to 20; Risk ID6961 'Governance' – increased from 12 to 16; Risk ID6956 'Capability, Leadership and Workforce Resilience' – increased 12 from to 16; Risk ID6957 'Capacity and Operational Delivery' – increased from 12 to 16; Risk ID6955 'Agility and System Adaptation' – increased from 9 to 12". Risk 6962 is shown at likelihood 4, consequence 5, score 20, risk appetite "Moderate (9)". Two workforce risks now list as a negative indicator "Inherited operational, transactional, cultural, contractual and employee relations issues inherited at point of transfer from integrated organisation, including non-standardised skill mix in relation to safer staffing requirements and increased vacancy rate", with a "Twice weekly tactical meeting, with escalation to People Committee, of inherited people issues". — p.41, p.53 and p.55, [Board Assurance Framework, Item 8]

[LEAD] The trust has taken on a Medway waiting list of more than 3,300 patients waiting over a year, including 2,645 children, with fewer than half of Medway patients seen within 18 weeks.
[Evidence] Subcontractor table for September 2026 (the last month before transfer): MCH "52+ Week Waits" 3,354 (3,724 in April); "Non Consultant-Led Access Waits (18 Weeks)" 42.8 per cent against a 92.0 per cent target; average length of stay 30.63 days against a 21-day standard. "MCH improved to 42.8% but remained well below target. MCH's 52+ week waits fell by 76 to 3,354... The principal risks remain MCH's access backlog and prolonged inpatient stays." The children's services paper gives "Medway validation of wating lists and trajectory to support reduction of over 52 weeks, current position: 2645 over 52 weeks (November 2026)" and lists as a priority "Complete Medway neurodevelopmental waiting-list validation, mobilise nurse-led assessments and align the clinical pathway with Kent's to address waits of over 52 weeks." — approx. p.205, [Integrated Performance Report, Community Services sub-contractor performance, Item 15]; p.72 and p.75, [Prevention, Inequalities and Children's Services Transformation Update, Item 9]

[Evidence] "KCHFT has been providing clinical and leadership support to Darland House since the MCH Board took the decision to temporarily close the service to new admissions in late July. [LEAD] The trust, an early adopter of the national NHS People Portal that is due to replace ESR, has told the national programme it will not commit to switching off its existing systems until the replacement is shown to work.

[LEAD] The NHS England paediatric audiology look-back at the trust recalled 25 children and has so far found three cases of moderate harm, with 14 harm reviews still awaiting a verdict.
[Evidence] "Records reviewed: 1,968. Required recall: 25 (1.3% of records reviewed). Harm reviews undertaken: 43 (2.2% of records reviewed). 23 of the 43 harm reviews (53.5%) were assessed as no harm, 14 (32.6%) are still awaiting a confirmed level of harm by the NHSE subject matter expert. Three (7.0%) were assessed as moderate harm, three (7.0%) as low harm with all care provided. There were none identified as causing severe harm." The paper describes this as "a five-year look-back review to identify children who may have been at risk of delayed or incorrect diagnosis" and lists it under "items of excellence". — p.74 and p.67, [Prevention, Inequalities and Children's Services Transformation Update, Item 9]

## Other items noted, not tiered

- Scale of the merged trust: "a single community health provider with 6,500 colleagues delivering more than 100 services across Kent and Medway"; "welcoming 1,250 colleagues"; "this is only the second such integration in NHS history" [of an NHS trust and a community interest company]. p.33, [CEO Report].
- Community hospital no criteria to reside: "in special cause variation (declining) at 37.9%, materially above the 15% target. The position reflects operational-flow pressures alongside site-level coding inconsistency." It was 35.2 per cent in the September pack. Approx. p.146, [IPR front sheet].
- Internal audit: "the clinical productivity audit received limited assurance, due to the unavailability at the time of the audit of an organisational wide framework for clinical productivity." The committee also suggests "the Board consider the role of TIAA in how it supports the wider remit of the Trust in the 2027/28 audit plan". External audit (Grant Thornton) briefing: "Further assurance is also required on contract and procurement management." p.101–102, [Audit and Risk Committee report].
- Medical gases: "the medical gases assurance remains red rated, reflecting outstanding audit activity... there is not yet a sufficiently clear improvement plan to demonstrate how the risk will reduce." Rated "Alert". p.93, [Quality Committee report].
- IT and telephony: "A planned transition away from legacy IT infrastructure was accelerated due to contractual requirements. This resulted in some temporary disruption to digital and phone services... Issues continue to be closely monitored and addressed, including any potential impact on patient safety." Separately, "a risk in maintaining continuity of the Trust's on call telephony provision as the organisation is migrated from a pager system to Ring Central". p.35, [CEO Report]; p.102, [Audit and Risk Committee report].
- Data and information governance: "Integration, unauthorised access and rising future standards remain material risks", rated "Alert"; the committee "supported the Trust's zero-tolerance approach to inappropriate access of confidential information". p.102, [Audit and Risk Committee report].
- Overdue BAF actions on risk 6957: "Enable swift deployment of therapy resources from acute into community settings via system deployment mechanisms" (due September 2026) and "Recruitment to key clinical roles identified including RN apprenticeships" (due October 2026) both "Overdue". The BAF also asks the board to extend two system-working actions; the front sheet says "from October 2026 to January 2027" while the risk page prints the original date as "October 2027". p.58, p.42 and p.61, [BAF].
