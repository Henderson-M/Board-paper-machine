## Top lines

[LEAD] CQC has begun a planned re-inspection of York and Scarborough's maternity services following the 2023 "inadequate" rating
[Evidence] *"The CQC inspection team attended York Hospital on 14 and 15 April, and Scarborough Hospital on 16 and 17 April. The inspection formed part of a planned re-assessment following the 2023 inspection, during which services were rated as inadequate."* — CQC Update p.238. Initial feedback letter received 21 April; additional evidence submitted by 6 May 2026.

[LEAD] Scarborough Hospital U&E and medical care services found in breach of **six regulations** — CQC final report published 20 March 2026
[Evidence] *"There was an unannounced CQC inspection of Urgent and Emergency Care and Medical Care Services at Scarborough Hospital between 7 and 9 October 2025. The final report was published on 20 March 2026… Six legal breaches in regulation were identified. A response to these breaches was submitted to the CQC by the deadline of 10 April 2026."* — CQC Update p.238. The report is publicly available; the breach detail is a direct lift.

[LEAD] Trust's 2026/27 financial plan fails NHSE requirements — not eligible for further Deficit Support Funding
[Evidence] *"The income and expenditure plan profile shows an expected cumulative deficit throughout the year with an outturn deficit of £22.6m at the end of March 2027. This plan has not met the requirements stipulated by NHSE and is subject to further discussion. The Trust is not currently eligible for further Deficit Support Funding."* — Finance Director report, Summary Dashboard p.226. WRAP delivery only 28% of £61.6m target (Finance p.231). M1 deficit £2.5m vs planned £2.1m.

[LEAD] CEO Clare Smith heading to London escalation meeting with Mackey and Dash
[Evidence] CEO writes: *"To finalise the position regarding the expected in-year deficit, and to agree any deficit support funding, we will be participating in a national plan escalation meeting in London with Sir Jim Mackey, Penny Dash, and NHS England's Regional Director and Regional Chair, along with other NHSE colleagues. At the meeting we will be required to present and discuss the action we are taking and the decisions we will need to make to ensure delivery of our financial plan and to achieve plan compliance."* — CEO report p.42.

[LEAD] NHS Providers Well-Led Developmental Review identifies "deep seated" cultural and governance issues
[Evidence] Review report from NHS Providers, dated December 2025, included in this pack as part of progress monitoring. Findings include: *"deep seated suspicion of 'management' including a clear disconnect and lack of visibility between the Board and executive team and the rest of the organisation, historic failure to address a number of cultural concerns, poor communication and feedback loops and a lack of clarity over accountability and roles and responsibilities that appears to have led to a lack of buy-in by operational leaders to the Trust 'ask'."* — Well-Led Review Overview p.96. 12 recommendations across 8 quality statements; progress update in the same paper (p.99–110). NHS Providers was the named external reviewer.

[LEAD] Bridlington Care Unit closing — botched stakeholder engagement triggers extraordinary council scrutiny meeting
[Evidence] CEO: *"Despite best intentions of colleagues, the correct sequence of events for this engagement was not followed as colleagues were advised of the change before stakeholders had been briefed, resulting in them learning of the plans before being formally notified and before discussions could take place. The Health, Care and Wellbeing Overview and Scrutiny Sub-Committee convened an extraordinary meeting on the matter on 11 May where a number of concerns were raised and recommendations made."* — CEO report p.42. Staff consultation under way for redeployment.

[WORTH WATCHING] EPR went live (reversal of February pause); rollout completed across York, Scarborough, Bridlington, Selby, White Cross Court, Nelson's Court, St Monica's
[Evidence] *"I am delighted to report that we have successfully completed the first phase of the Nervecentre Electronic Patient Record (EPR) roll out"* — CEO p.40. Partners reportedly described it as *"one of the best planned and smoothest go-lives they have experienced to date"* — p.41. Two further phases to come. Worth noting because the March pack reported the go-live as **paused** with no new date — the reversal in two months is itself a story arc, and HSJ already covered the pause.

[WORTH WATCHING] Open CQC cases jump from 19 (March) to 32 (May 2026); response capacity "impacted by team absence and recent inspection activity"
[Evidence] CQC Update p.239: *"As of 7 May 2026, there are 32 open CQC cases… The capacity to respond to CQC cases has been impacted by team absence and recent inspection activity. Responses to CQC cases will be prioritised over the next month."*

[WORTH WATCHING] Workforce churn at executive level continues — Lydia Larcum now Interim Director of Workforce & OD (replacing Polly McMeekin)
[Evidence] Staff Survey Improvement Plan paper (Item 17) lists *"Lydia Larcum, Interim Director of Workforce & Organisational Development"* as Director Sponsor — p.267. Polly McMeekin authored the equivalent paper in March. Joe Hague confirmed in post as Chief Nurse (CEO p.43). Sarah Coltman-Lovell Interim Director of Strategy until end of May (per March pack).

## Pack assessment

- Routine: **false**
- Top lines: 9 (LEAD: 6, WORTH WATCHING: 3, FOI: 0 inline — 7 in dedicated section)
- Colour quotes: 5
- This pack is even richer than the March pack. **Six** near-publishable LEAD items by themselves, including two distinct CQC regulatory stories (maternity re-inspection + Scarborough breaches), a named NHSE escalation meeting with two named seniors, a damning external well-led review, and a closure-with-botched-engagement story. The EPR went-live story is an arc closure to the previously reported pause. The Well-Led review (December 2025) plus the new Lydia Larcum interim appointment plus the M1 deficit position together would justify a "trust under pressure" piece in their own right.

## Methodology note (for the team)

This is the second run of the pack-analyser on this trust. The first run mistakenly skipped this pack because the file was titled "Agenda" rather than "Agenda and Papers"; the analyser inferred (wrongly) that the May papers weren't published yet. They were — the file was the full 298-page pack. The pack-analyser and scan-boards SKILLs have been updated to never trust title pattern and to always verify by file size and page count before deciding whether a pack is "live". RCB notes in `data/trust_urls.json` also corrected.
