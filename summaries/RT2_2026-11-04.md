## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England North West has placed Pennine Care at Level 2 of its new regional Performance Accountability Framework — the full letter from regional director Louise Shepherd (26 October) is in the pack, and describes a new four-level oversight regime introduced by letter on 25 September 2026.
[Evidence] "the Regional Support Group has agreed a PAF Level 2 assessment for Quarter 3 2026/27... a number of specific risks, priorities or performance challenges would benefit from targeted regional oversight and support" — p.344, [Chair & CEO report, appendix 1]. The framework "sits alongside the national NHS oversight framework" with levels "ranging from level 1, where there are no significant concerns to level 4, where serious or ongoing concerns require much closer oversight and intervention" — p.336.

[LEAD] The trust has moved from Segment 4 to Segment 3 of the NHS Oversight Framework at the Q1 2026/27 publication, and its board was told it ranked 48th of 58 "other Non-Acute" trusts nationally; it is targeting NOF 2 by Q4.
[Evidence] "Pennine Care NHS Foundation Trust has moved from Segment 4 into Segment 3 and our overall ranking against 'other Non-Acute Trust' was 48/58. Across the Northwest Providers we ranked 3rd." — p.99, [Integrated Performance Report]; "the Trust remained in NOF 3, with a clear improvement plan in place and an expectation of moving to NOF 2 by Q4. Eight metrics remained in alert." — p.55, [Performance & Finance Committee chair's report, 28 Oct]

[LEAD] A patient died on Walker Ward after being in seclusion; a patient safety investigation with external subject-matter expertise has been commissioned, covering care that also involved the local acute hospital.
[Evidence] "The Committee was informed of a serious patient safety incident on Walker Ward involving the death of a patient who had been in seclusion. A patient safety investigation had been commissioned with subject-matter expertise and would include the patient's care that also involved the local acute hospital." — p.47, [Quality Committee chair's report, 27 Oct]

[LEAD] A coroner has sent a Prevention of Future Deaths (Regulation 28) report to the Health and Social Care Secretary over the length of the autism assessment waiting list, after an inquest into the suicide of a Pennine Care learning disability patient; a second Regulation 28 went to NICE over absent guidance on weight-loss drugs for patients on antipsychotics.
[Evidence] "Conclusion: Suicide. Regulation 28 Report issued to Secretary of State for Health and Social Care in relation to length of waiting list for an autism assessment." (NB inquest, 27 Aug 2026); "Regulation 28 Report issued to National Institute of Clinical Excellence in relation to... not publishing national guidance in relation to the prescription of weight loss medication for patients who have been prescribed anti-psychotic medication" (GT inquest, 21–29 Sep 2026) — p.300, [Incident & Inquest Oversight report]. Context: the trust's own risk register records a March 2025 position of "9347 CYP waiting for completed assessment at various stages of autism, ADHD and Core pathways" — p.85, [Risk report, appendix 1]

[LEAD] The trust has told NHSE (via its Premises Assurance Model return) it needs a £150m new circa 140-bed inpatient facility to address its ageing estate, plus £1.5m for patient-environment deficiencies.
[Evidence] "The submission identifies a requirement of £150m for a new, modern circa 140-bed inpatient facility to help address the ageing estate." — p.367; "An indicative £150m requirement for a new, modern 140-bed inpatient facility was included within the return." — p.368, [Premises Assurance Model report 2025/26]

[LEAD] Assaults on staff more than doubled in two years (730 to 1,561; +114%), weapon incidents rose 41% in-year, and the trust logged four "alleged homicide" incidents in 2025/26 (up from none) — with the security report explicitly linking pressures to bed closures at other organisations.
[Evidence] "Assaults to Staff 730 / 1309 / 1561... +114%"; "Alleged Homicide 2 / 0 / 4"; "Weapon 45 / 44 / 62... +41%" — p.405; "these increases reflect better reporting, greater awareness and growing operational pressures across services with connections to reduced out of area placements and known system challenge resulting from bed closures within other organisations, availability of specialist placements and Intensive Care beds" — p.406, [Annual Security Report 2025/26]

[LEAD] The board has still not settled its position on applying single-sex requirements to inpatient placement decisions, after legal advice was reported to the People and Culture Committee — an alert-rated item now headed for a board-level discussion.
[Evidence] "The Committee considered legal advice reported during the meeting concerning the application of sex-related requirements to inpatient placement decisions... The Committee did not reach a settled organisational position and considered that a broader Board-level discussion was required. Interim weekly oversight of affected patients and access to on-call decision-making structures were reported to be in place." — p.40, [People and Culture Committee chair's report, 30 Sep]

[LEAD] Finance: month 6 is a £4k surplus against breakeven only with c.£2.8m of non-recurrent savings propping it up; worst-case forecast is a £5.6m deficit, recurrent savings are £1.5m short of the £17.8m VIP target, the 2027/28 outlook was alert-rated as "significantly challenging", and the bank-staff reduction target is formally declared off track with "no realistic plans" to hit it.
[Evidence] "the worst-case forecast deficit remained approximately £5.6m. The £17.8m VIP target was forecast to be achieved, but recurrent savings were forecast to be £1.5m short of plan" — p.56; "The current-year underlying deficit and reliance on non-recurrent VIP created a steeper starting position for future planning. A substantial value improvement requirement was expected" — p.55, [Performance & Finance Committee chair's report]; "Current performance shows only a 6% reduction and there are presently no realistic plans identified to achieve the full target during 2026/27" — p.329, [Business Plan Q1/Q2 update]; "At month 6, the position is supported by c.£2.8m in non-recurrent VIP delivery" — p.93, [IPR]

[LEAD] Employee relations cases are at their highest since July 2024, disciplinary cases are "generally at the serious end", and the disproportionate representation of Black African staff in disciplinary processes is continuing — with dignity-at-work and disciplinary activity concentrated in one part of the trust.
[Evidence] "Employee relations cases were reported to be at their highest level since July 2024, with particularly high levels of disciplinary and dignity-at-work cases... disproportionate representation of Black African staff within disciplinary processes continued. A concentration of dignity-at-work and disciplinary activity within one part of the Trust was highlighted as requiring focused leadership and cultural attention." — p.42, [People and Culture Committee chair's report, 30 Sep]. Related: an ED/NED service visit found "Staff in one service reported experiencing racist abuse and other harmful behaviour." — p.348, [Chair & CEO report appendix]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has been allocated £11m capital to build four 24/7 neighbourhood mental health hubs and one mental health emergency department by 2029 under the 10-Year Plan — but executives flagged there is no equivalent revenue funding, no national specifications and a limited evidence base.
[Evidence] "The Trust has been allocated a total of £11m capital investment to deliver 4 NMH hubs and 1 MH ED/Crisis Assessment Centre between 2026-2029"; "Although capital funding had been allocated, there was no equivalent clarity regarding ongoing revenue funding. The lack of national specifications and a limited evidence base from pilot sites added to the delivery risk." — p.64, [Trust Management Board chair's report, 26 Aug]

[WORTH WATCHING] Concerns about "openness and transparency" at Beckett Place (low secure): enhanced oversight is in place and a cultural diagnostic plus an external-style peer review of the clinical model are under way across the low secure pathway.
[Evidence] "The Committee received an update on concerns regarding openness and transparency at Beckett Place... Across the low secure pathway, a cultural diagnostic and peer review of the clinical model were under way, with additional investment intended to support a consolidated improvement plan." — p.50, [Quality Committee chair's report, 27 Oct]. Separately a £1.3m Low Secure Services investment case (specialised commissioning-funded) goes to this board — p.58.
