# University Hospital Southampton NHS FT (RHM) — Trust Board, 9 July 2026

Single combined pack (~29MB). Large acute + research FT. Hampshire and Isle of Wight ICS. In-year plan: breakeven; £7.3m deficit at M2 (on plan). Segment 3 (financial deficit override). Correspondent: Mimi.

## 1. Top lines

**LEAD — UHS has formally exited NHSE's national Recovery Support Programme after three years, moving to regionally led oversight.**
"It has now been confirmed, following a regional recommendation to the national executive team, that UHS has transitioned out of RSP and into regionally led oversight and support." — p.1 [Financial Undertakings – Self Assessment, item 7.3]. The trust "has been in the national Recovery Support Programme since June 2023 and has had varying levels of reviews and support during that time (including a Financial Improvement Director in 2025/26)." Board asked to submit "partial compliance" on two of the financial undertakings, "recognising the remaining level of financial risk within our improvement programmes."

**LEAD — c.£35m of the trust's £81m 2026/27 CIP target remains not fully developed, and internal assurance on delivery is "limited."**
"There remained c.£35m of not yet fully developed schemes under the Trust's CIP plan for 2026/27." — p.2, [Finance, Investment & Cash Committee Chair's Report, 22 June 2026, item 5.2]. Fully developed schemes had risen from £8.9m to £34.3m and unidentified CIP fallen from £13.4m to £2.2m, but "the level of assurance to achievement of the full year plan remains limited." NHSE target is "0% unidentified, 80% fully identified by the end of M3… meeting this target looks challenging for providers." — p.1, [Finance Report M2, item 5.10]. Finance report risk rating: **High**.

**LEAD — Trust declared a Critical Incident on 25 June 2026 amid a heat-health alert, escalating to a system-wide Major Incident; MRI and theatre capacity lost.**
"The Trust declared a Critical Incident at 12:29 on 25 June 2026 in response to the combined impact of the Red / Amber Heat-Health Alert, significant operational pressure, and heat-related disruption to clinical capacity and infrastructure." — p.2, [CEO Report, item 5.5]. "Due to cooling issues, the Trust experienced reduced MRI capacity and, because temperatures affected some theatre environments, some planned activity was stood down." The wider Local Resilience Forum escalated to a Major Incident. "Capacity and flow remains challenging."

**LEAD — Trust is tendering for financial recovery consultancy support, with a business case already approved by NHSE.**
"We are in the process of tendering for financial recovery consultancy support, with a business case approved by NHSE." — p.3, [Finance Report M2, item 5.10]. Also engaging separate consultancy on non-criteria-to-reside processes.

**LEAD — 9 of 12 board assurance framework risks now rated "critical" (75%), outside the trust's risk appetite; estates and finance risks have no clear route back to appetite.**
"The organisation continues to manage a significant level of risk resulting from increased operational and financial pressures… with 9 out of 12 risks rated critically and outside of the organisation's risk appetite." — p.1, [BAF Update, item 5.6]. Risk 5b (Estates) "there is not yet a clear route or timescale to achieve this due to insufficient funding levels." Risk 5a (Finances) reduction "does not align with our risk appetite." — p.2.

**WORTH WATCHING — Four never events recorded in Q4 2025/26; 11 of 21 reviewed patient safety incidents attributed to "failure to rescue," triggering a deep-dive.**
"There had been four never events recorded in the fourth quarter." "A review had been undertaken of 21 patient safety incident investigations, of which 11 were recorded as being due to 'failure to rescue'. A deep-dive was being conducted." — p.1–2, [Quality Committee Chair's Report, 1 June 2026, item 5.4].

**WORTH WATCHING — Well-Led review by external firm thevaluecircle completed; recommendations feeding into strategy launch and NHSE capability self-assessment.**
"An overview was provided of the people-related recommendations from the Well-Led review carried out by thevaluecircle." — p.1, [People & OD Committee Chair's Report, 22 June 2026, item 5.3]. CEO report links the strategy launch to "an action from our recent Well-Led review" — p.2, [CEO Report]. "It is likely we will draw on the report from the Value Circle as evidence in responding to the self-assessment." — p.1, [NHS Oversight Framework Update, item 5.7].

**WORTH WATCHING — Non-criteria-to-reside (NCTR) numbers climbing back to pre-fire levels; third-party review recommendations pending.**
"NCTR numbers are above our planned levels (160) and are heading towards pre-fire levels." — p.2, [Finance Report M2]. "Whilst NCTR reduced from 239 to 156 in the aftermath of the fire, it has gradually increased in May to 201." "The recommendations from the third-party non-criteria to reside review will be presented at the next committee meeting." — p.1, [FIC Committee report].

**WORTH WATCHING — Trust ranked 68 of 134 acute trusts in NHSE Q4 league table (up from 73); remains segment 3 due to deficit.**
"The Trust recorded an average score of 2.32 (0.11 lower than the previous quarter) and was ranked 68 out of 134 (73 out of 134 during the third quarter). Due to being in financial deficit, the Trust remains in segment 3." — p.3, [CEO Report].

**WORTH WATCHING — Trust below ERF (elective recovery) targets at M2, flagged as a financial risk not yet in the position.**
"Early indications are that the Trust is below ERF targets for M2, linked to operational pressures, loss of theatre capacity from the fire and industrial action… should this materialise in final data, the Trust will need to recover the position or identify mitigations to remain on plan." — p.2, [Finance Report M2]. Productivity: "NHSE have set a target for 2% productivity, and our plan requires delivery of 5%. Early indications in M1-M2 highlight a loss of productivity."

## 2. FOI leads

- **Well-Led review report (thevaluecircle).** The pack repeatedly references a completed external Well-Led review but does not publish the report or its findings. Request from UHS: the full thevaluecircle Well-Led review report, terms of reference, and management action plan. Why it matters: named external firm + governance review = near-certain story lift (cf. Doncaster & Bassetlaw).
- **RSP exit report.** "The RSP team provided an 'RSP exit report', which was reviewed at the Finance, Investment & Cash Committee in May 2026." — p.1, [item 7.3]. Request from UHS/NHSE South East: the RSP exit report and the regional recommendation to the national executive team. Why: documents the terms and conditions under which a trust leaves NHSE's most intensive support regime.
- **Financial recovery consultancy contract.** Tender "with a business case approved by NHSE" — p.3, [Finance Report]. Request from UHS: the approved business case, tender/contract value, term, supplier, and deliverables once let. Why: NHS-funded turnaround consultancy at a trust exiting RSP.
- **Non-criteria-to-reside third-party review.** Recommendations "will be presented at the next committee meeting" — not in this pack. Request: the third-party NCTR review report, commissioning firm and cost.
- **NHSE CEO letter, 30 June 2026 (Sir James Mackey).** Attached as Appendix A to CEO report — a maternity/mortuary 10-point action plan. Request the trust's board assurance response due to NHSE by 31 July on mortuary/maternity services (board self-assessment referenced in CEO report and the Mackey letter).
- **Section 250 Crime and Policing Act risk assessment.** "The Trust's local counter-fraud specialist has commenced work to develop a Section 250 risk assessment." — p.6, [CEO Report]. Request the completed risk assessment identifying areas of corporate criminal exposure (fraud, procurement, IG, H&S).
- **Inappropriate patient-record access sanctions.** "The Trust has recorded and reported a number of incidents of inappropriate access of patient records by staff members. In some cases, this has led to dismissal." — p.5, [CEO Report]. FOI: number of incidents, dismissals and other sanctions for unauthorised record access, 2023–26.

## 4. Context / notes

Not a routine pack. The stand-out trust-specific development is the **RSP exit** paired with the **£35m undeveloped CIP** and **fresh recovery-consultancy tender** — a trust leaving intensive NHSE support while still carrying high financial risk. The **critical/major incident (heat)** is a clean operational story. The **Well-Led review by thevaluecircle** is the strongest FOI target. Wider context: 1 Feb 2026 fire still driving reduced bed capacity (c.100 beds below pre-fire), lost theatre/ERF capacity. HIOW ICS reported an £81.43m deficit at 2025/26 year end (per May minutes) with £44.02m deficit support to be withdrawn. CEO report is heavily national/maternity-focused (Ockenden/Amos reviews, Mackey 10-point plan) rather than local commentary — no quotable local pushback line from the CEO this month.
