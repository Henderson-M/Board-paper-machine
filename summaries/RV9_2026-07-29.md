## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO Michele Moran discloses a set of newly agreed national mental health "stretch targets", set by the national MH chief executives' group she sits on and to be policed by ICBs rather than NHSE.
[Evidence] "Following work by the national Chief Executive CEO group (which I am part of) and agreement by the wider Mental Health CEOS. The following targets have been agreed; these will initially be monitored by ICBS not the NHSE. 1. Eliminate inappropriate Out of Area Placements (OAPs) - 95% reduction by March 2027 2. Reduce 12-hour ED breaches for patients already known to MH services - Lowest possible 12-hour breach level 3. Improve Children and Young People (CYP) waiting times - 50% seen within 4 weeks by March 2027" — p.42, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] Six named Humber & North Yorkshire provider CEOs have formed a new "Provider Leadership Group" that is actively scoping wholly owned subsidiaries and income diversification; it held its first meeting on 13 July 2026.
[Evidence] "The proposed Provider Leadership Group will comprise the CEOs across York and Scarborough THFT, Humber Health Partnership, Harrogate and District FT, Humber FT, City Health Partnership, and Yorkshire Ambulance Service." Initial priority areas for scoping include "Provision of wholly owned subsidiaries" and "Income diversification" — p.40-41, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] An Audit Yorkshire internal audit of digital governance issued two "Major" recommendations and links weak arrangements to a decline in the trust's "Well Led" digital maturity score; the trust's response is to bolt digital onto the Finance Committee and expand the finance director's job title.
[Evidence] "executive-level ownership of the digital strategy agenda is not clearly defined, contributing to reduced strategic alignment and to the decline observed in the 'Well Led' domain of the Digital Maturity Assessment... the absence of a formal committee with explicit responsibility for digital oversight". Recommendations: "Major 2 / Moderate 4 / Minor 3" — p.112-113, [Digital Governance Review, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust confirms it has been asked to bid to the ICB for up to £4.8m to redesign neurodevelopmental pathways, having earlier disclosed there is no commissioned service at all for East Riding adult ADHD referrals.
[Evidence] "The Trust have been asked to submit a proposal to the ICB to utilise an initial £2.4m investment (with a potential further £2.4m) to redesign neurodevelopmental pathways across Hull and East Riding... The aim is to reduce reliance on Right to Choose (RTC)" (p.71); "there being no commissioned service for East Riding adult referrals" (p.44) — [Chief Executive's Report, Item 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] Named NED Dean Royles has left the trust — recorded only in passing in the Freedom to Speak Up report, with no reason given, as the handover of the NED speak-up lead role.
[Evidence] "Carrie Woolerton has been appointed as the new Non-Executive Director Lead for speaking up replacing Dean Royles who has left the Trust." — p.99, [Freedom to Speak Up Annual Report 2025/26, Item 9]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's 2026 WRES data shows harassment/abuse of ethnically diverse staff running at roughly double the rate for white staff and above national benchmarks, with staff-reported harassment now above national comparator after a previous downward trend.
[Evidence] "40% of ethnically diverse staff reported harassment or abuse from patients or the public which is approximately double the rate experienced by White staff and exceeds national benchmarks"; "after a steady downward trend reports of harassment from staff have increased and are now above national comparator levels" — p.348, [People & OD Committee Assurance Report, Item 23]
