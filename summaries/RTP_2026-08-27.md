## Top lines

[LEAD] KPMG has referred Surrey and Sussex to the health secretary under section 30 of the Local Audit and Accountability Act for breaching its cumulative break-even duty.
[Evidence] "Other reporting — We issued a referral to the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care as required by Section 30 of the Local Audit and Accountability Act due to the Trust being in breach of the cumulative break-even duty" — p.4, [3.1.2 External Audits report / KPMG Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26]

[LEAD] KPMG found a significant weakness in the trust's value-for-money arrangements — because risks sat on its significant risk register with no plan to bring them down.
[Evidence] "we have concluded there is a significant weakness in the Trusts arrangements to achieve Value for Money due to the number of risks held on the significant risk register that did not have action plans identified in order to reduce them to an acceptable level of risk." — p.17, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[LEAD] The auditors found 31 risks past their review date, six estates risks with no improvement actions at all, and some risks that had been on the register for more than a decade.
[Evidence] "in March 2026 there were 31 risks that were past their review date, some of which were due to be renewed in October 2025. There were 6 risks, all within the estates domain, that had no actions to improve the rating. There were a further 12 risks that had only one action which was either not started, cancelled, or on hold... A higher than expected number of risks had been on the risk register for a long time, some upwards of 10 years." Target risk levels exist but "this information is not included in the version that are sent to the Audit, Assurance and risk Committee". — p.15, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[LEAD] The trust missed its 2025/26 deficit plan by £11.4m, and has now run up an accumulated deficit of £103m.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a deficit of £20.5 million compared with an original planned deficit of £9.1 million. This deterioration was driven in part by lower than planned delivery of its Cost Improvement Programme (CIP), with savings of £25.5 million achieved against a planned target of £33.3 million." And: "since then they have made significant deficits with a cumulative deficit over the past four years of £95.4m and an accumulated deficit of £103.0m." Year-end cash fell from £10.2m to £4.2m. — pp.11–14, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[LEAD] The trust was roughly £2m a month behind plan for seven months before it formally revised its forecast.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported internally that it was approximately £2 million per month behind plan between months 4 and 10 of 2025/26, driven primarily by missing their CIP target, before formally revising its forecast position." — p.12, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[LEAD] The CQC's well-led inspection has landed at Requires Improvement, criticising leadership, culture, governance, EDI and speaking up — and the trust's response leans on the eight months since the inspectors were there.
[Evidence] "In July the Care Quality Commission (CQC) published its report into trust leadership. The well-led inspection, which took place last November, resulted in the Trust receiving an overall rating of Requires Improvement... The report identified a number of areas where improvement is needed, particularly around leadership, culture, governance, equality, diversity and inclusion, and speaking up." — pp.2–3, [1.6 Chief Executive's report]. KPMG separately records that the trust "received a requires improvement CQC report in the year driven by the 'Safe' and 'Well-led' domains and received two S29A notices in the year" — p.11, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Internal audit could give only partial assurance on the trust's control framework — a downgrade on the previous year.
[Evidence] 2026 Head of Internal Audit Opinion: "The opinion states that the Board can take partial assurance that the controls upon which the Trust relies to manage these risks are suitably designed, consistently applied and effective, and that action is required to strengthen the control framework." The 2025 opinion had said the trust "has an adequate and effective framework for risk management, governance and internal control." — p.17, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Three of the trust's eight executive posts changed hands during 2025/26, and the chief finance officer has only just been made substantive after ten months acting up.
[Evidence] KPMG: "The Trust have had a high turnover in its executive officers in the year with three of the eight positions changing in the year." — p.11, [3.1.2 External Audits report]. CEO report: "we announced the substantive appointment of David Cooper as the new Chief Finance Officer for the Trust, after covering the role on an interim basis since October 2025." Stephen Wells also joins as substantive Executive Director of Estates and Facilities. — p.4, [1.6 Chief Executive's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] A high number of single tender waivers, mostly in estates — not enough for a significant weakness, but flagged.
[Evidence] "The Trust issued a high number of single tender waiver forms in the year however the total value decreased slightly from the prior year. We noted that the majority of these related to the estates department. We consider this large number of waivers does not give rise to a significant weakness since the total value... has reduced since the prior year". Two further control findings were raised "around journals and the valuation of estates". — p.19, [3.1.2 External Audits report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Q1 deficit of £4.8m this year, against a £35.6m capital programme, with the CEO warning Q2 looks worse.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a year-to-date deficit of £4.8m at the end of Quarter 1, with the in-month position improving to a deficit of £1.1m in Month 3. The financial headwinds look more difficult in the early part of Quarter 2 and the organisation is responding with significant work underway to deliver mitigations to keep our plans on track. The Trust is delivering a £35.6m capital programme". — p.3, [1.6 Chief Executive's report]

## FOI leads

- **The section 30 referral itself.** This is the strongest item in the pack. Request from the Department of Health and Social Care, and from the trust: the section 30 referral letter KPMG sent to the Secretary of State, and any DHSC response or acknowledgement. Also worth asking DHSC how many section 30 referrals it received for the 2025/26 audit round and which bodies they concerned — that is a national story in its own right.
- **The two section 29A notices.** KPMG records that the trust "received two S29A notices in the year". The CEO report does not mention them. Request from the CQC and the trust: both notices and the trust's responses.
- **The CQC well-led action plan.** The CEO says the trust "already implemented a comprehensive action plan overseen by the Board". Request the action plan and the board's progress reports against it.
- **The single tender waivers.** Request the single tender waiver register for 2025/26 — number, value, supplier and justification, broken down by department, given KPMG's finding that most sat in estates.
- **The medium-term financial plan.** KPMG notes "planned efficiencies within the MTFP were categorised according to their delivery risk, with a significant proportion classified as high risk" (p.16). Request the MTFP to 2028/29 with the risk-rating of each efficiency line.
