## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO Chris Oliver takes on a dual role running both LSCFT and Greater Manchester Mental Health FT from 1 July until he leaves in September; Hunter Healthcare appointed to run the substantive CEO recruitment.
[Evidence] "Chris will commence a dual role across both organisations commencing on 1 July 2026 until his official leaving date in September... Chris will remain as Accountable Officer for LSCFT during this period and will also become the Accountable Officer for GMMH dividing his time between LSCFT and GMMH across the week. On 1 July, Dr Gareth Thomas will become the full time Deputy CEO for LSCFT... and Dr Dush Mahadevan will become Interim Chief Medical Officer." / "Hunter Healthcare have been appointed to lead the recruitment process." — p.111, [Trust Chair's Report TB 150 26]

[LEAD] The trust is behind its financial plan at month 2, reporting a £1.25m–£1.3m deficit against a planned £0.7m deficit, driven almost entirely by efficiency slippage, with the full-year plan resting on a £39m efficiency requirement and a £6m risk-rated gap already flagged.
[Evidence] "The Trust has a deficit of £1.25m against a planned deficit of £0.71m. The position is impacted by slippage on efficiency schemes, increased costs for Independent Sector Beds... and industrial action." / BAF risk PR10: "Failure to deliver the financial plan for 2025/26... and a significant efficiency target (£39m). This impacts quality of care resulting in a reduction in headcount through redundancies, a constrained cash position and regulatory impact." / "the risk rated position is £6.0m." — p.140/142, [Month 2 Financial Update TB 153 26]

[LEAD] Deaths of service users with a learning disability and/or autism nearly doubled year on year, from 31 in 2024/25 to 60 in 2025/26.
[Evidence] "There was an increase in reported deaths of service users with a learning disability and/or autism (60 deaths in 2025/26 vs 31 deaths in 2024/25)." — p.63, [Learning from Deaths Annual Report 2025/26 TB 146 26]

[LEAD] The proportion of concluded mortality reviews demonstrating acceptable standards of care fell from 93% to 80% year on year, and stage 2 reviews rated satisfactory-or-above fell from 78.79% to 70.8%, with the trust attributing reduced sampling of expected deaths to "reduced staffing levels".
[Evidence] "80% of concluded mortality reviews (stage 1 and stage 2) demonstrate acceptable standards of care. This has fallen from 93% in 24/25." / "Concluded stage 2 reviews achieving an overall care rating of satisfactory or above has fallen from 78.79% in 2024/25 to 70.8%." / "Due to reduced staffing levels during 2025/26, the mortality review programme exercised stricter controls on sampling of expected deaths, to manage capacity and demand." — p.59/68, [Learning from Deaths Annual Report 2025/26 TB 146 26]

[LEAD] LSCFT is handing its ADHD and ASD waiting lists — 476 ASD cases and 2,079 untriaged ADHD cases — to Blackpool Teaching Hospitals, which becomes the new provider from June 2026, with the ICB yet to confirm arrangements beyond a commissioned 12-month period.
[Evidence] "in June 2026 Blackpool Teaching Hospitals will become the new service provider and all new referrals for ASD will be redirected... This relates to 476 cases which will transfer." / "there is an agreement that 2079 cases which have not yet been triaged... will also transfer to Blackpool Teaching Hospitals... Currently for ADHD this has been commissioned for a 12-month period and the ICB has yet to confirm plans for the management of those cases at the end of that period." — p.25-26, [Improving Access — Month 2 TB 144 26]

[WORTH WATCHING] Only 19% of patients requiring an acute mental health bed were admitted within 24 hours of a bed request, against a trust objective of 60% by April 2027.
[Evidence] "One of the Trust 12 key objectives is to achieve 60% of admissions within 24-hours by April 2027. Currently this position stands at 19% for our entire bed stock." — p.16, [Improving Access — Month 2 TB 144 26]

[WORTH WATCHING] Over 3,000 incidents were reported in May, with key themes of self-harm, violence towards staff and delays in admission escalated to the board.
[Evidence] "Over 3,000 incidents were reported in May, largely resulting in low or no harm... Key themes included self-harm, violence towards staff and delays in admission." — p.119, [Board Committee Report TB 152 26]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust benchmarks as the 28th (of 43) highest user of inappropriate out-of-area placements, averaging 7 beds per day in April 2026, and is being asked to deliver a 95% reduction on March 2026 levels for 2026/27.
[Evidence] "LSCFT currently Benchmark as the 28th (of 43) highest user of inappropriate OAPs, based on an average of 7 beds used per day in April 2026." — p.115, [Chief Executive Report TB 151 26]

[WORTH WATCHING] The £12.4m net funding gap on independent-sector beds is being treated by the trust as a commissioner responsibility, and its breakeven forecast is explicitly predicated on the ICB agreeing to fund it.
