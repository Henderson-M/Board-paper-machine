# Surrey and Borders Partnership NHS FT (RXX) — Trust Board (Public), 8 July 2026

- **Source:** SABP public Trust Board papers, meeting 8 July 2026 ("2026.07.08 - Public - Trust Board Papers - Final")
- **Pack:** single PDF, 264 pages (~10.3MB)
- **Trust type:** mental health, learning disability and community trust (Surrey / NE Hampshire)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-06
- **Page refs:** cite the PDF page number of the extracted text ("PDF p.X"); paper titles given to disambiguate.

Note: routine mental-health board pack, but with several substantive threads — a fall to NOF Segment 3 at year-end, a completed Grant Thornton well-led review, a disability-discrimination tribunal case, and worsening staff "speaking up" and racial-abuse-from-service-users scores. Weighted lead is the NOF downgrade.

## Top lines

[LEAD] SABP was rated Segment 3 in the National Oversight Framework at 2025/26 year-end — down from Segment 2 — with Effectiveness & Experience stuck in Segment 4 and Patient Safety in Segment 3, and its national ranking sliding from 22nd to 34th of 61 MH/community trusts.
[Evidence] "Regrettably in Quarter 4 we have been rated as Segment 3 at the year-end (31st March 2026)... Sustained areas of relative pressure remain in Effectiveness and Experience (Segment 4) and Patient Safety (Segment 3)... People and Workforce and Finance and Productivity also deteriorated by one segment in Q4." — Chief Executive's Report, PDF p.22. And: "In Q4 the trust ranks 34th out 61 Mental Health and Community Trusts, a deterioration from 22nd in Q3." — Integrated Performance Report, PDF p.75.

[LEAD] Two core operational-flow measures deteriorated materially into Q4: the crisis-referral 24-hour face-to-face metric worsened from a NOF score of 2.44 to 3.35, and the over-60-day adult acute length-of-stay metric from 3.11 to 3.43.
[Evidence] "the adult acute length of stay over 60 days measure deteriorated from a Q3 NOF score of 3.11 to 3.43 in Q4, while crisis referrals receiving face-to-face contact within 24 hours deteriorated from 2.44 to 3.35." — Integrated Performance Report, PDF p.74.

[LEAD] Grant Thornton has completed an external well-led developmental review of SABP's leadership and governance, with the resulting 2026/27 action plan approved by the board.
[Evidence] "the external well-led review commissioned and undertaken by Grant Thornton, to review where we are at and to ensure we focus in the right areas as we strive to be better." — Minutes of 8 April 2026 meeting, item 24.26, PDF p.9. And: "It also incorporates the actions arising from the developmental review of leadership and governance (Well Led) completed by Grant Thornton and previously reported to the Trust Board in public in April 2026." — Executive Board Report, PDF p.240.

[LEAD] Staff confidence in speaking up fell sharply in the 2025 NHS Staff Survey — the share feeling safe to speak up dropped 4.8 points to 63.6% — after the trust replaced its Freedom to Speak Up service in January 2026 having concluded the previous service "was not able to provide the service it wanted."
[Evidence] "63.6% of staff said they were feeling safe to speak up about anything that concerns me, down 4.8% on the previous year." And: "in the financial year 2025/26 we recognised that our Freedom to Speak up service was not able to provide the service it wanted to meet the needs of staff. As a result, a review was undertaken and we appointed The Guardian Service in January 2026." — Annual Staff Survey Summary Report, PDF p.91–92. (IPR reports the drop as 68% to 63.8% "a 10% drop since 2024," PDF p.83.)

[LEAD] Reported abuse of staff from service users, families and carers is rising: staff from under-represented ethnicities reporting such abuse jumped +8% year on year to "the highest reported percentage since 2020," now below the national average for MH/LD trusts.
[Evidence] "The number of people from under-represented ethnic backgrounds reporting experiencing abuse from people who use our services, their families and carers has significantly increased again for the second year (+8%), marking the highest reported percentage since 2020. We are also scoring below the national average for other mental health and learning disabilities NHS Trusts on this domain." — Executive Board Report (WRES), PDF p.240. The QAC referred the issue of racial abuse of staff to the People Committee (PDF p.248).

[LEAD] On the productivity/comparative-cost measure SABP ranks 59th of 71 providers nationally and expects a Segment 3 score in the emerging 2026/27 framework; a Productivity Diagnostic is under way.
[Evidence] "Comparative cost position remains an area of challenge and is currently indicative of a segmentation 3 score within the emerging framework. We are ranked 59th out of 71 providers nationally. The Productivity Diagnostic is underway to identify unwarranted variation..." — Integrated Performance Report, PDF p.77.

[WORTH WATCHING] A disability-discrimination employment tribunal case has surfaced at Audit Committee under losses and special payments, with a further review to decide whether it must be escalated to the People Committee.
[Evidence] "A further review of a disability discrimination tribunal case is required to assess learning and determine whether wider assurance or escalation to the People Committee is required." — Audit Committee Update, PDF p.257 (repeated PDF p.261).

[WORTH WATCHING] Out-of-area inpatient beds continue to exceed contracted levels and FIDC has commissioned a deep dive, flagging it as a risk for 2026/27 despite the trust hitting break-even; there was also a capital impairment during the year.
[Evidence] "The Committee seeks further assurance on the operational grip of crisis and flow as out of area beds continue to exceed contracted levels, which is a risk for the next financial year. A Deep Dive has been requested... The Committee sought assurance on a capital impairment..." — Finance, Investment and Development Committee Update, PDF p.256.

[WORTH WATCHING] A PSIRF safety-learning backlog has re-opened: 53 After Action Reviews are open (41 beyond 60 days from the incident response meeting), up from an earlier recovery position, with the greatest concentration in the neighbourhood (community) division.
[Evidence] "Currently we have 53 After Action Reviews (AARs) open in total (41 are beyond 60 days from Patient Safety Incident Response Meeting)... on 16th March 2026 we had 71 AARs open... there was a recovery plan put in place to reduce the backlog however this has not been able to be sustained... The greatest risk is currently held in the neighbourhood division." — Monthly High-Level Risk Report (Month 2), PDF p.71. New high risk 3297 (RRR 12) logged for this on 20/05/2026.

[WORTH WATCHING] Over half the trust's high-level risks are now overdue for review (28 of 52, up from 16 the previous month), and a new "extreme"-adjacent risk (RRR 12) has been logged over corporate nursing capacity due to sickness/absence, being tolerated rather than treated.
[Evidence] "There is a rise in the number of risks on the HLRR that are overdue with the amount rising from 16 last month to 28 this month representing over half of all risks on the HLRR." And risk 3296: "Risk of corporate nursing functions failing to deliver leading to compromises in quality, safety and oversight... Due to significant sickness and absence levels..." (RRR 12, Partial control, Tolerate). — Monthly High-Level Risk Report, PDF p.66, 70, 72.

[WORTH WATCHING] Enhanced (1:1/2:1) therapeutic observation hours are climbing steeply on adult acute wards — up 2,865 hours (24%) month on month to 16,715 in May 2026 — driven by patient acuity, with agency reliance a concern.
[Evidence] "Overall hours rose from 13,850 in April 2026 to 16,715 in May 2026, an increase of 2,865 hours, or 239 shifts... Efforts will focus primarily on reducing dependency on agency staffing to fill gaps... likely due to the complex needs and acuity of the current cohort we have on our working age adult inpatient wards." — Executive Board Report, PDF p.242.

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the full Grant Thornton external well-led / governance developmental review report and the board's action plan.
The pack repeatedly refers to a Grant Thornton well-led review "completed" and "reported to the Trust Board in public in April 2026," but only a summary and an action-plan endorsement appear in the papers; the findings themselves are characterised only as "encouraging." Request from SABP: the full Grant Thornton report, its terms of reference, and the complete action plan with the recommendations mapped. (Minutes 24.26, PDF p.9; Executive Board Report, PDF p.240.)

[FOI] Request details of the disability-discrimination employment tribunal case referenced by the Audit Committee.
The Audit Committee notes a "disability discrimination tribunal case" under losses and special payments requiring further review. Request from SABP: the case outcome, any payment made (settlement or award), the "learning" review, and whether it was escalated to the People Committee — plus any pattern of similar cases. (Audit Committee Update, PDF p.257, 261.)

[FOI] Request the NICHE independent homicide investigation reports and thematic analysis.
The Quality Assurance Committee refers to five NICHE independent investigations into homicides and an attempted homicide in 2020-2021 involving trust patients, making 27 recommendations across six themes, approved by NHSE's Independent Investigation Review Group. Request from SABP/NHSE: the NICHE reports (or thematic summary), the 17/27-action plan and current completion status, and the IIRG sign-off. (QAC Report, PDF p.248.)

[FOI] Request the FIDC "crisis and flow / out-of-area beds" deep-dive and the M11–M12 Value for Money reports.
FIDC requested a deep dive into out-of-area beds exceeding contracted levels and referenced a capital impairment. Request from SABP: the completed deep-dive report, the out-of-area placement bed-days and spend for 2025/26, and the nature/value of the capital impairment. (FIDC Update, PDF p.256.)
