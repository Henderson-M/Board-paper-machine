## Top lines

[LEAD] UHD abandons December 2026 Phase 3 acute reconfiguration and pushes the emergency/planned-care hospital split to April 2027 after the COAST building slips; trust will go into winter 2026 on split-site arrangements.
[Evidence] "The Coast programme continues to face delivery challenges, and confidence in a December 2026 completion is now low; the realistic expected clinically live date has slipped to April 2027… the programme cannot proceed with a December 2026 Phase 3 move with sufficient confidence in safety, commissioning or operational readiness. UHD will go into winter 2026 on the current split-site arrangements" — p.56–60, [Phase 3 Move Timeline and Options, Item 3.2]

[LEAD] Trust forecasts it will fall £38.1m short of its full-year savings requirement after risk-adjusting its efficiency programme — described in its own papers as "a material financial risk."
[Evidence] "The trust has identified total savings opportunities of £51.1 million, however when adjusted to reflect the risk of delivery in year, this is reduced to a current forecast of £30.4 million. Whilst this is an increase of £4.8 million from the April report, this is £38.1 million short of the full year savings requirement representing a material financial risk for the Trust." — p.263, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 4.7]

[LEAD] Trust reports a £6.249m year-to-date deficit at end May, £1.234m adverse to plan, including £518,000 of industrial-action costs that NHS England has confirmed it will not fund.
[Evidence] "the Trust has delivered a cumulative deficit of £6.249 million being £1.234 million adverse to plan. This includes unplanned Industrial Action costs of £518,000… NHS England has confirmed that there is no funding for Industrial Action costs which are expected to be mitigated locally." — p.262, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 4.7]

[LEAD] Delivery of the Phase 3 reconfiguration is the highest-rated risk on the board assurance framework, scored 20 (red), reflecting collapse of confidence in contractor Darwin's build timeline.
[Evidence] Transforming Care Together strategic risk — "Current Risk Rating 20" (severity 4 × likelihood 5); "There is a risk that delays and interdependencies across the Transforming Care Together (TCT) programme… result in a prolonged partial reconfiguration and impact delivery of Phase 3" — p.98–99, [Corporate Risk Register / BAF, Item 4.1]

[LEAD] Patients medically fit to leave hospital are stuck at more than double the level the reconfiguration requires, after community capacity was withdrawn.
[Evidence] "UHD currently has around 230 patients with no criteria to reside (NCTR), against a Phase 3 requirement of 110… 610 P1 hours were removed over six weeks (March to April 2026), directly correlating with NCTR rising from 212 to around 230. The Trusted Assessors Service is discontinuing on 30 June 2026, removing a further discharge enabler." — p.261, p.276, [Integrated Performance Report Appendix A, Item 4.7]

[WORTH WATCHING] Chief People Officer Melanie Whitfield has left the trust; CFO Pete Papworth is covering the executive people portfolio while a recruitment process runs.
[Evidence] "Melanie Whitfield has left her role as Chief People Officer at University Hospitals Dorset and will be taking up a new opportunity outside the Trust… Pete Papworth is currently covering the Board executive lead for our People Services whilst we consider the interim arrangements and run a recruitment process." — p.25, [Chief Executive Update, Item 2.2]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB's emerging "strategic ambition" signals a deliberate reduction in acute activity and spend, which the trust's own paper warns risks "positioning UHD as reactive within system-led contracts."
[Evidence] "The ambitions signal a deliberate reduction in acute share of activity and spend, which would result in changing how UHD delivers care… The emerging model risks positioning UHD as reactive within system-led contracts." — p.37–38, [ICB strategic ambition and Trust response, Item 3.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cancer standards missed at the start of 2026/27, with colorectal capacity hit partly by staff absence linked to "the conflict in the Middle East."
[Evidence] "the Trust did not achieve the 28 Day Faster Diagnosis Standard… reporting a position of 67.2%. The 62D Standard did not achieve the national recovery target (80%), reporting a position of 65.8%"; drivers include "50% reduction in staffing in the Colorectal service due to sickness" and "A loss of medical capacity and insourcing capacity due to staff being impacted by the conflict in the Middle East." — p.269, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 4.7]

[WORTH WATCHING] New corporate risk logged that the trust's existing electronic patient record will be unsupported from April 2027 and "is not fit for purpose," ahead of the Epic/HealthSet go-live in 2028.
[Evidence] "1950: The Trust Electronic Patient Record (EPR) will be unsupported from April 2027 and is not fit for purpose" (added to BAF 29 June 2026) — p.99, [Corporate Risk Register / BAF, Item 4.1]
