[Evidence] "The Oxfordshire Adult ADHD Service lacks sufficient operational capacity to meet demand - the service closed to new referrals in 2024 and has a waiting list of 1,842 patients awaiting assessment as of 1st June 2026 (last month at 1,887), while the average waiting time for assessment is 169 weeks. Longest waiter is 6.5 years." / "A patient safety thematic review has been started across Buckinghamshire and Oxfordshire services as there have been a number of deaths identified for patients on the ADHD waiting list since early 2022." — p.266–267, [Integrated Performance Report – July 2026, Item 11(a), Directorate exception reporting]

## Notes for the correspondent

- **Cross-cutting theme [INFERENCE]:** three separate data-quality failures surface in this one pack — the UCR submission error that cost the trust a segment, the WRES/WDES shortlisting error in the published 2024/25 returns, and the change in out-of-area placement classification methodology that drove the reported OAP reduction (p.157–158). The pack does not link them; the connection is ours.
- The out-of-area placement improvement is explicitly attributed to a reporting rule change, not to service change: "the reported reduction is primarily attributable to the implementation of revised reporting rules following clarification of the criteria used to define appropriate and inappropriate bed placements" — p.158, [IPR, Item 11].
- Separate ADHD/autism waits, same pack: CAMHS ADHD and Autism service 3,861 waiting, mean 83 weeks, longest 5.6 years, "Nearly half of the patients waiting have waited more than 2 years" (p.262); Bucks Adult ADHD 882 waiting, mean 151 weeks; Adult Autism 446 waiting, longest waiter 6.8 years (p.262).
- One inpatient suspected suicide in May 2026 — "Prior to this death the last inpatient suicide was in September 2024" (p.160) — and a "Road Traffic Accident and arrest for manslaughter (adult community mental health team)" listed among 12 patient safety reviews declared in May–June 2026 (p.289).
- CEO on bed capacity, from the May minutes: "Buckinghamshire had the lowest level of beds per head for the population in England, whilst Oxfordshire had a lower level of beds per head than average" — p.22, [Minutes of 20 May 2026, BOD 49/26(g)].
