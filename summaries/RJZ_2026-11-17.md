## Top lines

[LEAD] A Carnall Farrar review commissioned by South East London ICB and its three acute trusts projects the acute system deficit rising from £256m to £1bn by 2030/31 without change, with King's alone at a £369m "do-nothing" deficit, and is going to each trust board for formal consideration.
[Evidence] "The combined deficit for the acute providers is projected to rise from £256 million in 2025/26 to £1 billion by 2030/31 before CIP delivery. Further, deficit support funding is to be phased out from 2026/27 and the acute providers will be required to navigate the income impacts of deconstruction of the block." — p.181, [South East London Acute Services Review Summary Report, 8 October 2026, Item 15]. "They move from do-nothing deficit positions of £440 million at GSTT, £369 million at KCHT, and £201 million at LGT to a residual position of a £29 million deficit, breakeven (with a remaining £149 million residual disaggregation), and a £18 million deficit respectively." — p.189. "The ICB and acute providers jointly commissioned Carnall Farrar (CF) to undertake an Acute Services Review (ASR) to identify the system-wide transformation needed to move from structural deficit to financial balance." — p.175, [Acute Services Review cover paper, Item 15]. Note: the report text gives the ICB's do-nothing 2030/31 position as a £293m deficit (p.181) while the slide pack gives "£791m deficit by 2030/31 (excluding efficiency)" (p.195-196); the two are on different bases and the pack does not reconcile them in one place.

[LEAD] The same review recommends cross-trust elective surgical hubs, consolidation of breast surgery off some of its four current sites, a "smaller acute footprint" for dental services, and a joint review of maternity and general paediatrics across south east London.
[Evidence] "The three providers and the ICB should mobilise system-wide transformation and service change in the following areas: Outpatient transformation programme... Elective surgical hubs... Breast surgery to consolidate surgical activity to strengthen service resilience and improve quality." and "Maternity and general paediatrics should be jointly explored for transformation in South East London." — p.191, [Acute Services Review Summary Report, Item 15]. On breast surgery: "Breast surgery is delivered from four sites, with around 1,700 admitted spells in 2025: 762 at Guy's, 367 at the PRUH, 297 at Denmark Hill and 294 at QEH... Three of the four sites operate at less than half the national median provider volume." — p.187. On maternity: "As volumes decline, more sites will fall below viable scale, and over half are projected to drop under the 2,500-delivery sustainability threshold by 2035. This will further exacerbate the current financial challenge as every maternity site in South London is loss-making, with a combined deficit of over £140m." — p.190. On paediatrics: "the PRUH, UHL and QEH each requiring less than 15 beds almost entirely for non-elective activity. At these volumes, the cost of 24/7 consultant cover may not be proportionate to activity." — p.190. Four prioritised cross-provider programmes are "worth around £110m a year at full scale" — p.200.
