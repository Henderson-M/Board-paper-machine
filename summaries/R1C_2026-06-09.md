## Top lines

[LEAD] HIOW Healthcare names Suzanne Rankin (ex-Ashford & St Peter's, Cardiff & Vale) as new CEO from 3 September 2026; Ron Shields retires in July after 48 years, with CFO Paula Anderson as interim CEO in the gap.
[Evidence] "Suzanne Rankin will join us with effect from 3 September 2026… Ron Shields, our current Chief Executive, steps down in July after 48 years in the NHS… Once Ron has retired, and before Suzanne joins us, Paula Anderson, our Chief Finance Officer and Deputy Chief Executive will lead the Trust during this short interim period." — p.25, [Chair's Report, paras 1.1–1.3]

[LEAD] HIOW Integrated Care System ended 2025/26 with an £81.43m deficit against a planned £0.47m surplus — an £81.9m adverse variance — and NHS England withheld £19.14m of deficit support funding because of the system's financial performance.
[Evidence] "The System is reporting a final year end position of a deficit £81.43m, compared to a planned year-to-date surplus of £0.47m, so an £81.90m adverse variance to plan at financial year end… At M12 the System received £44.02m of DSF, with £19.14m withheld by NHS England due to the systems financial performance." — p.211, [System Report 2025/26 Month 12]

[LEAD] The trust's own Financial Recovery Plan (CIP) for 2026/27 has only 59% of schemes fully developed, with flagged reliance on non-recurrent savings; the F&P Committee chair has made it a standing board item.
[Evidence] "The Committee reviewed the Financial Recovery Plan (Cost Improvement Plan), noting the savings requirements, with 90% of schemes identified and 59% fully developed. Key risks were highlighted, including reliance on non-recurrent savings, delivery of corporate schemes, and workforce savings." — p.154, [Finance & Performance Committee report 26/05/2026]
[Context] At the previous (07/04/2026) meeting the F&P report cited "the £49m cost improvement programme for 2026/27, including £15m previously unidentified at the time of meeting." — p.13, [Minutes 07/04/2026, item 12.1.3]

[LEAD] External firm BDO has produced a lessons-learned review of "Project Fusion" — the transaction that created the trust — with 13 key findings; the board discussed a draft management response on 12 May but the findings are not set out in the public pack.
[Evidence] "At the workshop on 12 May the Board also received a report from BDO that had been commissioned by our Audit Committee Chair and Non-Executive Director, Chris Fisher, on Project Fusion (which was the name for the transaction activity that created our Trust as a new organisation) and a draft Management Response to the 13 key findings in the report, which we discussed." — p.26, [Chair's Report, para 1.13]

[LEAD] The trust has received its draft CQC Well-Led review report (inspection carried out November 2025) but it is not yet finalised or published; the trust is "currently reviewing this draft."
[Evidence] "We have received our draft CQC Well Led Review report relating to the inspection carried out across the Trust in November 2025. We are currently reviewing this draft seeking to understand the evidence base used prior to the report's finalisation and publication." — p.26, [Chair's Report, para 1.12]; agenda lists a verbal "CQC Well-Led Report" update (Item 12).

[WORTH WATCHING] Chief of Strategy & Transformation Alex Heritage left at the end of May, leaving the transformation/EPR programme and Green Plan without a substantive executive lead; an interim "reset" solution is being put in for the incoming CEO to review.
[Evidence] "we discussed the Transformation Programme and the need to find fresh leadership for this since Alex Heritage's resignation as Chief Strategy and Transformation Officer for the Trust (he left the Trust at the end of May). Having looked at this subject again at a Board Workshop on 12 May, I have now agreed with Ron that he and I will put in place an interim solution for this reset as soon as possible for Suzanne Rankin to review." — p.26, [Chair's Report, para 1.11]; action log shows "Executive Lead for the Green Plan to be identified" still open, with N&R Committee to consider interim cover "once the new Chief Executive is in post" — p.22, [Action Log ref 13.2.4].

[WORTH WATCHING] National staff survey results were "disappointing", with declines particularly on recommending the trust as a place to work; response rate 51%. Trust attributing partly to merger/organisational change but board told not to use that "as an excuse".
[Evidence] "the results were disappointing, though consistent with wider NHS trends and reflective of the challenges associated with organisational change and integration. Andrea commented on the scores that had declined, particularly in relation to recommending the Trust as a place to work… organisational pressure but emphasised that these should not be perceived as an excuse for the staff survey results." — pp.12, [Minutes 07/04/2026, items 11.1–11.2]; CEO update called results "disappointing… staff not feeling valued and lacking control in their roles" — p.8, [item 6.1.7].

[WORTH WATCHING] A "significant rise in formal cases and tribunals" reported to People Committee, with disparities "particularly affecting minority ethnic and disabled staff", plus continued workplace violence and aggression.
[Evidence] "The Employee Relations Six Month Update was received, noting a significant rise in formal cases and tribunals, reflecting ongoing cultural and operational pressures. It was noted that disparities persisted, particularly affecting minority ethnic and disabled staff." — p.114, [People Committee report 19/05/2026, Advise]

[WORTH WATCHING] Patients with no criteria to reside have run above the mean for eight consecutive months, driven partly by the Mid & North division expanding admission criteria to take patients transferred from University Hospital Southampton "following the critical incident"/fire there.
[Evidence] "The volume of bed days lost to patients with no criteria to reside (NCtR) has remained above the mean for 8 consecutive months. The majority of the issues in recent months relate to the Mid and North division where the admission criteria has been expanded to accept patients transferred from University Hospitals Southampton following the critical incident." — p.159, [IPR Feb/Mar 2026]; system report: "increasing in recent months following a reduction immediately after the fire" — p.208, [System Report M12].
