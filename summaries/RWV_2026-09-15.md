## Top lines

[LEAD] Devon Partnership Trust's own safer staffing report rates its inpatient staffing position red overall and states that care is being delivered below expected safety standards on every ward.
[Evidence] "Safe staffing is maintained at lower levels than required across all wards, with patient care delivered within lower than expected safety standards with adaptive approaches to skill mix at times of high acuity along with bank and as a last resort, agency staffing." Also: total shortfall of 79.37 WTE against required staffing; fill rate 87.8%; temporary staff 34.9% of actual staffing against an "NHSE temporary staffing target of less than 30%"; Adult Inpatient and Secure Inpatient both rated Red; "The current staffing position should be rated as Red overall." – supp p.12-15, [Safer Staffing – Workforce Assurance Report (July 2026 Data), Item 2.3.1]

[LEAD] The trust will tell NHS England it is not assured that high-risk patients and frequent emergency department attenders have crisis plans in place before winter, and says last winter's plans were below standard.
[Evidence] "B7.2: Crisis and relapse plans for frequent users and high-risk patients. Proposed response: No, not yet fully assured". On 2025-26: "Of 44 identified plans, 20 were reviewed and nine were shared. Mobilisation was late, the quality of plans was assessed as below the required standard, and plans did not consistently identify clear actions through which attendance at an emergency department might safely be avoided." The national Board Assurance Statement must be signed by the chair and accountable officer and submitted by 30 September 2026. – p.306-307 and p.311, [Winter Plan 2026/27 and Board Assurance Statement, Item 3.2.1]

[LEAD] The Information Commissioner's Office has launched an investigation into the LIO patient monitoring system (formerly Oxevision), which is not currently operating at the trust.
[Evidence] "The LIO solution is not currently operating within the Trust. The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has launched an investigation into the LIO solution, relating to compliance with key data protection requirements on the fair and lawful use of personal information, automated decision-making, privacy by design, information security, and data protection." Separately: "the Oxehealth consultation with the ICO remained paused, with no outstanding actions for the Trust while the ICO undertook wider investigation work." – p.63, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 1.6]; p.214, [Finance and Investment Committee Chair's Report, Item 2.3.4]

[LEAD] Bank staff spending is on course to breach the trust's nationally set ceiling by about £3.1m, and the cost improvement programme is forecast to miss its £26.5m target by £2.2m.
[Evidence] "Bank spend at month 4 is £4.0m. This is up on 25/26 spend of £3.2m at month 4. Based against national planning guidance, DPT is required to reduce Bank spend to £8.7m in 26/27. The current forecast is above plan by £0.6m and if current levels remain, the Trust will exceed the plan by approximately £3.1m." Also: "The Trust is forecasting to underachieve it's full-year CIP target of £26.5m by £2.2m"; "The Trust's underlying deficit remains at £11.6m"; "In order to achieve the forecast breakeven position, an underspend of £15.4m against £21.7m budget will be required from reserves". – p.218 and p.221, [Finance and Treasury Report Month 4, Item 2.3.4a]

[LEAD] A whistleblowing complaint to the Care Quality Commission triggered a rapid quality review of the trust's Langdon secure services, which found wider quality and safety issues but did not substantiate the prescribing concerns raised.
[Evidence] July minutes: "a decision has been taken with the Devon and Cornwall Cluster Director of Nursing and Safeguarding colleagues to enact a rapid quality review process in relation to DPT's Langdon Secure Services, following whistleblowing allegations made to the Care Quality Commission (CQC)." September papers: "the Rapid Quality Review did not substantiate concerns about inappropriate antipsychotic prescribing but identified wider known quality and safety issues. The Local Authority did not consider a whole-service safeguarding inquiry necessary". Secure services "remained in enhanced monitoring following the quarterly meeting on 2 September 2026", with a CQC Mental Health Act monitoring visit scheduled for 9 September 2026 and a request for "explicit gateway criteria and timescales" for step-down. – p.15, [Minutes 21 July 2026, Item 1.3]; p.138, [QuOC Chair's Report, Item 2.3.1]; p.114 and p.251, [SWPC Lead Provider Report, Item 2.2.1; EIPOC Chair's Report, Item 3.1]

[LEAD] £6.5m of national capital funding for the trust's enhanced mental health crisis assessment centre has been deferred into future years, and the absence of a crisis assessment centre is listed as a principal winter risk.
[Evidence] "A forecast underspend of £3.220m against plan is being reported at month 4 following the deferral of £6.500m in National Funding for the Enhanced Mental Health Crisis Assessment Centre into future years". Winter plan principal risks include "No crisis assessment centre". A further £909,000 of winter schemes is unfunded, including "Maintain six Elysium Exeter beds (1 Oct 2026–31 Mar 2027) c. £725,000". – p.236, [Finance and Treasury Report Month 4]; supp p.100 and p.121, [Winter Plan 2026/27 board assurance deck, Item 3.2.1]

[LEAD] Children's community waits run into years: 421 children were waiting more than 104 weeks in June, and the trust does not expect to clear 18-week speech and language waits until January 2029.
[Evidence] "CYP 104 weeks is dependant of national DQ issues being resolved with NHS England. To note the local position in June is 421." Speech, language and communication: "There are 2101 CYP waiting to be seen, who have been waiting for over 18 weeks; 1443 waiting over a year... Trajectories for improvement predict elimination of children waiting over 52 weeks by September 2028 and elimination of waits over 18 weeks by January 2029." Neurodiversity under-5s: "There are 489 CYP waiting to be seen, with the longest wait currently over 4 years. There has been a significant loss of Paediatrics clinic time in recent months (provided by the RDUH)." – p.261 and p.267, [Integrated Performance Report (July 2026 data), Item 3.1.1a]

[LEAD] Devon has a 45 per cent shortfall in daytime approved mental health professional cover, and local government reorganisation has stopped the redesign consultation.
[Evidence] "SEC noted a 45% deficit in daytime AMHP provision and better staffed out of hours provision with a 0.6 WTE deficit." Also: "local governance reorganisation has halted consultation on the AMHP service redesign and that the exemption request was not supported. The CEO has written to the Chief Executive of Devon County Council and will raise the issue at a meeting on 4 September." A private board paper "would consolidate AMHP, Section 12 doctor availability and urgent care pathway issues, seeking agreement for a formal request for an ICB commissioning review." – p.287, [Strategic Executive Committee Chair's Report, Item 3.2]

[LEAD] The trust is considering invoking step-in rights against its shared IT provider DELT over poor project delivery.
[Evidence] "FIC noted the planned September workshop with DELT to confirm project requirements and resourcing. If additional resource was not committed, the Trust would consider use of step-in rights and repatriation of specific project services." July minutes record that "the formal mediation process had been completed satisfactorily" and that "eight of the eleven commitments were Red RAG rated for adoption." – p.214, [FIC Chair's Report, Item 2.3.4]; p.24, [Minutes 21 July 2026]

[LEAD] The trust moved to segment 3 and 47th of 61 mental health providers in the first quarterly oversight results to use the expanded mental health metric set.
[Evidence] "The segmentation results for providers in relation to 2026/27 Quarter 1 performance were published last week. For our organisation (and other mental health providers) this represents a significant expansion in the number of metrics being used to make that assessment. The headline message is that the Trust average metric score improved to 2.57 from 2.78 (Q4 25/26). This has resulted in the Trust improving to Segment 3 and to 47th in the sector league table, up 7 places." The CEO also notes two of the four access domain metrics measure community waits over 18 and 52 weeks, which "specifically relate to the children's physical health services associated with the recently transfer Children Family Health Devon (CFHD) portfolio." – p.60, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 1.6]

[WORTH WATCHING] The board declined NHS England's request to formally adopt definitions of antisemitism and anti-Muslim hostility following the Lord Mann review.
[Evidence] "The Chair summarised that the Trust Board remains highly committed to delivering the anti-racism agenda and noted Trust Board fully supports the objective of preventing and responding to antisemitism, anti-Muslim hostility and all forms of discrimination and hatred, however does not agree to formally adopting either definition at this time, as is set out in the paper." The paper was presented with "a request from NHS England for all Trust's to respond to the Lord Mann review and associated anti-racism requirements, confirming the Trust Board's position of the adoption of definitions by the 30 July." – p.24-25, [Minutes 21 July 2026, Item 1.3]

[WORTH WATCHING] All Devon providers have deferred the 1 October go-live of the shared SBS digital finance platform over readiness and data risks.
[Evidence] "Devon providers collectively deferred the planned 1 October 2026 go-live of the SBS digital finance platform due to readiness and data assurance risks; a revised plan will be agreed." Rated Limited Assurance. The separate Devon People Digital Programme "remained under independent review because expected benefits had not been realised and available functionality did not yet exceed existing ESR self-service arrangements." – p.205, [Audit Committee Chair's Report, Item 2.3.3]; p.212, [FIC Chair's Report, Item 2.3.4]

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff fear of reprisal during the redundancy programme is a live board concern, with internationally educated staff worried about visa implications.
[Evidence] "QuOC noted continuing concern about fear of reprisal, particularly during organisational change and among internationally educated colleagues concerned about job security or visa implications." Context from July: consultations for "phase two of the organisational change programme, affecting around 80 roles"; the remuneration committee handled "the Voluntary Redundancy Scheme, a small number of Compulsory Redundancy approvals"; and the workforce forecast notes "further workforce reductions, for example those related to the compulsory redundancy programme will not impact until 2027/28." – p.138-139, [QuOC Chair's Report, Item 2.3.1]; p.13 and p.19, [Minutes 21 July 2026]; p.270, [Integrated Performance Report]

[FOI] Request the rapid quality review report and action plan for Langdon secure services, plus the whistleblowing correspondence with the CQC.
[Evidence] "The service has presented an action plan proposal from the service following the recent Rapid Quality Review process and this will be progressed over the next few months." Neither the review report, the allegations, nor the enhanced monitoring step-down criteria are set out in the pack. Request from Devon Partnership Trust and from the South West Provider Collaborative: the rapid commissioning quality report, the rapid quality review meeting minutes, the resulting action plan, and the written request for "explicit gateway criteria and timescales" for de-escalation. – p.114, [SWPC Lead Provider Report, Item 2.2.1]; p.251, [EIPOC Chair's Report, Item 3.1]

[FOI] Request the Health and Safety Executive notice of contravention served on the trust and the associated internal audit action.
[Evidence] "One 'high-risk' action remains open, relating to the Health and Safety Executive notice of contravention audit, however it was presented that the original action is complete but the progress of embedding remains under way." Also: "Health and safety actions were reported as progressing, although the final external response from HSE remains outstanding." QuOC approved "closure of the relevant HSE notice-of-contravention action". Request from Devon Partnership Trust and HSE: the notice itself, the trust's response, and the internal audit report. – p.206, [Audit Committee Chair's Report, Item 2.3.3]; p.251, [EIPOC Chair's Report]; p.141, [QuOC Chair's Report]

[FOI] Request the private board papers on patient safety incident investigation outcomes, and the list of open investigations.
[Evidence] "detailed outcomes of the PSIIs are provided to Trust Board in private due to the sensitivity of the content." The pack records 140 incidents in scope since 2023-24, 20 completed investigations, one investigation on "Extension Agreed (Subject to criminal process)" relating to a "Sexual Assault - Pt On Pt" at Coombehaven reported in December 2024, and "two complex homicide investigations" supported by the family engagement team. Nine after action reviews were awaiting allocation with "the oldest dating from December 2024". Request the private board PSII reports and the full open-investigation schedule. – p.369, p.376 and p.377, [PSIRF Report, Item 3.5]
