## Top lines

[LEAD] UHN is cutting executives' spending sign-off limits "in light of the severity of the Trust's current financial position" — with NHSE enforcement undertakings confirmed in place at both trusts, including potential licence revocation at KGH for non-compliance.
[Evidence] "In light of the severity of the Trust's current financial position, the Executive Tean [sic] considers that the existing delegated authority limits for committing expenditure are too high and should be reduced." Executive directors' approval limit drops from £100,000–£249,999 to £50,000; some senior officers drop to £1,000; ex gratia payments to patients capped at £10,000 from unlimited. "NHSE Enforcement undertakings are in place. KGH – Any failure to comply with the above undertakings will render the Licensee liable to further formal action by NHS England. This could include... revocation of the licence pursuant to section 89 of the Act." — p.126–128/133, [Temporary Revisions to the Scheme of Delegation, Item 10]

[LEAD] NGH maternity received a Level 2 Maternity Outcomes Signal System (MOSS) alert in October 2026 — escalating from an August Level 1 alert triggered by four stillbirths and one neonatal death — and is now under external NHS England peer review.
[Evidence] "Following the reporting period, NGH received a Level 2 alert in October 2026. A review is currently underway in accordance with national requirements, including external peer review by NHS England Maternity Improvement Advisors. No conclusions will be drawn until this review has concluded." — p.73/133, [UHN Perinatal Report, Item 7]. August Level 1: "triggered by four stillbirths and one neonatal death" — p.78/133, [Perinatal Scorecard]. One further case was added to the NGH Level 1 review in September — p.84/133.

[LEAD] New chief executive Adam Carson confirms UHN has "dissolved our Group structure with UHL" and asks the boards to approve a priorities reset, including "establishing our organisational form for the future" and "a credible path to breakeven".
[Evidence] "The last few months have been a period of significant change for UHN as we have dissolved our Group structure with UHL and established a new Executive team for the organisation." — p.102/133, [Resetting our Priorities, Item 8]. Priority: "retaining each hospital's identity and establishing our organisational form for the future"; "We will develop and deliver our financial plan and have a credible path to breakeven" — p.108/133.

[LEAD] A quarter of staff who contacted UHN's new outsourced Freedom to Speak Up service reported fear of reprisal, and 41% believed they had not been or would not be listened to; the external guardians say some middle managers "appeared defensive" when concerns were escalated.
[Evidence] "25% of staff who contacted GSL reported a fear of reprisal for speaking up... 41% of staff who contacted GSL felt that they had not been / would not be listened to when raising concerns with managers." "Some middle managers appeared defensive when FTSU concerns were escalated to them." 78 concerns 1 June–30 Sept; 65% from NGH. FTSU "is subject to a CQC action plan following the NGH Well Led assessment in October 2025". — p.110–111 and p.124/133, [FTSU Quarterly Report Q2 2026-27, Item 9]

[LEAD] KGH's RAAC-riddled Rockingham Wing replacement was paused over the summer after costs and timelines "exceeded previous assumptions"; a single-stage new build option has now been identified, and the trust will seek NHSE approval and funding for mitigation work on the existing building in the meantime.
[Evidence] "it was clear that original cost and delivery timelines exceeded previous assumptions, presenting substantial affordability and programme challenges. Consequently, development work was paused while alternative approaches to replacing Rockingham Wing were reviewed." — p.19/133, [CEO Report, Item 5]. Note internal inconsistency: the perinatal report says "No preferred option has yet been identified" and records estates incidents "including flooding, heating failures, steam leaks and lift outages" — p.73/133, [UHN Perinatal Report, Item 7]

[LEAD] Executive churn continues: a substantive chief nurse has been recruited (announcement "imminent", January start), Chris Dann joins as COO from Sherwood Forest on 9 November, and the pack still styles the CEO, CFO and chief nursing officer as interim.
[Evidence] "Over the last month we have recruited a substantive Chief Nurse for the Trust... We expect our new Chief Nurse to start in January." "Chris Dann as Chief Operating Officer (COO) from his current role as Deputy COO at Sherwood Forest Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust. Chris starts with us on 9th November 2026." — p.20/133, [CEO Report, Item 5].
