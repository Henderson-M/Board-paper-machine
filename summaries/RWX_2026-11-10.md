## Top lines

[LEAD] Berkshire Healthcare has a new substantive chair, Frances West — this is her second public board in the chair and neither of the two previous pack summaries (July, September) flagged her arrival.
[Evidence] Agenda item 1 "Chair's Welcome and Public Questions — Frances West, Chair" — p.1, [Agenda]; "The Chair commented that since joining the Trust she had been impressed by the breadth and quality of information generated through the patient experience programme" — p.8, [Minutes of Board meeting held in public, 8 September 2026]. The pack does not give a start date or who she replaced; the July 2026 minutes (per the September summary) record Mark Day stepping down as Vice-Chair "including in the role of Interim Chair" [INFERENCE: West succeeded an interim chair arrangement — confirm with trust]. Other board-level arrivals in the same pack: Manjeet Aujla "attending his first meeting as a Non-Executive Director" on 8 September and since appointed "Wellbeing Non-Executive Director Lead" (p.5, p.12); a further NED "with a partnership background" was being recruited with interviews "scheduled for 1 October 2026" (p.20, same minutes); a "new Director of Mental Health Services" is referred to, unnamed, in the Audit Committee minutes (p.264).

[LEAD] The trust is asking the board to approve public engagement on closing Oakwood Ward and consolidating Berkshire West community rehabilitation beds from three sites to two, cutting the routinely open bed base from 108 to 99 and saving £700k–£800k a year — with the paper insisting the "actual" reduction is three beds because six escalation beds would be kept.
[Evidence] "Oakwood Ward would close as a standalone rehabilitation site and capacity at Highclere Ward would increase"; "The routine operating bed base would reduce from 108 to 99 beds. Six escalation beds would be available at Wokingham Hospital, providing maximum capacity of 105 beds. The actual reduction in beds available to the system at times of significant pressure is therefore 3 beds"; "expected to deliver a recurrent net financial benefit of between £700k and £800K" — p.252–254, [Proposal to consolidate community hospital beds in Berkshire West, Item 9.1]. Clinical rationale: "Oakwood's single-bedded environment does not support easy or continuous observation of patients. This creates an inherent patient safety risk" — p.255. Timetable: "engagement would commence through Q3 2026/27, with formal staff consultation planned for Q4. Implementation would commence from April 2027" — p.257. Reading residents are the affected population: "During 2025, 60.2% of the 392 admissions for Reading residents were to sites other than Oakwood" — p.256. Note an internal inconsistency in the paper: p.255 says "Routine capacity would reduce from 109 to 100 beds... resulting in maximum system capacity of 106 beds", against 108/99/105 everywhere else. The same pack's safe staffing report says Oakwood "appears to have slightly less than optimal staffing to meet patient acuity" — p.104, [Six Monthly Safe Staffing Report].

[LEAD] The CQC's written feedback from the trust's September 2026 well-led inspection is reproduced in full and is almost entirely positive — praising culture, leadership visibility and data — with just two "queries", one on how informal concerns are recorded and how the quality concerns log interacts with risk processes.
[Evidence] Letter dated 29 September 2026 from Serena Coleman, Deputy Director of Operations, Mental Health, CQC, ref AP27862: "A consistent theme throughout the inspection was the strength of the trust's culture. Staff described a positive and psychologically safe environment to work in"; "The trust's use of data was impressive. In particular, Connected Care was highlighted throughout our interviews as a major asset"; "We had two queries, one around the governance process of recording informal concerns and how the quality concerns log interacted with wider risk processes" — p.133–134, [CQC feedback letter, appended to Executive Report]. Executive Report: on-site well-led element "took place 22–24 September 2026"; "The two queries within the letter have both been satisfactorily resolved"; draft reports take "around 85 working days from inspection completion" — p.124, [Executive Report, Item 7.0]. Six core-service inspections since March 2026: Campion (LD) and older people's MH wards both "Good across all key questions and overall", four others awaiting draft/final reports — p.123–124. Letter was copied to Sarah Bellars (Thames Valley ICB) and Andrea Lewis (NHSE regional chief nurse) — p.134.

[LEAD] An internal bullying survey reported in the same pack found 63% of ~200 respondents had witnessed or experienced bullying in the past year and only 4% reported it via Datix; the paper itself sets out a gap between 707 staff-survey reports of staff-on-staff bullying and 11 casework cases (2 upheld).
[Evidence] "A recent survey we created on bullying had some 200 staff provide the following data: 63% had witnessed or experienced bullying in the last year. 33% informed their manager, 4% reported the incident via Datix. Trust support was mixed, 42% didn't feel supported by their manager and 38% didn't feel supported by Trust Support Services"; "Part of the review will focus on numbers of staff reporting bullying via NSS (707 staff on staff incidents) vs bullying related casework (11 cases) and outcomes (2 upheld/partially upheld). There will also be a review of reporting (930 via NSS vs 19 on Datix and 30 FTSU cases)" — p.142, [Violence Prevention and Reduction Assurance Report, Item 7.1]. Caveat: self-selected survey of ~200 staff out of c.4,800 WTE. [INFERENCE: sits awkwardly alongside the CQC letter's "strength of the trust's culture" line on p.133 — the juxtaposition is the reporter's, not the pack's.]

[Evidence] "The bank target is not being achieved and is unlikely to recover in year. It was set by applying national rules to our 2025/26 expenditure and we over achieved against the NHSE target set for that year" — p.178; dashboard line "6b Bank Expenditure (under)/over YTD Cap 1.9 ... Forecast 2.3 ... No"; "6a Agency Expenditure (under)/over YTD Cap 0.2 ... No" — p.178; "there has also been an increase in the cost of bank usage compared with the first 6 months of last year this started in Q3 of 2025/26. Both measures are included in the National Oversight Framework" — p.180, [Finance Report September 2026, Item 8.0a]. Bank spend YTD £9.5m vs £7.8m plan — p.179. The September board minutes record the chair's view that "the narrative underpinning bank staffing performance was frequently more positive than suggested by headline national metrics" — p.14. The planned outturn is breakeven and on track; YTD reported deficit £0.5m vs £0.1m plan before PFI/IFRS16 adjustment — p.179.

[Evidence] "3rd out of 8 in Segment 1"; "Relative difference in costs (Finance, Productivity and innovation) – Ranked 58th out of 61 trusts"; "Percentage of patients in mental health crisis to receive face-to-face contact within 24-hours (Patient Safety) – ranked 40th"; "Percentage of children and young person's mental health related waits that are over 104 weeks (Access to Services), slight worsening, ranked 42nd" — p.196, [True North Performance Scorecard Month 6, Item 8.1]. [WORTH WATCHING] Out-of-area and PICU placements are £1.7m overspent at half year, with individual patients costing "in the region of £80K per patient per month" in extra observations and male PICU patients moved out "following safeguarding incidents between patients or assaults on staff"; the trust blames discharge delays partly on ICB and council funding approvals, naming Reading as "the top contributor".
[Evidence] "There remains a long-standing issue of a lack of female PICU placement beds locally alongside some very complex patients who have required significant additional levels of observations in the region of £80K per patient per month.
