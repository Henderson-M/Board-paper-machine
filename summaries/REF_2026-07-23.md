Extraordinary board convened for 45 minutes (12:30–13:15, MS Teams) with two substantive items only: the trust's response to the Lord Mann Review, and the Post Death Care / Mortuary Care Board Assurance Statement required by NHS England by 31 July 2026. Chair: David Allen OBE. **Acting** Chief Executive: Kim O'Keeffe BEM.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Royal Cornwall's board is being told that if its mortuary runs out of storage the trust could lose its Human Tissue Authority licence and all mortuary activity across the trust would have to stop — a risk scored 12, the highest in the pack.
[Evidence] "If the mortuary is unable to provide adequate storage this could lead to the loss of our HTA license and would mean all mortuary related activity (including storage) would have to cease across RCHT. This would lead to significant publicity and reputational damage to the Trust." — Risk ID 9150, risk score 12, p.17, [Post Death Care Board Assurance Statement: Nottingham Maternity Review Report findings and key actions from Fuller Inquiry, Item 04]

[LEAD] The trust has activated mortuary contingency plans over the past 12 months because its 79-space mortuary could not cope, and is using a private funeral firm and temporary storage units to hold the deceased.
[Evidence] "The mortuary facility at the RCH site has the capacity to store 79 deceased patients and over the past 12 months has had activate contingency plans to accommodate both an increase in the number deaths and an increase in the length of stay." Mitigations listed include "Cornwall Funeral Services (CFS) to retain / store deceased from the community when of the body store at RCH is nearing capacity", "Transfer of decease from the RCH mortuary to the WCH mortuary" and "Deployment of the 4 Nutwell temporary storage units." — Risk ID 9150, p.17, [Item 04]

[LEAD] The trust states in writing that it is **not compliant** with the Fuller Inquiry's mortuary security recommendations, and that the gap is a security risk.
[Evidence] "The Trust is not compliant with all the recommendations set out in the Fuller Inquiry report regarding security arrangements in the RCHT mortuaries, particularly the mortuary at the WCH site. This poses a security risk. We also risk reputational damage should an incident occur as a direct result of not implementing the recommendations." — Risk ID 10066, p.16, [Item 04]

[LEAD] That non-compliance risk was **downgraded from 12 to 8 on 9 July 2026** — two weeks before the board was asked to sign the assurance statement declaring itself assured.
[Evidence] "The risk score was amended from 12 to 8 following a review by Trust's HTA DI on 9 July 2026, noting the work that has already been completed." — Risk ID 10066, p.17, [Item 04]. The outstanding item is the Fuller 'swipe to exit' recommendation: "most points have been addressed, apart from the exit card readers at the RCH site. This system requires an upgrade to incorporate this recommendation." — p.16 and p.31, [Item 04 / Appendix 2 Board Assurance Statement of Mortuary Care – July 2026]

[LEAD] The board is being asked to return a "Yes" to NHS England on a mortuary assurance statement whose supporting work will not be finished until four days after the meeting.
[Evidence] Assurance statement 1 is answered "Yes – noting that the action being undertaken will ensure we are compliant by the 31 July 2026", with the qualification: "the overarching Trust wide policy requires updating with an organogram that reflects all the roles and responsibilities between the relevant teams for mortuary care… This will be completed by the 27 July 2026, which will then ensure we are compliant with this assurance statement by the deadline of the 31 July 2026." — p.15 and p.29, [Item 04 / Appendix 2]

[LEAD] The trust's assurance that it has met the Fuller Inquiry recommendations rests on its own self-assessment, and it has excluded eight of the 29 NHS-relevant recommendations as not applicable.
[Evidence] "Only 21 of the 29 recommendations are relevant to NHS hospitals that do not provide anatomical education using donors. All actions are complete." and "The Trust has assured itself against most recommendations via the Fuller Public Inquiry Phase 2 self-assessed questionnaire and created an action plan to address identified gaps." — p.15 and p.30, [Item 04 / Appendix 2]

[LEAD] Royal Cornwall's own race-equality data, published in its Lord Mann Review response, shows white candidates are twice as likely to be appointed and ethnic minority staff are increasingly over-represented in disciplinary processes.
[Evidence] "White candidates remain twice as likely to be appointed from shortlisting." / "Ethnic minority staff increasingly overrepresented in disciplinary processes." / "Discrimination at lowest level in 5 years, but still above national average." / "Confidence in career progression is below national benchmarks." — p.5, [Lord Mann Report: Trust Response and Actions, Item 03]

[LEAD] In a board paper responding to a national review into racism, the trust attributes ethnic minority staff integration difficulties to the pace of demographic change caused by its own international recruitment.
[Evidence] "Ethnic minority staff integration into living and working in Cornwall has been challenging due to the pace at which ethnicity has increased in the community following anchor institutions targeted global educated workforce recruitment over the past 5 years." — p.5, [Item 03]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has not completed its NHS Premises Assurance Model self-assessment — the mechanism NHSE has designated for Fuller security compliance — and will not put it to the board until 3 September, five days before the national deadline.
[Evidence] "The Premises Assurance Model is under development for this financial year following the release of the new version in May 2026… The timeline has been established to ensure this is with the Trust Board on the 3 September 2026 leaving sufficient time for submission on the 8 September 2026." — p.16 and p.31, [Item 04 / Appendix 2]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Lord Mann action plan's lead action has no deadline, and its measurable targets are modest.
[Evidence] The first action — rollout of "the new bitesize module co-created with faith leaders in the NHS" — carries Deadline "Not known at this time" and Dependencies "Launch date not known at this stage". Elsewhere the target KPI is "Staff survey results in key WRES/DES data have a sustained improvement of at least 1%" and "70% of people Managers trained in safe and inclusive leadership". — pp.7–9, [Item 03]

[WORTH WATCHING] No equality impact assessment was completed on the trust's response to a national review into racism.
[Evidence] "Equality Impact Assessment completed?" is marked **No**, with the comment "No adverse impacts identified; however, the review highlights systemic inequalities requiring improvement." — p.2, [Item 03]. On the mortuary paper, the equality, quality and data protection impact assessments are all marked N/A and all three 'triple aim' considerations answered "Not applicable." — p.13, [Item 04]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's Lord Mann statement is being aligned with Cornwall Partnership FT and is signed off by a shared "Dual Chief People Officer" — a joint-executive arrangement across the two Cornwall trusts.
[Evidence] Executive Director Lead: "Alison Thorne-Henderson, Dual Chief People Officer"; board recommended to "Approve the RCHT statement noting this aligns with Cornwall Partnership NHS Foundation Trust statement" — p.1 and p.3, [Item 03]. [INFERENCE] The "Dual" title and aligned statement indicate a shared people function across RCHT and CFT; the pack does not describe the arrangement.

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the HTA Designated Individual's biannual mortuary reports to the Quality Assurance Committee — the trust says the last was February 2026, and it is the document that identified security as the area of concern.
[Evidence] "Assurance is provided to the Board through the Designated Individual (DI) for HTA biannual Mortuary report to the Quality Assurance Committee. The last report was submitted in February 2026. The HTA DI only highlighted the outstanding work to upgrade the mortuary security arrangements as an area for concern." — p.16, [Item 04]. **Request from:** Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust — all HTA Designated Individual biannual mortuary reports and QAC minutes on them, 2024–2026.

[FOI] Request all correspondence between the trust and the Human Tissue Authority, including any inspection reports, conditions, or HTA reportable incident (HTARI) records.
[Evidence] NHSE's letter tells trusts the HTA has "a new requirement for mortuaries to review HTA reportable incident (HTARI) internal records from 2015-2026… to ensure that all reportable incidents during this period have been logged with the HTA, and that any missing incidents are reported retrospectively. A Regulatory update will be issued by the HTA in July setting out the parameters, timeframe, process and deadlines for the evidential compliance audit. The HTA will report findings to the Secretary of State in October." — p.20, [Appendix 1, PRN02500 System letter on mortuary oversight 26 June 2026]. **Why it matters:** the RCHT paper nowhere addresses this retrospective 2015–2026 HTARI audit, despite it being the operative new requirement in the letter the paper is responding to. [INFERENCE] Either the work is not yet scoped or it is being handled outside the board. **Request from:** RCHT and separately the HTA — RCHT's HTARI audit return, and any HTA correspondence, licence conditions or inspection findings for RCHT mortuaries 2020–2026.

[FOI] Request the full risk register entries and score history for Risk 10066 and Risk 9150, including the rationale for the 12→8 downgrade on 9 July 2026.
[Evidence] "The risk score was amended from 12 to 8 following a review by Trust's HTA DI on 9 July 2026" — p.17, [Item 04]. **Request from:** RCHT — full risk entries, all score changes with dates and approving officer, and the 9 July 2026 review record.

### Analyst notes

- This is a single-purpose extraordinary board driven entirely by two external national deadlines of 31 July 2026 (the NHSE mortuary Board Assurance Statement return, and the deadline to adopt the IHRA definition and NHS Race and Health Observatory Seven Anti-Racism Principles). [INFERENCE] The meeting appears to have been convened to obtain formal board sign-off before those deadlines rather than in response to a local incident — nothing in the pack refers to any incident at RCHT.
- **No admission of a mortuary care failing at Royal Cornwall is made anywhere in the pack, and no apology is offered.** The trust's stated failings are confined to non-compliance with Fuller *security* recommendations (exit card readers / 'swipe to exit') and a capacity risk. Both national triggers — the Ockenden NUH maternity review findings on post-death care, and the HTA report on NUH — relate to other organisations.
- The Lord Mann Review here is a national item: the pack contains RCHT's response to it, not any finding about RCHT. The trust's own exposure is the WRES/staff-experience data it volunteers at p.5.
- Accountability is precisely stated and is worth using: "Accountability for licensed mortuary activities, including the primary (legal) obligation responsibility under Regulation 12 of the Q&S Regulations sits with the Trusts Designated Individual." and "The Deputy Chief Medical Officer is the Licence Holder for HTA for the Trust." — p.17, [Item 04]. The Designated Individual is named in the pack as Stephen Davison, Head Biomedical Scientist (p.12); the Deputy CMO is not named.
- Promised but not yet delivered, with dates: updated trust-wide mortuary policy plus organogram (27 July 2026); RCH exit card reader upgrade (no date given); PAM self-assessment to board (3 September 2026) and submission (8 September 2026); Lord Mann bitesize faith module (date "Not known at this time"); local assurance framework for the national staff standard on tackling racism (Q4 2026/27).
- Cross-cutting: the Lord Mann recommendations also appear in Ashford and St Peter's July 2026 pack this cycle, consistent with the NHSE letter of 4 June 2026 to all NHS leaders (p.4, [Item 03]) and the 31 July 2026 national deadline — i.e. a national compliance thread, not a local one.
