## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO Ged Murphy is retiring; trust has interviewed for an interim replacement and CMO Dr John Hunter will act up as CEO in August and September 2026.
[Evidence] "Much of my time has been dedicated to recruiting a new Interim Trust CEO following Ged Murphy's retirement announcement. We received 14 applications and interviewed five candidates, resulting in an excellent appointment, subject to final checks. Dr John Hunter will cover this role on an interim basis as Acting CEO during August and September 2026" — p.24, [Chair's Commentary, item 26/080]

[LEAD] NHS England has confirmed East Cheshire will remain in tiering for both urgent/emergency care and elective performance for Q1 2026/27, reflecting continued challenges against agreed trajectories.
[Evidence] "On 28th May 2026, NHS England confirmed that the Trust will remain in tiering for UEC and Elective performances for Quarter 1 2026/27. This reflects the continued challenges in delivery against agreed trajectories... It was also confirmed that the Trust will move out of tiering for Diagnostics performance" — p.30, [CEO Commentary, item 26/081]

[LEAD] The trust's Summary Hospital-level Mortality Index has been "higher than expected" continuously for over three years and has risen sharply since June 2025, reaching 1.33 for the year to December 2025 (its highest in the series).
[Evidence] "The Trust's SHMI has been 'higher than expected' since November 2022"; table shows 1.33 (Jan 2025–Dec 2025), up from 1.28 (Dec 2024–Nov 2025) — p.113, [Learning from Deaths Report, item 26/084]. "Some of the most recent rise in SHMI may be related to data quality issues as there has been a sharp rise in SHMI since DCS went live in June 2025 which has not been accompanied by a concomitant rise in deaths." — p.114, same paper

[LEAD] The trust was ranked 126 out of 134 in NHS England's provider segmentation/league table for Q4 2025/26.
[Evidence] "The Trust is ranked as 126/134 in the league table and the NHS Oversight Framework was published on 11th June 2026" — p.37, [CEO Commentary, item 26/081]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's SHMI mortality-reduction plan shows key deteriorating-patient and seven-day-service actions rated RED, including NEWS2 compliance stuck at 55% against an 85% target and seven-day pharmacy cover paused as "cost prohibitive."
[Evidence] SHMI value KPI-01 marked RED, "Significant deterioration since DCS implementation in June 2025; known DQ"; NEWS2 compliance KPI-06 "55%" vs "≥85% all wards", RED; WS7-05 "Confirm seven-day pharmacy service for MAU and ED" status "On Hold", RAG RED, "Cost prohibitive in current financial climate" — pp.120–121, [Learning from Deaths Report, SHMI Mortality Reduction Action Plan & KPI Monitor]

[WORTH WATCHING] Two mortality reviews from Q2 met the threshold for further patient-safety investigation under PSIRF and have been commissioned.
[Evidence] "Two mortality reviews completed in Quarter 2 (WEB-114738 and WEB-114816) have met the threshold for further investigation under PSIRF. Patient safety reviews have been commissioned for both cases and are currently in progress." — p.107, [Learning from Deaths Report, item 26/084]

[WORTH WATCHING] A £750,000 investment in a new medical rota, adding two Tier 2 doctors on site 24/7, is being introduced in August to address delays in responding to deteriorating patients.
[Evidence] "£750,000 investment in new medical rota being introduced in August which will deliver 2 Tier 2 medical doctors on site 24/7. This will coincide with the opening of the Acute Medical Receiving Unit (AMRU)" (action WS7-03, RAG RED) — p.120, [Learning from Deaths Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff survey response rate fell 12 points to 48% overall, with a collapse among medical staff — Medical & Dental down 28 points (60% to 32%) and Healthcare Scientists down 29 points (84% to 55%).
[Evidence] "The Trust achieved a 48% response rate, a 12% decrease compared to the previous year"; Figure six shows Medical and Dental 60%→32% (-28%), Healthcare Scientists 84%→55% (-29%) — pp.168, 172, [Staff Survey Update, item 26/088]

[WORTH WATCHING] An average of 55 patients a day (about 20% of beds) no longer meet the criteria to reside, staying on average nine days beyond clinical need.
[Evidence] "an average of 55 patients per day (approx. 20% of the bed base) remaining in a hospital bed waiting for the next stage of the care outside of the hospital. The average length of stay after those patients no longer meet the criteria to reside is 9 days" — p.30, [CEO Commentary, item 26/081]
