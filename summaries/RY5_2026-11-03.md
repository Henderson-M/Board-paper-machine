# Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals Group board — 3 November 2026

- **Source:** https://lincolnshirecommunityhealthservices.nhs.uk/about-us/our-trust-board/trust-board-papers
- **Pack:** 1 file (combined bundle, "LCHG Board Meeting in Public Session 3 November 2026.pdf")
- **Analysed:** 2026-11-02T12:36:28Z

**NOTE — group pack.** This is a single joint/group board pack for Lincolnshire Community and Hospitals Group (LCHG): one board covering both Lincolnshire Community Health Services Trust (RY5) and United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals Trust (ULTH, RWD). The same pack is mirrored to RWD:2026-11-03. Content below covers both organisations; most of the pressure (deficit, CQC action, IG cases) sits on the ULTH side.

## Top lines

[LEAD] CQC served a Section 29a warning notice on ULTH in September over unsafe medicines management in Pilgrim Hospital's emergency department.
[Evidence] "on 16 September 2026, the CQC served a Section 29a Warning Notice on United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust (ULTH) for failing to meet the regulations related to the safe management of medicines within the Emergency Department at Pilgrim Hospital" — p.5, [Group CEO Report, Item 6]. The October Quality Committee adds that "despite significant work, previous improvement actions had not yet delivered sufficiently consistent and sustained improvement" with "increased reliance on escalation areas and corridor care, affecting privacy, dignity, infection prevention"; Lincoln ED remains under escalated oversight with "a team of experienced senior nurses" deployed — p.9–10 and p.6, [Quality Committee Upward Report October, Item 8.1].

[LEAD] The group is £7.7m off plan at half-year (£15.2m deficit) and has been formally required by NHSE to submit a financial recovery plan, which went in on 4 October.
[Evidence] "The Group reported a year-to-date deficit of £15.2m at Month 6, which is £7.7m adverse to plan... the Group were formally requested to submit a recovery plan to the regional regulator team. This recovery plan was approved by Group Leadership Team and Trust Board and submitted on 4 October." Drivers: activity £5.3m below plan, CIP £5.8m adverse, temporary staffing up; "The adverse variance is within ULTH position" — p.4–5, [Finance Briefing M6, Item 8.3].

[LEAD] Twenty-seven staff are under investigation and 29 are suspended over "suspected inappropriate access to patient records linked to two significant information governance incidents", with agency staff backfilling the gaps.
[Evidence] "Given the scale of the exclusions, the potential impact on operational delivery and the level of public and regulatory interest... The paper focuses on 27 cases arising from suspected inappropriate access to patient records linked to two significant information governance incidents"; "Current suspensions recorded as IG-related 29" — p.3–4, [Board Paper: IG cases, Item 9.2]. Total exclusions rose from 37 in September to 47 in October, "29 of the 47 exclusions were related to alleged Information Governance (IG) breaches... this increase in the number of exclusions had resulted in an increased use of agency staff to backfill" — p.9, [People Committee Upward Report October, Item 9.1]. Seven disciplinary panels have been cancelled; target to conclude all hearings by 30 November 2026.

[LEAD] Consultancy Newton's flow programme at ULTH must deliver a £3.5m saving and is expected to remove 56 beds recurrently — but the board deferred bed closures past winter.
[Evidence] "the programme had commenced on the 1 September with early results showing a 20% increase in flow and an 18% increase in discharge. The programme of work was noted to require a £3.5m saving... The Committee supported the decision not to remove beds during the peak-winter period so as not to compromise safety or performance. The expectation remained for the removal of 56 beds recurrently" — p.5, [Finance and Performance Committee Upward Report October, Item 8.4].

[LEAD] The group's orthodontics service has collapsed after its last two consultants resigned; new referrals are paused and decommissioning is being explored.
[Evidence] "the developments around the Group's fragile Orthodontics Service, with the resignation of its two remaining consultants. The Group was in discussions with the ICB about next steps" — p.7, [Finance and Performance Committee Upward Report September, Item 8.4]. Risk register (score 20, above appetite): "ICB service change process completed to pause new referrals"; actions include "Exploration of long-term service reconfiguration / decommissioning options" — [Appendix B, ULTH Board Risks rated High or Very High, Item 12.1, risk 5795996].

[LEAD] Merger full business case completed for end-October submission to NHSE; the two trusts plan to merge on 1 April 2027, with TUPE consultation from November and Secretary of State approval pencilled for March 2027.
[Evidence] "At the time of writing, the Full Business Case has been completed ready for submission to NHS England at the end of October" — p.5, [Group CEO Report, Item 6]. Timeline: "1 April 2027: Trusts merge — Subject to approvals and readiness assessment"; Nov 2026–Jan 2027 "Commencement of TUPE consultation"; March 2027 "Approval of transaction by Secretary of State" — slides 3–4, [Single Organisation Programme Plan, Item 6.1 Appendix 1]. The engagement exercise runs to 30 November; see colour below on the no-consultation-required line.

[LEAD] Ciro Rinaldi appointed substantive group chief medical officer after serving as interim since April 2026.
[Evidence] "following a robust external recruitment process, Professor Ciro Rinaldi has been appointed as substantive Group Chief Medical Officer. Having served as Interim Group Chief Medical Officer since April 2026" — p.7, [Group CEO Report, Item 6].

[WORTH WATCHING] ULTH ranked 124th of 134 peer trusts in NHSE's Q1 oversight ratings and stays in segment 4; LCHS improved to segment 2.
[Evidence] "United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust (ULTH) remained in Segment 4, ranked 124th out of 134 peer trusts... Lincolnshire Community Health Services NHS Trust (LCHS) has improved to Segment 2, ranked 26th out of 58 peer trusts" — p.4–5, [Group CEO Report, Item 6].

[WORTH WATCHING] ULTH maternity flags a "significantly increased" risk of submitting a non-compliant CNST maternity incentive scheme return because anaesthetists cannot be released for training, and can give only limited assurance on the Maternal Care Bundle deadline.
[Evidence] "delivery is now heavily reliant on attendance at the remaining sessions, particularly within anaesthetics, where workforce pressures and limited ability to release staff for training have significantly increased the risk of non-compliance"; the SAB appendix warns the trust would be "required to submit a non-compliant CNST MIS Year 8 return, irrespective of its performance against all other safety actions"; "Maternity Services are only able to provide limited assurance against the recommendation to achieve full implementation of the Maternal Care Bundle by March 2027" — p.5–6, [Quality Committee Upward Report October, Item 8.1] and [Appendix A, CNST MIS Year 8 SAB training risk].

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff survey speak-up confidence fell at both trusts — at ULTH only 37.6% believe concerns they raise would be addressed; at LCHS the same score dropped 7.4 points in a year.
[Evidence] ULTH q25f ("Feel organisation would address any concerns I raised") 42.0% (2024) to 37.6% (2025); LCHS q25f 59.1% to 51.7%; "There has been a continued decline in the results for the speak up questions at LCHS" — p.4–5, [FTSU Annual Report 2025-26, Item 9.1 Appendix 1].

[WORTH WATCHING] Thirlwall response: CCTV must be installed in neonatal drug rooms by 30 November, and the paper admits neonatal pharmacy provision is insufficient against national standards.
[Evidence] "Trust's should urgently install CCTV cameras that have capacity to store recordings for at least 28 days in neonatal units... installed by 30th November 2026 at the latest"; "currently, pharmacy provision within Neonatal services is recognised as being insufficient as per Neonatal GIRFT and BAPM... a Business Case has been drafted to increase pharmacy support" — p.5, [Board Report — Thirlwell, Item 8.2].

[WORTH WATCHING] Ten-year estates plan puts backlog maintenance at £137.4m; NHSE has approved £48m for the Lincoln maternity rebuild but the design is being reviewed to fit the envelope.
[Evidence] "approximately £137.4m of backlog maintenance" across a 187,160m² estate valued at £286.8m — p.4–5, [Covering Paper, Estates Operational Delivery Plan, Item 8.5]. "the NHSE had approved funding to the value of £48m for the Lincoln Maternity project. Steps were being taken to review the proposed building's design so that it is aligned with the approved financial envelope" — p.5, [Integration Committee Upward Report September, Item 10.1].

[FOI] Request the merger due diligence reports (finance, legal, IG, workforce) and the identity, contract value and deliverables of the unnamed "external partner" supporting the transaction — from both trusts.
[Evidence] Final due diligence reports went to the group board in private on 6 October and are not in the public pack; "SROs agreed that whilst there were 'no surprises' arising from the due diligence work"; "A programme of due diligence activities has been undertaken with support from the external partner secured to support development of the Strategic Case, Full Business Case and Post Transaction Integration Plan" — p.6, [Single Organisation Oversight Group Upward Report, Item 6.1] and slide 2, [Appendix 1]. The firm is never named.

[FOI] Request the financial recovery plan submitted to NHSE on 4 October and the NHSE letter/request that triggered it — from ULTH/LCHG and NHSE Midlands.
[Evidence] "the Group were formally requested to submit a recovery plan to the regional regulator team... submitted on 4 October" — p.5, [Finance Briefing M6, Item 8.3]. The plan itself is not in the pack.

[FOI] Request the CQC Section 29a warning notice and related inspection correspondence, plus the "High Profile Cases Report" summarising live cases with media/regulatory risk — from ULTH.
[Evidence] The warning notice is referenced but not reproduced — p.5, [Group CEO Report, Item 6]. The Quality Committee received "a summary of on-going high profile, significant patient safety-related cases that may also generate adverse media, social media or regulatory attention" with no detail published — p.7, [Quality Committee Upward Report October, Item 8.1].
