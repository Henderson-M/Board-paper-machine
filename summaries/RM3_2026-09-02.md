## Top lines

[LEAD] Northern Care Alliance is now under a stacked set of NHS England and CQC enforcement measures — a section 29A warning notice, enforcement undertakings and a System Improvement Board — following a CQC well-led inspection in July.
[Evidence] The trust "is currently subject to a connected set of regulatory and oversight arrangements arising from the CQC well-led inspection in July 2026 and NHS England's concerns about organisational quality governance and specific services. These arrangements include a **Section 29A Warning Notice, Enforcement Undertakings and enhanced oversight through a System Improvement Board**." — [Item 9 Regulatory Oversight]
[Evidence] "Juliette Cosgrove provided an overview of the report, which provided an update on actions taken in response to enforcement undertakings issued by NHS England, with particular focus on **spinal and gynaecology services**. It was noted that two rapid internal reviews had been completed" — [Minutes, item 9 Enforcement Undertakings]
[Evidence] "It was noted that the Trust had received the final reports from the CQC inspections at Fairfield for urgent and emergency care and medical [care]" — [Minutes]
[Evidence] The quality committee "received partial assurance regarding delivery of the Section 29A Surgical Services Improvement Programme, recognising that sustainable improvement remains the key objective." — [Quality Committee key issues report]

[LEAD] An independent review of the services covered by the enforcement undertakings starts on 10 September.
[Evidence] "The commissioned independent review is expected to commence on 10 September 2026 and will provide important validation of current controls, actions to date, and the sustainability of improvements achieved." — [Regulatory Oversight report, section 5.3]
[Evidence] "The Board also noted its previous decision, **endorsed by NHS England**, to commission independent reviews of these services to gain a fuller understanding of concerns and ensure any necessary additional actions are taken." — [Minutes]
[Evidence] "further consideration is being given to recommendations that may require additional external review" — [Minutes]

[LEAD] NCA is preparing a PDC revenue cash support application and says it may have to declare cash distress from October.
[Evidence] "NCA has prepared for PDC revenue cash support application, in anticipation of support required and is forecasting this may be required from October 2026. If System Deficit Funding is not achieved and/or there is significant CIP slippage, then the Trust is likely to **signal a cash distress position** impacting on Capital Programme delivery. NCA will need to trigger cash preservation actions ahead of request of any PDC Revenue Support." — [Month 4 Finance Report, Cash]
[Evidence] This is despite a headline cash position that looks comfortable: "Total cash at end of M4 = £53.9m against plan of £16.2m — £37.7m better than plan" — [same]

[LEAD] The reported on-plan position is being held up by £20.9m of non-recurrent mitigations; strip them out and the deficit roughly doubles.
[Evidence] "NON-RECURRENT MITIGATIONS £20.9m Supporting Month 1 to 4 position. **POSITION EXCL. MITIGATIONS £39.2m Deficit** if mitigations excluded... Reported position is on plan but supported by non-recurrent mitigations." — [Month 4 Finance Report]
[Evidence] "As at month 4, NCA Group is reporting a position £13k better than plan, with a net deficit of £18.3m. Excluding Deficit Support Funding (DSF) = £27.9m Deficit" — [same]
[Evidence] From the June position: the trust was "reporting a £10m deficit at the end of Month 2 in line with plan, but that this relies on £4.8m of deficit support funding and other non-recurrent mitigations and one-off measures, **leaving an underlying deficit of £14.8m** and creating ongoing financial risk." — [Finance and Performance Committee report]
[Evidence] "concerns remain that further action and faster delivery of savings and strategic service changes will be required to achieve the year-end target deficit of £28.8m." — [same]

[LEAD] The savings programme is £22m short of identification and £8.6m behind on delivery, with no improvement since month one.
[Evidence] "the Trust had identified £82.4m of CIP schemes (against a target of £104m)" — [Finance and Performance Committee report, Asif Umarji, Acting Chief Finance Officer]
[Evidence] "Cost Improvement Programme (CIP) delivery remains behind plan, reporting an underperformance of £8.6m, **with no significant improvement since month one**." — [same]
[Evidence] Some grounds for optimism are recorded: "a significant improvement in the identification of CIP opportunities, increasing from £59.3m at the same point last year to over £80m currently, reflecting strong work by new leadership teams and managing directors." — [same]

[LEAD] Temporary staffing spend is running £6.9m over the national limit.
[Evidence] "Temporary Staffing: YTD spend of £33.4m v National Limit of £26.5m. 7.9% of total pay expenditure (Agency 0.7%, Bank 7.3%)" — [Month 4 Finance Report, Other Metrics]
[Evidence] "WTE Changes: Net increase of 85 wte from prior month." — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is being run by an acting chief executive and an acting chief finance officer, with a turnaround director in attendance at board.
[Evidence] The agenda lists the "Acting Chief Executive Officer" as lead for the Chief Executive Horizon Scan, Regulatory Oversight, Clinical Leadership Model Implementation Update and the Change Portfolio report; the finance report is presented by "Asif Umarji, Acting Chief Finance Officer" — [Board agenda, items 8–14]
[Evidence] Attendance list includes "**Joe Gibson, Turnaround Director**" — [Minutes, In Attendance]

[WORTH WATCHING] A mutually agreed resignation scheme has taken 18 staff out of the organisation by the end of August.
[Evidence] "the organisation ran a Mutually Agreed Resignation Scheme (MARS)... Clinical Groups and Exec panels have reviewed the applications. **21 applications were approved but 3 have withdrawn. The 18 will leave the organization by the end of August.**" — [Programme Assurance | Corporate (FSP), 17 August 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Corridor care has been added to the board assurance framework as a principal risk, alongside the EPR and regulatory compliance.
[Evidence] "changes to the 2025/26 BAF include the introduction of three additional principal risks (**Electronic Patient Record, Regulatory Compliance and Corridor Care**), a move from an annual to a multi-year risk horizon" — [Minutes, BAF item]
[Evidence] "two new elements have [been] incorporated into the report: the inclusion of corridor care as a percentage measure and a summary of the overall corridor care position. Corridor care refers to patients who spend 45 minutes or more in a temporary escalation area rather than a designated bed space." — [Minutes, IPR item]
[Evidence] "Rachel Jones queried whether the apparent 8% corridor care figure represented a target and asked how confident the Trust could be in its d[ata]" — [same]

[WORTH WATCHING] Multiple savings and improvement programmes are RAG-rated red or amber with "limited assurance" recorded against them.
[Evidence] "There is also limited assurance that the programme plan includes the necessary actions to identify and address the gap to target, and impact assessment compliance is below 100%." — [Programme Assurance report, Medical Workforce (Value Shift)]
[Evidence] "The Governance status of the Length of Stay (FSP) programme has reduced to **red RAG rated** this month" — [same]
[Evidence] On the violence prevention and reduction standards: "The Trust is currently rated Green in one domain and Amber in six domains." — [VPR Standards report]

[WORTH WATCHING] A staff member's patient/colleague story to the board was an account of resigning after burnout following an ADHD diagnosis.
[Evidence] "Rachel gave an emotional account of resigning from the NCA after reaching psychological burnout following her ADHD diagnosis. Despite her commitment to the organisation and the availability of formal policies, she felt that workplace culture and day-to-day practic[e]" fell short — [Minutes, staff story item]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Freedom to Speak Up annual report had to be corrected and re-presented after duplicate records were found.
[Evidence] "a corrected version of the Freedom to Speak Up (FTSU) Annual Report presented to the Board of Directors on 6 May 2026. It was noted that since the original report was presented, a small number of duplicate records of FTSU [concerns had been identified]" — [Minutes, item 6.1]

## FOI leads

[FOI] **The section 29A warning notice and the enforcement undertakings.** *Request from NCA, the CQC and NHS England (North West):* the section 29A warning notice served after the July 2026 well-led inspection, the enforcement undertakings accepted from NHS England, the System Improvement Board's terms of reference and minutes, and the CQC's final inspection reports for Fairfield urgent and emergency care and medical care. *Why it matters:* the pack confirms all of these exist and describes them only in summary.

[FOI] **The two rapid internal reviews of spinal and gynaecology services**, and the independent review starting 10 September. *Request from NCA:* both completed rapid review reports, the terms of reference and appointed provider for the commissioned independent review, its contract value, and the NHS England correspondence endorsing it.

[FOI] **The PDC revenue cash support application and cash preservation plan.** *Request from NCA and NHS England:* the prepared PDC revenue support application, the cash preservation action list, and the papers setting out the "cash distress position" trigger criteria and what it would mean for the capital programme.

[FOI] **The mutually agreed resignation scheme.** *Request from NCA:* the total cost of the MARS scheme, the number of applications received and approved, the banded value of payments, the staff groups and grades affected, and the projected recurrent saving.

[FOI] **The Good Governance Institute engagement.** The audit/quality committee refers to "Good Governance Institute (GGI) improvement actions". *Request from NCA:* the GGI report(s), the contract value and term, and the improvement action plan.
