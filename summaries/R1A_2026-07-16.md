## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust's NHS Oversight Framework ranking fell from 37th to 44th of 61 mental health/community trusts, driven by data-quality problems in its children's mental health data.
[Evidence] "The Trust remains in segment 3 for the NoF but with a deterioration in ranking from 37 to 44, largely driven by data quality issues relating to the number of children and young people accessing mental health services." — Performance Report exec summary, [Enc E – Performance and Operational Report]. NoF slide: rank "44 out of 61 / Previous quarter's rank: 37 out of 61"; "Access to services 4 – Low performing". — p.1 of Q4 2025/26 deck, [Enc E]

[LEAD] More than 1,400 mental health patients are waiting over two years for treatment — 500-plus adults and 900-plus children and young people.
[Evidence] "Adult Mental Health patients waiting over 104 weeks: 500+." / "Children and Young People waiting over 104 weeks: 900+." — Performance Report exec summary, [Enc E]

[LEAD] Community paediatrics is the primary driver of long waits: 964 children waiting over 52 weeks and 9.31% of all community patients waiting over a year, putting the trust in the worst-performing quartile.
[Evidence] "964 Community Paediatric patients are waiting over 52 weeks." / "Performance against the Q4 NOF metric for community service waits over 52 weeks is 9.31%, placing the Trust in quartile 4 (worst performing). Community Paediatrics remains the primary driver of long waits". — [Enc E, exec summary and community-services metric page]

[LEAD] Fourteen months after go-live, the trust's RiO electronic patient record is still generating data-quality problems, with full recovery not expected until February 2027.
[Evidence] "Following the implementation of RiO in May 2025, issues with data recording and data quality have been identified. These issues have contributed to the current reduction in reported access metrics… The recovery plan commenced in June 2026, with key milestones set for data quality stabilisation by October 2026, significant backlog reduction by December 2026, and full recovery with sustained improvement by February 2027." — [Enc E, CYP MH access metric page]. A new "Digital Innovation Oversight Board (DIOB)" and RiO Optimisation Programme with a new Programme Director are being stood up. — p.2-3 (§3.2-3.3), [Enc G – Priority Programmes]

[LEAD] The trust closed its adult learning-disability respite services at Osborne Court and Church View on 30 June 2026; the board was earlier told the closure had hit "service resilience".
[Evidence] "During June, the Trust safely completed the closure of adult respite services at Osborne Court and Church View, with both services formally closing on 30 June 2026." — p.3, [Enc C – Chief Executive's Report]. May minutes: "The Board received an update on the Learning Disabilities respite care position, which has required significant contingency arrangements, including additional shifts and redeployment of colleagues. Despite these efforts, service resilience has been affected." — p.8 (min 061/2026), [Enc A – Minutes 21 May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Talking Therapies has a two-appointment bottleneck, with six in ten patients waiting more than 90 days between first and second appointment.
[Evidence] "60.4% of patients wait more than 90 days between first and second appointments." — [Enc E, Talking Therapies section]. Board previously told compliance expected "by October" (min 063/2026, p.14, [Enc A]).

[WORTH WATCHING] The board's own assurance framework flags children's and young people's services as the only strategic risk it rates "deteriorating" and outside risk appetite, warning delivery risk "is already materialising".
[Evidence] "CYP Services — Outside appetite (high and increasing) — Deteriorating — Long waits, unmet need and risk of harm to children and young people." — BAF risk profile snapshot, p.5, [Enc H – Board Assurance Framework]. "Evidence from CYP and other programmes suggests delivery risk is already materialising." — p.3 (§3.2), [Enc H]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust is midway through an operational restructure, collapsing five service delivery units into two divisions with a new care-group tier.
[Evidence] "a new senior operating model has been confirmed, moving from five Service Delivery Units to two Service Delivery Divisions, supported by a care group structure." — p.7 (min 061/2026), [Enc A]. "Divisional Directors have now formally transitioned into their roles… Recruitment to the Head of Care Group posts has been successfully completed." — p.4, [Enc C]

[WORTH WATCHING] Non-executive turnover on the board: Sue Hincks left on 30 June 2026, Carole Cumino made deputy chair and Ellie Bulmer joined as associate NED, with further NED recruitment outstanding.
[Evidence] "I would like to recognise and thank Sue Hincks, who left the Trust on 30 June 2026… I am delighted to congratulate Carole Cumino on her appointment as Deputy Chair… I am also pleased to welcome Ellie Bulmer, who has joined us as an Associate Non-Executive Director… Further updates on non-executive recruitment will be shared with the Board in due course." — p.2, [Enc B – Chair's Report]
