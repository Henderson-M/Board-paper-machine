## Top lines

[LEAD] ICB chief executive Sam Allen has taken a second job as interim NHSE North East and Yorkshire regional director while remaining the ICB's accountable officer — at Sir James Mackey's request, with the ICB's corporate meeting day shifted to accommodate her diary.
[Evidence] "On 01 July 2026, I took up an Interim role as Regional Director for NHS England North East and Yorkshire… I will remain the accountable officer for the ICB." — p.3, [Item 9 CEO Report]; "This was following a request from Sir James Mackey… Going forward, the formal corporate day will move from a Tuesday to a Wednesday" — p.4, [Item 8 Chair's Report]. (Allen also made CBE in the King's Birthday Honours — p.1, [Item 5 Minutes].)

[LEAD] An independent governance review by Mary Aubrey into breast-service failings at County Durham and Darlington FT found long-standing governance and clinical-oversight failures; the ICB has run a system-wide learning event off the back of it.
[Evidence] "This review identified serious and long-standing weaknesses in clinical oversight, leadership action and accountability, and governance processes which enabled poor clinical practice to persist. The Trust accepted the findings of the review." — p.7, [Item 9 CEO Report]. CDDFT also flagged as a "specific area of concern" via a CQC report — p.1, [Item 11.2 QSC Escalation Report].

[LEAD] The ICB's 2025/26 accounts (approved 16 June) disclose roughly £8.8m of exit packages for 157 people, driven by the mandated cut of ICB running costs to £19 per head — and the CFO himself expects media attention.
[Evidence] "total costs of approximately £8.8m had been recognised in respect of 157 individuals… all arising from national requirements to reduce ICB costs of commissioning to £19 per head." — p.5, [Item 11.3 App 1 Audit Committee Minutes 23 Apr]; CFO "highlighted the likelihood of media attention on exit package disclosures" — p.4, same paper. (16 June board minutes give ~£8.9m: £1.5m compulsory + £7.4m voluntary — p.4, [Item 5 Minutes].)

[LEAD] A GP practice serving ~18,000 patients in Cockermouth (Castlegate and Derwent Surgery) has been rated CQC Inadequate and put in special measures; the ICB served a Remedial Notice and shut down an unapproved subcontracted triage service where patient records were not being accessed.
[Evidence] "CQC published its full findings on 03 June, rating the practice as Inadequate overall… placed in special measures… a Remedial Notice was served… All subcontracting arrangements require ICB approval, which had not been obtained… resulting in the termination of the subcontract." — p.9, [Item 9 CEO Report]. Cumberland Council scrutiny committee took an update on 30 July.

[WORTH WATCHING] Ernst & Young has been appointed to build the business case for an Integrated Health Organisation covering Northumberland and North Tyneside, with a "start-state contract from April 2027".
[Evidence] "A procurement to appoint a partner to support the business case development has been awarded to Ernest and Young, who start on the 15 July… business case expected for the ICB and NHCFT Boards in October." — p.8, [Item 9 CEO Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] At month 2 the ICB reports breakeven but carries £24.2m of unmitigated net risk (down from £31.2m in plan), with a three-year plan that assumes ~£30m of unmitigated risk in each year and largely undeveloped efficiency plans for years 2 and 3.
[Evidence] "Net unmitigated risk… has reduced from £31m in plan to £24m at month 2." — p.1, [Item 15 Finance Update M2]; MTFP "contains around £30m of unmitigated net risk in each year with significant risk in particular around the delivery of required efficiency savings in years 2 and 3." — p.7, [Item 10 App 2 CRR, risk NENC/0065]. Largest single risk is elective activity at £22m with elective recovery funding "capped" — p.6, [Item 15 App 1 Finance Report]. System-wide (ICS) position is £6.7m adverse to plan YTD "largely reflecting additional costs due to industrial action and under-delivery of efficiencies" — p.3, [Item 9 CEO Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] NENC was the only system in the North East and Yorkshire region to deliver its 2025/26 financial plan and to keep its deficit support funding (~£33m retained); Humber and North Yorkshire is named as the most financially challenged part of the region.
[Evidence] "North East and North Cumbria was the only system within the region to have delivered its financial plan for 2025/26… the only system within the region not to lose deficit support funding, and that approximately £33m of support funding had been retained." / "Humber and North Yorkshire remained the most financially challenged part of the region and had not yet fully signed off their plans." — p.7, [Item 11.3 App 1 Audit Committee Minutes]. Full-year surplus £34.9m (running-cost underspend £7.9m), used to offset provider deficits across the ICS — p.6, same paper; unqualified opinion from Forvis Mazars — p.6, [Item 5 Minutes].

[WORTH WATCHING] GP practices in NENC have served notice on data-sharing agreements on BMA advice, opening a new corporate risk that GP data flows into the ICB are "disrupted / paused / ceased".
[Evidence] "GP Practices on advice from BMA have served notice on data sharing agreements for secondary uses that are not contracted or linked directly to patient care… operational tools reliant on primary care data, including the RAIDR platform, will not receive updated information." — new risk NENC/0136, scored 12, p.5–6, [Item 10 BAF Report] / p.4, [Item 10 App 2 CRR].

[WORTH WATCHING] Around 150–180 NHSE specialised- and direct-commissioning staff are set to transfer into NENC ICB, which is proposing to host pan-ICB commissioning (OPIC) on behalf of all four North East and Yorkshire ICBs.
[Evidence] "Around 150–180 specialised commissioning and direct commissioning staff are expected to transfer into the ICB from NHS England… North East and North Cumbria ICB act as host organisation on behalf of the four ICBs." — p.4, [Item 9 CEO Report]; TUPE expected May 2027 — p.2, [Item 5 Minutes].
