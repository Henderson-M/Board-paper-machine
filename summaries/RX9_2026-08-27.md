# East Midlands Ambulance Service NHS Trust board — 27 August 2026

- **Source:** https://www.emas.nhs.uk/about-us/trust-board/next-board-meeting
- **Pack:** 10 files
- **Analysed:** 2026-08-24

## Top lines

[LEAD] The share of EMAS's black and minority ethnic staff reporting harassment, bullying or abuse from patients, relatives or the public jumped from 28.7% to 46.81% in a year — an 18-point rise, and the trust says the level "is not acceptable".
[Evidence] WRES Indicator 5: "Percentage of staff experiencing harassment, bullying or abuse from patients, relatives or the public in last 12 months. BME 28.70% [2024/25] 46.81% [2025/26] 18.11" — p.3, [PB-26-149 WRES and WDES Reports]. Covering paper: "The WRES and WDES data indicate that there has been a significant increase in the percentage of staff experiencing harassment, bullying or abuse from patients, relatives or members of the public within the last 12 months... This level of reporting is not acceptable, and the Trust is committed to addressing these issues." — p.2

[LEAD] EMAS abandoned its 2026/27 private ambulance procurement and extended the incumbent contract instead, and non-pay is £3.06m overspent.
[Evidence] "Non-pay expenditure is overspent by £3.059 million against plan. Central Private Ambulance Services are showing an overspend due to EMAS ceasing the 2026/27 procurement process and extending the existing contract in order to maintain the necessary staff resourcing levels. This overspend is being funded by the vacancies on the front line." — p.5, [PB-26-148 Financial Performance Report]

[LEAD] EMAS is not recruiting a director of operations at all — it is replacing the post with a chief operating officer, and has brought in an interim for six months while it does.
[Evidence] "As part of wider executive leadership arrangements, the Trust will not be recruiting directly to the Director of Operations post. Instead, recruitment will commence shortly to a new Chief Operating Officer role... To support this transition, Ben Evans joined the Trust on 20 July 2026 as Director of Service Delivery for an initial six-month period while recruitment to the Chief Operating Officer role is undertaken." — p.5, [PB-26-146 Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] EMAS lost 12,603 hours to handover delays in July alone, and 49,632 hours so far this year — the CEO names it as the single thing stopping response times improving.
[Evidence] "Handover hours lost remained broadly stable at 12,603 hours" (July); year-to-date figure 49,632 hours — p.2 and performance table, [PB-26-147 Integrated Board Report]. CEO: "The principal constraint to achieving further performance improvement continues to be hospital handover delays, which reduce available ambulance capacity and impact response times across the region." — p.3, [PB-26-146 Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] Category 2 response times are running five minutes worse than plan year to date.
[Evidence] "As of 18 August 2026, the Trust's year-to-date average Category 2 (C2) response time was 34 minutes and 31 seconds, which is above the annual plan of 29 minutes and 22 seconds. For July, the Trust's reported C2 response time was 33 minutes and 59 seconds against a planned position of 30 minutes and 36 seconds." — p.3, [PB-26-146 Chief Executive's Report]. Category 1 mean was 8:51 against a 7:00 target — performance table, [PB-26-147 Integrated Board Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] EMAS and West Midlands Ambulance Service are working towards shared operational systems — with WMAS potentially adopting EMAS's computer-aided dispatch — but the board is being asked to note, not approve.
[Evidence] "WMAS is considering reprocurement of key operational systems, including telephony, Electronic Patient Record (EPR) and Computer Aided Dispatch (CAD)... [an option is] for WMAS to use EMAS CAD, mobilisation, Mobile Data Terminal and communications". "The Board is not being asked to approve any joint procurement, shared service or [transfer]". Named risks include "Unrealistic expectations of early savings" and moving "too quickly toward shared systems without" adequate governance. — pp.5–8, [PB-26-152 Digital Collaboration Agreement with West Midlands Ambulance Service]

[WORTH WATCHING] Only a third of this year's required savings had been delivered by the end of July.
[Evidence] "Within the financial plan there is an assumption that the Trust will deliver new cash releasing efficiency savings of £14.450 million to meet the control total. In addition, there is £1.6 million of non-recurrent schemes from 2025/26 to deliver recurrently... The value of efficiencies delivered as at the end of July was £4.817 million." — p.7, [PB-26-148 Financial Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2025/26 accounts are now public with a clean audit opinion from KPMG and no value-for-money issues.
[Evidence] "The External Auditors, KPMG, issued an unqualified opinion which means that they believe the accounts give a true and fair view of the financial performance and position of the Trust... KPMG did not report any issues in respect of value for money. Also, KPMG did not consider it necessary to issue any other reports in the public interest." Opinion issued 18 June 2026; Head of Internal Audit Opinion "Significant assurance". — pp.1 and 4–6, [PB-26-151 External Audit Annual Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Lincolnshire non-emergency patient transport is deteriorating and EMAS is in dispute with commissioners over activity.
[Evidence] "The Operations directorate has developed a financial recovery plan for Lincolnshire NEPTS, whilst potential improvements have been identified the underlying run rate has deteriorated due to the use of taxis. EMAS is still awaiting agreement with Lincolnshire commissioners regarding the activity issues that have been raised." — p.6, [PB-26-148 Financial Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] EMAS is buying 40 more double-crewed ambulances by April 2028 under the programme it used to call "Right Sizing".
[Evidence] "This programme increases frontline ambulance capacity through the introduction of 40 additional double-crewed ambulances and associated workforce growth by April 2028." The CEO ties it explicitly to staff feedback: it "forms an important part of our response to colleague feedback from the 2025 Staff Opinion Survey regarding capacity and workload pressures." — pp.4–5, [PB-26-146 Chief Executive's Report]

## FOI leads

- **The abandoned private ambulance procurement.** The finance report says EMAS ceased the 2026/27 procurement and extended the incumbent contract. Request from EMAS: the decision paper and any waiver of standing financial instructions / single-tender justification, the value and end date of the extension, and the name of the incumbent provider.
- **The WMAS digital collaboration business case.** The paper says a future decision will need a "benefit statement, risk assessment, financial analysis and confirmation of each Trust's" position. Request the IT contracts baseline review referred to at p.5 ("a reliable baseline of contracts, costs, expiry dates"), and the end-of-life assessment for current call-handling systems.
- **The Lincolnshire NEPTS dispute.** Request from EMAS and from NHS Lincolnshire commissioners: the financial recovery plan for Lincolnshire NEPTS and the correspondence on the disputed activity.
- **The director of operations decision.** Request the executive structure paper and remuneration committee minutes covering the decision not to fill the director of operations post and to create a chief operating officer role, plus the terms of the six-month interim engagement (day rate, whether via agency).
