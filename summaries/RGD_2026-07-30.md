## Top lines

[LEAD] CQC has downgraded three of the trust's core mental health services and issued a warning notice, following inspections.
[Evidence] "Adult acute wards and our Psychiatric Intensive Care Unit (PICU) – the overall rating for this service has moved from good to requires improvement, Wards for older people with serious mental illness – the overall rating for this service has moved from good to requires improvement, and Long stay or rehabilitation mental health wards for working age adults – which following inspection was issued with a warning notice to ensure immediate improvements were made in specific areas. This service has maintained the requires improvement rating overall." — p.34, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] The trust is set to drop "York" from its name and merge with Leeds Community Healthcare, with a new organisation to launch on 1 April 2027.
[Evidence] "The final recommendation approved by the committee is to adopt the name Leeds Partnership NHS Foundation Trust (LPFT)"; key dates: "Draft full business case to be reviewed by both trust boards at the end of September… FBC submission to NHSE national transaction team and regional team October 2026. Process for the appointment of a new Trust Board to commence in August and conclude by end of October 2026 that will come into effect from 1st April 2027." — p.36-37, [Chief Executive's Report]. Name consultation drew 2,132 responses; visits to York-based services (Clifton House, Mill Lodge) held because the new name "doesn't include York recognising this would be a loss for these services" — p.35.

[LEAD] The trust sits in the highest national quartile for adult acute mental health length of stay, with one patient approaching 1,000 days in a bed.
[Evidence] "Benchmarking from The Model Hospital confirms that the Trust sits within the highest national quartile for length of stay, significantly above both national and regional medians (approximately 47–48 days)… This places the service among the highest LoS providers." — p.168, [Report of the Chief Operating Officer]. "14 patients (11% of the cohort) have a LoS greater than 200 days… Patients exceed 400 days, including one approaching 1,000 days" — p.166; Becklin Ward 1 "highest average LoS (124.7 days), driven by a concentration of long-stay patients, including one approaching 1,000 days" — p.167.

[WORTH WATCHING] External specialists VOT Health were commissioned for a diagnostic of the acute pathway, and the trust's flow programme is rated red, with 25 inappropriate out-of-area placements and patients sent up to ~145 miles from home.
[Evidence] "Earlier this year the Trust commissioned independent specialists VOT Health to complete a phase 1 diagnostic across the acute working age pathway. The diagnostic has identified a set of high impact changes…" — p.94; "the following priorities are reporting as red (alert): Delivery of our Patient & Flow Programme" — p.91; "At the end of quarter one we have 25 adult acute and PICU inappropriate out of area placements" — p.95; out-of-area table shows placements at "ELLINGHAM FARM x2, 136-146 miles" from home — p.165, [COO report / Organisational Priorities Q1]. Also: "23 service users waiting for admission more than 12 hours [in ED], with the majority waiting over 24 hours" (May–June) — p.171.

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2025 NHS staff survey shows declining experience — burnout up to 25.6%, fewer than half feeling valued — and the CEO states the trust has more staff reporting discrimination than comparable trusts.
[Evidence] "reported burnout has increased to 25.6%, fewer staff feel valued (46.6%), and confidence in speaking up has reduced. Equality indicators also show areas of concern, with worsening reports of harassment in WRES data…" — p.272; "68.9%" of free-text comments coded negative — p.272/275, [2025 NHS Staff Survey Trust-level Action Plan]. CEO: "As a Trust we have more staff reporting discrimination at work than similar NHS trusts. There needs to be a firmer response to this." — p.32, [Chief Executive's Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] Internal audit gave "limited assurance" opinions on minimising self-harm and suicide, and on medicines management, at the July audit committee.
[Evidence] "The 'Minimising Self-harm and Suicide' internal audit report was received with an overall opinion of limited assurance… The 'Medicines Management' internal audit report was recevied [sic] with an overall opinion of limited assurance"; plus a "split assurance opinion" on the 'Ward to Board' escalation report (system operation element "limited assurance") — p.280, [Chair's Report from the Audit Committee, 14 July 2026].

[WORTH WATCHING] At month 3 the trust reports a £42k surplus but an underlying recurrent deficit, against a regional position described as one of the most financially challenged in the country.
[Evidence] "At the end of June we are reporting a small £42k surplus against a balanced plan" — p.77; "This shows an improved position and underlying deficit of £1.3m" (0.48% of recurrent income), improved from a £4.3m underlying deficit in the February plan — p.78-79; efficiency targets: "£11.4m" run-rate plus a separate "£13.6m recurrent budget efficiency" devolved to services — p.79; out-of-area placement expenditure "£1.8m over plan collectively, with £1.2m attributable to WAA" — p.78. Region: "As a region NEY is one of the most financially challenged in the country ending 25/26 with an aggregate deficit of £378.6m, which was 145.3m off plan." — p.81, [CFO Finance Report].
