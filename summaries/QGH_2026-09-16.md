# Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB board – 16 September 2026

- **Source:** https://herefordshireandworcestershire.icb.nhs.uk/meetings/board-papers
- **Pack:** 10 files
- **Analysed:** 2026-09-15T11:40:00Z

Note: this is a "meeting in common" of the Coventry and Warwickshire ICB and Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB boards. All papers are joint and cover both ICBs, so much of the material below concerns the wider cluster and its seven provider trusts.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Worcestershire Acute finished 2025/26 roughly £7m worse than its expected deficit, drew national attention as the only system to miss plan, and is losing about £11m of deficit support as a result.
[Evidence] "In Herefordshire and Worcestershire (HW), however, significant challenges were highlighted, particularly within WAHT, which reported a circa £12m deficit against an expected £5m, representing a material variance that attracted national attention as the only system not to deliver its plan. As a consequence, around £11m of deficit support is expected to be withdrawn in the following year, creating cash pressures." – p.29, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, Strategic Finance Committee Part 2 minutes, 15 May 2026]

[LEAD] Independent-sector Right to Choose providers are recruiting staff away from the NHS neurodiversity service, and the trust has now had to suspend it.
[Evidence] "Workforce challenges within the last quarter have increased significantly due to recruitment by independent sector organisations who are undertaking assessments via right to choose. This has impacted core capacity at Herefordshire and Worcestershire Health and Care NHS Trust (HWHCT). Concerns over delivery and service sustainability have been escalated by HWHCT through established commissioner meetings." – p.6, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A, priority 1.1a]
[Evidence] "Herefordshire and Worcestershire Health and Care NHS Trust has advised that its clinical neurodivergence service for children and young people in Worcestershire and Herefordshire will be temporarily suspended from 14 September 2026 following significant staffing losses." – p.6, [CEO Report]
[Note] The pack gives two different start dates and scopes for the restriction: the CEO report says the clinical neurodivergence service is "temporarily suspended from 14 September 2026", while the performance report and BAF both say "HWHCT is suspending its ADHD assessment service from 7th September 2026 for 4 weeks" (p.16, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report]; p.6, [BAF]). Worth pinning down with the ICB.

[LEAD] Children in Herefordshire and Worcestershire are waiting more than 62 weeks for an ADHD medication review and no new appointments are being offered at all.
[Evidence] "The current waiting time for a new medication review exceeds 62 weeks, with no new appointments currently being offered." – p.16, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A, Mental Health CYP Community 104 Week Waits]
[Evidence] Cluster-wide: "As of June 2026, 3,731 children are waiting for an ADHD assessment across the combined system (1,744 of them over 104 weeks) and 7,702 are waiting for an autism assessment (1,210 over 104 weeks)." – p.6, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A]

[LEAD] Right to Choose neurodiversity spend has risen fifteenfold in two years and is now forecast at £28m across the cluster for 2026/27.
[Evidence] "Limited financial resource available to address capacity gap against significant growth in use of Right to Choose, leading to associated significant growth in non-contracted activity spend, projected to be £28m for 2026/27 across the cluster." – p.17, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A, priority 1.1e]
[Evidence] "HWICB saw a significant increase in referrals through the right to choose for Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), with the average monthly expenditure in 2024/25 of £0.1m rising to £1.0m in 2025/26 (noting that the last six months of 2025 saw an average of £1.2m a month)... for HWICB the average monthly run rate is £1.5m over the first 3 months of 2026/27." – p.3, [Financial Plan and Delivery, Month 4]

[LEAD] NHS England told the cluster's finance committee the Midlands region is £323m in deficit year to date before deficit support funding.
[Evidence] "NHS England reported a challenging regional financial position, with the Midlands reporting a year-to-date deficit of £323m before deficit support funding. Within the Coventry & Warwickshire and Herefordshire & Worcestershire cluster, the Quarter 1 position is comparatively stronger, with a £5.5m adverse variance and approximately two-thirds of organisations delivering plan... NHS England advised that financial scrutiny will increase during Quarter 2." – p.7, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Strategic Finance Committee, 17 July 2026]

[LEAD] Roughly three-fifths of the cluster's £92.4m efficiency target for 2026/27 is non-recurrent, and delivery is already £3.2m behind.
[Evidence] "The ICB's 2026/27 efficiency target of £44.0m includes approximately £24.0m of non-recurrent savings." – p.7, [Financial Plan and Delivery, Month 4, Appendix A, Herefordshire and Worcestershire]
[Evidence] "The ICB's 2026/27 efficiency target of £48.4m includes approximately £32.0m of non-recurrent savings." – p.14, [Financial Plan and Delivery, Month 4, Appendix B, Coventry and Warwickshire]
[Evidence] "At Month 4 across the Cluster, the ICBs position reflects £3.2m of under delivery, with £4.8m against Continuing Healthcare (CHC) mitigated by a favourable variance of £1.6m in Prescribing." – p.2, [Financial Plan and Delivery, Month 4]

[LEAD] Paediatric audiology is flagged as the single biggest quality concern across all five acute trusts in the cluster, with only one holding accreditation.
[Evidence] "Paediatric audiology is the most significant specialty with specific quality concern across the five acute providers. Key risks include limited standard operating procedures, peer review and audit; immature quality management systems; workforce and leadership constraints; weak trainee pipelines; and inconsistent accreditation. UHCW is the Cluster's only provider with Improving Quality in Physiological Services accreditation for paediatric audiology." – p.12, [Spotlight: Planned Care]

[LEAD] The ICBs have asked NHS England to approve a merger from April 2027, creating a single body for about 1.9 million people, with compulsory redundancies possible.
[Evidence] "NHS Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB and NHS Coventry and Warwickshire ICB submitted a proposal to NHS England in July recommending that the two ICBs merge on their existing clustered footprint from April 2027... The proposed merger is also expected to deliver approximately £1 million in additional recurring annual running cost savings (on top of the £22 million that was recurrently saved from the clustering process)... NHS England is currently considering merger proposals from ICB clusters nationally. A decision on the proposed arrangement is awaited." – p.1, [CEO Report]
[Evidence] "Where colleagues cannot be matched to available roles or the required skills and experience do not align with remaining vacancies, compulsory redundancies may be necessary." – p.2, [CEO Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB and NHS England agreed a formal overnight tolerance for ambulance handover delays of up to four hours at Worcestershire Acute.
[Evidence] "Overnight tolerance of no delay greater than 4 hours was agreed with Worcestershire Acute and Regional team in February 2026." – p.10, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A, priority 1.1b Ambulance Handovers]
[Evidence] "Corridor care metrics have identified Herefordshire and Worcestershire as a current outlier, although data quality and interpretation are still being refined nationally." – p.4, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, IQPAC 26 August 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England's annual assessment says the ICB stood down its own System Quality Group repeatedly during the year, and that its council partners submitted no feedback at all.
[Evidence] "there is a lack of assurance that other quality domains are receiving appropriate scrutiny, as the ICB System Quality Group has been stood down on multiple occasions throughout the year." – p.19, [CEO Report, Appendix A, annual assessment of Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB, 3 August 2026]
[Evidence] "it is acknowledged that the ICB's local government partners have not shared any feedback with NHS England on the ICB's performance or capability in 2025/26, so this cannot be incorporated into this assessment." – p.19, [CEO Report, Appendix A]
[Evidence] "No tiering is in place for the ICB, although Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust (WAHT) and Wye Valley NHS Trust (WVT) are both currently in tiering for urgent and emergency care (UEC) services, and WAHT is in tiering for elective, cancer and diagnostics." – p.19, [CEO Report, Appendix A]

[WORTH WATCHING] End of life care in Herefordshire and Worcestershire costs about £44m a year in acute beds, with a quarter of patients' last 90 days spent in hospital, and the ICB wants to cut acute use by a quarter by 2029.
[Evidence] "approximately 26 per cent of patients' final 90 days of life are currently spent in acute hospital settings... approximately £44 million being spent annually on acute bed-based care for patients in the end-of-life phase." – p.15, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, IQPAC minutes 24 June 2026]
[Evidence] "the overall ambition of the programme is to reduce acute hospital utilisation in end-of-life care by 25 per cent by 2029" – p.16, [same]

[FOI] Request the Regulation 28 prevention of future deaths report sent to Worcestershire Acute in July 2026, and the trust's response of 19 August 2026, from Worcestershire Acute Hospitals Trust and the relevant coroner.
[Evidence] "Regulation 28 Reporting of Future Deaths Reports. WAHT July 2026. Concerning a patient who had been admitted following a fall at home and later discharged to a community hospital despite signs of deterioration, family concerns and abnormal test results. Coroner has concerns regarding discharge processes. WAHT has until 19th August 2026 to respond." – p.8, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A, Quality: Provider Risk and Escalations]
[Why] The pack confirms a PFD report exists and gives its subject but not its contents or the trust's answer. The same pack separately records that Regulation 28 notifications are rising across the system (p.19, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, IQPAC minutes]).

[FOI] Request the ICB's rapid quality review of Herefordshire and Worcestershire Health and Care Trust's ADHD and neurodiversity services, including terms of reference and recommendations, from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB.
[Evidence] "A rapid quality review is being undertaken and ongoing reporting has been requested." – p.2, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, IQPAC 26 August 2026, Alert]
[Evidence] "Rapid review to be undertaken with ICB Quality and Safety team, to understand HWHCT position and develop recommendations." – p.6, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A]
[Why] The review is under way and its findings are not in the pack. It goes to the heart of a service that has been suspended.

[FOI] Request the quality visit report on the Coventry integrated single point of access, from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB or NHS England Midlands.
[Evidence] "Coventry iSPA. A recent quality visit by Regional Integrated Urgent Care colleagues and the ICB identified findings requiring formal reporting. The resulting report will be shared with ICB colleagues and UHCW to inform any required improvement actions." – p.11, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A, UEC Exception Report]
[Why] The pack states findings exist and require "formal reporting" but does not say what they are.

[FOI] Request the clinical harm review into ophthalmology at George Eliot Hospital Trust, including the number of patients reviewed and harm graded, from George Eliot Hospital Trust and the ICB.
[Evidence] "ophthalmology services at GEH remained the most significant patient safety concern and a comprehensive clinical harm review was underway." – p.9, [Public Board Notes, 15 July 2026, Item 12]
[Evidence] "Ophthalmology remains a fragile service at GEH, with ongoing concerns regarding governance, clinical input and the pace of operational recovery despite progress in reducing the backlog." – p.15, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A, Elective Care Exception Report]
[Why] A harm review is confirmed to exist across "Glaucoma, Shared care, Medical Retina, and General" backlogs (p.8, [IQPR Appendix A]) but no outcome, scale or harm findings are published.

## Other specifics worth holding

These are datapoints rather than standalone leads, captured because they name organisations and numbers.

- Worcestershire Acute recorded 57.0 per cent on the 62-day cancer standard in June 2026, against 85.4 per cent at Wye Valley; Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB ranks 28th of 36 ICBs on the measure, Coventry and Warwickshire 14th. p.4 and p.2, [Spotlight: Planned Care]
- University Hospitals Coventry and Warwickshire holds all 34 of the cluster's 65-week waits and has 3.2 per cent of its list waiting over 52 weeks; its RTT 18-week position is 57.9 per cent. p.2, [Spotlight: Planned Care]
- Coventry and Warwickshire ICB is "ranked 36/36 for adults and children" on learning disability and autism inpatient numbers. p.16, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A]
- NHS England's annual assessment of Coventry and Warwickshire ICB records autism inpatient numbers "ranked 41 of 42 systems nationally", Individual Placement and Support delivery at 62 per cent making it "a regional outlier", and 43.5 per cent of over-65s unvaccinated for flu. p.10 and p.11, [CEO Report, Appendix A]
- Five of six stroke teams in the cluster are rated SSNAP level D, scores ranging from 40.0 at Hereford County Hospital to 58.9 at the Worcestershire Stroke Rehabilitation Unit; only George Eliot achieves level C at 64.6. p.17, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A]
- South Warwickshire University Foundation Trust has received two Maternity Outcomes Signal System Level 1 alerts, in June and July 2026, and remains under enhanced scrutiny. p.8, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A]
- Worcestershire has had 24 confirmed measles cases since April 2026, linked to both hospital and community transmission; UKHSA incidents at Worcestershire Acute and in the county are now closed. p.8, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report, Appendix A]
- Wye Valley Trust provides no seven-day and no out of hours end of life care service for children and young people. p.8, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A, priority 1.1a iv]
- Worcestershire Acute is in Tier 2 and Wye Valley in Tier 1 for NHS England urgent and emergency care support. p.11, [Board Assurance Framework, Appendix A]
- New national guidance on "patient not present" activity is unfunded and presents "a potential cost pressure of approximately £5m to the ICB". p.30, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, Strategic Finance Committee Part 2 minutes, 15 May 2026]
- The cluster self-assessed 63 out of 100 against the national Neighbourhood Health Framework, scoring 3 out of 10 on elective pathways and 4 out of 10 on community waits; "Six NNHIP applications across the four Places only 2 successful". p.9, [Neighbourhood Health Framework Review, Appendix B single-page summary]
- Worcestershire's frailty pathway is credited with "29% fewer 75+ medical attendances at the ED". p.6, [Neighbourhood Health Framework Review, Appendix B]
- Named external firms and products in the pack: Source Medical (validating 32,000 UHCW patient records, expected 20-30 per cent reduction), Accurx text validation, Luna Rover AI validation tool, SpaMedica (glaucoma contract variation, £50,000 indicative year one), Innovate Healthcare Services (single IG, PHM and BI provider after CSU closure), Modality and TSC (UHCW outsourcing and insourcing). p.15, [IQPR Appendix A]; p.14, [BAF Appendix A]; p.42, [Committee Escalation report Appendix A, SCC minutes 1 July 2026]; p.41, [BAF Appendix A]
- Coventry and Warwickshire is one of four national pilot sites for Fit Note Reforms, with a £617,266 DWP-funded direct award to Coventry and Warwickshire Integrated Primary Care. p.41, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, SCC minutes 1 July 2026]
- Neighbourhood health centre capital approvals have slipped: "the time frames for approval have been pushed back with approvals set to be decided in the Autumn". Herefordshire and Worcestershire's schemes require £222.4m from the national programme from 2030/31 onwards. p.7 and p.10, [Financial Plan and Delivery, Month 4]
- Running cost allowance target is £19.60 per head by 31 March 2027; running cost reduction across the cluster is put at "c. 50% (approx GBP 23m)". p.39, [BAF Appendix A]; p.9, [Neighbourhood Health Framework Review, Appendix B]
- The Wave 3 consultation covered "approximately 425 staff for 443 posts", with more than 80 per cent matched. p.33, [Committee Escalation and Assurance Report, Appendix A, Corporate Management Committee minutes, 18 June 2026]
