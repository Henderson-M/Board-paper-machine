[LEAD] Berkshire Healthcare — a segment 2, "outstanding"-led trust — ranks second-from-last in England on the NHS Oversight Framework's relative cost metric.
[Evidence] "Relative difference in costs – remained in second from last position. Ranked 60th out of 61 trusts. Metric value at 135.35% compared to a median of 104.85%. Material changes have been implemented to improve position through the activity recording project" — p.179, [True North Performance Scorecard Month 2 (May 2026)]

[LEAD] 86 children are waiting more than 52 weeks for the trust's community services, against a target of zero, on a newly-scoring national oversight metric.
[Evidence] "Number of children on community waiting lists over 52 weeks (excluding neurodiversity)… – 86 against a target of 0. This is a new NHS Oversight Framework (NOF) scoring metric." Also "Number of children on mental health waiting lists over 52 weeks… – 3 against a target of 0." — p.178, [True North Performance Scorecard Month 2]

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England redistributed unused deficit support funding away from systems that missed their plans and gave it to providers that delivered — Berkshire Healthcare's share was £1.638m.
[Evidence] "NHS England had decided to redistribute Deficit Support Funding (DSF) not used by systems that failed to deliver their plans to providers that did deliver, provided those providers were part of a system that met plan… The Trust's share of this funding was £1,638k." — p.13, [Minutes of 12 May 2026, min 26/090]; current-year receipt confirmed: "We have received the £1.3m deficit support funding" — p.172, [Finance Report May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Reported staff assaults rose sharply year-on-year, with the trust conceding a "culture of acceptance" of violence and under 10% of incidents reported to police.
[Evidence] "923 physical assaults against staff were reported during the period, which was an increase of 254 compared to 2024-25" and "1,268 non-physical assaults… an increase of 242" — p.16, [Minutes of 12 May 2026, min 26/094, Annual Health and Safety Report]; "less than 10% of the incidents were reported to the police… there was culture of acceptance of violence especially amongst mental health staff" — p.12, [Minutes, min 26/089]

[WORTH WATCHING] PICU placement overspend is being driven by a local shortage of female intensive-care beds and rising Mental Health Act prison-transfer demand.
[Evidence] "There remains a long-standing issue of a lack of female PICU placement beds locally. The demand for PICU beds has also been affected by an increased number of requests for prison transfers for assessment or treatment under the Mental Health Act (MHA)." PICU overspend £109k YTD; total placement overspend £235k YTD — p.170, [Finance Report May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] A surge in complaints is being blamed on AI-generated submissions that misread the law and obscure what redress is sought.
[Evidence] "There was a significant increase in the number of complaints this quarter, largely due to lengthy and complex AI-generated submissions across multiple services. In some cases, AI had misinterpreted legislation and it was not always clear what redress the complainant was seeking." — p.8, [Minutes of 12 May 2026, min 26/083, Patient Experience Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Consultant and SAS doctor strike ballots could see three grades of doctor taking industrial action simultaneously in July 2026.
[Evidence] "The Director of People confirmed that both groups of doctors could potentially be involved in industrial action simultaneously in July 2026." (re British Medical Association ballots for consultants and SAS doctors overlapping with resident doctors) — p.10, [Minutes of 12 May 2026, min 26/086]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's over-60-day inpatient length-of-stay is worsening and sits well below the national median for non-acute trusts.
[Evidence] "Percentage of inpatients with over 60-day length of stay with a worsening position. Ranked 37th out of 47 trusts. Our score 30.25% against a median of 24.52%." — p.179, [True North Performance Scorecard Month 2]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's anti-racism programme is entering "consolidation and review", with workstreams moving into business-as-usual and options set out for how the taskforce should "evolve next".
[Evidence] "At this stage, the programme is entering a phase of consolidation and review. Some workstreams have achieved their original remit… and are now operating effectively within business-as-usual (BAU) arrangements." — p.240, [Anti-Racism Strategy: Progress, Delivery and Programme Review]
