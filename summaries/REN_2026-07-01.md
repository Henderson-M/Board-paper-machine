## Top lines

[LEAD] Clatterbridge is proposing to apply for Advanced Foundation Trust status, and has built its new five-year strategy around the requirements of that regime.
[Evidence] "Given the performance of the Trust in both the National Oversight Framework and the Provider Capability Assessment process it is proposed that Clatterbridge pursues and application for Advanced Foundation Trust Status." — p.26, [Chair and Chief Executive Update Report TB67]. The Board is explicitly "asked to: Discuss the intention to pursue Advanced Foundation Trust status" — p.25, [same]. The strategy's delivery statement of intent: "We will deliver this strategy with the discipline, governance and assurance expected of an Advanced Foundation Trust" — p.253, [Trust Strategy 2026–2031 TB76].

[LEAD] The trust's plan to join the University Hospitals of Liverpool Group has been dropped, disrupting delivery of its "great place to work" objective.
[Evidence] "Plans to join the UHLG are no longer going ahead however the Trust has continued the strategy as it encompasses the work required to deliver progress..." and "'Being a great place to work' was the objective most impacted by the decision not to join UHLG as the majority of actions were related to supporting staff during the transition." — p.14, [Draft minutes 3 June 2026, item 66–67].

[LEAD] The trust is a "significant outlier" on antibiotic prescribing versus comparator trusts, and has added it to the risk register with a standing quarterly report.
[Evidence] "the Trust is a significant outlier in the total antibiotic prescribing defined daily doses per 1,000 admissions... Key concerns were highlighted with the total antimicrobial prescribing at the Trust being much greater than at comparator Trusts and concerns regarding incorrect data reporting." Committee "requested that this is added to the risk register. A regular report will be added as a Quality Committee agenda item until the data are understood and control is exerted." — p.35, [Quality and People Committee Assurance Report, 24 June 2026].

[LEAD] The trust's single highest-scoring risk (20) is the potential complete loss of its supranetwork cutaneous lymphoma dermatology service from June 2026, which could force patient transfers out of Cheshire and Merseyside.
[Evidence] "If C&M skin lymphoma dermatology provision is not in place to support the CCC skin lymphoma treatment service, then there is a risk that the current service will be unsustainable and require transfer out of the C&M region which may result in pathway / treatment delays and impact upon patient experience." Controls note: "The new risk is complete loss of dermatology provision from June 2026." — p.413, [Trust Key Risks Report July 2026 TB85].

[WORTH WATCHING] A "well-led review" of the board is imminent, described as an independent review of the effectiveness of the Board that will form the basis of an improvement plan.
[Evidence] "The imminent Well Led review will provide an independent review of the effectiveness of the Board and any actions will form the basis of an improvement plan." — p.384, [Committee Effectiveness 2025/26 TB82]. Board-preparedness for a CQC inspection also flagged: "The Committee agreed that work is also required to improve Board level preparedness." — p.10, [minutes item 38]. The trust's last CQC inspection was Dec 2018–Jan 2019 (rated good, "requires improvement" for well-led) — p.407, [Risk Management Strategy].

[WORTH WATCHING] Genomic testing transfers to Greater Manchester and slow turnaround at the North West Genomic Laboratory Hub are named as drivers of cancer-standard breaches.
[Evidence] Risk 401: "North West Genomic Laboratory Hub's turn-around times are not aligned with CCC's pathways and result in patients breaching 24 days at CCC and 62 overall." — p.263. Risk 715: "HODS genomic testing service transferred to Greater Manchester, with associated risk of delay in Haemato-Oncology diagnostic pathways which may affect 28 day and 62 day performance." — p.264, [Integrated Performance Report Month 2 TB77].

[WORTH WATCHING] CIP delivery is running ahead of plan on paper but £4.9m of the £8.9m programme is risk-adjusted, and the trust has put an escalation process in place from mid-June.
[Evidence] "The Trust CIP requirement for 2026/27 is £8.9m, representing 4.4% of turnover." "In total, the Trust has £5.9m of CIP schemes at various stages of implementation and as at M2, these schemes risk adjust to £4.9m (55%) using the NHSE risk adjustment methodology. The Trust... have formally put the escalation process in place from mid-June." — p.318–319, [Finance Report Month 2 TB78].

[FOI] A PwC "Rapid Financial Diagnostics Report and Key Recommendations" was considered by the Performance Committee during 2025/26 but is not summarised in the public pack — request it.
[Evidence] Listed among items the Performance Committee considered: "PriceWaterhouseCooper Rapid Financial Diagnostics Report and Key Recommendations" — p.385, [Performance Committee Annual Report 2025/26]. Request the report, its scope/terms of reference and the recommendations from the trust.

[FOI] The May 2025 minute amendment refers to a deep dive into ward 4 and 5 activity where recorded activity "did not triangulate" with the dashboard — request the deep-dive report.
[Evidence] "it was noted that activity levels recorded in the report did not triangulate with those recorded on the dashboard and it was agreed to complete a deep dive into activity on Wards 4 and 5 which will be presented to Quality and People Committee." — p.4, [minutes item 3]. Request the ward 4/5 activity deep dive presented to Quality and People Committee.
