## Top lines

[LEAD] Rotherham is £3.1m adverse to plan at month 6 with £4.3m of efficiencies undelivered, is "within enhanced regional financial oversight", and is still reporting a break-even forecast to NHSE while its own finance committee was told the run-rate points to a year-end deficit of £11.5m (£7.6m risk-adjusted).
[Evidence] "For the six months to 30 September 2026, the year-to-date Control Total deficit was £5,832k, £3,093k adverse to plan. The position reflects £4,310K efficiency under-delivery and £500K from April industrial action" — p.511, [Finance Report P22/26]; "At month 6, the Trust reported to NHSE a forecast break-even outturn, consistent with the planned position. Achievement of the forecast depends on delivering the £24.1m efficiency programme" — p.512, [Finance Report P22/26]; "At month five, the Trust reported a year-to-date deficit of £4.7 million against a planned deficit of £2.9 million... On the current run rate, the forecast year-end deficit was approximately £11.5 million, reducing to around £7.6 million after risk-adjusted turnaround benefits" — p.22, [Finance & Performance Committee Chair's Log, 23 Sept 2026]; "The Trust's placement within enhanced regional financial oversight, reflecting the high risk of non-delivery and requirements for additional reporting and oversight" — p.3, [Minutes of 2 Sept 2026 board, P107/26]; "The planned £7m revenue PDC has not yet been required but may be needed later if cash-releasing efficiencies are not delivered" — p.511, [Finance Report]. [INFERENCE] The gap between the break-even forecast submitted to NHSE and the committee's £7.6m–£11.5m run-rate figure is the story; the pack does not reconcile the two.

[LEAD] Trust has fallen to half its H1 savings target, a "new Turnaround Director" has recommended "difficult actions", and the board has been told corporate costs must fall by 20% over three years via a joint operating model with Barnsley.
[Evidence] "despite initial positive progress, the Trust has fallen behind its financial improvement plan at the end of Half One, with delivery of £4.2m savings against a target of £8.5m. To support Half Two delivery the new Turnaround Director has made a range of recommendations to the Trust. This includes potential further ideas / opportunities to explore, improvements in governance and control and a range of difficult actions that the Trust may need to take." — p.415, [Organisational Priorities 6-month update P19/26, Financial Sustainability]; actions already taken: "consolidating and reducing its bed base, reducing its pay rate for NHSP nursing, offering two Mutually Agreed Resignation Schemes (MARS), turning off insourcing into Outpatient and Theatre activity" — p.415; "the requirement to reduce corporate costs by 20% over three years, assurance was limited at this stage because the future operating model, delivery milestones, workforce implications... were not yet sufficiently developed" — p.22, [F&P Chair's Log 23 Sept]; "The Committee acknowledged progress in reducing bank, agency, insourcing and outsourcing expenditure, but noted that delivery had plateaued and that larger, more transformational actions would now be required" — pp.22–23, [F&P Chair's Log]. CEO: "we are still short in terms of delivery of savings this year against our plan (£1.8m off plan)" — p.164, [Chief Executive Report P17/26]. The Turnaround Director is not named anywhere in the pack.

[LEAD] Maternity triage is running at half the midwifery staffing the new national triage standard requires, with only around half of assessment timeframes met, labour-ward staffing failing to match acuity on 17% of assessments, and neonatal nursing below BAPM standards — all while the trust tells the board safety outcomes are stable.
[Evidence] "Maternity triage continues to operate at approximately 50% of the recommended midwifery model, with only around half of assessment and review timeframes achieved" — p.605, [Maternity & Neonatal Safety Report P24/26]; "The new Triage standards recommend 3 midwives per 12-hour shift (2 face-to-face, 1 telephone triage), this is not yet in place. Current staffing provision is approximately 1.5 midwives per 12-hour shift, requiring delivery of both face to face and telephone triage services with half the recommended workforce" — p.619; "Additional staffing support is sent on 35-36% of shifts" — p.619; "Assessments recorded at each 4-hour period show a negative staffing balance increased from 14.1% in Q1 to 17.0% in Q2, while reports of being unable to fill vacant shifts increased from 127 to 229" — pp.52–53, [Biannual Midwifery, Maternity and Neonatal Staffing Report, appendix to Quality Committee Chair's Log]; "delays in continuing induction beyond 24 hours increased from 6 to 62" — p.52; "Caesarean capacity business case: Risk on risk register – score 16... Business case for an extra lists has been deferred until November. Currently developing a second business case for an extra theatre to be built" — p.612; "neonatal establishment is not yet BAPM compliant" — p.606; "The service has been operating with a 2 WTE consultant gaps and significant sickness levels within the resident doctor's rota" — p.62. An NHSE North East & Yorkshire regional maternity assurance visit took place on 23 September 2026: "positive verbal feedback, not concerns escalated and final report expected within 6 weeks of visit" — p.644, [BAF P1].
