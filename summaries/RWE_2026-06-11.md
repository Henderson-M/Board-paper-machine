## Top lines

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire group (Kettering + Northampton) has a non-compliant 2026/27 financial plan, meaning it cannot access Deficit Support Funding and is relying instead on dividend-bearing PDC support, with cash flow and liquidity flagged as a "primary concern."
[Evidence] "Cash flow and liquidity is a primary concern. Both KGH and NGH cash balances are below plan, and the group's non-compliant 2026/27 plan means Deficit Support Funding is not available, with reliance instead on dividend-bearing PDC support. Pressure on supplier payment runs, risk to Better Payment Code compliance, and the risk that NHSE may continue to approve only part of requested borrowing support." — p.4, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Use of Resources]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire's finance committee gave only "Limited" assurance on its medium-term financial plan, recording that its 7% cost-improvement requirement is "already at the upper limit of deliverability" and that NHS England has signalled the potential for an increased savings requirement.
[Evidence] "The committee noted a verbal update on the financial plan which remained subject to further discussion with NHS England. The committee noted: • The potential for increased savings requirements • The current 7% CIP requirement is already at the upper limit of deliverability. • The need to identify clear routes to deliver additional in-year savings." (Assurance level: Limited) — p.1, [F4 UHN Finance, Investment and Performance Committee, 26 May 2026]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Leicester is carrying a £99m cost-improvement programme for 2026/27 that its own board assurance framework scores 20 (high) and calls "a material uncertainty"; the trust also flags that redundancy costs are not included in its plan assumptions.
[Evidence] Risk: "There is a risk the Trust does not deliver the annual financial plan and required cost improvement programme due to the scale and deliverability challenge of £99m CIP… leading to an in-year deficit, increased cash requirements and pressure on liquidity… increased regulatory intervention." Gap: "Redundancy costs are not included in plan assumptions, creating a potential hidden pressure." — p.21–22, [O2 UHL Board Assurance Framework, Risk 04-Finance]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Leicester reported three Never Events in April 2026, all under full review.
[Evidence] "UHL: Three Never Events in April, all subject to full review." — p.3, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Safe]. BAF echo: "Three Never Events reported in April are under active review, with learning to be captured and shared." — p.7, [O2 UHL BAF, Risk 01-Quality-1]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Leicester's cash position deteriorated by £13.8m in a single month, falling to £20.8m at the end of April.
[Evidence] "UHL: The cash position is showing deterioration, with its month-end cash position at £20.8m, a reduction of £13.8m from March, and continues to face pressure from operational disruption, workforce costs and productivity gaps." — p.4, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Use of Resources]

[LEAD] Kettering General Hospital's maternity service is under NHS England Maternity and Neonatal Improvement Support Team "Intensive" support, with the programme running from January 2026 to January 2027 and continued regulatory recovery cited as an objective.
[Evidence] "Level of support: Intensive… Support start date: January 2026 … Support end date: January 2027." Recommendation asks the board to support oversight "to sustain progress and support continued regulatory recovery." — p.8 / p.3, [H KGH MatNeoIST Summary Report May 2026]

[LEAD] Northampton General Hospital's standardised mortality (HSMR and SMR) is "above expected," which the trust attributes partly to a "residual April 2025 data error" expected to persist until May 2026.
[Evidence] "NGH: HSMR and SMR 'above expected' - multifactorial, including SDEC removal, reduced coding of complex patients, and residual April 2025 data error expected to persist until May 2026." — p.3, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Effective]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Leicester's mortality review reported three adult deaths and one neonatal death thought "more likely than not due to problems in care," with four named cases under patient safety incident investigation.
[Evidence] "3 adult deaths were thought to be more likely than not due to problems in care. There has also been one neonatal death where it was felt that different management would probably have affected outcome… Currently we will be reporting that 0.13% of deaths in 2025/26 were due to problems in care." Named cases include "Patient who was discharged from the Emergency Department with abnormal blood results and then represented in cardiac arrest" and "Patient who was given breakfast when Nil By Mouth post surgery, subsequently vomited and aspirated." — p.9, [F1 appendix, UHL Mortality and Learning from Deaths Quarterly Report]

[LEAD] University Hospitals of Leicester reported its extreme preterm birth rate has risen to statistical significance and its neonatal death rate remains more than 5% above its peer group.
[Evidence] "the main notable change was that our previously noted high rate of extreme preterm births has increased further and has now reached statistical significance and will contribute disproportionately to the overall mortality rate." "For neonatal deaths we remain more than 5% higher than our peer group." — p.7, [F1 appendix, UHL Mortality and Learning from Deaths Quarterly Report — Perinatal Mortality]

[LEAD] All three trusts missed their 2025/26 planned financial positions, the group CEO confirmed.
[Evidence] "Financial performance across both organisations improved last year compared to the previous year, although UHL, KGH and NGH did not meet their original planned positions. It is important we make more progress this year." — p.3, [D Group Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Northampton General's 2025 NHS Staff Survey scores declined across all themes — four of them statistically significant — leaving NGH below the national average for every theme except "we are safe and healthy"; Kettering sits below the national average across the board.
[Evidence] "NGH saw declines across all themes; with four being noted as statistically significant declines… NGH are below the National Average for all themes, except 'We are safe and healthy'." "When comparing the theme results to the national average KGH are below the national average." — p.2–3, [L Response to the NHS Staff Survey 2025]. NGH Staff Engagement fell -0.21 and Morale -0.22 (p.9 Appendix 3).

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire reported a notable rise in formal employee-relations cases, with 35 open cases at each of Kettering and Northampton and 21 new formal cases opened in April alone.
[Evidence] "UHN: Notable rise in formal employee relations cases, with 35 open cases at both KGH and NGH and 21 new formal cases opened in April, creating organisational risk and case-management burden." — p.6, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Well-Led]

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals of Leicester's elective and cancer position remains scored at the maximum BAF rating of 20, with the trust stating its 62-day cancer standard was "last achieved in 2014" and that loss of capacity in two large tumour sites (Breast and Head & Neck) is driving deterioration.
[Evidence] "Delivering cancer care within the defined national waiting time standards continues to be challenging, with 62 day standard last achieved in 2014… due to unexpected loss of capacity in two large tumour sites (Breast and H&N) this has directly impacted performance." — p.17, [O2 UHL BAF, Risk 02-Activity, Cancer Section]

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals of Northamptonshire's finance committee said there was "limited evidence of sufficient system wide response" to delayed discharges, which it links directly to constrained financial recovery, and flagged prolonged OPEL 4 status at Northampton.
[Evidence] "Delayed discharges remain significantly above system expectations. This is constraining bed capacity, patient flow and financial recovery. There is limited evidence of sufficient system wide response." "Prolonged OPEL 4 status, particularly at Northampton, indicating sustained system pressure." (Assurance level: Limited) — p.1, [F4 UHN Finance, Investment and Performance Committee]

[WORTH WATCHING] University Hospitals of Leicester's estate backlog needs £10m–£12m a year of capital to address, against a BAF risk that warns of "catastrophic asset failure"; Kettering is building a new maternity unit extension to mitigate RAAC concrete.
[Evidence] BAF note: "£10m-£12m/year capital investment needed to address BLM" — p.28, [O2 UHL BAF, Risk 08-Estate]. CEO: "work progressing at Kettering General Hospital (KGH) for a new maternity unit extension to mitigate the impact of RAAC concrete in the estate." — p.2, [D Group CEO's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Ambulance handover performance deteriorated sharply at Northampton in April, falling to 69.32%, with Northampton bed occupancy at 99.21%.
[Evidence] "Ambulance handover performance deteriorated in April at UHN, falling to 84.11% at KGH and 69.32% at NGH." "high bed utilisation (97.58% KGH; 99.21% NGH, 91.5% UHL)." — p.5, [Integrated Performance Report cover, Paper E, Responsive]
