## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England cut South East London ICB's allocation by £21.4m on an assumption its restructure was complete by 1 April — which the ICB says is untrue.
[Evidence] "Breakeven against its management costs allocation. YTD underspends (£1,680k) are being used to offset the allocation reduction (£21,400k) due to the national assumption that staff restructures arising from the Change Programme were implemented by 1st April 2026 which is not true for the ICB." — p.432, [Month 2 ICB Financial Update, Item 10]. Also p.431: "Any YTD underspends against running cost and programme corporate budgets are being used to mitigate the reduction to the ICB's overall allocation (£21,400k) in respect of the Change Programme."

[LEAD] Carnall Farrar is running an Acute Service Review across south east London's three acute trusts, and the acute provider collaborative has only been able to set an *interim* budget and workplan until it reports.
[Evidence] "The APC Executive approved a revised funding allocation and interim workplan for the APC, pending the outcome of the Carnall Farrar review." — p.325, [Overall Report of the ICB Committees, Appendix 7 — Acute Provider Collaborative, Item 8]. Same page: "The SSG lead for this work and the Carnall Farrar team have continued to attend a number of EAG and subgroup meetings as this work has progressed, with governance structure for the Acute Service Review linking to the APC governance structure at multiple points".

[LEAD] The two highest-scoring risks on the ICB's entire board assurance framework are both about its own change programme — scoring 20 against a tolerance of 15.
[Evidence] "601 ICB Change Programme – workforce capacity risks … 15 … 20" and "602 ICB Change Programme – impact on staff … 20", key controls for 602 being "Weekly CEO-led all staff briefings in place, dedicated intranet and MS Teams space for transparent communication, regular 'ask HR' sessions in place." — p.270, [BAF review, Item 7]. No other BAF risk scores above 16.

[LEAD] The ICB is transferring its estates function to Oxleas and Guy's and St Thomas', and has been advised to proceed to COSoP — the formal staff-transfer stage.
[Evidence] "the SEL Transition Committee reviewed the transfer of the estates function (to Oxleas and GSTT) and recommended it proceed to COSoP (Cabinet Office Statement of Practice guidelines for transfer of employees from one public sector employer to another). They also reviewed an early draft of the proposal to transfer the Lewisham Neighbourhood Development Team to Lewisham and Greenwich Trust (as the Integrator partnership host)." — p.406, [SEL and SWL ICB Joint Transition Committee Chair's Report]. Functions earmarked for transfer are "Estates, HR, Medicines Management and Digital" — p.9, [Minutes of 8 April 2026, Item 3.01].

[LEAD] NHS England has named South East London an outlier on continuing healthcare appeals, with half of the ICB's decisions overturned on independent review.
[Evidence] "NHS England has highlighted SEL as an outlier for the number of Independent Review Panel decisions that have been overturned as the ICB had 50% of CHC decisions overturned by the NHSE led Independent Review Panel Process. The AACC team is developing an improvement plan following the review of the recommendations of all 2025/26 cases." — p.446, [Quality and Nursing, Item 11]. The ICB's internal auditors separately gave "a partial assurance opinion on a deep dive into the CHC appeals process" — p.296, [Overall Committees Report, Audit & Risk Committee 23 April 2026].

[LEAD] The ICB's system-wide Never Event count for the year was revised up to 20 after it emerged the ICB had not been seeing one provider's Never Events at all.
[Evidence] "There have been 20 Never Events reported across the system year to date. This number has changed as a data quality issue was identified whereby the ICB did not have full oversight of a provider's Never Events, resulting in an incorrect reported total. This has now been reviewed and corrected." — p.441, [Quality and Nursing, Item 11]. The provider is not named.

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB's 2025/26 audited annual report and accounts are now public: unqualified opinion, a £117k surplus on c.£5.9bn spend, and a £9.1m running-cost underspend driven by how £12.6m of redundancy money was classified.
[Evidence] "The ICB received an unqualified opinion on the annual accounts from its external auditors… An unqualified opinion on the financial statements… Nothing to report in respect of the ICB's arrangements for securing economy, efficiency and effectiveness in its use of resources." — p.238, [2025/26 ICB Annual Report and Accounts, Item 5]. "a surplus of £117k against its overall Revenue Resource Limit… an underspend against its Running Cost allocation (RCA) of £9.1m. This is primarily due to the full allocation (£12.6m) received in respect of redundancy costs being badged as RCA whereas some of the expenditure has been classified as programme costs… compliance with the Mental Health Investment Standard (£12.8m above requirement)" — p.239. One unadjusted misstatement: "the actuals when received differed by £2.1m (an overstatement). Management agreed with the audit team not to adjust… given total ICB expenditure was circa £5.9bn." — p.238.

[WORTH WATCHING] A governance overhaul going to the board removes all six place executive leads from ICB board membership and cuts the quorum, formalising the SEL/SWL cluster under a jointly appointed chair, CEO and executive team.
[Evidence] "Removing the six Place Executive Leads as Ordinary members; Add a Chief Commissioning Officer as Ordinary member" and "Amend quoracy requirements to require at least three executive directors (from five previously)… Amend quoracy requirements to require at least one partner members (from three previously)" — p.30–31, [ICB Governance Framework, Item 3, Appendix 1]. "Whilst place no longer forms part of the formal committee structure of the ICB, it remains a fundamental organising principle for delivery" — p.24. "the organisation is operating with significantly reduced management capacity in response to national expectations regarding running costs, and a new leadership model with a jointly appointed Chair, CEO and executive team… as new management structures are recruited to and postholders in place between July and December 2026" — p.22–23. Effective 1 October 2026, subject to NHSE approval of constitutional changes.

[WORTH WATCHING] Bromley's community services have gone to the incumbent, Bromley Healthcare CIC, on a five-year direct award worth an unstated sum — with several services quietly dropped from the mobilisation plan.
[Evidence] "This paper confirms that the Bromley Community Services Contract mobilisation plan is meeting milestones… This is the one of conditions precedent for the PSR Direct Award C to the incumbent Provider (Bromley Healthcare CIC (BHC)) with service to commence of 1st December 2026, for a period of 5 years and with the option for a 2-year contract extension." — p.227, [Bromley Community Services Contract Award, Item 4]. "This decision followed a review of the PSR options with advice from NEL Commercial Services (Procurement Hub) and legal advice from Capsticks." — p.228. "As of 3rd June, 51% of mobilisation actions have been completed." Children and Young People's Occupational Therapy actions are all marked "NO LONGER A REQUIREMENT" — p.233. No contract value is disclosed in the paper.

[WORTH WATCHING] An unnamed south east London trust has capped maternity bookings because of demand and a booking/scan backlog.
[Evidence] "A trust within SEL decided to put in place a temporary cap on maternity bookings. The measure was introduced due to increasing demand across the service and a backlog in booking and scan availability… To date the plan is showing positive results with bookings by 10 weeks of pregnancy now at 54% compared to 20+% just months ago." — p.447, [Quality and Nursing, Item 11]. A related item notes "an increase in London-wide capping of out-of-area maternity referrals" — p.296.

[WORTH WATCHING] SEL acute trusts were diagnostic outliers against London and national performance at the start of 2025/26 and, on the trusts' own aggregate figures, still are.
[Evidence] "Published data shows that at the beginning of 2025/26, SEL acute trusts (aggregate) and SEL ICB (registered population) were outliers on diagnostic performance when compared with London and the national performance picture… Data for April 26, however, shows that the gap has since been closed, with performance for SEL ICB patients now in line with the London and national picture. Aggregate SEL trusts while significantly improved are still above." — p.488, [Diagnostics in South East London, Item 13]. "The western half of the region lacks localised diagnostic capacity, creating a clear geographic inequality." — p.490; GSTT is proposing a Lambeth/Southwark CDC delivering "100,000 additional diagnostic appointments each year" — p.491.

## Other datapoints worth holding

- Leadership: Dinah McLannahan appointed Chief Finance and Compliance Officer across both SEL and SWL ICBs; Ed Waller joins as Chief Commissioning Officer and Deputy CEO across both, from Kent and Medway ICB, in early September; Elaine Clancy starts as Chief Nurse and Quality Officer across both on 7 September; Mike Parkinson replaces Peter Matthew as non-executive member. Departures: Diane Jones (interim chief nurse) and Mike Fox (CFO). — p.242, [CEO's Report]
- Mental health is the ICB's main in-year pressure: "The YTD position for Mental Health services is an overall overspend of £974k… ADHD and ASD assessments continue to be a significant financial pressure for all boroughs." — p.432, [Month 2 Financial Update]. Six of the 14 BAF risks — one per borough — are ADHD/autism waiting times, all scoring 16 — p.273.
- Performance: 3,179 patients waiting more than 52 weeks at SEL trusts (p.417); 65-week waits down to 64 in March from "a peak of 792 in August 2025" (p.412); A&E four-hour performance 76.1% across the three acute trusts in April (p.415); G&A bed occupancy "98% at KCH, 97% at LGT, 88% at GSTT" (p.415); mental health out-of-area placements ended the year at 10 against a trajectory of 4 (p.413).
- Infection control: "There were 51 methicillin-resistant staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) bacteraemia cases across all settings against a threshold of zero." — p.449, [Quality and Nursing]
- Learning disability inpatients: "The end of year position was 51 adult inpatients against a target of 47", CYP seven against a target of five, and "One of the three discharges expected of people in hospital over ten years was achieved during March." — p.449–450
- Greenwich general practice: "Six of 29 Greenwich practices are CQC-rated Requires Improvement. One practice is under caretaker management following GP partnership registration cancellation in March 2026. Wider pressures include closures, retirements, contract changes, investigations, estates issues and two Serious Incidents." — p.252, [CEO's Report]
- Staff survey: "Although ICBs are not required to national staff survey, SE ICB has participated, pausing participation during the management programme, replacing it with quarterly pulse surveys." — p.244, [CEO's Report, Lord Mann Report gap analysis]
- Hospices: joint SMT "Discussed an update on Hospice care in SEL and SWL noting concerns about financial sustainability across providers and mitigating actions." — p.298, [Overall Committees Report]
- QEH Woolwich urology: joint SMT "Discussed a paper on the urology service at Queen Elizabeth's Hospital in Woolwich outlining extensive system wide enagagement on clinical, workforce and performance pressures associated with the service and agreement of mitigating actions." — p.301, [Overall Committees Report]
- Palantir: a public question at the April meeting asked about the Federated Data Platform and a "Break clause in February 2027"; the ICB replied "The ICB was not using this particular service, and providers were responsible for making their own decision." — p.16, [Minutes of 8 April 2026, Item 13]
- Running-cost context stated in the pack: "in March 2025 all ICBs had been asked to bring their running costs to within £19 per head of population. Subsequently south east London and South west London ICBs had taken the decision to form a cluster" — p.9, [Minutes of 8 April 2026, Item 3.01]
