# Hull University Teaching Hospitals (HUTH) + Northern Lincolnshire & Goole (NLAG) — Trust Boards-in-Common — 11 June 2026

- **Source:** https://www.nlg.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Board-papers-June-2026.pdf (Humber Health Partnership joint board)
- **Pack:** single combined PDF, 521 pages
- **Analysed:** 2026-06-05 (full read-through of all 521 pages completed)
- **Note on group:** HUTH and NLAG run a single "Boards-in-Common" as the Humber Health Partnership (HHP). Both trusts are in NHSE's Intensive Recovery Programme ("special measures"). Lyn Simpson is substantive Group CEO; Alan Downey is Group Chair (in post since ~April 2026).

## Top lines

[LEAD] HUTH is the bottom-ranked acute trust in England — 134th of 134 — on NHS England's National Oversight Framework.
[Evidence] Q3 2025/26 NOF: HUTH average score 3.23, **rank 134 out of 134** (down from 130th the previous quarter), Segment 4; "Access to services", "Finance and productivity" and "Patient safety" domains all rated "4 – Low performing." League table names the next-worst as Nottingham University Hospitals (132nd) and York & Scarborough (131st). NLAG ranks 114th of 134, Segment 4, with "Patient safety" and "People and workforce" both "4 – Low performing." — Integrated Performance Report [BIC(26)151], National Oversight Framework (pp.6–8) & Trust League Tables (p.42)

[LEAD] Consultancy Carnall Farrar brought in to review whether the HUTH/NLAG group model should be broken up or restructured, reporting summer 2026.
[Evidence] "The Partnership has commenced the next phase of improvement and organisational review work, supported by NHS England (NHSE) through the Intensive Recovery Programme (IRP) and an independent review by Carnall Farrar (CF). This work is expected to conclude over the summer of 2026." … "a number of structural challenges remain. The next phase will therefore look at whether the current model provides the right basis for long-term improvement, stronger accountability and sustainable services." — Group Chief Executive Update [BIC(26)132], §2

[LEAD] Partnership forecasting a £40.2m year-end deficit at Month 1, with its savings programme already £46.4m off track — and a "straight-line" run-rate pointing to a far larger gap.
[Evidence] "Month 1 adverse out turn of £2.2m (Industrial Action impact is £1.3m across the Group). YE projection £40.2m adverse … CIP month 1 £4.6m adverse … CIP Year end forecast £46.4m adverse." Assurance: "Not assured." — Performance, Estates & Finance Committees-in-Common escalation report, mtg 2 June 2026 [BIC(26)137], p.3–4. The underlying picture is larger: the Finance Report sets a plan-compliant £63.1m planned deficit but an adjusted straight-line position of **£138.7m with a ~£40.2m unidentified gap**, an **underlying deficit of £116.2m** (HUTH £67.3m / NLAG £48.9m), a £119.1m (6.8%) efficiency target of which only £2.7m was delivered at M1 (39% unidentified), and external revenue cash support requested of £6.35m (May) + £7.98m (June). YTD variance to plan worsens to **−£36.5m** by year-end on the oversight scorecard; CIP delivery YTD down to 74.9%. — Finance Report M1 [BIC(26)144] / IPR oversight scorecard [BIC(26)151]

[LEAD] Trusts in breach of their provider licence and rated "Red" for capability by NHS England, following a February 2026 enforcement letter.
[Evidence] Assurance gaps listed as "Non compliance with provider licence condition NHS2.2, NHS2.5 (a to g), NHS2.6 (a to f)" and "Capability Rating of Red"; "NHSE Provider Board Capability assessment" and "Provider licence compliance" both rated **Minimal**. Action 79 "Enforcement actions — In response to Feb-26 letter an action plan is being produced that will also incorporate the recommendations of the ValueCircle review." — Patients-Access BAF slides [BIC(26)149] (also within [BIC(26)137])

[LEAD] Internal auditors downgraded their year-end opinion, giving "amber-red" assurance on the trusts' governance and risk management.
[Evidence] "the Committees had received a Draft Head of Internal Audit Opinion, which differed from the previously reported position. The revised conclusion identified four areas relating to governance, risk management, financial reporting, and data capture … the 'amber-red' ratings for governance and risk management were anticipated … a number of audit reports remain outstanding." — May 2026 minutes [BIC(26)129], §5.1

[LEAD] HUTH/NLAG had the joint 12th-highest number of Never Events of any NHS provider in England in 2025/26.
[Evidence] "The latest published data for 2025/26 shows that HUTH/NLAG has the joint 12th highest number of Never Events from all NHS providers in England." Five Never Events at each trust in the rolling 12 months to end-April 2026; HUTH's latest (Feb 2026) "relating to a procedure in 2023 where a guidewire was retained." — Monthly Patient Safety Report April 2026 [BIC(26)134], §5 / Quality IPR Never Events slide [BIC(26)151]. Other recent cases include a wrong intraocular lens implanted in cataract surgery (June 2025) and an insulin overdose (Aug 2025).

[LEAD] Coroner issues Prevention of Future Death report after a patient developed sepsis following flawed triage in Hull Royal Infirmary's A&E.
[Evidence] "There has been one Regulation 28 Letters issued in respect of the initial triage of a patient in the emergency department at HRI, who presented with an infection and developed sepsis. The concern relates to the need for a single validated triage assessment tool to be universally used and the need for staff to be appropriately trained to use it." — Patient Safety Report [BIC(26)134], §6. Two further fatal sepsis cases detailed: a young woman (sepsis/pancolitis, died Nov 2024 — "ED overcrowding, staffing pressures, IT documentation failures") and an 83-year-old woman (urosepsis, died 2 Nov 2024) — §8.

[LEAD] HUTH sits in the worst quartile of all English trusts for the volume of staff whistleblowing concerns, and more staff report suffering detriment for speaking up.
[Evidence] "Model Hospital … 12-month rolling average (data to Q3 2025/2026) shows HUTH in the highest 25% (quartile 4) compared to all other Trusts in England." 307 concerns in 2025/26 (record). "There was an increase from 5 (2024/2025) to 11 individuals reporting they had received negative treatment or detriment as a result of speaking up." All four staff-survey speaking-up questions below national and regional averages. — Freedom to Speak Up Guardian Annual Report [BIC(26)139]

[LEAD] HUTH maternity exits the national Maternity Safety Support Programme and has its Section 31 conditions lifted, three years after being rated "Inadequate".
[Evidence] "Following the Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspection in March 2023, maternity services at HUTH were rated Inadequate and Section 31 conditions were imposed … Since the last staffing report HUTH has exited the Maternity Safety Support Programme and the conditions of the Section 31 have been removed. A full CQC inspection is anticipated soon." — Bi-Annual Maternity & Neonatal Staffing Report [BIC(26)136]

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts sit in the worst national quartile for C. difficile, and HUTH's nurse staffing levels are in the lowest quartile in England.
[Evidence] On the NOF "Safe" domain, C. difficile is banded red / Quartile 4 (worst) at both trusts (HUTH score 3.85, YTD 16 cases vs 61 target; NLAG 3.77). On Care Hours per Patient Day, "HUTH is in the lowest quartile nationally" (≈7.4–7.6 vs a 9.0 default target; NLAG ≈9.0–10.0). — Quality IPR Infection Control & Care Hours per Patient Day slides [BIC(26)151]

[WORTH WATCHING] Septicaemia deaths running higher than expected at both trusts — escalated as a 2026/27 quality priority.
[Evidence] "For the conditions for which SHMI is calculated by NHS Digital — HUTH is identified as having a higher than expected SHMI for: Septicaemia" (HUTH SHMI 1.0268; NLAG 0.9989, also flagged for septicaemia). "Septicaemia is a Quality Priority for the group and remains an area of focus through the Mortality Improvement Group." — Quality IPR Mortality (SHMI) slide [BIC(26)151]

[WORTH WATCHING] An overdue national patient-safety alert at HUTH on bed rails carrying a "risk of death from entrapment or falls".
[Evidence] "Overdue – 1 alert: Medical beds trolleys bed grab handles and lateral turning devices: risk of death from entrapment or falls. Closure report and evidence collation has 2 actions that lack evidence for: Action 3 – Inventory/database of assets (incomplete ~90% capture on Tagnos system) and Action 4 – subcontracted maintenance process oversight. Escalation for resolution with Estates and Clinical Engineering." — Quality IPR Patient Safety Alerts slide [BIC(26)151]

[WORTH WATCHING] HUTH still treating patients in corridors — board told it is "on plan to eradicate by end July".
[Evidence] "Corridor care on plan to eradicate by end July" listed under HUTH's assurance summary; "Positive improvements in UEC: HUTH, Ambulance waits, corridor care, 12 hour waits … sustained reduction for the past 4 weeks in 12 hour waits, corridor care." — Integrated Performance Report [BIC(26)151], HUTH Assurance Summary (p.16)

[WORTH WATCHING] Roughly £60k of Guardian of Safe Working fines run up in a single department — paediatric surgery — over rota gaps.
[Evidence] Paediatric surgical department incurred ~£60,000 of Guardian of Safe Working fines during 2025/26 through exception/treatment reports linked to resident-doctor on-call arrangements; a business case for an additional post was discussed. Gender pay gap reported at 14.4% median (HUTH) / 21.25% (NLAG) with a 67% bonus gap at HUTH. — Workforce, Education & Culture Committees-in-Common minutes (March 2026)

[WORTH WATCHING] A cluster of neonatal deaths at HUTH is being investigated, with neonatal cot availability downgraded on the risk register.
[Evidence] "There was an increase in neonatal deaths reported during this period. We were able to identify themes of: Level 3 NICU accepting regional intrauterine/ex-utero transfers [and] Extreme prematurity <26 weeks." "Neonatal death cluster (escalated by neonates) … Neonatal unit cot availability downgraded to moderate on the risk register." — Maternity Q4 Thematic Review & PMRT papers [BIC(26)136]. High-value maternity claims linked to delayed intervention put at £90.06m.

[WORTH WATCHING] Cleanliness and estate condition deteriorating across both trusts — board told it needs a "broader discussion".
[Evidence] "HUTH has deteriorated in all domains except for food and hydration scores. NLAG has deteriorated in all domains except for food hydration scores. The CIC expressed concern regarding the cleanliness and deteriorating estate scores." Assurance: "Minimal assurance was given and a broader discussion with Board was required." "Investment required due to the aging estate – to be escalated to the Board." — Quality & Safety CiC escalation report, 28 May 2026 [BIC(26)143]

[WORTH WATCHING] Group restructuring its boards — moving toward a unitary/Group board model and new "Group NED" roles, with remuneration changes.
[Evidence] "in the last couple of weeks I have reached agreement to the appointment of what will effectively be Group NEDs … we will shortly commence recruitment of an additional Group NED and three associate Group NEDs." — Group Chair's Update [BIC(26)131]. Constitutional amendments on Group NED remuneration taken in private (May minutes §5.2).

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff perceive the trusts as prioritising finance over patient care; BME staff report worse treatment; engagement scores falling.
[Evidence] "staff from minority groups continue to report fewer opportunities compared to others, and higher levels of bullying and harassment when compared to White staff." "the perception within the survey that there was a greater organisational focus on finance than on patient care and staff welfare." Staff-survey engagement theme scores have fallen (HUTH ~6.0 from 6.4; NLAG ~5.9 from 6.4) and only ~47–48% would recommend the trust as a place to be treated; consultant vacancy rate 16.6% against a 10% target. — May 2026 minutes [BIC(26)129], §4.1 / People report [BIC(26)138] / Quality IPR People & Workforce [BIC(26)151]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board discussing complaints apparently written by AI — and whether the trust is itself using AI to respond.
[Evidence] "consideration was also being given to whether complaints had been received that had been created by artificial intelligence (AI), with some responses appearing to have been generated in this way." "whether the use of AI in responding to complainants should be subject to further consideration, particularly in relation to patient confidentiality." — May 2026 minutes [BIC(26)129], §3.1

[WORTH WATCHING] Tower block fire-safety enforcement notice will be missed; extension to be sought.
[Evidence] "the Tower Block enforcement notice was due to expire on 16 July 2026 … although a 12-month period had been available to complete the required works, this would not be achieved within the timeframe. An extension would therefore be requested 28 days prior to expiry … work continued in collaboration with the Fire Service." — May 2026 minutes [BIC(26)129], §4.2
