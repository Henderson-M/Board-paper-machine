## Top lines

[LEAD] Thames Valley ICB signs off a winter board assurance statement rating itself "5 - Full assurance" overall despite answering "No" to the national requirement that its system co-ordination centre run 08:00-18:00 seven days a week.
[Evidence] Checklist item 8.2, "The Board is assured that SCCs will be fully operational 08:00 to 18:00 7 days per week and have the ability to extend hours of operation during periods of extreme pressure", answered "No", with the comment: "Thames Valley ICB SCC operates Mon-Fri 0900-1700 only with on-call arrangements in place outside of these hours. Additional SCC support can be stood up if required" – p.63, [Winter Planning 26/27 Board Assurance Statement]. The dashboard on the next page records "Overall Assessment Rating: 5 - Full assurance", with every Section B domain rated 5 and governance rated "4 - Substantial assurance" – p.64.

[LEAD] The ICB's reported month 4 break-even depends on reserves it says it "would not normally be entitled to access", and which it told NHS England it needed as a condition of submitting a balanced plan at all.
[Evidence] "£8.6m of reserves having been committed YTD, with a remaining £23m of reserves available. However, if the current expenditure run rate is not addressed then it is likely that these reserves will be insufficient to deliver a break-even position at year end. The reserves available include prior year benefits and unutilised specialist commissioning and other reserves, which the ICB would not normally be entitled to access, but the need for this was notified to NHSE as a pre-requisite condition for the submission of a balanced plan for the year" – p.22, [Chief Executive and Chief Officers' Report, Chief Finance Officer section]. The same report says the month 4 position "contains some very material risks" – p.22.

[LEAD] Two neighbouring ICBs have lodged a formal claim on Thames Valley's allocation under the Frimley break-up agreement, and the board is being asked to consider it.
[Evidence] "Additionally, HIOW and Surrey and Sussex ICBs have submitted a claim under the terms of the South Frimley transfer MOU for the transfer of a material allocation to them from TV where they consider that their national allocation does not cover costs of services to the population for which they are responsible. This matter will be considered by the Board" – p.22, [Chief Executive and Chief Officers' Report].

[LEAD] NHS England's annual assessment letter, published in the pack, tells Thames Valley it carries an underlying deficit and is funded 1.85 per cent below its fair shares target.
[Evidence] "Thames Valley ICB also has an underlying deficit which presents a recurrent financial risk requiring sustained action and reduced reliance on non-recurrent mitigations. The ICB's 2026/27 recurrent allocation is 1.85% below fair shares target reinforcing the need for contract management grip, and a close focus on efficiency delivery and productivity" – p.19, [Annual assessment of BOB and Frimley ICBs' performance in 2025/26, David Radbourne, NHS England South East, 13 July 2026]. The letter also flags "some issues with contract agreements linked to the disaggregation of Frimley ICB, which need to be resolved, and with the payment of invoices (including those relating to CHC) following the ISFE2 implementation and ICB merger", and that the BOB and Frimley cluster "did not develop a single five-year plan narrative as part of the planning process" – pp.19-20.

[LEAD] The ICB's unpaid invoice backlog stood at 13,175 invoices, with agency staff drafted in and small suppliers invited to declare cashflow distress.
[Evidence] "ISFE2 invoice backlog – Whilst still at unacceptably high levels, the total number of unpaid invoices was now down to 13175. Four WTE agency staff had been deployed to support rapid reduction of the backlog. The CFO confirmed that a notice to small businesses had been circulated asking them to contact the ICB directly if they were experiencing financial issues" – p.87, [Finance and Performance Committee AAA report, meetings 25 June and 28 July 2026].

[LEAD] Financial sustainability is the only principal risk on the board assurance framework scored outside the ICB's own risk appetite, at 20 against a threshold of 12, with the board's own text warning external intervention is the likely consequence of failure.
[Evidence] "If the ICB fails to deliver in-year financial balance and recurrent financial sustainability then it is likely that external intervention will ensue. That intervention is likely to adopt a short-termist approach to financial balance which places at risk the ICB's strategic objectives." Current risk score 20, appetite "OPEN / 12", status "OUT" – p.102, [Board Assurance Framework, BAF REF 2A].

[LEAD] The audit committee raised "urgent concerns" about cyber security, naming outdated systems across the GP estate as a critical risk, and the BAF records that the ICB's cyber capability sits with a single post-holder.
[Evidence] "The Committee raised urgent concerns about cyber security, noting a partial assurance rating, two high-priority findings, delays in audit organisation, and insufficient assurance over the past year. Members highlighted the critical risk posed by outdated systems across the GP estate and weaknesses in third-party access controls" – p.85, [Audit Committee AAA report, 21 July 2026]. The BAF gap "Specialist cyber knowledge and delivery capacity are concentrated in a single ICB role" carries the update: "The Executive assurance report identifies a material key-person and capacity dependency, with no established access to dedicated specialist cyber support" – p.100, [BAF REF 1B].

[LEAD] RSM gave the ICB's cost improvement programme internal audit a "partial assurance" rating, with non-executives challenging whether management actions are in hand.
[Evidence] "The Non-Executive Members present raised a challenge regarding the recent CIP Internal Audit that had resulted in a rating of 'partial assurance' by RSM. Clarification is being sought as to whether the identified management actions are in hand. Early signs are appearing that AACCC and S117 CIP delivery is under pressure" – p.87, [Finance and Performance Committee AAA report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The board is asked to approve a 12-month system-wide review of maternity and neonatal services across all Thames Valley providers, prompted in part by concerns about Oxford University Hospitals.
[Evidence] "Locally, concerns raised by women and families regarding maternity services at Oxford University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust have further reinforced the importance of understanding the quality, safety and sustainability of services across Thames Valley" – p.78, [Thames Valley Maternity and Neonatal Services Review]. The review runs three phases over 12 months (mobilisation 3 months, evidence gathering 6 months, final report 3 months) – p.84. Its stated exclusions include that it will not "Make decisions regarding future service reconfiguration" or "Replace statutory public consultation processes should future changes be proposed at a later stage" – pp.82-83. The commissioning intentions "set out the ambition for a Thames Valley Clinical Services Review within which maternity services were identified as a priority area" – p.80.

[WORTH WATCHING] The quality, performance and finance committee has asked for further work on ED attendances and patient deaths among people on neurology waiting lists.
[Evidence] "Concerns were raised around neurology performance and impact on patient safety with further work required to reported ED attendance and patient deaths whilst on the waiting lists. It was noted that there is a national problem with a significant shortage of neurologists" – p.89, [Quality, Performance and Finance Committee AAA report, 27 August 2026]. The same report flags that "ADHD waiting lists also remained high".

[WORTH WATCHING] Two of the ICB's four places have named the ICB's own restructure, and specifically the removal of place-based urgent and emergency care teams, as a winter risk.
[Evidence] Berkshire West risk table lists "Removal of place-based UEC teams under the new Model ICB", owner "ICB", mitigation "Discussions under way with NHS England and providers on the ICB's approach to winter oversight and resilience" – p.47, [2026/27 Winter Plan]. Buckinghamshire lists "The ICB's own organisational consultation, which will see UEC place-based teams no longer part of the organisational structure, with changes likely during the summer" – p.46. East Berkshire lists "organisational transition and finance – unclear accountability or slow authorisation during the ICB's own restructuring" – p.48.

[WORTH WATCHING] Cost improvement delivery is behind plan and deteriorating, with mental health savings at less than a tenth of plan at month 4.
[Evidence] "At Month 4, the programme has delivered £19.2m of savings against a planned £21.7m, resulting in a £2.5m adverse variance to plan, representing a deterioration of £0.4m from the £2.1m adverse variance reported at Month 3." Principal shortfalls: "Mental Health, with savings of £0.2m delivered against a plan of £2.3m, an adverse variance of £2.1m"; "All-age Continuing Care, delivering £2.6m against a plan of £4.9m"; "Community Healthcare, where no savings have been delivered against a planned £0.6m" – p.36, [Finance Report]. Total 2026/27 efficiency requirement is £162m, comprising "£65m of ICB CIP savings, £69m of 2% Contract Efficiency requirements, and £28m of Acute Avoidance, Left Shift and Contract Lever efficiencies" – p.41.

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB's restructure has left 45 staff at risk, with a second voluntary redundancy round running and 214 staff transferred out to Frimley Health Foundation Trust on 1 September.
[Evidence] "To date, 502 colleagues from a baseline of 725 have received clarity on their future position, including 214 colleagues transferring to Frimley Health Foundation Trust (FHFT) on 1st September, 243 colleagues being matched into roles and 45 colleagues identified as being at risk. The second voluntary redundancy (VR) process is ongoing, with 42 applications currently being progressed" – p.25, [Chief Executive and Chief Officers' Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The commissioning and population health committee warns the transformation money available, about 1 per cent of system funding, will not be enough, and the ICB is exploring non-NHS investment.
[Evidence] "the likely scale of investment required to support prevention, neighbourhood health and wider system transformation is likely to exceed the existing NHS funding source of 1%. Exploratory work is therefore underway to assess the feasibility of attracting additional non-NHS investment to support long-term strategic commissioning ambitions while simultaneously trying to ascertain what floor of investment can be funded by the NHS budget over the medium term" – p.90, [Commissioning and Population Health Committee AAA report, 1 September 2026]. The CFO separately reports "a headline figure of £59m to be allocated to support preventative, proactive and neighbourhood care models", adding that this will require the ICB "to decommission or reduce the breadth of provision in order to release resource for investment in transformation" – p.24.
