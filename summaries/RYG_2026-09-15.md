## Top lines

[LEAD] Deloitte forced the trust to restate its 2025/26 accounts from a £3.9m adjusted surplus to a £2.9m adjusted deficit after ruling that a £6.0m "saving" booked as a cost improvement was an invalid release of a previously recognised liability.
[Evidence] "Following a review of the requirements of IFRS 9 and a determination that the conditions for de-recognition had not been met, the Trust now reports a deficit for the year of £4.8m against a planned surplus of £2.1m. The Adjusted Financial Performance is a deficit of £2.9m against a planned adjusted surplus of £2.2m. This significant adverse variance reflects the removal of the invalid non-recurrent CIP. The achieved CIP for 2025/26 is now £6.5m (53.4% of plan)" – p.144, [Auditor's Annual Report 2025/26, Enc 14]. Also: "The initial inclusion of the £6.0m liability release as a CIP was a material misstatement due to misapplication of accounting standards" – p.145. The trust had initially reported delivering "£12.5m (102.5% of plan)" of efficiencies – p.144. Audit Committee records the same movement and adds: "NHS England confirmed that the accounting adjustment would not adversely affect the Trust's deficit support arrangements or create any financial penalty in future years" – p.184, [Audit Committee Assurance Report, Enc 17].

[LEAD] The trust's 2025/26 audit opinion carries a qualification on other matters because its fair pay disclosures breached the DHSC group accounting manual, plus a second year of emphasis of matter over an unresolved PFI adjudication.
[Evidence] "A qualified opinion on 'other matters' was included due to non-compliance with DHSC requirements relating to fair pay disclosures in the Remuneration and Staff Report. An emphasis of matter was included regarding the uncertain outcome of a PFI adjudication process. This is in line with the prior year's audit opinion." – p.132, [Auditor's Annual Report cover, Enc 14]. Deloitte has still not issued its audit certificate: "We have not yet issued our audit certificate ... until we are advised by the National Audit Office that the audit of the NHS group consolidation is complete, which is expected to be in the autumn" – p.136.

[LEAD] Two patients who had been assessed as needing an inpatient bed died while waiting, both now Patient Safety Incident Investigations, and the trust has commissioned a thematic review covering up to ten such cases over a year.
[Evidence] "Two reported incidents in months 3 and 4 will be reported as Patient Safety Incident Investigations (PSII). The cases both involve the deaths of individual patients who had been assessed as requiring an inpatient bed. They will be included as part of commissioned Thematic Review regarding bed waiting, considering data and up to ten cases from over a one-year period." – p.67, [Integrated Performance Report Month 4, Enc 9]. The Quality Committee escalated the same point as an Alert: "Patient safety risks, associated with patients assessed as requiring inpatient admission but awaiting beds, require continued oversight. Thematic review commissioned" – p.50, [Quality Committee Assurance Report, Enc 6].

[LEAD] The chair's report records that clinical staff wrote to the chief executive and chair saying bed waits may be a factor in unexpected deaths, and that the trust now needs a summer surge plan for mental health.
[Evidence] "You will also be aware that some clinical staff have shared with Mary, Richard and I their concerns about the impact on patients waiting for access to a bed as a possible factor in unexpected deaths and offered some solutions to better manage this risk." – p.34, [Chair's Report, Enc 4]. The CEO's report confirms two reviews in response: "we have commenced a thematic patient safety review ... In addition, we have commissioned a comprehensive review of the adult mental health pathway, encompassing inpatient, crisis and community services" – p.40, [Chief Executive's Report, Enc 5].

[LEAD] The finance and performance committee was told on 1 September of a "significant deterioration in the underlying financial position", that virtually all balance sheet flexibility will be used up this year, and ordered a formal financial recovery plan for October.
[Evidence] "At Month 4, the Trust is reporting an overall £0.051m surplus after technical adjustments ... Whilst the reported position remains broadly in line with plan, the Committee noted a significant deterioration in the underlying financial position, with increasing pressures across prescribing, inpatient services, workforce, income delivery and CRP performance. The Committee was advised that virtually all available balance sheet flexibility is expected to be utilised during 2026/27 ... It was requested that a formal Financial Recovery Plan (FCP) from the Financial Control Board (FCB) is brought to the October FPIC meeting for consideration." – p.121-122, [FPIC Assurance Report 1 September 2026, Enc 12]. The same meeting recorded a "£3.5m gap between identified schemes and the forecast savings requirement" for 2027/28 – p.122. The month 4 finance report confirms the reported position "required unplanned release of £0.5m balance sheet flex" – p.126, [Finance Report Month 4, Enc 13].

[LEAD] The trust is breaching the bank staffing cap imposed by NHS England every month and is forecasting a full-year breach of £2.8m.
[Evidence] "Bank spend reporting above the NHS England imposed cap, (£1.0m) to date and breaching by 17%. Current forecast shows a (£2.8m) breach, 16%." – p.124, [Finance Report Month 4, Enc 13]. Also: "It should be noted that spend on bank staff is breaching the cap every month, being £1.0m above the cap by M4." – p.128.

[LEAD] The CQC rated three of the trust's service groups Requires Improvement, with breaches of nine regulations between them, in the same round that rated the trust Good for well-led; Coventry community mental health team is under a formal turnaround programme.
[Evidence] "The CQC have published their reports following the inspection of three assessment services groups; they rated the services as 'Requires Improvement' in each of the inspections." – p.98, [CQC Regulatory Report, Enc 10]. Breaches: community services for adults of working age, "breaches with four regulations" (p.98); crisis services and health-based places of safety, "breaches with three regulations" (p.99); wards for older people, "breaches of two regulations" (p.99). Quality Committee Alert: "Coventry CMHT remains an area of significant quality and performance concern and is subject to a formal turnaround programme." – p.50, [Quality Committee Assurance Report, Enc 6]. The well-led report scored 2 out of 4 ("evidence shows some shortfalls") on shared direction and culture, freedom to speak up, and governance, management and sustainability – p.100, and found "Staff from minority ethnic groups reported more adverse experiences at work, such as harassment and discrimination" and "Staff had reported negative experiences of organisational restructuring" – p.100.

[LEAD] The auditor benchmarks the trust's reliance on one-off savings at nearly double the average of the other mental health and community trusts it audits, and names two external consultancies now working on the trust's finances.
[Evidence] "The Trust has a relatively higher reliance on non-recurrent efficiencies at 55.6% planned for FY26 (before correction). This is higher than the average percentage of 30.6% noted for other mental health and community trusts we audit." – p.145, [Auditor's Annual Report, Enc 14]. Also: "At the point of submission of the plan, 45% of required savings plans were still in development. The level of required savings indicates a risk to delivery of the plan for 2026/27." – p.145. Firms named: "The Trust engaged Kingsgate, an external consultancy firm to support the development of the 2026/27 savings plan" – p.145; and "the Brooklands Financial Improvement Programme following the diagnostic work undertaken by Baxendale. Members noted progress in establishing programme governance and six workstreams designed to address the identified run-rate financial deficit." – p.122, [FPIC Assurance Report 1 September 2026, Enc 12].

[WORTH WATCHING] The chief executive says she is "disappointed" the trust is still in NHS oversight framework segment 4 for Q1 2026/27, and the board will sign off its provider capability self-assessment in private on 2 October.
[Evidence] "We are disappointed to remain in Segment 4 of the National Oversight Framework (NOF), despite early signs of recovery and the significant effort being made across the organisation to improve performance." – p.41, [Chief Executive's Report, Enc 5]. Also: "NHSE Provider Capability Assessment – we are currently rated Amber-Green by NHSE ... The Board will review its ratings in the confidential meeting in time to meet the national submission on 2 October. Indications are that final ratings will be published nationally alongside NOF Quarter 2 reports." – p.42. The auditor records the segment move: NHS Oversight Framework segment "4" in 2025/26 against "3" in 2024/25 – p.143. Underlying NOF positions include community 18-week waits at 57.92% in June, ranked 104 of 122; CYP 104-week waits at 38%, ranked 51 of 69; 72-hour follow-up ranked 49 of 52; national cost collection index 118.72%, ranked 65 of 71 – p.91-92, [IPR Appendix, Enc 9].

[WORTH WATCHING] Six acute mental health wards lost one healthcare assistant per day shift from May, and the nursing report records a threefold rise in incidents where staff could not respond to personal alarms.
[Evidence] "An initial comparison of quarter 1 2025/26 and quarter 1 2026/27 data indicates an increase in reported shortage of staff incidents, including a threefold rise in incidents where staff were unable to respond to PIT alarms, or where responses were delayed, limited or absent." – p.172, [Safer Nurse Staffing Exception Report, Enc 16]. The establishment change: "it was agreed at Financial Control Board to reduce the number of HCAs by one in the day on Beechwood, Hearsall, Larches, Swanswell, Willowvale and Westwood wards" – p.172; the impact review reports to board in November – p.168. Ward sickness: "In July, Edgewick ward's sickness levels remained high at 22.5%, as did Hearsall ward at 24.5%. Willowvale reported 20.7% sickness, Heathlands reported 23.3%, and Beechwood ward reported 24.7% sickness." – p.170. Injury absence: "There were 30 RIDDOR-reportable absences recorded in June and 25 in July ... The total number of calendar days lost to RIDDOR-reportable absences was 355 in June and 543 in July." – p.171. Trust-wide sickness was 7.38% in July, "a 0.26% increase from the June 2026 position" – p.65.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has had no substantive chief people officer since July, and its ICB is proposing to merge with Herefordshire and Worcestershire from April 2027.
[Evidence] "Geoffrey Etule has been appointed as our new Chief People Officer and will be joining us on 1 November ... Since 2020, he has served as Chief People Officer at Wye Valley NHS Trust" – p.43, [Chief Executive's Report, Enc 5]. The July minutes record the outgoing postholder: "this would be Mr Emerson's final Board meeting before taking up a national role" – p.13, and that both the chief people officer and deputy chief people officer were leaving – p.30. On the ICB: "the proposal submitted by the ICB would see Coventry and Warwickshire, Herefordshire and Worcestershire move from operating as two Boards in Common to a merged arrangement from April 2027. He noted that this structure would remain in place until at least 2028, when it would be reviewed." – p.13, [Minutes 21 July 2026, Enc 2].
