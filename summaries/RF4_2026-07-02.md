Confirmed elsewhere: "NHS Resolution had temporarily withheld claims data following instruction linked to the Amos Inquiry. [LEAD] Chief executive Matthew Trainer is leaving BHRUT to run King's College Hospital; Fiona Wheeler is interim CEO.
[Evidence] "Over the last four years, working alongside Matthew Trainer (who is becoming the Chief Executive at King's College Hospital NHS Foundation Trust)…" — p.26, [Chief Executive's Board report July 2026]. Confirmed in May minutes: "this was his final Board report as the outgoing Chief Executive, and that he was moving to King's College Hospital at the end of June… Fiona Wheeler would serve as Interim Chief Executive Officer (CEO) whilst a substantive CEO was recruited." — p.6, [Draft minutes 7 May 2026]

[LEAD] Trust two months into the year already £5m adverse to a planned £41.3m deficit, with cost-cutting programme rated red and a £17.8m hole in it.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a year to date deficit of £15.7m, which is £5.0m adverse to plan. This includes £1.3m industrial action costs… Delivery of the £41.3m plan remains dependent on closing the CIP gap and reducing the underlying run rate." — p.136/139, [Finance Report to 31 May 2026 (Month 2)]. "The overall status of the 2026/27 Trust wide programme at the end of Month 2 is BRAG rated as Red." — p.136. "The annual CIP Target for 2026/27 is £51.9m… forecast delivery is £34.2m, leaving a residual gap of £17.8m." — p.142, [Finance Report]

[LEAD] CEO tells staff the trust is overspending by around £250k a day.
[Evidence] "We are currently spending around £250k a day more than we receive, and this is £81k a day more than we planned… We need to harness that drive and apply it to our work to save £51.9m in this financial year." — p.27, [Chief Executive's Board report July 2026]

[LEAD] Two maternity negligence claims settled in Q4 at £31m and £28m, both linked to delays in delivery.
[Evidence] "Two cases were settled in Quarter 4, with settlement values of £31 million and £28 million respectively. Both cases related to delays in delivery, with the latter also identifying concerns regarding timely obstetric review and fetal monitoring." — p.41, [Maternity Services Report, item 10b]

[LEAD] Trust escalating a bid for a Level 3 neonatal intensive care unit to the board, citing rising extreme prematurity and the risks of in/ex-utero transfers.
[Evidence] "A key strategic recommendation arising from the review is the development of a Level 3 Neonatal Unit, recognising the increasing number of extremely preterm births and the risks associated with in-utero and ex-utero transfers." — p.39, [Maternity Services Report]. "Members discussed the ongoing case for Level 3 neonatal intensive care unit provision. Further clarity was required on whether the Trust's submission had been formally considered and what the associated funding route would be. It was agreed to escalate this to the Trust Board." — p.55, [Quality Assurance Committee Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust is paying just two-thirds of its supplier invoices on time and concedes a "clear deterioration" in payment performance.
[Evidence] "Month 2 performance is reported at 67% by value of invoices paid within terms… 10 of the last 12 months have fallen below the 95% target, showing a clear deterioration in underlying payment timeliness." — p.145, [Finance Report]. Current ratio reported at 0.65 (below 1) — p.146.

[WORTH WATCHING] Diagnostic (DM01) performance at 51.3%, with imaging accounting for 99% of all breaches.
[Evidence] "Provisional May data shows performance of 51.3%… Imaging performance is the main driver behind our current position, accounting for 99% of all Trust breaches, with MRI and MOUS being particular areas of concern." — p.194, [Integrated Performance Report]. RTT 67.3%, "off our submitted RTT trajectory (by 4.3%) a key driver behind our current National Oversight Framework score." — p.194.

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust-wide patient-safety backlog: 44% of learning responses overdue and duty of candour compliance at 46%.
[Evidence] "High volumes of overdue learning responses (44%), including PSIIs and AARs (with majority in Emergency and Acute Medicine), are limiting the Trust's ability to embed learning and drive improvement." — p.[Integrated Quality and Safety Report, item 12]. "Statutory DOC compliance for moderate+ harm incidents remains below target at 46%." Same report: "There are 24 PSII in progress, 7 are under investigation with MNSI."

[WORTH WATCHING] Three maternity cases referred to the national MNSI investigator in Q4, including two stillbirths where the attending midwife could not detect the fetal heart.
[Evidence] "P0 40 weeks gestation; attended maternity triage with suspected labour, on attendance, the midwife was unable to auscultate the fetal heart, and a bedside ultrasound scan subsequently confirmed an intrauterine death (IUD)." — p.38, [Maternity Services Report]. Of the three MNSI referrals, one was "Rejected; parental consent withdrawn."

## FOI leads

[FOI] Request the Independent National Maternity and Neonatal Investigation Report ("Amos Report") for BHRUT and its terms of reference.
[Evidence] Tabled at the board as item 10a "to follow after national publication date 30th June 2026"; the trust's legal representatives "formally challenged" NHS Resolution's temporary withholding of claims data "linked to the Amos Inquiry" (p.55). Request from BHRUT and/or NHS England: the full report, its scope/terms of reference, and the trust's response/action plan.

[FOI] Request details of the two Q4 maternity claims settled at £31m and £28m.
[Evidence] p.41, [Maternity Services Report]. Request from BHRUT/NHS Resolution: dates of incidents, nature of harm, and any duty-of-candour correspondence — both relate to "delays in delivery."

[FOI] Request the maternity thematic-review and PMRT documentation behind the perinatal-death programme running since June 2023.
[Evidence] "This report presents the tenth ongoing thematic review of perinatal deaths at BHRUT… as part of a programme of work initiated in June 2023 following an observed increase in perinatal mortality" (p.36); "High rates of declined postmortem examination (approximately 60%)" (p.36). Request the thematic-review reports and the Level 3 NICU business case.

[FOI] Request the maternity EPR funding correspondence after the ICB route was withdrawn.
[Evidence] "The anticipated ICB funding route for the maternity EPR was no longer available, and the Trust had been asked to pursue an alternative route. This presented a risk to delivery of Care Quality Commission recommendations." — p.55, [Quality Assurance Committee Report]. Request the correspondence and the risk it poses to CQC recommendation delivery.
