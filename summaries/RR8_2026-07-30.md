## Top lines

[LEAD] Leeds is one of only two trusts in England (with Sussex) where NHS staff will be placed under a new statutory duty of candour to co-operate with an independent maternity review — confirming a Leeds Maternity and Neonatal Independent Review is coming.
[Evidence] "A new duty of candour and assistance will apply to the independent maternity and neonatal reviews in Leeds and Sussex. This is part of the Hillsborough Law, the name commonly used for the Public Office (Accountability) Bill... NHS staff who are asked to provide evidence to the reviews would be legally required to co-operate fully" — p.3, [Maternity and Neonatal reports, Item 10.1(i)]. See also "preparations for the Leeds Maternity and Neonatal Independent Review" — p.3, [PIAC Chairs Summary, Item 10.1].

[LEAD] The trust's maternity services remain rated Inadequate and are operating outside the board's risk appetite, under a CQC Section 29A warning notice and NHSE's Maternity Safety Support Programme.
[Evidence] "The services at both hospital sites were rated as Inadequate. Neonatal services... rated services at both sites as Requires Improvement" (Jan 2025 inspection, reports June 2025) — p.2, [Perinatal Improvement Plan, Item 8.3(ii)]; "the Section 29A Warning Notice that was issued under the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014" and services "operating outside the risk appetite for the level 2 risk category (patient safety and outcomes)" — p.6, [Maternity and Neonatal reports, Item 10.1(i)].

[LEAD] A "Fundamental Review" put the trust's worst-case 2026/27 deficit at £93.7m, against a submitted breakeven plan carrying a £95m savings target; Q1 already £14m in deficit.
[Evidence] "The financial plan submitted for 2026/27 is a breakeven position and includes a waste reduction programme (WRP) of £95m. For Quarter 1, the Trust reported a year-to-date deficit of £14.0m which is £3.1m adverse to the NHSE plan... achievement of the breakeven plan in the best case, a deficit of £49m in the mid case and £93.7m deficit in the worst case" — p.26, [IQPR July 26, Item 9.2]. Drivers cited: resident doctors' industrial action in April, medical pay award funding shortfall, WRP under-delivery.

[LEAD] A Human Tissue Authority inspection of the trust's mortuaries found three major shortfalls, and the trust rates itself non-compliant on two of the Fuller Inquiry's mortuary recommendations.
[Evidence] "The HTA carried out an inspection of the mortuary at LGI and St James's in April 2026... Three major and one minor shortfall were found against standards for traceability processes and premises, facilities and equipment" — p.3, [Maternity and Neonatal reports, Item 10.1(i)]; "Of the 20 Fuller Inquiry recommendations applicable to the Trust, 11 are assessed as fully compliant, seven as partially compliant and two as non-compliant" — p.1, [Care of the Deceased, Item 8.4]. Trust also flags "significant capital investment in the mortuary estate will be required" — p.3, [Item 8.4].

[LEAD] The trust remains under monthly NHSE-chaired special oversight following a September 2025 CQC Well-led inspection, and is outside board risk appetite across workforce, regulatory, patient-safety and patient-experience risks.
[Evidence] "the Integrated Quality Improvement Group (IQIG), which is Chaired by the Regional Director, North East NHS England... monitoring progress of the Perinatal and Well-led Improvement Plans" — p.2; "the Trust is outside the risk appetite set by the Board for Workforce risk... External risk (Regulatory risk) and Clinical Risk (Patient Safety and Outcomes and Patient Experience risk)" — p.7, [Well-led Improvement Update, Item 8.3(i)]. Well-led programme: 71 actions, 1 off track; external well-led review by NHS Alliance reporting to September board.

[LEAD] Leeds has volunteered as a DHSC pilot site for a Wholly Owned Subsidiary to run income generation from innovation projects.
[Evidence] "The Board is alerted that the Trust has volunteered as a pilot site for DHSC with the current proposal anticipating confirmation of next steps by September 2026. It was noted that a key principle of the proposal is that there will be no staff transfers to the WOS" — p.2, [F&P Committee Chairs Summary, Item 14.1].

[WORTH WATCHING] More than a quarter — and at times over 70 per cent — of planned home births were suspended over the past six months because of insufficient midwifery staffing.
[Evidence] "the percentage of home births that have been suspended due to insufficient staffing has ranged between 28.57% and 71.43%" — p.7, [Perinatal Assurance Report, Item 10.2]. Same paper: midwifery "deficit of 18 WTE from the Birthrate plus recommendations and 33 WTE gap following the revised budget"; workforce paper articulates "a need for an additional 16 obstetric consultants" — p.7.

[WORTH WATCHING] Anonymous whistleblowing jumped from 2.3 per cent of Freedom to Speak Up cases to 18 per cent in a year, and every staff-survey "raising concerns" score fell.
[Evidence] "18% of concerns were raised anonymously... Nationally, the NGO report that 12%"; table shows anonymous concerns 2.3% (2023-24), 2.3% (2024-25), 18% (2025-26); "activity is planned to understand why colleagues are not comfortable revealing their identity" — p.4; staff-survey "The organisation would address any concerns that I raised" fell to 46.8% (from 53.9% in 2024) — p.8, [FTSU Guardian Annual Report, Item 11.3]. 259 cases in 2025/26.

[WORTH WATCHING] Board asked to review its tolerance of persistently high numbers of patients medically fit for discharge, and a new corporate risk for corridor care has been created.
[Evidence] "concern at the continued impact of high volumes of patients [with No Criteria to Reside] on hospital flow... the Board is requested to consider its tolerance level of the NCtR position" — p.2, [F&P Chairs Summary, Item 14.1]; "The Committee approved a new corporate risk around corridor care running alongside CRRC4" — p.3, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 7]. NOF: trust remains segment 3, score 2.48, ranking slipped to 92/134 from 88/134 — p.2, [Item 7].
