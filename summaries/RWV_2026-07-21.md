## Top lines

[LEAD] Devon Partnership's board is being asked to decline NHS England's request to adopt the IHRA working definition of antisemitism, ten days before NHSE's 31 July response deadline.
[Evidence] "Trust Board therefore fully supports the objective of preventing and responding to antisemitism, anti-Muslim hostility and all forms of discrimination and hatred… This objective is without the adoption of either definition at this time." The paper adds the board "has also taken account of legal advice publicly circulated within the NHS regarding the adoption of these definitions. This advice highlights that NHS organisations are not under a legal obligation to adopt either definition" and that "As an apolitical healthcare organisation, the Trust distinguishes opposition to discrimination from endorsement of specific definitions." NHSE recommendation 10 requires trusts to "confirm whether your organisation has adopted the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance (IHRA) definition of antisemitism and the Government definition of anti-Muslim hostility… by Friday 31 July 2026." — p.222–225, [Anti-Racism: Implementing the DPT Anti-Racism Statement… and responding to the Lord Mann Review and NHSE recommendations, Item 3.3]

[LEAD] A trust safer-staffing paper states in its formal assurance statement that inpatient care across every ward is being delivered below expected safety standards.
[Evidence] "Safe staffing is maintained at lower levels than required across all wards, with patient care delivered within lower than expected safety standards with adaptive approaches to skill mix at times of high acuity along with bank and as a last resort, agency staffing." Same paper: 19 per cent inpatient vacancy rate (134.43 FTE of 697.12 funded establishment); temporary staffing at 31 per cent against an "NHSE target: <30%"; four wards above 120 per cent establishment use including "Chichester House: 178.04%"; and on two occasions in April "a ward lacked a rostered RMN", against a "minimum one RMN per duty [which] is a legal requirement". — p.4–6/14, [Safer Staffing – Workforce Assurance Report (April 2026 Data), Item 2.3.1.2, **supplementary pack**]

[LEAD] The trust is moving to compulsory redundancy, with about 80 posts in scope, after voluntary redundancy failed to close the savings gap.
[Evidence] "The reduction in posts from the voluntary redundancy scheme generated a notable financial contribution to our cost saving efforts over a full year. While this was significant it does not, combined with our other cost improvement plans, address the scale of savings that we need to make over the next couple of years. We are, therefore, now moving into the second phase of our plans to reduce workforce costs further… It will move us away from a voluntary approach to a process that will involve formal staff consultation in due course. We will be working towards the future through a carefully considered combination of managing staff turnover and proposed compulsory redundancy. About 80 posts will be 'in scope' for this phase of consultations. Fewer than 30 of these roles are proposed to be removed." — p.25, [Chief Executive's Report to Trust Board, Item 1.6]

[LEAD] DPT remains in NHS England oversight segment 4 and is ranked 54th of 61 mental health providers in the published league tables.
[Evidence] "During the 25/26 the Trust experienced a modest improvement on the initial score with the Trust achieving the greatest relative 'metrics score' improvement in the Southwest Region. The Trust however remains in segment 4. Within the published league tables, based on the Q4 results the Trust is ranked 54 out of 61 within the Mental Health Sector, this is an improvement on the initial scoring of 60 out of 61 published at the beginning of the year." The NOF action plan was rated Limited Assurance, with the board noting "uncertainty in achieving higher segmentation levels due to the relative benchmarking and evolving national performance measures." — p.282, [Integrated Performance Report – NHS Oversight Framework, Item 4.1.1]; p.143, [Extraordinary FIC / Trust Board Chair's Report (2 July 2026), Item 2.3.3b]

[LEAD] Audit committee has issued Limited Assurance on the trust's handling of internal audit recommendations, with 89 open and 32 overdue, and criticised managers for repeatedly extending deadlines while still reporting actions as on track.
[Evidence] "Audit Committee reviewed the growing volume of open audit recommendations (89 open, 32 overdue), recognising this as a key governance pressure point in the year… Concerns were raised about repeated extensions masking underlying capacity or complexity issues, where extended actions continue to be reported as 'on target'." The committee agreed to "Limit audit recommendation actions to one extension only, after which delays will be formally reported", and "did not condone the practice of extensions for extended periods of time (1 year plus)." E-rostering drew a further limited assurance rating: "a limited assurance rating for e-rostering, reflecting persistent control weaknesses despite incremental improvements since 2022… risks remain around consistency, compliance, and potential financial leakage." — p.128–130, [Audit Committee Chair's Report (July 2026), Item 2.3.2b]

[LEAD] A new corporate risk scoring 20 has been opened on the availability of Section 12 doctors, with the trust saying delays are hitting emergency departments and police.
[Evidence] The corporate risk register lists "4634 Availability of S12 Doctors - New" among "11 corporate risks scoring 20". The Strategic Executive Committee paper records: "SEC reviewed a paper detailing the concerns regarding system fragility and reliance on limited independent doctors for Section 12 duties. Issues identified include delays in assessments impacting on Emergency Departments, Police and patient safety and a lack of clarity on system responsibilities." Audit committee called it "a significant system-wide issue impacting timely Mental Health Act assessments." — p.33, [Corporate Risk Register and Board Assurance Framework Report, Item 2.1.1]; p.311, [Strategic Executive Committee Report, Item 4.4]; p.129, [Audit Committee Chair's Report (July 2026)]

[LEAD] The 2025/26 annual report and accounts were signed off at an extraordinary board on 23 June with a clean external audit but an internal audit opinion pinned at the bottom of its rating band by repeat limited-assurance findings.
[Evidence] "Confirmed on 23 June, overall, the Trust retained a Satisfactory Assurance opinion, albeit at the lower end of satisfactory because of the limited opinions." The opinion "referenced three limited opinions, including two linked to previous limited opinions in the workforce governance domain". On external audit: "Trust Board was informed that there were no significant findings arising from the External Audit", though "a payroll reconciliation, where final reconciliation had not been completed at the year-end due to staff changes" generated "a low-level external audit management letter point… attributable to capacity and work prioritisation". The board "APPROVED the Audited Financial Statements for the Year Ended 31/03/2026, Final Review on Trust's Use of Going Concern Basis and the Letters of Representation and Related Parties", and "It was confirmed that the Letter of Representation was in standard form, with no unusual additions." — p.21–22, [Extraordinary Trust Board (ARAQA) Draft Minutes, 23 June 2026, Item 1.3b]; p.122–123, [Combined Audit / QuOC Committee Chair's Report – June 2026, Item 2.3.2a]

[WORTH WATCHING] Average adult acute length of stay jumped 13 days in a month and around 40 per cent of adult acute beds are occupied by patients staying over 60 days, with supported housing the single biggest blocker.
[Evidence] "DPT Beds… Days April May Change… Adult 62 75 13". "Despite the discharge of a number of long stay patients in May, the percentage of patients occupying beds on DPT adult acute wards for greater than 60 days remains high (c40%)". "At time of writing there are 19 patients currently waiting for discharge or onward placement arrangements… The primary driver of delay is housing-related issues, specifically awaiting supported accommodation, accounting for 9 of the 19 cases (47%). This indicates that access to appropriate supported housing remains the single greatest barrier to discharge". FIC separately recorded: "Around one ward equivalent of patients is clinically ready for discharge at any time." — p.275, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 4.1.1]; p.136, [FIC Chair's Report (16 June 2026), Item 2.3.3a]

[WORTH WATCHING] The NHS England South West regional mental health team is being cut to two clinical posts, and the trust's own chief executives' oversight group has agreed to ration what it asks the region to attend.
[Evidence] "The regional mental health team is undergoing a significant reduction in clinical capacity, with only two clinical posts to be retained following recent structural changes. There was broad agreement that, given these limitations, regional input should be prioritised towards strategic and policy-focused forums rather than routine operational groups, supported by improved coordination and forward planning across partners." — p.78, [South West Provider Collaborative Lead Provider Report, Item 2.2.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] A consultation on the trust's approved mental health professional service has been paused by Devon County Council because of local government reorganisation, and the CEO is escalating the risk.
[Evidence] "13 July 2026: SEC received a verbal update that the consultation had been paused by DCC following the Local Government Reform (LGR). It was agreed that the risk associated with the pause to developments of the AMHP service would be escalated by the CEO." Rated Limited Assurance "because the consultation has been paused and the associated risk is being escalated." — p.311, [Strategic Executive Committee Report, Item 4.4]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's continued block-purchase of private mental health beds was approved at a meeting that was not quorate.
[Evidence] "SEC approved that continuation of the current block-purchased bed arrangements remained necessary in the immediate term to mitigate patient safety risks… SEC approval was pending formal approval outside the meeting due to the meeting not being quorate." FIC recorded that a further August update "will cover discharge facilitator arrangements, internal capacity and Elysium contract scenarios, including risk, quality and financial implications." — p.312, [Strategic Executive Committee Report, Item 4.4]; p.136, [FIC Chair's Report (16 June 2026), Item 2.3.3a]

## Other items noted, not raised to a top line

- **Waiting lists.** ADHD: "There are 6563 people waiting, of which 6180 have been waiting over 18 weeks". ASC: "There are 2833 people waiting, of which 2769 have been waiting over 18 weeks". Children over 5 neurodiversity: "5347 CYP waiting to be seen, who have been waiting for over 18 weeks. 3795 waiting over a year", with the under-5s list having a "longest wait… over 4 years". Speech, language and communication trajectories "predict elimination of children waiting over 52 weeks by September 2028 and elimination of waits over 18 weeks by January 2029". Adult CMHTs: "average waits for first contact remaining at 50 days". — p.276, 279, 281, [Integrated Performance Report, Item 4.1.1]
- **CIP.** In May the board was told "The Trust is currently reporting 61% of schemes as Green, which is below the expectation of 75% by the end of April" and that "DPT is applying a more rigorous interpretation of RAG rating definitions" (p.16, May minutes). By 16 June FIC reported "CIP delivery was improving with approximately 72–75% now rated Green" but rated medium-term financial planning Limited Assurance (p.135). Extraordinary FIC on 2 July gave Limited Assurance to contingency planning and "accepted the CEO's view that the Q1 run-rate data should be considered before major decisions are made in enacting any of the contingency plans" (p.143).
- **Estates backlog.** "A new national and ICB-aligned backlog methodology is expected to increase the reported backlog, currently around £22.5m." — p.138, [FIC Chair's Report (16 June 2026)]
- **SWPC.** Early distribution of "a £2.6m planned risk share" approved, against "Available contingency (£4m)", citing "a material gap between provider level income and provider level costs for the provision of tier 4 specialised services". The collaborative's next contract from April 2027 "will follow the Direct Award A procurement process under the Provider Selection Regime". — p.143, [Extraordinary FIC Chair's Report (2 July 2026)]; p.83, [SWPC Lead Provider Report, Item 2.2.1]
- **Medium secure conversion abandoned.** "FIC was advised that the Trust will not proceed at this stage with converting a ward to low secure provision. The position reflects regional demand changes and the need to retain medium secure capacity." Context from the May minutes: the closure of St Andrew's Healthcare left "close to 100 individuals placed without onward pathways identified" in the Midlands, prompting a call for mutual aid. — p.136, [FIC Chair's Report (16 June 2026)]; p.17–18, [May minutes]
- **NETS response rate collapse.** "Non-medical response rates more than halved, dropping from 27.5% (2024) to 12.1% (2025) despite a smaller overall cohort", with SEC rating the item Limited Assurance. — p.178, [NETS 2025 Results, Item 3.2]
- **Communications audit** (supplementary pack) drew 129 responses over eight weeks, and flags implications for "Perceptions of transparency and trust in senior leadership". — p.9–10/14, [Communications audit, Item 4.4 Appendix A, **supplementary pack**]
- **National Guardian's Office closure.** "Following the acceptance of the recommendations made in the Dash Review of patient safety across the health and care landscape, the National Guardian's Office (NGO) will close at the end of June. From 1st July 2026, NHS England will assume delivery of some activities previously undertaken by the NGO." 144 FTSU concerns were raised in 2025/26. — p.239–240, [Freedom to Speak Up Guardian Annual Report, Item 3.4]
- **Bank staff above cap risk.** "Agency spend remains within expected levels, but bank usage is increasing and may exceed the national cap if sustained." Workforce plan shows substantive staff 92.31 WTE under plan at month 2 and bank 50.89 WTE over. — p.137, [FIC Chair's Report (16 June 2026)]; p.281, [Integrated Performance Report]
- **CEO title inconsistency.** The printed agenda lists item 1.6 as presented by "Phill Mantay, Acting Chief Executive" (p.1 of agenda / pack p.1), while the CEO report cover sheet and the online agenda list him as "Chief Executive" (p.25), and his report refers to "The previous Chief Executive's report" (p.25). RTSC on 7 July considered a "Chief Executive Appraisal" (p.149).
