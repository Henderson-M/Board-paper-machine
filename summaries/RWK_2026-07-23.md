## Top lines

[LEAD] ELFT has completed a thematic review of twelve homicides and one death by dangerous driving involving its service users after identifying an apparent rise in homicides by its patients in 2024-25.
[Evidence] "This thematic review was commissioned by the ELFT Chief Nurse and Chief Medical Officer, to better understand any trends, patterns or themes arising from our homicides, following identification of an apparent increase in numbers of homicides perpetrated by ELFT service users in the preceding year (2024-2025). The review examined eleven suspected homicides and one death by dangerous driving, that occurred between 2020 and 2025 under the care of the trust." — p.114, [Quality Report, Item 13]. Findings: "All perpetrators were under ELFT mental health services (mostly community-based). No clustering by specific teams was identified... However, non-engagement was common, often leading to reliance on families and remote contact." — p.115. The review "was presented and ratified at Quality Committee in February 2026 and by Quality Assurance Committee in March 2026" — p.116.

[LEAD] A safeguarding enquiry found 18 continuing healthcare patients had been unlawfully deprived of their liberty in the community, with further cases later identified in the learning disability service; the action plan does not complete until March 2027.
[Evidence] "Following a S42 safeguarding enquiry, it was uncovered, in June 2025, that 18 Continuing Healthcare (CHC) funded patients receiving Community Health Services (CHS) had been deprived of their liberty in the community without authority... In August 2025, similar concerns were raised with the MHLMG that the Learning Disability service also had CHC funded patients who have been deprived of their liberty in the community without authority... A task and finish group was established to work through an action plan to address the unlawful deprevations [sic]." — p.149, [Mental Health Law Annual Report, QAC appendix Item 11]. The action "authorisation from the Court of Protection for CHC patients who are deprived of their liberty in the community" carries a target date of March 2027 — p.155. The report's own risk line: "The report identifies risks of legal challenge around continuing healthcare patients who are being deprived of their liberty in the community" — p.141.

[LEAD] A coroner told the trust that concerns about the quality and falsification of clinical observations first raised in 2021 are still not fixed, and persist across two wards despite repeated action plans.
[Evidence] "This case concerns the death of an inpatient, with longstanding concerns about clinical observations first raised in 2021, including poor quality and falsification. Despite repeated Trust action plans, these issues persist across two wards." Coroner raised eight concerns including "Observation practice (1:1, within-eyesight and general observation)... ward security (door-locking / fob system); staff attitude, including tasks left for the next shift; and effective clinical oversight over a bank holiday weekend." — p.25, [Annual Patient Safety Report, PFD-02, QAC appendix Item 3]. Six PFD reports were received or brought to the trust's attention in 2025/26 — p.25.

[LEAD] ELFT's chief operating officer and deputy chief executive Edwin Ndlovu is leaving to become chief executive of North East London FT, with no departure date set.
[Evidence] "Our Chief Operating Officer and Deputy Chief Executive, Edwin Ndlovu MBE, has been appointed as the next Chief Executive Officer of North East London NHS Foundation Trust (NELFT)... Edwin's departure date has not yet been confirmed, and I will share further details once arrangements are finalised." — p.39, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 8]. Same report confirms Mike Fox joined as chief finance officer on 25 June from NHS South East London ICB, and that Sasha Singh's director of quality post is "a 12-month fixed-term role introduced to provide dedicated leadership for our quality portfolio while we reflect on the longer-term arrangements for the Chief Quality Officer role" — p.39.

[LEAD] CQC's well-led inspection ended on 1 July and its initial feedback flags the condition of the trust's estate as an inspection theme, with the full report not due until the autumn.
[Evidence] "Estates; The CQC outlined that the condition of estates has been a theme through their inspection, which impacts on the quality of experience for people using services and staff. The CQC are undertaking further exploration to understand whether the strategic estates plans are robust and address concerns raised during inspection. We await a final detailed report expected to be received in the Autumn." Also flagged: "Staff wellbeing; during their inspection staff outlined pressures which were impacting their ability to provide high-quality care." — p.33, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 8]. The trust also has a draft CQC report for its crisis services core service visit and "expect a further four reports in due course" — p.33. The finance report lists "Potential costs of CQC recommendations" under "Watching" — p.180, [Finance Report Month 3, Item 20].

[LEAD] North East London ICS was £15.5m adverse to its deficit plan at month three, with Barts Health and BHRUT together £15.8m off plan year to date.
[Evidence] "The month 3 position, as at working day 7, was a deficit of £39.3m, which is a £15.5m adverse to the deficit plan. £4.8m of this relates to the impact of Industrial Action in April in the Acute trusts, with higher staff costs and reduced patient-care income." Table shows BHRUT YTD actual deficit £22.379m against £15.235m plan (£7.144m adverse) and Barts £14.359m against £5.719m (£8.640m adverse). "The North East London ICS plan for 2026-27 is a £41.3m deficit position. This includes £12.1m of Deficit Support funding for Homerton Healthcare." — p.192, [Finance Report Month 3, Item 20].

[LEAD] A podiatry negligence claim over an above-knee amputation has been valued at £1.37m in damages alone, with a second amputation claim valued by NHS Resolution at £500,000.
[Evidence] "This is a case that the Trust will need to consider settling. At present potential damages alone have been calculated to the amount of £1,372,653." The investigation "found that the service user was clearly suffering from mental health issues and it was unclear at the point of amputation as to whether appropriate mental health services were involved in his care. It was also found that the podiatry team's policy required review to see how 'homebound' service users could be accommodated." — p.260, [Legal Claims Annual Report, QAC appendix Item 11]. On the second: "the NHSR has valued potential damages at £500,000" — p.261.

[LEAD] Trust admitted or is settling 64% of open staff violence claims because it could not defend them — citing missing care plans, risk assessments, staffing levels and untrained bank staff.
[Evidence] "We have admitted or are currently settling 64% of these claims as the Trust was unable to defend these due to lack of updated care plans and risk assessments, insufficient staffing levels, lack of Safety and Intervention training for Bank staff and no proper documentation on health and safety processes and safety huddles." One such claim settled for "£8,991 in damages and £55k in Cost Recovery Unit benefits which the Trust has had to re-imburse the Department of Work and Pensions... This is the highest CRU payment that the Trust has ever made." — p.263, [Legal Claims Annual Report, QAC appendix Item 11].

[WORTH WATCHING] Staff survey confidence that concerns are acted on ranges from 14% to 91% across ELFT teams, against a trust average of 54%.
[Evidence] "While 76% of staff feel able to raise concerns, only 54% believe those concerns will be acted on... staff are more willing to speak up than they are confident that anything will happen as a result." Named lowest-scoring teams: "Tower Hamlets Early Intervention Service (14.3%), City & Hackney Early Intervention Service (16.7%), B&L Admin Hub (17.6%), Bedfordshire Personality Disorder Service (18.2%), Tower Hamlets Liaison and Psychological Medicine (18.2%)" — pp.214-215, [Freedom to Speak Up Annual Report, QAC appendix Item 9]. 2025/26 FTSU concerns included bullying and/or harassment (68) and inappropriate attitudes and behaviours (104) — p.201.
