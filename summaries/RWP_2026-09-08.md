## Top lines

[LEAD] Auditors found a trust building had been wrongly accounted for since 2022, forcing a retrospective restatement of the accounts.
[Evidence] "As part of the 2025/26 audit, the Trust's external auditors, Grant Thornton, concluded that the Charles Hastings Education Centre had not been accounted for appropriately in 2025/26, with an adjustment required back to IFRS 16 lease transition date of 1st April 2022. To align with the audit position and ensure compliance with IAS 8 ... management has agreed to treat this as a prior period adjustment and restate the comparatives" — p.13, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26]. Opening taxpayers' equity for 2024/25 was restated down by £(11,692)k — p.94, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26]. [INFERENCE] At the 14 July 2026 board meeting the Audit and Assurance Committee chair said an unqualified audit opinion was "expected in due course, subject to the resolution of an outstanding technical issue" (p.6, [Draft Public Trust Board Minutes 14 July 2026]); the pack does not explicitly confirm this was the CHEC lease issue, but the timing and subject matter line up.

[LEAD] NHS England has withheld the trust's Q2 deficit support funding after it fell off its financial plan, and the trust has warned suppliers could be put "on stop" if further funding is withheld.
[Evidence] "Following the year-to-date deficit, NHSE withdrew the Q2 allocation; the Trust has retained Q3 and Q4 funding in the forecast on the assumption that performance returns to plan." And: "If CPIP delivery slips or further funding is withheld, additional Provider Revenue Support will be required to protect payroll and maintain payments within agreed supplier terms. Otherwise, suppliers may place the Trust on stop, putting patient-care supplies and services at risk." — p.25/25, [Integrated Performance Report, Sep-26]. Month 4 deficit support funding of £3.5m specifically was withheld "but remains recoverable if the Trust returns to plan" — p.19/25, [Integrated Performance Report].

[LEAD] The trust's cash position collapsed to £2.8m by July, propped up by emergency Provider Revenue Support payments substituting for the withheld NHSE funding.
[Evidence] "The Trust cash position at the end of Month 4 was £2.8m (actual balance in bank). Included in this balance is Provider Revenue Support of £3.516m, which is equal to the Deficit Support Funding that was withdrawn for July... Notification has been given that the Trust will receive an additional £7.032m of Provider Revenue Support in August... A cash application will be submitted to NHSE for the final month of quarter 2, September 2026 on 14 August 2026 for £11.5m." — p.24-25/25, [Integrated Performance Report]. For comparison, statutory cash at 31 March 2026 was £7.484m, down from £35.616m a year earlier — p.94, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26].

[LEAD] The trust is running £4.3m adverse to its 2026/27 financial plan after four months, with its cost improvement programme £3.2m behind target.
[Evidence] "In M4 we report a YTD £9.4m deficit of £25.5m against a planned deficit of £21.2m, excluding deficit support funding — an adverse variance of £4.3m... Including deficit support funding (adjusted for withholding of July DSF), the adverse variance is £7.9m." — p.21/25, [Integrated Performance Report]. "YTD actuals are £8.178m against £11.383m plan, and under delivery of YTD of £3.205m" — p.22/25, [Integrated Performance Report]. The 2026/27 plan itself assumed £42.2m of non-recurrent deficit support funding and a £57.1m (6.8% of operating expenditure) CIP target just to reach adjusted breakeven — p.11, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26] and p.19/25, [Integrated Performance Report].

[LEAD] Corridor care at Worcestershire Royal and the Alexandra worsened in July, with more than 11,000 patient-hours spent in ED corridors in the month.
[Evidence] "In July at WRH an average of 40 Pts per day spent over 45 minutes in an ED corridor location at WRH and 27 Pts per day at the Alexandra hospital. In total they spent 11,007 hours occupying these spaces which is an increase compared to June. Plans are underway with the ambition being to eradicate corridor care towards the end of quarter 2." — p.3/25, [Integrated Performance Report]. The same page states fractured neck of femur performance "remains very poor due to operational challenges to the Worcester bed stock and theatre capacity."

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust needed £47.3m of ICB deficit support funding to hold its 2025/26 deficit at £11m, against an original £14m forecast.
[Evidence] "The Trust has a forecast outturn for 2025/26 of £14m deficit, with a stretched ambition to reduce this to £11.6m (as at month 10, January 2026) after receipt of deficit support funding from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB of £47.3m. The Trust's final position for 2025/26 was a £11m deficit." — p.11, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26].

[WORTH WATCHING] External auditors' Value for Money conclusion flags continuing significant weaknesses in the trust's financial sustainability and urgent and emergency care performance arrangements.
[Evidence] "Financial Sustainability: Significant weakness in arrangements identified and a key recommendation made relating to the Trust's financial position and delivery of recurrent savings." "Improving economy, efficiency and effectiveness: ... identified a significant weakness in arrangements in respect of Urgent and Emergency Care (UEC) performance." — p.68, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26]. The Annual Governance Statement separately concludes: "This Annual Governance Statement has identified financial governance underpinning financial delivery as a significant internal control issue. The Board is overseeing the development of an improvement plan for 2026/27, which will be approved and monitored by NHS England." — p.69, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26].

[WORTH WATCHING] Senior leadership churn across the year: both the chief finance officer and chief nursing officer left their posts, alongside a year-long secondment of the Foundation Group chief executive to NHS England.
[Evidence] "Neil Cook left the Board on 6 January 2026 but remained employed by the Trust until 31 March 2026. Katie Osmond joined the Board as Interim Chief Finance Officer on 12 January 2026." "Sarah Shingler, Chief Nursing Officer left the Board on 3 September 2025. Hayley Flavell joined the Board as Interim Chief Nursing Officer on 4 September 2025 and was appointed to the post substantively on 8 October 2025." "On 1 April 2025 Glen Burley was seconded to a national role with NHSE, retaining his position as Foundation Group Chief Executive but stepping back temporarily from his responsibility as Accountable Officer for the Trust until 31 March 2026. Stephen Collman, Managing Director was appointed Acting Chief Executive for the duration of the interim secondment as a joint role for both the Trust and for Wye Valley NHS Trust." — p.43-44, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26].

[FOI] Audit and Assurance Committee reviewed an independent Value for Money review of the UEC capital project, but the pack does not disclose its findings.
[Evidence] "The Committee reviewed an independent assessment of the VFM of the UEC capital project, which completed in 2023/24." — p.5/20, [Audit & Assurance Committee Annual Effectiveness Review]. Request the VFM review report and any management response from the trust.

[FOI] The 2024/25 pay-ratio disclosures have been restated after "correction of an error" in the previously reported highest-paid director's remuneration; the nature of the error is not explained.
[Evidence] "The prior-year pay ratios have been updated following the correction of an error in the reported highest-paid director's remuneration in the 2024/25 Annual Report." — p.79, [Annual Report and Accounts 2025-26]. Request from the trust what the original figure was and what caused the error.
