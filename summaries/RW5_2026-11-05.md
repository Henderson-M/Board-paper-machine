## Top lines

[LEAD] CQC has suggested a wider review of culture across the trust's learning disability inpatient services, after unannounced visits that followed incidents at Water Meadow View, where patients were transferred from the former Whalley learning disability houses.
[Evidence] "The Committee received an update following the transfer of patients from the Whalley learning disability houses to Water Meadow View and subsequent unannounced CQC visits across inpatient learning disability services. The CQC had taken verbal assurance and suggested consideration of a wider review of culture across learning disability inpatient services." — p.172, [Quality Committee chair's report, Board Committee Report TB 235 26]. The escalation to board adds "Safeguarding notifications and CQC updates had been made following incidents" and asks the board to note the position "through the appropriate confidential reporting route" — p.176. A "Recovery Management Group" is overseeing the service, with monthly committee oversight.

[LEAD] The trust is £2.4m worse than plan at month 6 (£3.0m deficit vs £0.5m planned), its efficiency programme is rated Red with a £4.2m forecast gap, and breakeven for the year depends on a recovery plan the committee itself calls high risk.
[Evidence] "The Committee noted year-to-date efficiency delivery of £5.9m against a planned £8.0m. The risk-adjusted forecast, including pipeline schemes, was £13.5m against the £17.7m target, leaving a forecast gap of £4.2m, and the programme remained rated Red." — p.167, [Finance and Performance Committee chair's report]; "elements of the recovery plan remained high risk" — p.167. Finance paper: "the Trust is returning a deficit of £3.0m against a plan of £0.5m deficit" — p.180, [Month 6 Finance Summary TB 236 26]. Independent sector bed activity is above the 70-bed plan, leaving "a £2.6m additional funding requirement" from the ICB under a newly agreed MOU — p.190.

[LEAD] Interviews for LSCFT's next substantive chief executive take place in early November — the Nomination and Remuneration Committee meets on 5 November, the same day as this board — with Gareth Thomas interim CEO since Chris Oliver's departure and headhunters Hunter Healthcare running the campaign.
[Evidence] "Interim leadership arrangements had been implemented following Chris Oliver's departure, with Dr Gareth Thomas commencing as Interim Chief Executive on 7 September 2026. Formal interviews for the substantive role were scheduled for early November 2026." — p.11, [Minutes of 1 October 2026 meeting, TB 208 26]; "The Nomination and Remuneration Committee is due to meet on 5 November 2026 in connection with the appointment of the Trust's new Chief Executive." — p.143, [Lead Governor Report TB 231 26]. Note also the chief medical officer role is filled on an acting basis (Dr Dush Mahadevan, Acting CMO, previously Deputy CMO — p.16, agenda) — [INFERENCE] two of the five most senior executive posts are held on interim/acting terms.

[LEAD] The trust is formally preparing for the abolition of foundation trust governors: the board is asked to approve a constitutional amendment extending governors' terms "until legislation comes into force to remove the Council of Governors", and to replace the council with a non-statutory "Partnership Council".
[Evidence] "a governor who is due to complete either their first or second term of office [may] have their term extended... for a period of up to 12 months or until legislation comes into force to remove the Council of Governors, whichever occurs sooner" — p.151, [Proposal to Amend the Trust Constitution TB 233 26]; "Ten governors are due to complete their first term of office on 31 December 2026" — p.152. The companion paper cites "the proposed abolishment of Councils of Governors and members" and proposes a Partnership Council plus a new board-level Commissioning and Partnerships Committee — p.147–149, [Future of Council of Governors and Trust Engagement TB 233 26].

[WORTH WATCHING] NHS England North West has published a regional "Performance Accountability Framework" (September 2026) sitting on top of the national Oversight Framework, governing escalation, de-escalation and intervention for trusts in the region.
[Evidence] "In September 2026, NHS England published the Performance Accountability Framework (PAF)... The PAF is the regional operating mechanism for how the North West will use local intelligence, delivery confidence, board capability and in-year concerns to determine the nature and intensity of regional support, oversight and intervention." — p.155, [CEO Report TB 234 26]. [INFERENCE] A region layering its own escalation regime over the NOF may be a story in itself — worth obtaining the PAF document.

[WORTH WATCHING] Medical agency spend is 21% above plan and rising, and the finance paper says the trust must "anticipate the national guidance requiring an absolute stop on agency use over the next four years".
[Evidence] "Agency costs are above the plan by c21% (£1.0m) which relates to medical staffing... There is significant pressure against medic agency spend which has seen increasing use and which needs to be addressed as a matter of urgency but also to better anticipate the national guidance requiring an absolute stop on agency use over the next four years." — p.181, [Month 6 Finance Summary]. Forecast agency outturn £11.2m against a £9.2m NHSE ceiling — p.180.

[WORTH WATCHING] A Deanery (NHSE NW workforce, training and education) review of The Harbour found improvement needed in resident doctor experience, cross-cover arrangements and workplace culture, with "external scrutiny" continuing ahead of a November re-review.
[Evidence] "Resident doctor experience, workplace culture and training environment remained as areas requiring sustained improvement and external scrutiny." — p.164, [People & Culture Committee chair's report]; "the review identified opportunities for improvement relating to resident doctor experience, communication, cross-cover arrangements and aspects of workplace culture" — p.163.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's own safer staffing paper says its care hours per patient day (13.3) is the highest of all mental health trusts in the North West and in the top national quartile — and references a "2025 CQC regulatory breach" on staffing perceptions.
[Evidence] "the Trust remains above the national average and in the 4th Quartile (highest 25%) nationally and the highest out of all Mental Health Trusts in the Northwest region" — p.225, [Monthly Safer Staffing Report TB 241 26]; "Targeted listening events... alongside an external peer review, informed the Trust's response to the 2025 CQC regulatory breach." — p.225.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust moved from Segment 4 to Segment 3 of the NHS Oversight Framework and says it is working towards Segment 2; the 2025/26 provider capability self-assessment cites "£39m of efficiencies and a surplus position in 2025/26".
