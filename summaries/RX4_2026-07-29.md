## Top lines

[LEAD] CNTW has dropped into NHSE oversight **segment 4** for Q4 2025/26 — despite its average metric score improving — and now ranks 48th of 61 non-acute providers.
[Evidence] "Segment 4 this is a change in position from segment 3 in Quarter 3. This is despite an overall improvement in the Average Metric Score, where performance improved from 2.68 to 2.60." / "Ranking in the national league table for non-acute NHS providers is 48 out of 61." / "The Trust was very close to a segment 3 allocation and a score of 2.57 would have meant an overall score of segment 3." — p.152, [Chief Executive Report Item 8.2]

[LEAD] CNTW was rated **Requires Improvement** at its CQC Well-Led assessment (published 16 April 2026) with one breach of Regulation 17 (Good Governance); the board formally "accepts full accountability" and is commissioning an independent external governance review for April 2027 and asking the CQC to re-assess in 18 months.
[Evidence] "The trust received the final report and received a rating of 'Requires Improvement'... The CQC found one breach of regulation (Regulation 17 – Good Governance)". The five concerns: "Weaknesses in governance and assurance / Inconsistent organisational culture / Staff not feeling safe, valued or heard / Persistent inequalities in workforce experience / Failure to reliably identify, escalate and learn from patient safety risks." — p.44 & p.46, [CQC Well Led Assessment Improvement Plan Item 2.3]

[LEAD] The board is being asked to approve the **temporary closure of Elm House** — a 14-bed community rehabilitation unit in Bensham, Gateshead — for six months, redeploying staff to the acute pathway amid "sustained pressure on inpatient provision and urgent care demand".
[Evidence] "In order to maximise the capacity within Willow View (level 2 rehabilitation)... it is proposed to temporarily close Elm House as an inpatient facility, subject to Board of Directors approval." Elm House = "Level 1, community-based rehabilitation) in Bensham, Gateshead, with 14 beds". — p.62, [Service Change Update Item 3.1]

[LEAD] The trust is forecasting a **residual c.£14.7m gap to break-even** for 2026/27 with only "limited assurance" on its cost improvement programme, and national cost data shows CNTW running 12% less cost-effective than peers.
[Evidence] "Reported forecast break-even position for the year relies on assumptions including unidentified savings. An unadjusted position forecasts a gap of c.£14.7m." Cost pressures: "drugs, out-of-area placements, pay awards"; delayed scheme delivery (Mitford); "£4.1m unidentified efficiency target." Benchmarking: "the Trust is reported as 12% less cost-effective than peers (including NOF)." — p.77–78 & p.79, [Resource and Business Assurance Committee Triple A Item 5.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Only just over half of decision-making staff have a valid declaration of interest on record — a level the audit committee called "unacceptable" and escalated to the board.
[Evidence] "Of 2,026 decision-making staff, 1,028 had current declarations in place, giving compliance of just over 50%. A total of 998 staff had either no declaration recorded or a declaration more than 12 months old. The committee considered these unacceptable". — p.94, [Audit Committee Triple A Item 7.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Whistleblowing contacts have jumped to 98 (from 72) in six months, the staff survey shows a deterioration in staff feeling safe to speak up, and the trust is moving its Freedom to Speak Up service to an **external provider** by September.
[Evidence] "102 concerns were raised... Freedom to Speak Up Guardian - 98 (increase from 72)." / "Results of the latest staff survey also showed a deterioration in the question 'I feel safe to speak up about anything that concerns me in this organisation'." / "A recent decision at Executive Management Group will see the Trust move from its current internal FTSU model to external provision of the service... in place by September." — p.68 & p.72–73, [People Committee Triple A + Raising Concerns Appendix Item 4.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] Employee-relations activity has risen sharply — 333 cases (up from 308), with disciplinary hearings "more than double the previous year".
[Evidence] "333 cases were reported with 146 progressing to the formal stage... (308 cases in total and 116 formal)." / "53 disciplinary hearings concluded during the year, more than double the previous year." — p.68–69, [People Committee Triple A Item 4.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2025 staff survey presents a deteriorating picture: fewer staff would recommend CNTW as a place to work or to be treated, and ethnic-minority and long-term-condition staff report bullying and discrimination.
[Evidence] "Fewer colleagues would recommend CNTW as a place to work, fewer would be happy with the standard of care provided if a friend or relative needed treatment... Staff also report lower levels of enthusiasm about coming to work than in previous years." / "Staff from ethnic minority backgrounds and colleagues living with long-term health conditions continue to report poorer experiences in some areas, including bullying and discrimination". (Response rate up to 57.9% from 42.2%.) — p.141–142, [Chair's Report Item 8.1]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's digital/cyber BAF risk — described as "one of the Trust's most significant live risks" — was not discussed at the June committee and the audit committee wants stronger "grip".
[Evidence] "the BAF risk was not discussed at the June meeting and was deferred... The lack of actions in comparison to the gaps in control and assurance was also noted... this remains one of the Trust's most significant live risks". — p.94–95, [Audit Committee Triple A Item 7.1]
