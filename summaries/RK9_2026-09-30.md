[LEAD] The trust has 968 overdue subject access requests, is "materially non-compliant" with data protection law, and has been asked by the ICO for assurance; the backlog has grown since the 747 baseline it reported to the ICO in August.
[Evidence] "As of 17th September 2026, the trust had 968 overdue SARs, comprising of 952 Health Records SARs and 16 employee requests. The oldest outstanding request was 422 days old" (p.300). "NHS England assessed the Trust's June 2026 DSPT submission as 'Approaching Standards', and the ICO requested further assurance on 4 August 2026" (p.299). "The Trust remains materially non-compliant, while delivery of the plan is not yet evidenced and depends on unvalidated assumptions" (p.299). "We are currently unable to report compliance data for requests completed within Epic" (p.300). Recovery "delays approximately 26 Band 2 Epic workforce benefits, with an estimated £53,000 impact on CIP delivery to December 2026" (p.302). Demand rose from 3,846 requests in 2022/23 to 5,163 in 2025/26 (p.307). The trust's full reply to the ICO, signed by Sarah Brampton as SIRO, is reproduced at Appendix B (p.306-309). [Backlog Recovery Plan and Future Management of Subject Access Requests, Item 18]

[LEAD] Winter modelling shows a peak requirement of 987 beds against a safe operating capacity of 829, a 122-bed unmitigated gap; even if every scheme delivers, a residual gap of about 30 beds remains, and the Board is asked to fund a £600k 22-bed escalation ward and accept that efficiency and transformation programmes may be paused.
[Evidence] "Winter modelling identifies a peak requirement of 987 beds against a core adult bed base of 850 beds and a safe operating capacity of approximately 829 beds. Without intervention this would result in a peak capacity gap of up to 122 beds" (p.104). "modelling demonstrates that a residual operational capacity risk of approximately 30 beds remains even when the full improvement programme is successfully delivered" (p.104). "The temporary 22-bed Winter Escalation Ward requires an estimated £600k investment for the four-month peak winter period: £375k nursing/HCA workforce, £145k non-pay and £80k medical cover if required" (p.106). Board asked to "Note that elements of the Trust's investment, transformation and efficiency programmes may need to be temporarily paused or deferred during periods of sustained winter escalation" (p.105). Key risk: "The loss of CFVW-funded frailty, respiratory and proactive MDT capacity may reduce the ability to deliver preventative and admission avoidance interventions at the scale achieved in previous winters" (p.134). NB the residual figure is variously given as 27 (p.106), 30 (p.104) and 32 beds (p.110). [Winter Plan 2026/27, Item 10]

[LEAD] CQC's feedback letter from unannounced inspections on 26-27 August says the trust's surgery action plan has "likely" not been met because it chose to prioritise Epic training over mandatory training, and lists safety findings across end of life care, critical care and children's services.
[Evidence] "Mandatory training levels still require focus, we are aware the trust made a decision to focus on EPIC training. However, our inspection of surgery was in April 2025 and we would have expected for training to be in an improved position prior to the implementation of EPIC. It is likely the action plan has not been met" (p.34). End of life care: "We were informed of an incident in which a surgical patient did not receive appropriate pain relief for approximately 24 hours due to issues relating to a syringe driver" (p.32). Critical care: "On Penrose ward two fire call points were obstructed by items" ... "1 pre-filled syringe of amiodarone and one set of sterile scissors out-of-date on the emergency trolley" (p.33). Children and young people: "For children and young people who regularly attend the trust we have not been able to see risk assessments, safety plans or management plans. No access to food and facilities to shower while placed in the emergency department for periods of time" (p.34). "The inspection is not complete and the CQC team will be reviewing data and requesting further interviews via teams" (p.31). Letter dated 27 August 2026 from Amy Bance, CQC Operations Manager. [Chief Executive's Report, Appendix 2: CQC inspection letter, p.31-35]

"Insufficient data completeness to provide an Aug-26 position at present following EPIC implementation" (p.90, all four cancer charts). [Integrated Performance Report, Item 9]

[LEAD] The trust's £91.6m cost improvement programme (about 8%) is "substantially greater than any programme previously delivered", £18.5m of it will not mature until cases for change are completed, a £23m revenue cash support application is required, and there is no month 5 finance report in the public pack.
