## Top lines

[LEAD] Longstanding chair Dr Jackie Craissati leaves at end of June, replaced by a new *joint* chair across KMMH and Kent Community Health FT — a signal of closer working between the two trusts.
[Evidence] "I wanted to recognise formally today that our longstanding Chair Dr Jackie Craissati will be leaving us at the end of June... I also want to take this opportunity to welcome our new joint Chair, Colin Lynch to the Trust. Colin will be the chair of our trust and Kent Community Health Foundation Trust (KCHFT)... he will commence his role on the 1st of July." — p.32, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] Trust has three independent external reviews running into patient safety — including a review of patient deaths over two years where drugs/alcohol were a factor — due to report end of July 2026.
[Evidence] "We have started three independent external reviews focusing on psychiatric liaison services, the patient safety team (including how learning is embedded), and patient deaths over the past two years where drug or alcohol use was relevant. All three reviews are underway and are expected to be completed by the end of July 2026." — p.38, [Chief Executive's Report, "What we learned / What we changed"]

[LEAD] Independent governance review rated the trust's quality governance maturity as "Developing, with significant Lagging features" and found no reliable ward-to-board assurance system in place — commissioned after a CQC warning notice and serious incidents in community services.
[Evidence] "The review assessed the Trust's quality governance maturity as 'Developing', with significant Lagging features', and concluded that a consistently reliable, end-to-end assurance system from frontline services to the Board is not yet in place." — p.17, [Minutes of previous meeting, TB/25-26/157] (Independent Quality and Safety Governance Review)

[LEAD] Board Assurance Framework given only "Limited Assurance" and being torn up and rebuilt; Audit and Risk Committee complained it is not aligned to the new strategy, and risk scores have not moved at all since March.
[Evidence] "Assurance: Limited Assurance" / "Since the last Board review in March 2026, there have been no changes in risk scores, no new risks added, and none removed... suggesting that either controls are still maturing or they are not yet having sufficient impact or worst, that they are the wrong controls." — p.43–44, [Board Assurance Framework]

[LEAD] CQC Well-Led report (March inspection) is delayed and not expected until later this year; one element of the March 2025 Section 29A warning notice has been lifted but the Health Based Place of Safety element remains unresolved.
[Evidence] "While we can expect the full and formal report to be shared with us later this year following CQC's internal quality assurance, inspectors have provided high-level feedback..." / "One element of the Section 29A warning notice issued after the March 2025 community inspection has been lifted. We remain in discussion regarding the element relating to the Health Based Place of Safety." — p.33 and p.39–40, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] WRES/WDES data shows BME staff 2.78x more likely than white staff to enter the formal disciplinary process, and BME staff far more likely to report harassment/abuse — at a trust whose chair flagged staff were unaware racism response was a board priority.
[Evidence] "BME staff are 2.78 times more likely than White staff to enter the formal disciplinary process." / "43.8% of staff from Mixed/Multiple, Asian, Black and Other ethnic groups reported harassment, bullying or abuse from patients, relatives or the public in the last 12 months, compared with 25.7% of White staff." — p.120 and p.123, [Workforce Deep Dive — WRES/WDES 2026 Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Acute bed occupancy at 97.9% with the trust in OPEL 4 for three consecutive months; clinically-ready-for-discharge patients account for over 20% of bed days.
[Evidence] "Bed occupancy across our acute beds has remained high throughout quarter 4 and into April (97.9%)... CRFD continues to be a significant pressure accounting for over 20% of bed days. We have frequently operated in Operational Pressures Escalation Level (OPEL) 4 for the last 3 months." — p.83, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] KMMH is a named partner in the East Kent system response to corridor care at East Kent University Hospitals FT, with monthly progress meetings with NHS England and the ICB; 18 patients waited 12+ hours for a mental health bed across QEQM and WHH in ~6 weeks.
[Evidence] "The KMMH six-point improvement plan is a core component of the East Kent system-wide programme to eliminate Corridor care, developed in response to the CQC visit to East Kent University Foundation Trust (EKUFT) hospitals... Between late March and early May, 18 patients waited over 12 hours for a mental health bed across QEQM and WHH, and a further 63 patients experienced waits over 12 hours not related to mental health bed availability." — p.86–87, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Restraints rose 20% year-on-year (1,015 to 1,276) and prone restraints up 10%, with the trust acknowledging restraints are disproportionately applied to the most deprived and to BME patients.
[Evidence] "1276 restraints; an increase from 1,015 in 2024 (20%)" / "Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic groups were also disproportionately over-represented at 19.2% compared to population percentage of 11.4%." — p.138 and p.143, [Restrictive practice trends deep-dive]

### Notes for the reporter
- Finance is genuinely routine this month: breakeven on plan at Month 1, surplus of £2.02m reported at Month 11 (in line with plan), trust remains in Segment 1 of the NHS Oversight Framework ("the highest segment (segment 1), across all the non-acute trusts in England", p.79). External bed spend down £0.66m YoY. The only finance flag is the CYP/AAED transfer (from NELFT, 1 April 2026) pushing the capital position £8.30m over plan, with NHSE discussions ongoing, and 17.7 WTE medical vacancies in the transferred CYP/AAED services (p.116, p.118). Not a lead on its own.
- The BAF was authored "with input from Andrew Hughes, ANHH Consulting" (p.42) — an external consultancy engaged on governance; worth noting as a named-firm signal even though the BAF rebuild is presented as routine improvement work.
- The new BAF will "align with the KCHFT model" (p.45) — reinforces the joint-chair / closer-working-with-Kent-Community-Health direction.
