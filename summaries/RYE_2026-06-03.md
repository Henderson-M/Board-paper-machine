## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust warns its financial enforcement undertakings will stay in place — and its oversight rating could fall back — if it cannot identify its full savings target by the end of June.
[Evidence] "if we are not able to identify our full CIP target by the end of June, our financial enforcement undertakings that were applied across our system will not be lifted, which could in turn negatively impact our NOF rating." — p.2 of CEO report, [Chief Executive Officer's Report, Item 6]

[LEAD] SCAS has a £19.7m cost improvement target for 2026/27 with £4.5m still unidentified at month 1, and the in-month break-even is being held up only by deliberately back-loaded savings phasing.
[Evidence] "The Trust currently has a total CIP gap of £19.7m, of which £4.5m remains unidentified." … "had the CIP been phased in equal twelfths, the in-month plan would have been £370k higher, indicating that the current favourable position is supported by planned phasing rather than recurrent savings delivery." — pp.4-5, [Month 1 Financial Performance, Item 11]

[LEAD] The trust is now run by an acting chief executive (Stuart Rees), following the departure of substantive CEO David Eltringham, while a new Group Chief Executive (shared with South East Coast Ambulance) has been appointed and a Group Chair has just started.
[Evidence] CEO report authored by "Stuart Rees, Acting Chief Executive Officer"; previous board minutes record former CEO "DE presented his final Board report"; press-release list confirms "Group Chief Executive appointed" and "Group Chair confirmed". — p.1 [CEO Report, Item 6]; p.[minutes 6.1, Item 3]; [Comms Update media activity, Item 21]

[LEAD] SCAS and SECAmb are formalising a "group model" with a shared Group Chair and Group Chief Executive — the BAF flags a risk of the two trusts ending up on an unequal footing.
[Evidence] "Differences in commissioning funding levels across ICBs restrict SCAS's ability to invest in modernisation programme at a pace comparable to Group partners, creating a risk of a two-tier profile within the Group model." — p.[Enabling Services BAF, Item 12]; milestones list "Group Chair to Start" / "Group Chief Executive to Start" — [Fit for the Future Plan, Item 8]

[LEAD] SCAS is in the bottom three ambulance trusts nationally on the new 999 call-answer oversight metric, despite reporting it as a "pass".
[Evidence] "this is a new metric within the NOF and the Sector average for April 2026 and much of 2025/26 was 2 seconds. For April, SCAS is in the bottom 3 Trusts for this metric." — p.[IPR & Performance Scorecard, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's Integrated Performance Report system failed in May 2026 and was still being recovered when papers were written.
[Evidence] "During May 2026, the publishing and reporting system on which the IPR is located, experienced a failure and at the time of writing, work is on-going to recover the report and associated background data infrastructure." — p.[IPR & Performance Scorecard, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] SCAS's 2025 staff survey scores declined across all relevant oversight-framework metrics and it remains in the bottom national grouping.
[Evidence] "SCAS has seen a decline in scoring across each of the relevant metrics … and have maintained their rankings within the bottom grouping." — p.[IPR & Performance Scorecard, Item 10]; minutes record DH calling the results "disappointing" — [minutes 17.1, Item 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] Category 2 mean response was 25.08 minutes in April against a target the trust narrowly missed at year-end; the 2026/27 commissioned standard has been set at 27:30 mins.
[Evidence] "Category 2 mean performance for the month of April was 25.08 minutes, which falls short of our target for the month." — p.2 [CEO Report, Item 6]; "delivering a Category 2 full year position of 27:30 mins" — p.[IPR, Item 10]

[WORTH WATCHING] Multi-Year Plan submitted to NHSE was accepted only conditionally.
