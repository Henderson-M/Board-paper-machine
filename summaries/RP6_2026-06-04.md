## Top lines

[LEAD] Two external governance reviews, commissioned after an unspecified "consultant letter", are awaited and have been formalised as a board-level strategic risk scored 12 (likelihood 3 × impact 4).
[Evidence] "External reviews: If we do not handle receipt of the external reviews well then we risk prolonging a period of disruption leading to attention being diverted from service delivery and patient care." — p.141, [Item 18(ii) BAF] (risk opened 3 February 2026; lead exec Peter Ridley). And: "This work will be further updated once the reports and recommendations of the independent reviews (the reviews commissioned following the consultant letter) have been received" — p.18, [Item 07 CEO's report]. And: "These improvements are being put forward in the context of the two external governance reviews that are expected to make a number of recommendations." — p.144, [Item 19(i) Governance improvements]

[LEAD] The trust disclosed a patient data breach — outcome letters sent to the wrong patient and wrong GP after a cloud migration of its integration engine — reported to the ICO.
[Evidence] "a data breach has occurred in this reporting period. Following a configuration change to our Trust Integration Engine during its migration from on-premise servers to the cloud. The result is that a number of patient outcome letters have been sent to the wrong patient and wrong GP… We have reported the incident to the Information Commissioners Office (ICO) and are contacting all patients who may have been impacted." — p.16, [Item 07 CEO's report]. A second IG-breach IT incident and a Bedford data-migration issue affecting 580 patients were also reported: "A Bedford data migration which commenced on 31 March 2026, resulted in an issue effecting 580 patients… The ICO has been informed." and "A second IT incident… This incident involved an IG Breach and the ICO has been informed." — p.27, [Item 09 CN quality report]

[LEAD] Work on the £200m+ Oriel new-hospital project was forced to halt on site after the building control approver went into administration, leaving the project with no accountable approver and requiring Building Safety Regulator sign-off to restart.
[Evidence] "On 30th April the Registered Building Control Approvers (RBCA) for the Oriel Project advised the Trust that their company were preparing to go into administration… As a result of the cancellation notice being served the Oriel Project would no longer have an accountable RBCA in place and all 'notifiable works' would need to cease on site." — p.17, [Item 07 CEO's report] (Oriel capital spend £120.0m in 2025/26 alone — p.67, [Item 15(ii) Finance M12])

[LEAD] The 2026/27 cost improvement programme target is £17.6m but at month 1 only £9.63m is forecast to be delivered — an £8.0m forecast shortfall, with £9.5m of savings unidentified or non-recurrent.
[Evidence] "£9.63m forecast, compared to a plan of £17.63m, £8.0m adverse to plan." and "£9.5m represents the value of un-identified and non-recurrently identified savings." — p.86, [Item 15(iv) Finance M1]. CMO/FPC flagged the scale jump: "The cost improvement programme for 2026/27 would be £17.6mn, significantly higher than" 2025/26 — p.11, [Item 04 Minutes 26/03/26]

[LEAD] A Never Event (wrong intra-ocular lens implanted) was reported, alongside issues with consent and how lens selection is recorded.
[Evidence] "There were two patient safety incident investigation (PSII) reports – a delayed review of an MRI, and a Never Event (incorrect intra-ocular lens)… The discussion around the incorrect intra-ocular lens Never Event highlighted potential issues with consent and also how lens selection is recorded." — p.26, [Item 09 CN quality report]

[LEAD] BME representation on the trust board fell to 0.0% in 2025, and senior/very-senior-manager BME representation was 0.0%, against a workforce that is 58.8% BME; recruitment disadvantage for BME applicants worsened.
[Evidence] "WRES 9: BME Board membership… 2025 0.00%" (from 15.0% in 2021) and "WRES 1: BME representation (overall)… 2025 58.80%" / "WRES 1: BME representation (VSM)… 0.0%" — p.61, [Item 13(ii) 2025 EDI annual report]. "2025: This worsened to 1.586, moving further from parity. Recruitment remains the most persistent structural inequality signal." — p.51, [Item 13(ii) 2025 EDI annual report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Moorfields is temporarily taking on King's College Hospital ophthalmology patients to cut King's long waits, which will inflate Moorfields' own waiting list; trust RTT 18-week performance had dropped to 84.3% with patients breaching 52 weeks.
[Evidence] "Moorfields was also providing temporary support to King's College Hospital to provide appointments for patients who had been waiting a long-time there. This would lead to a temporary increase in Moorfields waiting list… referral to treatment times (RTT) which had dropped to 84.3% against the 18-week standard with 30 patients breaching the 52-week standard." — p.12, [Item 04 Minutes 26/03/26]. (April IPR reports "only five patients waiting over 52 weeks" — p.91, [Item 17(ii) IPR M1])

[WORTH WATCHING] Commercial trading income was hit by reduced patient activity in Dubai, which the trust attributes to "the middle east conflict".
[Evidence] "Commercial trading income was £3.72m, £0.38m adverse to plan driven the middle east conflict and reduced patient activity in Dubai." — p.71, [Item 15(ii) Finance M12]

[WORTH WATCHING] The CEO told the board the abolition of NHS England and the Health Bill could remove foundation-trust governors in their current statutory form, and is setting up a Q2 working group on the future role of governors.
[Evidence] "The Bill fundamentally recentralises the NHS, abolishing NHS England and transferring its functions to DHSC… The role of foundation trust governors is substantially reduced, potentially removed in its current statutory form… In Q2 I will be setting up a working group to consider options on the future role of governors." — p.18-19, [Item 07 CEO's report]
