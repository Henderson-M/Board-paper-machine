## 1. Top lines

**[LEAD] EMAS declared a critical incident on 27 June 2026 amid a heatwave and its busiest day in over two years, with Category 2 response times pushed nearly 8 minutes above plan.**
[Evidence] "A critical incident was declared on 27 June due to exceptional demand, hospital handover delays, and heatwave pressures... before standing down on 29 June. Services remain under high demand at REAP level 4." Busiest day Friday 26 June, "handling over 2,800 incidents... 12 percent above plan and 16 percent above the average in June last year." June month-to-date C2 was "37 minutes and 52 seconds" against a June plan of "30 minutes 8 seconds"; over the heatwave 24–28 June "the month's C2 position deteriorated by 4 minutes and 55 seconds." — p.3, [Chief Executive's Report PB/26/123]

**[LEAD] Category 2 mean response times remain far above the trust's recovery trajectory, at 36m 33s in May against a plan of 30m 4s, with all four response categories breaching national standards.**
[Evidence] "In May 2026, Category 2 mean response time was 36 minutes and 33 seconds, which was 6 minutes and 29 seconds above the monthly modelled trajectory of 30 minutes and 4 seconds. Performance across all ambulance response categories (Categories 1 to 4) remained longer than the national standards." Category 4 90th percentile in May was 10:19:26; year-to-date C2 mean 33:11 against a 27:03 plan. — p.2 and Operational Performance slide, [Integrated Board Report PB/26/124]

**[LEAD] All four Workforce Race Equality Standard indicators in the 2025 NHS Staff Survey scored below the ambulance-sector average, and the trust has confirmed the staff survey results have affected its National Oversight Framework rating.**
[Evidence] "One alert had been raised regarding the Trust's scores for the four Workforce Race Equality Standard (WRES) indicators in the NHS Staff Survey 2025, which were all below the average score for the ambulance sector. A number of actions had been taken to address the concerns, including the development of an improvement plan." — p.8, [Minutes 9 June 2026 PB/26/120]. CEO separately told staff of "the impact of the recent Staff Opinion Survey results on the Trust's National Oversight Framework rating." — p.4, [Chief Executive's Report PB/26/123]

**[LEAD] "Right Sizing" expansion under way: 40 new double-crewed ambulances funded by ICB clusters, with £12m ambulance-capacity funding baked into the plan but first receipt not expected until Q4.**
[Evidence] "increasing our operational capacity by adding 40 new double crewed ambulances into our service" — p.4, [CEO's Report PB/26/123]. Plan "relies on operational delivery of Category 2 at an average of 29.22 minutes across the year, utilisation of ambulance capacity funding of £12 million and a cost improvement programme of 4% (cash releasing)... The first receipt of this funding is expected in Quarter 4 of 2026/27." — p.5, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/125]. Recruitment: "941 newly Qualified Paramedics and Qualified Paramedics applying for roles." — p.5, [Annual Business Plan Update PB/26/126]

**[LEAD] Freedom to Speak Up contacts jumped 87% year on year, and a Q4 detriment case — a worker reporting harm after raising a concern — is under investigation.**
[Evidence] "the service received 152 new contacts in total... These totals brought the end of year total for 2025/26 to 269, demonstrating an increase of 87% compared to year 2024/25." "One concern was received in Quarter 4 where it was considered that detriment had been experienced following the raising of a concern. This is undergoing investigation." Key themes: "worker safety and wellbeing as well as inappropriate attitudes and behaviour." — p.1–2 of report, [Freedom to Speak Up Report PB/26/129]

**[WORTH WATCHING] Non-emergency patient transport is missing key contractual standards, with only 43% of renal/timely discharges within two hours and a financial recovery plan being drawn up for the Lincolnshire NEPTS contract.**
[Evidence] "Timely discharge within two hours remained well below the 90% standard, with performance at 45% in April and 43% in May." Renal patient arrivals 74% YTD vs 90% standard. — p.9 IBR. "The Operations directorate are developing a financial recovery plan for Lincolnshire NEPTS... the Trust is still awaiting agreement with Lincolnshire regarding the activity issues that have been raised." PTS Lincolnshire YTD variance (158). — p.6, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/125]

**[WORTH WATCHING] Hospital handover delays keep rising — 24,425 hours lost year to date by May, with named acute sites Northampton General (avg 50m 09s handover; 33% over 45 mins) and Kettering General (43m 17s) the worst offenders.**
[Evidence] "Handover hours lost continued to increase, rising from 11,588 hours in April to 12,837 hours in May, bringing the year-to-date position to 24,425 hours." Site table: Northampton General 00:50:09 avg / 33% delayed over 45; Kettering General 00:43:17 / 21%; Queens Medical Centre 00:37:09 / 23%. — Operational Efficiency slide, [Integrated Board Report PB/26/124]

**[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is carrying a 4% (£14.5m cash-releasing) efficiency requirement scored at only 31% probability of delivery, though M2 finances are ahead of plan.**
[Evidence] M2 position "a surplus of £0.144 million against a planned deficit of £0.85 million." Risk table: "4% Efficiency required for 2026/27", value £14.5m, probability 31%, adjusted value £4.5m; total financial risk £32.2m (adjusted £6.27m). — p.1, p.9, [Financial Performance Report PB/26/125]

**[WORTH WATCHING] Lord Mann's review into tackling racism and antisemitism in the NHS is on the 7 July agenda (PB/26/131), following the WRES alert.**
[Evidence] Agenda item PB/26/131 "Lord Mann's Review into Tackling Racism and Antisemitism within the NHS" raised by Director of People. — p.2, [Agenda]. Prior action: "Kerry Gulliver to present a report to the Board on the recommendations from the Lord Mann review in July 2026." — p.8, [Minutes PB/26/120]

## 2. FOI leads

- **Staff survey / NOF impact.** The CEO says the 2025 Staff Opinion Survey has affected the trust's National Oversight Framework rating, but neither the current NOF segment nor the specific WRES scores are stated in the public pack. Request from EMAS: the current NOF/oversight segment and any NHSE correspondence on the segment; the four WRES indicator scores in the 2025 NHS Staff Survey and the associated improvement plan. Why it matters: signals a possible oversight-segment change at a named trust.
- **FTSU detriment case.** Q4 2025/26 detriment-after-raising-a-concern case "undergoing investigation." Request from EMAS: the number of open detriment investigations, outcome/terms of reference of the Q4 case, and any settlement/NDA use. Why it matters: worker-detriment finding is a governance/culture signal.
- **Category 2 Performance Recovery Plan (Appendix 2).** The pack repeatedly references a "Performance Recovery Plan" to deliver required C2 performance "by year end" but the appendix itself is not in the scanned public set. Request: the full Category 2 Performance Recovery Plan and its milestone trajectory. Why it matters: sets out how/whether EMAS expects to hit the 29:22 annual plan.
- **Manchester Arena Inquiry / Major Incident commissioning.** Recovery plan shows a RED-rated action to "Review Manchester Arena Inquiry requirements and agree appropriate commissioning plan" (due 31 Mar 2027), and the trust has "met with NHSE in March... regarding the outstanding recommendations that need additional funding." Request: the outstanding MAI recommendations EMAS has not implemented and the funding gap. Why it matters: named public-inquiry recommendations unfunded at a named trust.
- **Lincolnshire NEPTS recovery.** A financial recovery plan is being developed and the trust is "awaiting agreement with Lincolnshire regarding the activity issues." Request: the NEPTS financial recovery plan, the Lincolnshire contract value and the disputed activity issues. Why it matters: contract underperformance/dispute (>£1.8m income contract).
- **Additional Funding for Vehicles (PB/26/136).** Governance item "Additional Funding for Vehicles" (for approval) is listed on the agenda but not published. Request: the paper, the sum sought and the funding source.

## 4. Pack assessment

Not a routine pack. This is an ambulance trust in visible operational distress: a fresh critical incident (27–29 June), C2 response times running ~6.5 minutes over trajectory, Category 4 90th-percentile waits over 10 hours, and mounting handover-hour losses — all set against a self-declared hit to its National Oversight Framework rating from the 2025 staff survey and a below-sector WRES position now colliding with the Lord Mann review. Finances are the one bright spot (M2 surplus), but resting on a 4% CIP scored at 31% likelihood and £12m of capacity funding not landing until Q4. The strongest single publishable line is the 27 June critical incident tied to the specific C2 deterioration figures; the WRES/NOF thread and the FTSU 87% surge + detriment case are strong secondary leads.

**Tier counts:** LEAD 5, WORTH WATCHING 4, FOI 6.

**Routine flag:** No.
