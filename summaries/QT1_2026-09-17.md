# Nottingham and Nottinghamshire ICB board, 17 September 2026

- **Source:** https://notts.icb.nhs.uk/about-us/our-icb-board/
- **Pack:** 1 file (single combined PDF, 354 PDF pages, internally numbered to 376)
- **Analysed:** 2026-09-15T10:23:40Z

Note: this is a **meetings in common** pack shared by three ICBs: Derby and Derbyshire, Lincolnshire, and Nottingham and Nottinghamshire (the "DLN cluster"). Almost every paper is joint, so the leads below cover all three ICBs and their providers. Chair: Dr Kathy McLean. Chief executive: Amanda Sullivan. Page references are the pack's own "Overall page x of 376" numbering.

## Top lines

[LEAD] The three DLN systems are £31.2m adverse to plan at month four, with Nottinghamshire providers responsible for almost all of it, while every organisation still forecasts delivery of its full-year plan.
[Evidence] "At month 4, the DLN ICS is reporting a deficit of £101.3m against a planned deficit position of £70.1m, an adverse variance of £31.2m ... Nottinghamshire reporting a £31.3m adverse variance." Named drivers: "Nottingham University Hospitals (£17.6m adverse), reflecting pay cost pressures and under-delivery of efficiencies, Sherwood Forest Hospitals (£6.9m adverse) ... and Nottinghamshire Healthcare (£6.8m adverse)". Efficiency delivery is "£11.5m behind the planned £150.5m". Cash is "£91.3m overdrawn against a straight-lined profile". Overall p.292 and p.295, [Finance Report, ICB CIC 26 053]

[LEAD] An NHS England assessment printed in the pack states the cluster's neighbourhood health transformation money has been diverted to acute pressures, with no ring-fenced investment line, no workforce plan and no named senior responsible officer.
[Evidence] Under "Enablers": Finance, "No ring-fenced cluster NbH investment line. Devolved budgets at 2 of 4 N&N PBPs (£3.28m, £3.5m); none in Lincolnshire. Transformation fund stated at roughly £33m a year, 2026/27 funds diverted to acute pressures. [EARLY STAGE]". Workforce, "No cluster NbH workforce plan and no named NbH SRO in any cluster-supplied document. Mandated cost reduct[ion] absorb change capacity." Overall p.86, [CEO's Report Appendix B, NHS England neighbourhood readiness annex, 4 September 2026]

[LEAD] The same NHS England annex scores the cluster below the England mean on all five neighbourhood health goals and says not one of its six Places is rated above the lowest maturity band.
[Evidence] "Cluster mean 2.03, every Goal below the England mean of 2.48 ... N&N holds the only Red, G5 1.00 with all four NHS Staff Survey 2025 themes bottom-quartile (motivation rank 41/42); D&D is weakest on G4 at 1.67 on virtual ward capacity 10.2 per 100k (rank 41/42). All three ICBs Q4 on RTT 18-week." And: "No Place is rated above MOBILISING, because no neighbourhood footprint has yet been determined or agreed at a Health and Wellbeing Board." Stage 1 self-assessed score 39/100. Overall p.86, [CEO's Report Appendix B]

[LEAD] Five of the cluster's trusts are now in oversight segment four and the provider position has deteriorated sharply, with University Hospitals of Derby and Burton recording one of the largest falls in the country.
[Evidence] "At Q4 2025-26 there are 5 trusts in segment 4, 5 trusts in segment 3 and 1 trust in segment 2 ... UHDB fell 28 places in the ranking and moved to segment 4. 5th in the trusts with the largest reductions nationally ... SFHT fell 22 places ... 9th in the trusts with the largest reductions nationally ... EMAS dropped 2 segment levels, from 2 to 4 ... The overall position of the providers across DLN has worsened." Segment four: Nottinghamshire Healthcare, Nottingham University Hospitals, United Lincolnshire, Chesterfield Royal and UHDB. Overall p.118, [Quality Oversight Report Appendix 1, ICB CIC 26 050]

[LEAD] The ICBs ran a new provider performance escalation process over July and August and have issued contract performance notices to two trusts, with a third under an eight-week review before a decision on formal action.
[Evidence] Sherwood Forest: "The Trust entered the ICB Escalation process on the 3rd July, being issued a CPN for UEC and Diagnostics". United Lincolnshire: "The ICB had issued a CPN specifically for diagnostics prior to the ICB Performance escalation process". UHDB: "The ICB has requested a Shadow RAP from the Trust targeting the 4 programme areas of concern, followed by a fortnightly improvement progress discussion for 8 weeks, following which a decision will be taken as to whether to progress to a CPN". Provider Recovery Oversight Meetings are paused at UHDB, Chesterfield Royal, United Lincolnshire and NUH. Overall p.267, [Commissioning Oversight Delivery Report, ICB CIC 26 052]

[LEAD] Lincolnshire ICB reports 52.1 per cent of diagnostic patients waiting over six weeks against a 32.8 per cent plan, and the pack ranks it last of 36 ICBs nationally on that measure.
[Evidence] "In June, Derbyshire reported 27.4% of patients waiting over six weeks against a 17.1% plan, Nottingham and Nottinghamshire 29.3% against 22.6%, and Lincolnshire 52.1% against 32.8%." National ranking table: "Diagnostics >6 Weeks (%) Jun-26 ... LICB 36/36 ... ULH 133/134". Overall p.270 and p.278, [Commissioning Oversight Delivery Report]

[LEAD] The joint winter plan is built on no new money, with £22.9m of transformation funding folded into acute trusts' baselines, and still leaves a 77-bed shortfall across the cluster in January.
[Evidence] "System partners and providers are aware there is no additional funding for winter". "In developing the winter plan acute trusts were asked to ensure they acknowledged the position in relation to the Transformation Funding which has been included in acute baseline positions this year totalling £22.9 million": ULHT £7.9m, SFHFT £7m, UHDB £4m, CRH £2m, NUH £2m. Provider winter bed gap table, January: NUH -42, SFH -24, ULTH -10, UHDB -6, CRH +5, "DLN Total -77". Overall p.151, p.153 and p.155, [Winter Plan 2026/27, ICB CIC 26 051]

[LEAD] The ICBs' quality committee took only "limited assurance" on maternity oversight and discussed concerns about racism within maternity services.
[Evidence] "Members emphasised the importance of robust contractual oversight, independent verification of provider progress ... They discussed concerns regarding organisational culture and experiences of racism within maternity services". And: "the Committee took limited assurance regarding the ICBs' ability to secure sustained improvement where provider performance remained below expectations. It sought greater clarity on the contractual and commissioning levers available to secure timely improvement". Limited assurance is defined in the pack as "significant material weaknesses in control" requiring "immediate and fundamental remedial" action. Overall p.342, p.343 and p.357, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 2 and Appendix 6, ICB CIC 26 055]

[WORTH WATCHING] A new high-scoring risk says notice has been served on four Derbyshire urgent treatment centres, with the ICBs needing to recommission them.
[Evidence] "RR165 (NEW) ... Notice has been received by DCHS on the delivery of 4 Derbyshire Urgent Treatment Centre services. Failure to commission alternative services within required timescales may result in a risk of disruption to same day urgent care provision in the county". Scored High 16 (I4 x L4). The pack does not say who served notice, on what date, or which four centres. Overall p.363, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] A new high-scoring risk questions the leadership capacity, capability and culture at Nottinghamshire Healthcare, the trust subject to the statutory Nottingham inquiry.
[Evidence] "RR071 (NEW) ... If NHCT's leadership capacity, capability, and organisational culture do not fully support effective improvement and responsive decision-making, then the Trust may be unable to meet operational and strategic demands, resulting in challenges ... in delivering safe, high-quality services and achieving regulatory compliance." Alongside RR072 on whether the trust "lacks the capacity and capability to implement sustainable quality improvements identified through serious incidents, deaths, quality reviews". The July minutes record that the Nottingham Inquiry "had recently concluded its evidence hearings and a report was expected to be released early in 2027", and CQC ratings for the trust remain suspended. Overall p.364, p.20 and p.145, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 7; Minutes 16 July 2026; Quality Oversight Report]

[FOI] NHS England's feedback on the draft DLN winter plan is referenced as an appendix but the appendix contains no content. Request the feedback and the recommendations log from NHS England (Midlands region) and from the ICBs.
[Evidence] "Appendix 4: NHSE feedback following submission of draft Winter plan" appears as a divider slide with nothing behind it (Overall p.245). The narrative confirms substance exists: "a dedicated log has been developed to capture all recommendations and areas identified for strengthening through the KLOE review process". Worth pairing with the ICB Board Assurance Statement, which self-rates "5 - Full assurance" on all eight winter domains (Overall p.254) despite the residual bed gap, and with the NHS England winter assurance visit "on 22 September at the Queens Medical Centre". Overall p.151, p.245, p.248 and p.254, [Winter Plan appendices]

[FOI] The ICBs have decided not to publish the Lincolnshire mental health inpatient transformation year two report. Request it from Lincolnshire ICB.
[Evidence] The Joint Strategic Commissioning Committee "Noted the NHS Lincolnshire ICB Mental Health Inpatient Quality Transformation Programme Year Two Report and Summary and agreed that the Year Two update would not be published as a standalone report, noting that it was an internal programme assurance report and that future public reporting would be aligned across the three ICBs." Overall p.350, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 3]

## Also noted (secondary, for the reporter's file)

- **ICB annual assessments 2025/26.** NHS England has told Nottingham and Nottinghamshire ICB that areas "requiring sustained attention during 2026/27 include urgent and emergency care performance, cancer waiting times, financial recovery, productivity improvement and reducing inequalities", and "further noted the need for continued improvement in communication and engagement arrangements to support external scrutiny and oversight". Overall p.64, [CEO's Report, ICB CIC 26 046]
- **Cluster workforce.** Turnover across the three ICBs is "34.6% a rise of 12.7% compared to May", whole time equivalents down 153.1 on the month, appraisal completion 42.5 per cent, sickness 4.3 per cent with "anxiety, stress and depression" the most common cause. Overall p.78, [CEO's Report Appendix A]
- **Transition savings.** The Transition Programme "remained on track to deliver the required £52.9m savings ... with £36.2m of annualised savings realised by July 2026". Wave 3 recruitment has slipped "from September to the end of October 2026", which members noted "increased the risk of prolonged uncertainty for staff". Overall p.351 and p.352, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 4]
- **Efficiency risk.** "£173.8 million of savings had been identified equating to 106.5% of the requirement. The risk adjusted year end forecast was estimated to be a £29.8 million gap to target." Overall p.338, [Committee Highlight Reports Appendix 1]
- **Emerging financial risk from NHS England.** The finance committee "requested further consideration of the financial aspects of the risk relating to autism and ADHD assessments and of an emerging financial risk arising from NHS England's approach to performance monitoring". Not described further. Overall p.341
- **Corridor care.** July 2026 experimental returns record "4,769 entries across the selected providers, of which 3,480 (73%) occurred in emergency departments"; cluster ED daily average 22.45 against an England average of 16.72; highest provider UHDB at 60.19 a day. Overall p.107 and p.124
- **Mortality and coroners.** Nottingham University Hospitals SHMI 1.1677, "higher than expected", with a caveat about invalid diagnosis coding. All three Regulation 28 prevention of future deaths reports in Q1 2026/27 relate to Nottingham and Nottinghamshire. Overall p.109 and p.122
- **Continuing healthcare.** Nottingham and Nottinghamshire accounts for 26 of 31 incomplete over-28-day CHC referrals, "increased 63% from Quarter Four 2025/26". Overall p.109 and p.141
- **Board assurance framework.** Five of nine strategic risks are above appetite: population health intelligence, financial sustainability and contracts, quality improvement, access and performance, and cyber resilience. Risk 11 has been rewritten around "the transition to a merged ICB". Overall p.298 and p.305, [BAF Mid-year update, ICB CIC 26 054]
- **Structural change.** Lincolnshire Community Health Services and United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals "have announced their intention to pursue a formal merger"; the ICB merger is dated "from April 2027"; the government's September 2026 review "paused implementation activity" on local government reorganisation. Overall p.68, p.39 and p.86
- **Missing paper.** Agenda item 10, "Maternity and Neonatal Services: Response to Local and National Reviews and Improvement Priorities", is marked "(to follow)" and is not in the published pack, despite the CEO having committed in July to "bringing a full report to the next Board meeting". Overall p.3 and p.21
- **Marked NOT PUBLIC.** A slide headed "NOT PUBLIC" setting out NHS England oversight framework capability ratings and programme tiering for each cluster trust appears in the public pack. Overall p.119
- **Appointments and ratings.** Elaine Baylis appointed chair of Nottingham University Hospitals Trust. CQC rated Lincolnshire Partnership Foundation Trust "Good" for well-led after a June 2026 inspection. Overall p.68 and p.69
