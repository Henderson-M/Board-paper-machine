[LEAD] Black Country Healthcare has been placed in **Segment 4** of NHS England's National Oversight Framework, and its NOF ranking has moved the wrong way in the latest published period.
[Evidence] "An update was received from NHS England under the National Oversight Framework, with the Trust currently positioned in Segment 4. The Board noted this as an interim position pending further clarification from NHS England and agreed to review implications once additional detail is available." — p.17, [Enc. 2.4 Public Trust Board May Minutes 2026]. And: "The NOF Dashboard shows that the Trust's rank position moved in a less favourable direction in the published period, noting that a higher average score reflects a worse position." — p.149, [Enc. 5.1 July Trust Integrated Quality Performance Report (May Data)]

[LEAD] CQC issued the trust **five requirement notices** (disaggregated to seven actions) over Hallam Street Hospital's adult working-age wards, the core service stays Requires Improvement, and the CQC came back for a second, unannounced full core-service inspection of every adult acute ward three days after publishing the first report.
[Evidence] "The trust received five requirement notices, (historically referred to as must do actions) from the CQC… 1. The Trust must ensure that care plans are detailed and person centered… 3. The Trust must ensure that environmental concerns are addressed inclusive of infection prevention and control concerns/ cleanliness and fitness for purpose of the buildings… 7. The Trust must ensure that all staff are compliant and receive supervision in line with the trust policy." — p.260–261, [Enc. 7.2 CQC HSH 2025 2026 Visit outcomes]. Timeline: "The final report was published on Friday 5th June 2026" (p.258) and "Following the publication of the Hallam Street report on Friday 5th June, the CQC undertook an unannounced full core service inspection of all wards for the Working Age Inpatient Core Service, week commencing 8th June 2026." — p.263, same paper

[LEAD] June 2026 CQC inspection raised **fire door integrity, ligature-assessment knowledge gaps, medicines storage and staff burnout risk** across the adult acute estate, and found staff who did not feel able to speak up.
[Evidence] "Issues were also noted in clinical practice and environments, including some staff not being familiar with ligature risk assessments, inappropriate use of clinic rooms – in storing ice packs, medicines storage concerns, and estates risks including fire door integrity and safety and the continued recognition that Hallam Street Hospital is sub optimal for the provision of high-quality care. From a patient perspective, feedback highlighted concerns about food quality and delayed discharges, whilst in Well-led, some staff reported feeling unable to speak up or influence decisions." — p.265, [Enc. 7.2 CQC HSH 2025 2026 Visit outcomes]. Also: "Concerns were raised about staffing levels and the ability to ensure patient leave and the potential impact on staff well-being, the risk of staff becoming burn-out" — p.264, same paper

[LEAD] The trust invoked **OPEL level 4** for urgent mental health care in June 2026 — the top of the operational pressure escalation scale — during a Met Office red heat warning.
[Evidence] "As I'm writing this report, we are managing a period of extreme heat, and our region is subject to a Met Office 'red' weather warning. At the same time, we have been experiencing extremely high demand for urgent mental health care, and we have invoked escalation to level 4 of the OPEL (Operational Pressure Escalation Level) framework." — p.52, [Enc. 3.2 Chief Executive's Report, July 2026]

[LEAD] Mental Health Legislation Committee reports **Mental Health Act detentions lapsing** — described to the board as "not unlawful but poor practice" — alongside CQC Mental Health Act Review actions on three wards that have blown their deadlines and are still unanswered.
[Evidence] "Section 'lapses' deep dive… Implications: Not unlawful but poor practice, Legal, regulatory, and reputational consequences." — p.200, [Enc. 6.7 Committee Chairs Escalation Report – MHLC – Board July 2026]. And, under Alert: "However, outstanding actions with Feb 2026 deadlines remain in relation to older MHARs on Cedars and Linden wards (Aug 2025) and Kinver ward (Dec 2025) which have been repeatedly chased and escalated to operational manager/matron, with responses awaited" — p.196, same paper

[LEAD] As lead provider for Black Country mental health commissioning, the trust closed 2025/26 **£2.9m overspent** on placements and packages against a planned £2.1m deficit, driven by PICU and learning disability packages.
[Evidence] "At month 12 of the 2025/26 financial year the Lead Provider Operations position for placements, packages of care and commissioned contracts is overspent by £2.9m, driven by cost improvement requirements, reduction in Service Development Funding and pressures associated with Psychiatric Intensive Care (PICU) and Learning Disability packages.
