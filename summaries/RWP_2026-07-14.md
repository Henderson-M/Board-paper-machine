## 1. Top lines

**LEAD — Trust is in NHS England Oversight Framework Segment 4 (the lowest segment bar national intervention) and has applied to NHSE for £25m of emergency revenue support for Q2.**
Segmentation shown as "Segment 4 (Q4)", having sat at Segment 3 in Q2 before falling back to Segment 4 in Q3 and Q4 — p.246, [BAF Report, BAF05]. Financial Recovery Board and NHSE-supported financial improvement taskforce are being established — p.239, [BAF01].

**LEAD — £7.3m year-to-date deficit at Month 2, £1.7m worse than plan, with the position propped up by £7m of deficit support funding the trust concedes it is receiving "despite being off plan."**
"In M2 we report a YTD £7.3m deficit against a YTD £5.6m deficit plan, an adverse variance of £1.7m… At this point we have assumed continued receipt of Deficit Support Funding despite being off plan at M2" — p.32/34, [IPR, Financial Performance / Income & Expenditure]. "Should this be removed the deficit would increase by £7m" — p.34, [IPR, Income & Expenditure]. CPIP delivery is £3.8m against a £5.2m YTD target, within a full-year cost-improvement requirement of £57.1m (6.8% of operating expenditure) — p.33, [IPR, Finance Headlines].

**LEAD — Cash is forecast to fall to £2m by end of June and supplier payment runs have been cut to protect payroll, with only 47% of invoices paid within 30 days against a 95% target.**
"Creditor payment runs were reduced to ensure that payroll and associated costs can be met in June, with the predicted closing balance for June being £2m. This adversely impacted the prompt payment performance" — p.38, [IPR, Cash]. "BPPC – Invoices Paid < 30 days (% volume) May: 47% against 95% target" — p.33, [IPR, Finance Headlines].

**LEAD — Hospital measles outbreak described as "unprecedented for the trust"; three patients (a pregnant woman and two organ-transplant patients) needed immunoglobulin, and the trust has no reliable way of monitoring the vaccination status of its ~8,500 staff.**
"Seven positive cases of measles had been confirmed, described as unprecedented for the Trust. An outbreak had occurred involving two staff members on a higher risk ward… Three patients had received Human Normal Immunoglobulin (HNIG), including a pregnant woman and two patients with solid organ transplants" — p.40, [QGC Minutes 28 May 2026, Item 4]. "Issues had been identified in occupational health processes, including a lack of robust information flow to managers regarding staff vaccination status… A look-back exercise was under way across the Trust's approximately 8,500 staff." The 9 June board recorded "20 confirmed and three suspected measles cases… since late April" — p.2, [Board Minutes 9 June 2026].

**WORTH WATCHING — Trust remains under enhanced NHSE infection-control monitoring, with an inspection expected in July or August 2026.**
"The Trust remained at an enhanced NHS inspection monitoring level. Following an anticipated inspection visit in July or August 2026, the Trust hoped to move to routine monitoring" — p.41, [QGC Minutes, Item 5.2]. IPC annual report notes "NHSE rating reduced from requires intensive scrutiny and monitoring to enhanced monitoring and support" — p.51, [IPC Annual Report 2025-26, Foreword]. C. difficile threshold met for the first time in several years (114 cases v 129 threshold), with NHSE cutting the threshold ~10% for 2026-27.

**WORTH WATCHING — Maternity safety cluster: two Maternity Outcomes Signal System alerts, a Q4 spike in stillbirths/neonatal deaths, and Freedom to Speak Up reports of a "toxic culture… favouritism and racism."**
A level-2 amber MOSS alert was triggered "by an increase in term stillbirths and neonatal deaths… including a small cluster in quarter four" — p.3, [Board Minutes 9 June 2026, Item 6.3]; a second (level 1) MOSS alert followed "a further intrauterine death" — p.40, [QGC Minutes, Item 4]. "Five stillbirths and four neonatal deaths had occurred in Q4" — p.42, [QGC Minutes, Item 5.4]. "Freedom to Speak Up feedback had identified concerns around toxic culture, perceived favouritism and racism… A deep dive had been undertaken by CNO and CPO" — p.42, [QGC Minutes, Item 5.4]. One MBRRACE/Embrace 2024 case was rated D, relating to "an inadequate risk assessment at the onset of labour, which had resulted in a woman commencing her birth journey in the Meadow Birth Centre rather than the delivery suite" — p.45, [QGC Minutes, Item 7.2].

**WORTH WATCHING — Surgical skin-preparation product in use "may not hold the appropriate licence" for its application; procurement processes under review.**
"The surgical skin preparation product currently in use may not hold the appropriate licence from its supplier for the specific use to which it was being applied… a regulatory concern remained. A formal stakeholder meeting had been arranged… including a review of procurement processes" — p.41, [QGC Minutes, Item on Surgical Skin Preparation Product].

**WORTH WATCHING — External audit downgrades trust's value-for-money assessment to "3 significant weaknesses"; Provider Capability Assessment sits at Amber/Red.**
"External Audit Value for Money assessment 2025/26: 3 significant weaknesses" (up from "2 significant weaknesses"), direction of travel down; "Provider Capability Assessment Rating Feb 26: Amber/Red (2025/26)" — p.240, [BAF01 independent assurance].

**WORTH WATCHING — Alexandra Hospital remains under two Fire Authority enforcement notices, one still open, with full remediation described as "unaffordable."**

**WORTH WATCHING — Urgent and emergency care remains the trust's top-scored risk (5x4=20) even as May performance improved; May saw a record 14,882 type-1 attendances.**

## 5. Pack assessment

**Not routine.** This is a heavy, story-rich pack for a trust visibly under financial and regulatory pressure. The dominant thread is finance: an off-plan £7.3m YTD deficit sustained only by £7m of deficit support, a live £25m Q2 revenue-support application to NHSE, cash forecast down to £2m, suppliers being paid late, Segment 4 status and a deteriorating external VfM opinion — a coherent "distressed trust" picture. Alongside it sit two strong non-finance leads: an "unprecedented" hospital measles outbreak exposing a gap in staff-vaccination oversight, and a maternity safety cluster paired with a named culture problem. The CEO report is largely national/group boilerplate (resident-doctors deal, Lord Mann, NHS Online, oversight framework) with limited exclusivity, though the Segment-3 override quote is locally specific. Best single HSJ angle: the finance/Segment-4/£25m-support story, with the measles outbreak and maternity cluster as separate follow-ups.
