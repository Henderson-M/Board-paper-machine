## Top lines

[LEAD] Kent and Medway ICB is paying its four acute trusts £103.1m more than the priced value of the activity they deliver in 2026/27 — a payment its own financial strategy calls a subsidy for inefficiency — and plans to halve it next year.
[Evidence] "It is again noted that the ICB is paying in excess of priced activity in Acute providers by £103m across the ICS in 2026/27. This is being paid in contracts as part of the Financial Recovery Directors' recommendations to support the acute provider's transition to financially sustainable positions. Whilst this is supporting the recovery, the payment is a supplement to compensate for inefficiency. The ICB plans to taper this relief in 2027/28 and 2028/29" — p.197, [Appendix 6 Draft Financial Strategy 2027/28] [Appendices]. Table 7 breaks the payments down by trust: East Kent Hospitals £53.7m (7.0% of a £762.9m contract), Maidstone and Tunbridge Wells £36.8m (6.4% of £576.7m), Medway FT £9.4m (2.3% of £403.6m), Dartford and Gravesham £3.1m (1.0% of £320.8m); total £2,063.3m contract value, £103.1m supplement, 5.0%. Indicative 2027/28 supplementary payments fall to £51.6m — EKHUFT £26.85m, MTW £18.4m, MFT £4.7m, DGT £1.6m — p.197 [Appendices].

[LEAD] An EY review of the Kent and Medway health system has put the system's underlying deficit at around £300m and identified £312m of efficiency opportunity inside the four acute trusts, driven partly by a rise of nearly 3,000 WTE.
[Evidence] "An analytical review of the Kent and Medway health system undertaken by EY has reported on an underlying circa £300m deficit." The paper lists total identified drivers of "£462m, with the largest opportunities behind this being in Non-Elective (£191m), Elective (£23m), and Corporate Services (£42m)", plus "twelve actionable areas within acute trusts which were clear outlier areas identified a financial opportunity of £312m", the most material being "Emergency Medicine £69m; Obstetrics £54m; Trauma & Orthopaedics £29m; General Surgery £29m; Gynaecology £19m; Facilities management £33m; Corporate Services £42m". "A significant factor behind this was the increase in provider WTEs of nearly 3,000WTE." Named cost-per-WAU comparisons: "Maidstone & Tunbridge Wells (circa £3,850 per WAU v peer WAU cost of £3,400), East Kent (circa £3,750 per WAU v peer WAU cost of £3,450), and Medway (circa £3,550 per WAU v peer WAU cost of £3,450)" — p.197–198, [Appendix 6 Draft Financial Strategy 2027/28] [Appendices].

[LEAD] Deficit support funding withdrawal will strip £35.2m of income from Kent and Medway providers in 2027/28 alone, with East Kent Hospitals losing £18.2m in each of two consecutive years.
[Evidence] "Deficit Support Funding (DSF) is being tapered. This represents a loss of £35.2m of income in the ICS in 2027/28 alone." Trust-by-trust: "The deficit support funding reductions in 2027/28 are: DGT: £8.1m; EKHUFT: £18.2m; MFT: £8.9m"; 2028/29 "DGT: £4.3m; EKHUFT: £18.2m; MFT: £8.9m"; 2029/30 "EKHUFT: £3.6m; MFT: £8.9m"; 2030/31 "MFT: £6.1m" — p.193–194, [Appendix 6 Draft Financial Strategy 2027/28] [Appendices].

[LEAD] Three of Kent and Medway's four maternity providers are under NHS England enhanced oversight, and the ICB's new maternity review will look at acute sites where birth volumes sit below national thresholds.
[Evidence] "Kent and Medway's maternity, neonatal and women's health services are under sustained and escalating pressure. Three of the four provider trusts are currently subject to NHS England South East Enhanced Support and Oversight, and active safety signals are present across the provider landscape. The ICB's aggregated perinatal safety position places Kent and Medway in the upper national quartile on the NHS Oversight Framework; however, this aggregate conceals material provider-level variation. No strategic system-level review of the configuration, resilience or value of maternity services has previously been undertaken by NHS Kent and Medway." Scope covers "the acute layer prioritised first in Phase 1, given the safety escalation and birth volumes below national thresholds at a number of sites" — p.115–116, [Kent and Medway Maternity Services Review: Terms of Reference, Item 12]. The paper also records that "The Review is driven by active safety escalation" — p.113.

[LEAD] The ICB's audit committee has told the board it cannot give assurance that the organisation is managing its own strategic risks.
[Evidence] "The Committee concluded that the current Board Assurance Framework and Corporate Risk Register do not yet provide sufficient assurance that strategic and operational risks are being consistently identified, monitored and actively managed across the organisation." Also: "The BAF highlighted risks to achieving the strategic objectives, however the controls listed are predominantly process based. Many of the controls detailed lack specificity and there are few metrics used which can show how risks are being managed. The Corporate Risk Register is out of date in several instances and lacks specificity in terms of the controls stated to manage and address the risks. The Committee questioned whether Management were using these documents to manage the strategic and operational risks to the organisation." — p.399, [Audit and Risk Committee report, Item 21.4]. Separately, all five principal BAF risks are out of appetite: "At Quarter 1 all five principal strategic risks remain above the Board's defined risk appetite levels" — p.315, [Corporate Risk Register and Board Assurance Framework, Item 19].

[LEAD] Kent and Medway ICB is going out to consultation on commissioning intentions that commit it to decommissioning or transferring every contract worth under £100,000 a year, with a long-term aim of holding only contracts worth £500,000-plus.
[Evidence] "Alongside this, there is a clear intention to decommission or transfer low value services--commencing with those with a contract value under £100,000 per year--to more suitable partners, e.g., providers, or Local Authorities, ensuring that resources are directed where they can have the greatest impact. In the long run, ICB aims to concentrate on contracts worth at least £500,000 per year, so that its resources are used as effectively as possible." The same policy flags that equalising legacy CCG-era provision "may necessitate making difficult choices to ensure the best outcomes for our communities" — p.227–228, [Appendix 8 KMICB Strategic Commissioning Policy] [Appendices]. A non-executive raised the risk at the May board: "EB supported the aim to reduce the number of contracts and sought clarification on how this would be achieved, expressing concern about losing small but critical contracts, especially in the voluntary sector" — p.11, [Minutes (Part 1) ICB Board 19 May 2026, Item 5].

[LEAD] ADHD spending is running at about £3m a month and would blow a £13m hole in the ICB's plan if left unchecked.
[Evidence] "It is noted that activity and costs for ADHD have risen significantly more than planned levels, with spending for Month 1 and Month 2 on ADHD is running at circa £3m per month – leading to a potential overspend at the year end of £13m if actions are not implemented to reduce spending. Additional interventions, planned to return the forecast to breakeven at year end are currently underway." — p.125, [ICB Finance Report Month 2, Item 13]. The ICB is on plan at Month 2 with "an in-month deficit of £4.2m, a year-to-date deficit of £8.5m, and a forecast year-end deficit of £50.8m consistent with the approved financial plan", and an "underlying 2026/27 deficit of £8.4m" — p.129–130.

[LEAD] Kent and Medway ICB is losing its deputy chief executive, its chief medical officer and its chair within months of each other, and is recruiting to all three.
[Evidence] "I would also like to recognise the contribution of Ed Waller as he prepares to leave the organisation at the end of August to take up a new role in South-East and South-West London ICB's." And: "The ICB Chair role has now been advertised, with interviews scheduled for 7 September 2026. Recruitment to the Chief Commissioning Officer role is also progressing, with interviews taking place throughout July. Recruitment to the Chief Medical Officer role is underway and progressing as planned. The Board will have an opportunity at its September meeting to formally recognise and thank Kate Langford for her significant contribution" — p.30, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 8]. Angela McNab is described as "Interim Chair" — p.18, [Chair's Report, Item 7] and p.376, [AGM Agenda]. The May minutes record "Dr. Kate Langford would be retiring at the end of September" — p.7, [Minutes 19 May 2026]. Also confirmed: Mark Orchard as CEO of Medway FT, Amanjit Jhund as CEO of Dartford and Gravesham, Catherine McDonald as Intensive Recovery Programme Director at East Kent Hospitals, and "A process is underway to appoint a new Chair for East Kent Hospitals" — p.30.

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB has endorsed a case for change covering all 18 of its urgent treatment centres, with modelling suggesting half their activity could go elsewhere — while insisting no closure decision has been taken.
[Evidence] "Kent and Medway currently operates 18 UTCs, delivering over 550,000 attendances annually and accounting for approximately 44% of all Emergency Department (ED) activity in 2025/26." Modelling: "a proportion of UTC activity may be suitable for alternative services where clinically appropriate: 50% through coding analysis, 56% through HRG acuity analysis and 32% through provider self-assessment"; and "Around 53% of UTC attendances may be suitable for primary care, with the highest concentration of potentially GP-appropriate activity in Canterbury and Coastal East Kent." Unit costs: "primary care approximately £37, UTC Type 3 approximately £113, and Type 1 ED £173 to £563." The paper repeatedly disclaims a decision: "For clarity, no decisions have been made about the future of any individual UTC, and no preferred option is being presented to the Board." — p.95, 100, 106, [Kent and Medway Urgent Treatment Centres Review: Case for Change, Item 11].

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB's own benchmarked performance is going backwards, and its executive committee has ordered a "rapid improvement approach".
[Evidence] "The number of metrics within the lower two quartiles of Red and Amber/Red has increased further from 27 to 31 and the number of metrics within the Green and Green/Amber quartiles has decreased from 15 to 12." Primary, community and neighbourhood is worst: "As at Q4, 0 of 14 total metrics are RAG rated Green... 12 metrics (85.7%) rated Red (6 metrics) or Amber/Red (6 metrics)." Mental health and learning disabilities: "of 5 total metrics, all metrics are rated in the lowest two quartiles, which is a deteriorated position from Q3 to Q4 2025/26." — p.144–147, [Strategic Commissioning Performance Report, Item 15]. The executive committee "expressed concern about the overall deterioration in performance against national comparators, and agreed that a rapid improvement approach was required, including more detailed performance analysis, targeted interventions, and direct executive oversight" — p.382, [Report from Executive Committee, Item 21.1].

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB is commissioning an independent SECAmb review of stroke ambulance travel times after MPs and scrutiny members challenged the East Kent reconfiguration — while conceding the alternative site will not attract investment.
[Evidence] "In response to concerns raised by Members of Parliament, the Kent and Medway Health Overview and Scrutiny Committee (HOSC) and other stakeholders regarding ambulance travel times from patients' homes to William Harvey Hospital, the ICB has undertaken a structured review of the evidence" — p.88. The board is asked to "Note that the ICB has developed the Terms of Reference to commission an independent review by SECAmb of 2025/26 stroke ambulance travel times to William Harvey Hospital and is in discussion with SECAmb to formally commission this from them" — p.84. Legal risk flagged: "Given the previous judicial reviews undertaken which went in favour of the reconfiguration plan, there might be legal implications if that decision is altered." — p.86, [Stroke Services in East Kent: Update Report, Item 10].

[WORTH WATCHING] An NAPC independent review commissioned by the ICB found under-doctoring in Kent and Medway is structural rather than a headcount problem, concentrated in Medway, Swale and parts of East Kent.
[Evidence] "NHS Kent and Medway ICB commissioned the National Association of Primary Care to undertake an independent review of under-doctoring across Kent and Medway. The review found that under-doctoring is not simply a shortage of GP headcount, but a structural, geographically uneven and system-amplified issue... Pressures are most acute where deprivation, ageing populations, lower GP full-time equivalent capacity and workforce fragility combine, particularly in Medway, Swale and parts of East Kent." The report itself states: "Nationally the system is the 5th lowest for under-doctoring, with a general concentration in the South East" — p.35, [Response to NAPC report, Item 9] and p.52, [Appendix 1 Kent and Medway NAPC final report]. The board is asked to approve a 24-month improvement plan, with the ICB reserving the right to use "commissioning levers, including commissioning additional capacity, developing new or expanded provision, or exploring alternative providers" — p.36.

[Evidence] "The independent financial governance review and the contracting and procurement review have now concluded. Implementation plans are being developed, with clear expectations around strengthened accountability, improved discipline and greater transparency." — p.28, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 8]. [Evidence] "The formal consultation for staff in the Data and Digital function transferring to NHS Surrey and Sussex was launched on 22 June. We are now working closely with colleagues to consider feedback and ensure a safe and effective transition to the new operating model." — p.29, [Chief Executive Officer Report, Item 8]. Request the business case, the consultation document, posts in scope, and the running-cost saving attributed to the transfer. Request the number of voluntary redundancies agreed and their total cost.
