## Top lines

[LEAD] Portsmouth accounts for around a third of everyone in England waiting more than six months for an endoscopy, and needs up to £750k it does not have to fix it.
[Evidence] "PHU accounts for approximately one-third of all patients in England waiting more than six months for an Endoscopy procedure, and national scrutiny has increased." The recovery plan's "financial requirement is currently estimated at between £500k and £750k... If additional funding is not available, delivery may require reprioritisation of existing resources." — p.169, [Quality and Performance Committee Chair's Report]

[LEAD] NHS England has told both trusts it could claw back deficit support funding retrospectively if they miss their financial targets.
[Evidence] "The Committee was alerted to the heightened financial risk compared with previous years, including NHS England's indication that Deficit Support Funding could be recovered retrospectively if agreed financial targets are not achieved. This could worsen the future financial position of the organisations if delivery against plan is not sustained." — p.89, [Finance and Infrastructure Committee Chair's Report]

[LEAD] NHS England ran a CIP "deep dive" into Portsmouth on 8 July whose outcome may determine the trust's deficit support funding.
[Evidence] "NHS England scrutiny of the PHU CIP programme is increasing, with a CIP deep dive scheduled for 8 July. The Committee noted that the primary purpose of the review is to provide assurance to the Regional Director of Finance regarding delivery and governance, and that the outcome may inform recommendations regarding Deficit Support Funding." — p.89, [Finance and Infrastructure Committee Chair's Report]

[LEAD] Portsmouth declared a critical incident at the end of June when its hospital chillers failed in the heatwave, taking out theatres, MRI, cardiac cath labs, pathology and digital infrastructure.
[Evidence] CEO report: "At the end of June we declared a critical incident at PHU as a result of infrastructure failure associated with the heatwave." — p.22, [Chief Executive Officer's Report]. Committee detail: "failure and reduced resilience of the main hospital chiller systems affected essential clinical services, including theatres, MRI, cardiac catheterisation laboratories, pathology and digital infrastructure... the site remained vulnerable to further heat-related pressures." — p.169, [Q&P Committee Chair's Report]

[LEAD] Between 11% and 15% of activity is going uncoded, against a contract that allows 5% — the trusts put roughly £3.5m of income at risk.
[Evidence] "accurate counting and coding is essential under the revised contractual arrangements, which require 100% of activity to be included by the freeze date and no more than a 5% variance between flex and freeze submissions. A 10% level of uncoded activity could equate to approximately £3.5m of lost income, while current returns are running at approximately 11 to 15%." — p.89, [Finance and Infrastructure Committee Chair's Report]

[LEAD] Two never events across the group, one of them a wrong-eye anaesthetic caught before surgery.
[Evidence] Ophthalmology: "the wrong eye had initially been anaesthetised for surgery. The error was identified before incorrect surgery took place and the correct procedure was completed without patient harm. However, the incident remains classified as a Never Event because of the significant risk presented." — p.174, [Q&P Committee Chair's Report]. Insulin: "one low harm never event was recorded when the wrong device was used to draw up insulin for an intravenous insulin / glucose infusion." — p.51, [Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Portsmouth is among the worst in the country for corridor care, and patients were being nursed in corridors in areas above 30 degrees, with heat-related harm incidents recorded.
[Evidence] "PHU remains amongst the highest-ranked organisations nationally for corridor care in emergency departments. The Committee noted particular concern about the risks of patients being cared for in corridor settings during periods of extreme heat, with some areas of hospitals exceeding 30 degrees and a small number of heat-related harm incidents reported." — p.174, [Q&P Committee Chair's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts are on plan but not in the way they planned: Isle of Wight is £1.3m in deficit and holding plan on non-recurrent mitigations; Portsmouth is £8.6m in deficit with CIP £3.2m adrift.
[Evidence] IWT: "Year to date financial position of £1.3m deficit versus £1.3m deficit plan (i.e. a nil variance to plan). CIP delivery for YTD is £5.6m against a planned position of £5.1m." — p.39. PHU: "Deficit position of £8.6m vs £8.6m plan. YTD CIP adverse to plan £10.7m vs plan of £13.9m." — p.51, [Integrated Performance Report]. Committee: "IWT maintained plan through non-recurrent mitigations" and PHU faces "a £2m step-change... in Month 3" — p.89–90.

[WORTH WATCHING] Isle of Wight has 66 improvement actions to work through after the CQC's February assessment of its urgent and emergency care and medical care.
[Evidence] "The programme comprises 66 Improvement Actions across two service-specific plans... The overall rating for St Mary's Hospital remains Good." — p.195, [Update and Action Plan in response to CQC Inspection IWT February 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] Chief medical officer Natalie Borman is leaving after more than 20 years at Portsmouth.
[Evidence] "I would like to record my thanks to Natalie Borman, who is attending her last Board as Chief Medical Officer (CMO). Natalie has worked at PHU for more than twenty years..." — p.22, [Chief Executive Officer's Report]. No successor named in the pack. [INFERENCE] The chief nursing officer role also appears to be filled on a temporary basis — Steve Thomas is described as both "Acting" and "Interim" Chief Nursing Officer at different points in the same pack.

## FOI leads

- **The financial undertakings self-assessment.** The pack says NHS England requested a "compliance with financial undertakings self-assessment" and that the committee "support[ed] a cautious and balanced approach, requesting strengthened narrative evidence before final submission" (p.90). Request from both trusts: the NHSE request itself, the draft and final self-assessment returns, and any covering correspondence. Also worth asking NHS England South East whether the same return went to every trust in the region.
- **The 8 July CIP deep dive.** Request from Portsmouth Hospitals University NHS Trust, and from NHS England South East: the terms of reference, the deep-dive report, and any recommendations made to the regional director of finance about deficit support funding.
- **The Isle of Wight section 29A notice.** Minutes record an "Isle of Wight Section 29A notice relating to nursing cover in the waiting room" (p.15). Request from the CQC and from the trust: the notice itself and the trust's response.
- **The critical incident report.** The CEO says "A report covering the incident, the remedial actions and the longer-term estate implications will come to the Finance and Infrastructure Committee" (p.22). Request that report, plus the estates condition and backlog-maintenance assessment for the chiller plant at Queen Alexandra Hospital.
- **The two never event reviews.** Request the After Action Review for the insulin device incident, and the divisional assessments against the "previously agreed Never Events improvement plan" (p.174).
