## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust forecasts it will run out of cash in November and needs to apply to NHS England for £11m of emergency revenue support (PDC).
[Evidence] "although cashflow has improved following confirmation of the Q2 DSF allocation, the current forecast indicates a cash deficit in November requiring an application to NHSE for £11m of revenue support PDC. Board approval will be required prior to submission of the application by 30 September 2026." — p.183 (Finance Report M03, section 7), [Finance Report – Month 3]

[LEAD] Finance report assurance rated **low**; the trust is only "on plan" at month 3 because of £2.3m of one-off technical mitigations, with the underlying position adverse.
[Evidence] Performance & Finance Committee record: "Trust reporting on plan position at M3, through technical mitigations, with underlying performance remaining adverse to plan. CIP delivery and pay expenditure a concern... Assurance position reduced to low." — p.180, [Finance Report – Month 3]. YTD deficit "£9.3m (excluding deficit support) in line with plan, with £2.3m of mitigations needed" — p.181. Conclusion: "in line with plan at M03, primarily due to one off upsides/technical mitigations, which offset failure to deliver CIP and pay bill run rate reductions" — p.182.

[LEAD] New Hospital Programme has told the trust to cut roughly 4,000 sqm from the new Leighton Hospital design and warned it "will not fund... any rework" if conditions are missed — conditional Control Point 2 approval only.
[Evidence] NHP letter from Chief Programme Officer Natalie Forrest, 16 July 2026: "The approval to progress is however conditional to the below issues being resolved... NHP will not fund either by programmatic, or Trust held contingency, any rework as a consequence of any of these Approval Conditions not being met." Condition CL4/HP2: "the total onsite reduction should be approximately 4,000 sqm" (with a proportion of outpatients moved to a community setting), to be met within 6 weeks (by 13 August). — p.210–211, [Healthier Futures Programme Board Report, Appendix 1]. Programme Board notes "The current building size remains above target and capital allocation" — p.207.

[LEAD] Cost improvement programme carries a risk-adjusted gap and is heavily backloaded; the CIP risk score was raised from 16 to 20.
[Evidence] "The CIP programme has a £28m target, with a £31.5m gross pipeline and a £24.9m risk-adjusted pipeline, leaving a £3.5m pipeline gap and a £2.8m risk-adjusted gap to target. Delivery is also heavily backloaded, with £22.2m of savings currently forecast between October 2026 and March 2027, representing 79% of the total programme." — p.26 (CEO Report §29). ESRAG July: "the CIP risk score increased from 16 to 20 due to a remaining £3m risk-adjusted gap against the required savings programme" — p.30. Finance report notes the risk-adjusted plan submitted to NHSE "contains a stretch of £5.8m" — p.179.

[LEAD] Trust has climbed out of NHS Oversight Framework segment 4 into segment 3 and stepped down from Tier 1 NHSE performance management for elective and cancer.
[Evidence] "The Trust's overall published NOF position has improved in the June release (Q4), moving from a rank of 118/134 in March 2026 release (Q3 data) to 94/134, climbing 24 places (7th highest improving) with the overall segmentation improving from segment 4 to segment 3." — p.72, [IPR — NOF Summary]. "Stepped down from Tier 1 NHS England performance management (elective and cancer)" — p.28, [ERAG Chair's Report June].

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust remains non-compliant with four of its 16 NHSE enforcement undertakings; NHSE is to issue a revised outcome letter with new metrics for 2026/27.
[Evidence] "The Trust is compliant with 12 of 16 undertakings. Non-compliance remains against four measures, primarily relating to urgent and emergency care and elective access standards. NHS England has advised that a revised outcome letter will be issued with an updated set of oversight metrics for 2026/27." — p.72, [IPR — NOF Summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] Board asked to approve a £4.9m (revenue, over three years) emergency department staffing investment because it exceeds delegated approval limits; ED four-hour performance was 55% in August 2025.
[Evidence] "Financial Implications: ED Revenue £4911k (phased over 3 years) ED Capital £37k estate works... (Note: the paper is internally inconsistent — preferred "option 1" per the case, but "Option 2 has been approved to go to PAF and Trust Board" at Executive Finance Group; WTE figures differ between p.321 and p.323.)

[WORTH WATCHING] Internal audit gave the trust's patient booking processes only "limited assurance", with two high-risk findings on the use of waiting-list data and performance reporting.
[Evidence] "Internal Audit: review of Patient Booking Processes received limited assurance, identifying two high-risk findings relating to the use of waiting list data and performance reporting. Whilst mitigating controls were in place and there were no immediate patient safety concerns identified, management action was required..." — p.228, [Audit Committee Chair's Assurance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] CQC carried out its well-led review in June 2026 plus a run of unannounced inspections; outcome is pending.

We also hosted the CQC for our anticipated well-led review in June. Early feedback is being considered..." — p.24, [CEO Report §11–12]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust has signed an MOU with The Christie for a stand-alone ambulatory cancer unit ("Christie Neighbourhood Cancer Centre") in Crewe as part of the new Leighton Hospital.
[Evidence] "the signing of a Memorandum of Understanding between Mid Cheshire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust and The Christie NHS Foundation Trust. This sets out plans for a new stand-alone ambulatory cancer unit as part of the new Leighton Hospital, creating a Christie Neighbourhood Cancer Centre in Crewe." — p.24, [CEO Report §13]
