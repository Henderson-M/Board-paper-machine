## Top lines

[LEAD] SCFT's 2026/27 breakeven plan rests on the largest cost-improvement programme in its history — a £24m / 6.2% efficiency target — and the trust has not yet identified all the schemes needed to deliver it.
[Evidence] "Progress is being made towards the £24m programme needed for the Trust's 2026/27 breakeven plan, with more to do to identify all the schemes needed." — p.129, [IPR Month 1, Finance Executive Summary]. "The CIP efficiency is currently 6.2%, larger than any previous year." — p.136, [FIPC Chair's Report, 24 Mar 2026]. The Financial Sustainability BAF risk (current score 15, above appetite) states "it remains possible at this stage that there will be a deficit at the end of the year" and that "there will be no contingency to mitigate unplanned cost pressures." — pp.46–47, [Financial Sustainability BAF]

[LEAD] SCFT's reported 2025/26 surplus of £1.6m exists only because of a £1.6m Deficit Support Funding redistribution from NHSE; underlying performance was a £11k surplus, and £3.3m of last year's savings were one-off, worsening the 2026/27 position.
[Evidence] "Full year outturn was £1.6m surplus (subject to audit) following receipt of Deficit Support Funding (DSF) redistribution funds from NHSE (£1.6m). Underlying performance delivered £11k surplus in line with plan." "overall £21,312k of savings were delivered (target £21,300k). However, this included an additional £3,302k of non-recurrent schemes, which will adversely impact the coming year." — pp.139, [FIPC Chair's Report, 28 Apr 2026]

[LEAD] The number of patients waiting more than 52 weeks rose 147 in a single month to 1,663, and only 52.9% of patients are seen within 18 weeks against a 78% national target for March 2027 — almost four fifths of the backlog sits in the Sussex MSK Health service.
[Evidence] "The number waiting more than 52 weeks increased month-on-month by 147 to 1,663." "SCFT is currently at 52.9% (76.5% when excluding Sussex MSK Health)" against "a target to improve performance to 78% by March 2027." "Almost four fifths of those currently waiting, and the majority over 52 weeks, are in MSK." Cause: "Sussex MSK Health inherited a significant backlog of long waits at the start of the contract and, by the end of 2025, had received 60% more referrals than anticipated." — p.121, [IPR Month 1, Operational Performance Exception Report]

[LEAD] More than 2,300 children are waiting for an autism assessment, with 513 having waited over 18 months at year-end — 58 above target — and eliminating 52-week-plus waits by March 2027 requires 40 extra assessments a month, funding for which is still being negotiated with the ICB.
[Evidence] "Autism pathway – overall numbers on pathway continues to show small downward trend with 2350 children currently waiting for assessment. At year end, 513 children had been waiting more than 18 months, 58 above the 455 target… modelling has been done to deliver zero 52+ week waits by March 2027. This requires an additional 40 assessments per month and funding is being discussed with ICB." — p.140, [FIPC Chair's Report, 28 Apr 2026]. Minutes separately record "455 children were waiting over 18 months at the end of March" on the Neurodevelopmental Pathway. — p.13, [Minutes 26 Mar 2026]

Note for reporter: SCFT is a financially controlled community trust (agency spend at historic lows, ~1% of paybill; year-end cash £27.5m) but the 2026/27 picture is tighter than the headline £1.6m surplus suggests — that surplus is entirely DSF redistribution, underlying was breakeven, and the £24m / 6.2% CIP is described as the largest ever with schemes still unidentified and no contingency. The live operational stories are the MSK and children's autism/neurodevelopmental waiting lists (1,663 over 52 weeks; 2,350 children waiting for autism assessment). CEO is Siobhan Melia (now also NHS Trust partner member on the merged NHS Surrey and Sussex ICB from 1 April); chair Giles York; CFO/Deputy CEO Mike Jennings. New Secretary of State James Murray took post 14 May 2026 (noted in CEO report, not a trust story). No comparative claims made; all numbers as stated in the pack. The 2% vs 3% community-growth uplift and unsigned ICB contract are worth standing up against the wider Surrey and Sussex financial position the reporter holds.
