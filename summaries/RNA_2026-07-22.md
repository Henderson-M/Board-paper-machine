# The Dudley Group NHS Foundation Trust board — 22 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.dgft.nhs.uk/about-us/board-of-directors/board-papers/
- **Pack:** 1 file (154pp combined public pack, joint Group Board of Directors)
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-16T00:00:00Z

> **Context note:** This is a **joint Group Board (Committee in Common)** covering both **The Dudley Group NHS Foundation Trust (DGFT/RNA)** and **Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust (SWB)** under shared Group Chief Executive Diane Wake and shared Chair Sir David Nicholson. Many of the strongest items are SWB-side; Dudley-specific items are flagged. Both are in scope for HSJ. The Baroness Amos maternity investigation into SWB is already public (published 29–30 June 2026, ITV/Birmingham Mail coverage) — new material here is the board's response and the regulatory/support consequences.

## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has classed Sandwell and West Birmingham's 2026/27 financial plan as "non-compliant", accepting an £8m deficit plan that includes £14m of deficit support — an unsupported deficit of £22m — with conditions set out in a 5 June 2026 sign-off letter.
[Evidence] "The financial plan submitted by Sandwell and West Birmingham is classed as non-compliant by NHS England as it is a plan to deliver an £8m deficit financial position by 31st March 2027… The Trusts agreed financial plan is an £8m deficit position including £14m of national deficit support funding i.e. an unsupported deficit position of £22m." — p.55, [Addressing Financial and Operational Non-Compliance, Enclosure 5]

[LEAD] SWB has commissioned an external "Drivers of the Deficit" review — up to £250k of consultancy via an approved framework provider, NHSE-mandated — starting 20 July 2026, with a Turnaround Director also under consideration.
[Evidence] "The Trust submitted a business case to NHS England to commit up to £250k of consultancy expenditure for an external comprehensive Drivers of the Deficit review and initial financial turnaround support… Approval was granted and this work is expected to commence from the 20th of July 2026 with an approved framework provider." — p.57, [Enclosure 5]; "Consideration has also been given to the potential benefits of appointing a Turnaround Director to provide focused leadership and support the delivery of sustained financial improvement." — p.34, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 3]

[LEAD] NHS England has downgraded SWB's Provider Capability Assessment rating from Amber-Green to Amber-Red after its Q1 2026/27 review, citing financial concerns.
[Evidence] "NHS England has advised in a recent letter to the Trust that is has revised Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust's Provider Capability Assessment rating from Amber Green to Amber-Red following its Quarter 1 2026/27 review. The assessment reflects concerns regarding the Trust's financial performance in 2025/26 and the challenges associated with the 2026/27 planning process." — p.23, [Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 2]

[LEAD] An endoscopy backlog at Midland Metropolitan University Hospital (SWB) has exceeded 1,950 patients, with eight cases of harm linked to delayed cancer diagnoses.
[Evidence] "The Endoscopy backlog at MMUH remains a significant concern, with over 1,950 patients awaiting treatment and eight cases of harm associated with delayed cancer diagnoses." — p.37, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 3]

[LEAD] Dudley Group's own auditor has flagged a deterioration in staff survey results, which committees linked to the impact of the trust's financial recovery "grip and control" measures on morale.
[Evidence] "The Auditor's Annual Report also identified a deterioration in staff survey results compared with previous years. Members noted that this may reflect the cumulative impact of the financial recovery programme and associated grip and control measures on staff morale and engagement." — p.36, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 3]

[LEAD] Dudley Group reports a £221k surplus at Month 2 but carries a £9.484m cost-improvement delivery risk, driven by its external consultancy-led transformation programmes — Newton's "Learn, Adapt, Transform" (£2.011m at risk) and "Fit for the Future" (£7.473m at risk).
[Evidence] "After technical changes, the May cumulative position is a £0.221m surplus… As at the end of May the Trust highlights a delivery risk of £9.484m. The schemes contributing to the total delivery risk are the 'LAT' (Newton) (£2.011m) and Fit for the Future (£7.473m)." — pp.50, 52, [DGFT Month 2 Financial Position, Enclosure 4b]

[LEAD] SWB was one of only 12 trusts investigated in the Baroness Amos national maternity and neonatal investigation, and some expectant mothers are now choosing to give birth elsewhere following the findings.
[Evidence] "Families have voiced concerns over the findings of the report, and this has led to some families choosing to birth their babies at other organisations." — p.99, [Group Perinatal Quality Oversight Paper, Enclosure 11]; SWB "has entered the NHS England Maternity and Neonatal Improvement Support Team (MatNeoIST) programme… anticipated the support will remain ongoing for 12–18 months." — p.101, [Enclosure 11]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWB and Dudley sit in NHSE's National Oversight Framework at 100th and 58th nationally respectively, both in Segment 3.
[Evidence] "The Dudley Group NHS Foundation Trust is positioned in the top third nationally 58th within Segment 3… Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust is ranked 100th nationally placing it among more challenged providers but within segment 3." — p.25, [Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 2]

[WORTH WATCHING] SWB is reviewing potential disinvestment of its circa £9m investment in 7-day working at MMUH to reduce its expenditure run rate.
[Evidence] "the full investment of circa £9m (original investment in 2024/25) into 7-day working at MMUH is under review for potential disinvestment." — p.42, [SWB Finance Report Month 2, Enclosure 4a]

[WORTH WATCHING] The new Group model prompted a staff/public question warning that services could be cut and residents forced to travel to Dudley — answered only in writing.
[Evidence] "There are concerns within the community that services will be merged, requiring local residents from Aston Witton and others local area to travel to Dudley for appointments. Additionally, given the financial difficulties facing the Trust, will services be cut…" (question from Ansar Khan, GMB); "The Chair confirmed that both questions will be answered in detail in writing." — p.8, [Minutes 20 May 2026, Enclosure 1a]

[WORTH WATCHING] Both trusts are showing rising harm/quality risks: SWB's complex endometriosis service is suspended (BSGE accreditation non-compliance, 13 women affected); the BMEC ophthalmology backlog exceeds 11,000 patients with no clear clearance trajectory; group complaints up 50% in front-door services; and Dudley reports recurring incorrect blood-labelling incidents plus a Neurology/Dermatology clinical letter backlog.
[Evidence] "The Complex Endometriosis Service at SWB remains suspended following non-compliance with British Society for Gynaecological Endoscopy (BSGE) accreditation requirements, affecting 13 women"; "The BMEC backlog, currently exceeding 11,000 patients… no clear trajectory for complete backlog clearance"; "complaints have increased by 50%"; "incorrect blood labelling in Dudley." — pp.37, [Integrated Committee Chairs Report, Enclosure 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] Dudley Group's RTT position has deteriorated below plan, with a rising and spiking waiting list.
[Evidence] "Performance against the 18-week RTT standard for May was 65.14% of patients treated within 18 weeks vs 73.01% planned target… Total Waiting List, ours has spiked and continues to rise as referrals continue to increase." — p.27, [Chief Executive's Report, Enclosure 2]

[WORTH WATCHING] Perinatal mortality is elevated at both trusts: SWB at 7.40 deaths/1,000 (above national, with a "statistically significant increase in neonatal deaths"), and Dudley's neonatal death rate up to 2.01/1,000 against a 1.60 national benchmark.
[Evidence] "The overall perinatal mortality rate at Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust remains above the nationally reported average for England, at 7.40 deaths per 1,000 births… a statistically significant increase in neonatal deaths has been observed, at 2.3 deaths per 1,000 births." — p.95; "the crude rate increasing to 2.01 per 1,000 births in June 2026, above the national comparator of 1.60." — p.94, [Group Perinatal Quality Oversight Paper, Enclosure 11]
