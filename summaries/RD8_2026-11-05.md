## Top lines

[LEAD] MKUH has declared a never event — a retained vaginal swab following surgery — one of three new Level 1 patient safety investigations declared since the board paper was written, alongside an intrauterine death at 36+4 weeks and a preterm delivery on a surgical ward.
[Evidence] "Since this report was written a further three Level 1 Patient Safety Incident Investigations (PSIIs) have been declared: • Intrauterine death at 36+4 weeks • Retained vaginal swab following surgery (Never Event) • Preterm delivery on ward 21b (surgery)" — p.1, [Bi-monthly Patient Safety Report, Item 11]. The reporting period itself added four Level 1 PSIIs including a neonatal death and "a patient leaving the ward and falling from the car park". 36 PSII safety actions are overdue, "the oldest being 17 months overdue" (p.1).

[LEAD] The trust's break-even plan now rests on a national arbitration ruling over its unsigned 2026/27 contract with Central East ICB — the Month 6 position "assumes" the arbitration outcome is turned into a signed agreement, and the finance committee escalated the "continuing commissioner contract position" to the board at end of September.
[Evidence] "The M6 position assumes that the arbitration outcome regards the Central East ICB contract is recognised into a signed agreement" — cover sheet, [Finance Report Month 6, Item 13]; "Clinical Income is adjusted to reflect the latest contract position with Central East ICB following the outcome of national arbitration" — p.4, [Finance Report Month 6]. F&I escalation, 28 Sep: "The continuing commissioner contract position and the strategic approach to system engagement" — p.3, [Finance & Investment Committee Assurance Report, Item 24.2]. YTD deficit £2.3m at M6, £67k favourable to plan; full-year control total break-even (p.4).

[LEAD] A safeguarding (section 42) investigation into a hospital-acquired grade 4 pressure ulcer concluded that a shortage of registered nurses "likely influenced the overall outcome" — and the trust's own board paper says liability is "highly likely to be accepted, with substantial damages payable".
[Evidence] "Additionally, a lack of adequate staffing, especially of registered nurses, played a substantial role in the care AA received and likely influenced the overall outcome." and "liability is highly likely to be accepted, with substantial damages payable" — p.7, [Integrated Quality Governance Report, Item 23, claim MK 3239].

[LEAD] The trust is fighting an ongoing judicial review brought by a patient "with no right to reside" challenging the charges levied for her NHS maternity care — and admits the legal costs are high but says the risk of further such claims justifies the fight.
[Evidence] "The Trust's legal judicial review relating to a patient, with no right to reside, challenging the charges levied for their NHS maternity care remains ongoing... Whilst it is recognised that the legal costs incurred are high, the reputational risk for the Trust and the possibility of other such cases being presented are also high and need to be considered in context." — p.5, [Integrated Quality Governance Report, Item 23].

[LEAD] MKUH operated at OPEL 4 — the highest escalation level — for 29 of 31 days in August, with an escalation ward open 27 of 31 days and 342 nurse staffing red flags, 86% of which could not be fully mitigated with like-for-like staff.
[Evidence] "the Trust operating at OPEL 4 for 29 of 31 days in August 2026"; "Ward 2B was open for 27 of 31 days in August 2026, a significant increase from 7 of 31 days in July"; "Of the 342 red flags, 294 (86%) could not be fully mitigated on a like-for-like staffing basis" — [Safe Staffing and Nursing Workforce Report, Item 18.1]. Nursing sickness absence rose to 7.4% in August vs 5.09% in August 2025 (same paper).

[LEAD] Manual audit shows only 39.68% of category 2 caesarean sections meet decision-to-delivery compliance, 12 women were affected by elective caesarean cancellations in a single month, and the trust's eCare system cannot reliably report caesarean delays at all.
[Evidence] "Manual audit identifies compliance in category 1 76.92% and only 39.68% . in category 2"; "eCare documentation still prevents reliable automated reporting"; "Bed pressures and elective caesarean cancellations remain a safety concern. 12 service users affected in 7 incidents July (15%)"; "Induction of Labour delays -30 service user affected over 19 incidents – reported via national SITREP" — [Maternity Assurance Group Update, Item 12.1]. A neonatal death PSII is also open with "learning awaited" (same paper).

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust scored 4 (second-worst) in the NHS Oversight Framework "Access to Services" domain (overall segment 3), with RTT at 59.3%, diagnostics at 76.6% and A&E four-hour performance at 66.0% in August — and blames reduced elective activity partly on "contract uncertainty".
[Evidence] "The Trust scored a '4' for the 'Access to Services' and 'Effectiveness of Care and Population Health' domains"; "RTT Incomplete Pathways <18 weeks was 59.3%... impacted by reduced activity since the beginning of this financial year due to contract uncertainty, industrial action and embedding new digital processes" — executive summary, [Integrated Performance Report M05, Item 9]. 882 patients waiting over 52 weeks.

[WORTH WATCHING] MKUH is named a national outlier in two clinical audits — three-year breast cancer survival "currently among the bottom three Trusts" — while its own information team is refusing data extracts for mandatory national audits, which the paper flags as a safety assurance risk.
[Evidence] "The Trust is performing well against most metrics, although three-year survival rate is currently among the bottom three Trusts" (National Audit of Primary Breast Cancer); "Rejection of data requests by Information team for mandatory national audits due to competing strategic priorities presents a risk to timely data submission... and limit the Trust's ability to provide assurance on performance and patient safety"; Urology "will not participate in the current cycle of the mandatory BOO... audit, as the required data submission deadline was missed" — pp.9-10, [Integrated Quality Governance Report, Item 23].

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is using AI agents to write its emergency-preparedness business continuity plans, claiming "80–90% efficiency gains" — in the same report disclosing a cancelled mass casualty exercise (industrial action), a postponed CBRN exercise (drought restrictions) and a non-compliant CBRN store.
[Evidence] "Artificial intelligence agents are helping to draft, review and standardise Business Continuity plans...
