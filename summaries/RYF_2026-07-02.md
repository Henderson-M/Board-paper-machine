## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust escalated to REAP Black and declared a critical incident during the late-May 2026 heatwave, when it recorded the busiest day and week for ambulance activity in its history.
[Evidence] "the Trust escalated to REAP Black on 26th May and declared a critical incident on 27th May in light of the escalating position (Critical Incident stood down on 28th May)." — p.20 [Integrated Corporate Performance Report May 2026]; "25,189 incidents for the week commencing 25th May. This was by far the highest levels of activity the Trust has seen in its history, with activity rising to 3,931 incidents on Tuesday 26th may (busiest individual day for ambulance activity in the Trust history)." — p.4 [ICPR]

[LEAD] Hospital handover delays jumped sharply in May, with the Trust losing 17,041 hours to handovers — the equivalent of roughly 50 ambulance shifts a day absorbed by delays.
[Evidence] "The time lost to handover delays increasing from 10,508 hours lost in April 2026 to 17,041 hours lost in May 2026. This increased the average handover times from just under 30 minutes in April to over 39 minutes per patient in May." — p.22 [ICPR]; "The remaining delays equate to an average of 550 hours lost per day in May or the equivalent of around 50 full ambulance shifts (based on 11-hour shifts)" — p.23 [ICPR]

[LEAD] SWASFT moved up an NHS Oversight Framework segment — from segment 4 to segment 3 — describing itself as the second most improved ambulance score in the country.
[Evidence] "The Trust has improved from Segment 4 to Segment 3, and moved from 9th to 8th place nationally (improving from 10th in Quarter 2). Across the first year the Trust has improved its score from 2.91 to 2.52 a 13% change and second most improved ambulance score in the country." — p.13 [Chief Executive Report]

[LEAD] The Trust had an unannounced CQC inspection in April 2026 and is awaiting the draft report, with a well-led inspection to follow.
[Evidence] "The Trust underwent an unannounced Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspection, in April 2026. Some preliminary findings have been received which did not highlight any major concerns at this stage, we await the draft full report. A further well-led inspection is expected to follow, for which the Trust will receive six weeks' notice." — p.13 [Chief Executive Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Despite year-on-year improvement, SWASFT reported the longest Category 2 mean response time of all 10 English ambulance trusts in May (40 mins 24 secs) and the longest Category 1 time.
[Evidence] "SWASFT reported the longest Category 2 mean response times at 40 minutes 24 seconds with East Midlands at 36 minutes 27 seconds the second longest in May." — p.7 [ICPR]; "Whilst SWASFT has improved we are still reporting the longest [Category 1] response times with a mean of 9 minutes 21 seconds compared to the national average of 7 minutes 52 seconds" — p.7 [ICPR]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cardiac arrest survival and ROSC outcomes declined in the latest data, ending a run of above-mean performance.
[Evidence] "In January 2026, the proportion of patients in the Utstein comparator group who sustained ROSC to hospital arrival following an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest was 34.1%, a drop in performance which ends a run of months above the mean line. The rate of survival to 30 days for the Utstein comparator group also declined further, to 13.6%" — p.8–9 [ICPR]

[WORTH WATCHING] Backlog maintenance and the condition of the Trust estate is the highest-scored risk on the corporate risk register (20).
