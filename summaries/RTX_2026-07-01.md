## Top lines

[LEAD] UHMB has dropped into NHS Oversight Framework segment 4 (from segment 3) and fallen 22 places in the national acute league table.
[Evidence] "Our Trust position has deteriorated to NOF Segment 4 from NOF Segment 3 (based on performance from January to March 2026). According to the new ratings, we are now ranked 117th out of 134 acute and specialist trusts - compared with 95th out of 134 trusts previously." — p.47, [CEO's Report, para 54]

[LEAD] CEO says the trust is one of the worst in the country on discharge delays, with performance declining about twice as fast as the national average.
[Evidence] "the time patients are waiting between being ready to leave hospital and actually being discharged has gotten worse - and we are one of the most challenged in the country among similar organisations for this measure. Over the past nine months, our performance in this area has declined about twice as fast as the national average." — p.47, [CEO's Report, para 56]

[LEAD] Risk-adjusted mortality at UHMB is "above that expected" versus peers, with SHMI at 135.40 (red) and seven red-alerting diagnostic groups; HSMR red at 146.06 (895 observed deaths vs 612.76 expected).
[Evidence] "There is quantitative evidence to demonstrate that risk adjusted mortality at UHMBT is 'above that expected' when compared to peers. The SHMI latest rolling 12-month position is 135.40. We have 7 red alerting diagnostic groups which are a cause for concern." — p.91, [Bi-annual Mortality Report]; "The Hospital Standardised Mortality Ratio (HSMR) rolling 12-month score is a red alert at 146.06 with 895 observed deaths versus 612.76 expected." — p.104, [Bi-annual Mortality Report, para 16]

[LEAD] The trust's mortality reviewers cannot comply with the national Learning from Deaths policy; its executive group has formally accepted non-compliance.
[Evidence] "we request that EDG accept that we will not be compliant with the National and Local Learning from Deaths Policy on reviewing all alerting diagnostic groups and the Mortality Reviewers will only be able to possibly review an additional 5-10% of deaths in each alerting group and will rely on sub-speciality reviews." — p.108, [Bi-annual Mortality Report, para 27]

[LEAD] The trust must find £30m of savings this year and is already £0.9m adverse to plan two months in, with a M2 deficit of £3.4m.
[Evidence] "Further urgent work is necessary to ensure that the Trust has a fully developed WRP plan for this year, which requires a total of £30m of WRP savings and transformations to be delivered." — p.41, [CEO's Report, para 8]; "The cumulative financial position at month 2 is an actual deficit of £3.4m against a planned deficit of £2.5m which is £0.9m adverse to plan." — p.120, [Integrated Performance Report cover]

[LEAD] Multiple executive churn: COO Scott McLean has left on secondment to NHSE, CFO Helen Cobb is retiring, both replaced by interims, plus a new substantive CNO.
[Evidence] "Scott left the Trust on 19 June 2026 to join NHS England North West's Executive team as Regional Director of Delivery and Transformation." — p.49, [CEO's Report, para 73]; "These interim positions follow the departure of our Chief Operating Officer, Scott McLean ... and Helen Cobb, our current CFO, who will be retiring this month after 41 years' NHS service." — p.50, [CEO's Report, para 80]; interim COO Gavin Macdonald (from Synnovis/SYNLAB) and interim CFO Lesley Macleod both from 29 June 2026 (paras 78-79); Anna Telfer appointed substantive CNO from 1 August 2026 (para 66)
