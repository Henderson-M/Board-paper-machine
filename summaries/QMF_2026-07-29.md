## Top lines

[LEAD] BHRUT electronic patient record go-live has collapsed the trust's diagnostic and cancer performance and triggered an NHS England-ordered Trust-led review, with the trust in NOF segment 4.
[Evidence] "BHRUT is receiving ICB support and NHSE oversight in relation to RTT (waiting list growth and RTT performance below trajectory), diagnostics waits (significantly above plan), cancer (FDS and 62-day performance below plan) and data quality recovery post-EPR implementation. NHSE have requested a Trust led review in collaboration with the ICB to provide assurance re the effectiveness of the planning, oversight and assurance arrangements that underpin delivery of the agreed plan to be submitted in mid-July. BHRUT is also under national oversight NOF level 4 (poor performing)." — p.122, [Performance report Item 7.2, exec summary by exception]

[LEAD] BHRUT's 6-week diagnostic (DM01) performance fell to 47.4% in April 2026, from consistently above 95% before the EPR went live.
[Evidence] "NEL ICB diagnostics (DM01) performance has deteriorated, driven by significant deterioration following the return to national reporting by BHRUT post EPR implementation for the first month in Apr-26. Pre-EPR BHRUT were consistently achieving >95% within 6-weeks. MRI and ultrasound are the main challenges." BHRUT DM01 table figure: 47.4% (Apr-26) vs 96.6% (Apr-25). — p.126, [Performance report, Diagnostics | DM01]

[LEAD] The Cancer Alliance has put an £800,000 cash injection into BHRUT amid its post-EPR cancer performance drop (62-day at 67.0%, FDS at 70.5% in April).
[Evidence] "Cancer Alliance support to BHRUT, including weekly oversight meetings, expert reference group leads, and a cash injection of £600,000, plus an additional £200,000 for sustainability plans." — p.127, [Performance report, Cancer | FDS performance]

[LEAD] BHRUT chief executive Matthew Trainer is leaving to run King's College Hospital FT — one of six named senior departures across NEL flagged in one CEO report.
[Evidence] "Matthew Trainer, chief executive officer of Barking Havering Redbridge University NHS Trust has been appointed as chief executive officer of Kings College Hospital NHS Foundation Trust." Same list: Edwin Ndlovu (ELFT COO/deputy) → NELFT CEO replacing Paul Calaminus; Emma Rowland (Homerton COO) → NHSE exec director for performance and delivery; Simon Ashton (Newham general CEO) → CEO of a "new group ambulance service... combining South East Coast NHS Foundation Trust with South Central NHS Foundation Trust"; Amanjit Jhund (Whipps Cross CEO) → Dartford and Gravesham; Hardev Virdee (Barts Health group finance officer/deputy CEO) → Metropolitan Police. — p.31, [Chair and CEO report Item 4.1]

[LEAD] The NEL system is £15.6m adverse to plan three months in and is running a £39.3m deficit against a planned £23.8m deficit, driven by its providers — including industrial action costs.
[Evidence] "The NEL system is facing a range of financial pressures at Month 3, reporting a £15.6m adverse year to date variance, which is a £39.3m deficit against a planned £23.8m deficit... Barts Health (£8.6m adverse) and BHRUT (£7.1m adverse) account for the majority of the system variance, reflecting industrial action costs, savings delivery shortfalls and underlying cost pressures. Homerton is £0.6m adverse to plan (Industrial action costs), while ELFT and NELFT remain broadly in line with plan." The ICB itself reports a £15k YTD surplus (breakeven), £0.8m favourable. — p.111, [Financial overview Month 3, ICS Financial Position Summary]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB is forecasting to spend £10m above the Mental Health Investment Standard target.
[Evidence] "The ICB is forecasting to invest £10m additional resources compared to the MHIS target. This relates to the additional MH investment agreed through the operating plan process." — p.111, [Financial overview Month 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB has begun compulsory redundancies in its restructure; 155 staff have already left via voluntary schemes and members have flagged a "reduction in nursing and quality capacity".
[Evidence] "This month, we said goodbye to the first cohort to leave under compulsory redundancy" (p.24, CEO report); "155 staff had been approved through the schemes [VR/MARS], most of whom have now left the organisation" and "Members raised concerns regarding the apparent reduction in nursing and quality capacity and sought assurance that quality expertise, nursing leadership and statutory quality functions would continue to be delivered effectively" — p.165 / p.31, [Transition Committee exception report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Barts Health's hospital mortality index (SHMI) has risen over 18 months; the trust attributes it to Same Day Emergency Care recording, not more deaths.
[Evidence] "SHMI has increased over the past 18 months but remains within the expected range. Barts Health says that the increase is not due to more deaths but is linked to changes in recording Same Day Emergency Care (SDEC) activity, which is recognised by NHS England (NHSE) as having the potential to increase the SHMI." — p.163, [Quality, Safety and Improvement Committee exception report]

[WORTH WATCHING] NEL is moving its maternity oversight out of the LMNS model into a new "MNQIS" model, kept within the ICB rather than transferred to a provider, in response to the Ockenden and Amos reports.
[Evidence] "North East London ICB is moving from the current Local Maternity and Neonatal System (LMNS) model to a Maternity and Neonatal Quality Improvement System (MNQIS) model... Support the plan for MNQIS to remain part of the ICB (rather than transfer to a provider organisation)." — p.37 / p.44, [Summary of the Ockenden and Amos reports Item 5.1]
