# Barts Health NHS Trust (R1H) — Trust Board (Part 1), 8 July 2026

Meeting held in public, 11am, Newham Hospital. Correspondent: Matt Discombe.
Source: "Trust Board papers – 8 July 2026 part 1" (187pp). **Part 1 only — Part 2 (confidential/private session) not yet published.** IPR (TB 41/26) is a slide-based report; chart data did not extract to text, so operational figures below are taken from the Finance, Investment & Performance Committee narrative (TB 42/26a) and previous minutes.

Barts Health is a large multi-site group: Royal London/Mile End, St Bartholomew's, Newham University, Whipps Cross — each with its own hospital CEO.

---

## 1. Top lines

**LEAD** — Barts Health jumps from NHS Oversight Framework Segment 3 to Segment 1, "biggest climber in the country"
> "Barts Health moved from Segment 3 to Segment 1 in the NHS Oversight Framework for Quarter 4 2025/26. We were the biggest climber in the country moving from 74th place to 21st place nationally." — p.19, [Group Chief Executive's report TB 38/26]

**LEAD** — Three hospital-group CEO/exec vacancies open at once: Group CFO and Whipps Cross CEO both leaving in autumn 2026, plus Newham CEO
> "Congratulations to Hardev Virdee, Group Chief Financial Officer for his new role as Chief of Strategy and Investment for the Metropolitan Police… Congratulations also to Amanjit Jhund, Whipps Cross CEO who for his new role as CEO of Dartford and Gravesham NHS Trust… The recruitment process is underway for both of these roles and the Newham CEO role." — p.19, [Group CEO's report TB 38/26]. Confirmed at p.17 [Board membership TB 37/26]: both depart "in Autumn 2026."

**LEAD** — Trust already £5.8m adverse to plan at Month 2 of a "balanced" 2026/27 plan, CIP being strengthened
> "The Trust was reporting an adverse variance to plan position of £5.8m on the year to date. The variance to plan is in part driven by industrial action in April… work is being undertaken to strengthened the Cost Improvement Programme to enable the Trust to achieve a breakeven position." — p.113–114, [Finance, Investment and Performance Committee Report TB 42/26a]

**WORTH WATCHING** — CFO warns underlying deficit persists despite breakeven headline (from May minutes)
> "despite stronger foundations, an underlying deficit challenge remained and the need for hard decisions would remain in order to achieve savings requirements." — p.7 [Minutes of 13 May 2026, item 45/26], attributed to Chief Finance Officer Hardev Virdee (the outgoing CFO)

**WORTH WATCHING** — A&E four-hour performance below plan; Newham the most challenged site
> "the Trust delivered 74.31% against the four hour standard, 1.79% below plan. Newham performance was the most challenged of the three hospitals." — p.113, [FIP Committee Report TB 42/26a]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Complaints up 16% year-on-year to 2,466; whole-system complaints review deferred to 2026/27 amid recruitment freezes
> "The Trust recorded 2,466 complaints in 2025/26, a 16.27% increase compared with 2024/25… challenges recruiting to key complaints management posts. This has meant postponing some of our complaints management improvement priorities to 2026/27." — p.171–172, [Complaints and PALS Annual Report TB 45/26b]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Children stuck in hospital as placements fail; 37% jump in child mental-health social-care referrals (systemic flow signal)
> "Increase in the number of children and young people having extended stays in hospital due to behavioural and mental health issues due to availability of placements… a 37% increase in referrals made to children social care due to child mental health and behavioural concerns since 24-25." — p.147–148, [All Age Safeguarding Annual Report TB 45/26a]

**WORTH WATCHING** — SHMI on an upward trend; trust working with NHSE on whether SDEC coding distorts the metric
> "Summary Hospital-level Mortality Indicator (SHMI) remains within the expected range. Whilst the recent upward trend continues to be monitored… work continues with NHS England to understand the impact of Same Day Emergency Care activity on SHMI methodology." — p.116, [Quality Assurance Committee Report TB 42/26b]

**WORTH WATCHING** — Growth in clinical negligence claims costs flagged; committee asks for deeper report
> "The growth in claims costs was noted and the Committee asked for a more detailed report to be provided to the Quality Assurance Committee." — p.117, [Audit and Risk Committee Report TB 42/26c]

## 2. FOI leads

- **Group CFO / Whipps Cross CEO exits — settlement / recruitment terms.** Both departures are framed as career moves (Met Police; Dartford & Gravesham CEO). Request from Barts Health: any settlement or compromise agreements; VSM salary tiers being advertised for the three vacant senior posts (CFO, Whipps Cross CEO, Newham CEO). The Nom/Rem committee report (p.119, TB 42/26d) notes it discussed "salary tiers for vacant VSM posts being recruited to" — request those papers.

- **Group Director of Development departure (Aug 2025).** Nom/Rem report references "revised executive portfolios following the departure of the Group Director of Development in August 2025" (p.119, TB 42/26d) — an exec departure not otherwise announced. Request name, reason, and any settlement.

- **Royal London PFI / Managed Equipment Service market test.** The seal was used on "a deed in relation to a Managed Equipment Service Market Test relating to a Project Agreement dated 27 April 2006… between Barts Health NHS Trust and Capital Hospitals Ltd" (p.185, TB 46/26). Capital Hospitals is the Royal London/Barts PFI vehicle. Request the deed, the market-test outcome and any change in MES contract value — the PFI is one of the largest in the NHS.

- **National Submissions Audit.** Referenced by both Audit & Risk and FIP committees (p.113, p.117) covering DM01, RTT, UEC, cancelled operations and community data, with "reporting improvements… implemented as a result." Request the audit report and what data errors were found across the five statutory returns.

- **Limited-assurance internal audits.** QAC notes "limited assurance reports relating to complaints and nutrition and hydration" and the "Group Chief Nurse's response" (p.116, TB 42/26b). Request the full internal audit reports and management responses.

- **Full IPR chart data.** The Part 1 IPR is a slide deck; site-level and equity-broken-down figures did not extract. Request the underlying IPR data pack (RTT/A&E/diagnostics/cancer by hospital, equity wait-time tables by IMD/ethnicity/LD).

## 4. Assessment

Not a routine pack, but a broadly positive one — the trust is publicly banking a big Oversight Framework improvement and a 2025/26 breakeven. The genuine trust-level leads are: (1) the Segment 3→1 jump with the "biggest climber in the country" framing; (2) an unusual cluster of three senior group vacancies (CFO, Whipps Cross CEO, Newham CEO) simultaneously; and (3) the early-year finance wobble (£5.8m adverse at M2 against a plan the CFO himself calls underpinned by a persistent underlying deficit). The safeguarding paper's children's-placement/A&E signal and the SHMI upward trend are softer follow-ups. Part 2 (private) is not yet published — worth revisiting for the finance detail and any exec-departure specifics.
