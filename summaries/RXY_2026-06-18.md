## Why this extraordinary meeting was called

Single-purpose, 30-minute meeting (08:45–09:15, MS Teams) to approve year-end statutory documentation — Annual Report & Accounts 2025/26, External Audit Report, Letter of Representation, and Audit & Risk Committee reports — ahead of the end-June national reporting deadline. **[INFERENCE]** The "extraordinary" framing is procedural (a deadline sign-off slotted between scheduled boards), not a crisis call. But the documents being signed off contain the substance: an auditor control finding on the trust's governance statement, plus a year of CQC and independent-review findings on governance and patient safety. Lead on those.

## Top lines

[LEAD] Grant Thornton told the trust its draft Annual Governance Statement understated in-year control weaknesses and risked "misleading governance disclosures".
[Evidence] "The draft AGS did not fully reflect the extent of in-year governance, risk management and control weaknesses… There is risk that significant control deficiencies could be understated, resulting in incomplete and potentially misleading governance disclosures to stakeholders, and non-compliance with GAM requirements." — p.108, [External Audit Report, control finding 2 (Amber)]

[LEAD] An independent governance review (Moorhouse, Jan 2026) rated the trust's quality governance "Developing with significant Lagging features", citing unreliable safety controls and "fragmented governance architecture".
[Evidence] "Quality governance is 'Developing with significant Lagging features' in safety critical controls, governance discipline, assurance confidence, risk management, regulatory readiness and organisational culture." Key findings: "Inconsistent reliability of core safety controls; Fragmented governance architecture; Weak closed-loop assurance; Data quality and documentation issues; Patient and family voice not yet adequately heard; Regulatory readiness not yet evidenced." — p.45, [Annual Governance Statement]

[LEAD] CQC's March 2026 well-led inspection found significant risks missing from the corporate risk register and weak data scrutiny.
[Evidence] CQC identified "The BAF and Corporate Risk Register require development; Significant risks missing from the corporate risk register; Risk mitigations lacked clarity on ownership; Quality Impact Assessments were inconsistently applied; Committee workplans and data scrutiny require strengthening; Agency rostering oversight requires improvement." — p.45, [Annual Governance Statement, Well-Led Regulatory Feedback]

[LEAD] CQC issued a Section 29A warning notice in April 2025 after finding the trust routinely detained patients in s136 suites beyond the lawful expiry of their detention.
[Evidence] "a Section 29A Warning Notice being issued in April 2025 requiring urgent action. The warning notice concerns included: The Trust had been routinely detaining service users in Section 136 suites (health-based places of safety) beyond the expiry of their Section 136 detention under the Mental Health Act 1983, without an appropriate legal framework." — p.46, [Annual Governance Statement]

[WORTH WATCHING] Chair Dr Jackie Craissati is leaving, and the trust will share a "Joint Chair" with Kent Community Health FT from July 2026 — a step toward closer working between the two organisations.
[Evidence] "We will be welcoming new faces into our Board… including a Joint Chair working across our trust and Kent Community Health NHS Foundation Trust from July 2026." / "I want to take this opportunity to express my sincere thanks to our Chair, Dr Jackie Craissati, who this year will conclude her term." — p.07, [CEO statement]; Chair's own "final statement as Chair" — p.09, [Chair's statement]. (Note: CMO Dr Afifa Qazi already holds a dual CMO role across both trusts since Nov 2025 — p.50.)

[WORTH WATCHING] Internal audit gave only "limited assurance" on the trust's Fit and Proper Persons Test arrangements — the one limited-assurance review of the year.
[Evidence] "One area received limited assurance, this was the Fit and Proper Persons Test, where it was assessed that the effectiveness of some of the internal control arrangements provided 'limited assurance'." — p.49, [Audit & Risk Committee / Head of Internal Audit opinion]

[WORTH WATCHING] An independent rapid review (Dec 2025) into community incidents found weaknesses in risk assessment, safety planning and self-harm reporting thresholds.
[Evidence] "The review identified weaknesses in risk assessment, safety planning, incident investigation, documentation, communication with families, data quality and thresholds for reporting self harm." — p.45, [Annual Governance Statement, Independent Rapid Review of Community Incidents (McCallion, Hasler & Scott, December 2025)]
