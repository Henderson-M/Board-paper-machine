## Top lines

[LEAD] EPUT has now spent £17.1m in cash on the Lampard Inquiry, with a further £6.6m cost pressure flagged from the accounting treatment of the provision.
[Evidence] "As at the end of June 2026 (Month 3), cumulative cash expenditure incurred by the Trust in relation to the Lampard Inquiry totals £17.1m. During 2026/27, the Trust has incurred expenditure of £1.3m, of which £0.1m has been charged to the revenue position in accordance with accounting standards, with the remainder recognised within the provision." — p.46, [Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Report, section 2.4 Finance]. The board assurance framework separately records "Financial allocation £8.6m to end of 2026" and "(Note additional cost pressure of £6.6m to end of 2026 as consequence of accounting treatment)" — p.122, [BAF Report, SR5 Statutory Public Inquiry].

[LEAD] The Lampard Inquiry has issued EPUT with a Section 21 compulsion notice covering eight Rule 9 requests — logged in the trust's own risk register as "negative assurance".
[Evidence] "S21 Notice issued in respect of eight Rule 9s request from the Lampard Inquiry (negative assurance) - All Section 21 requests have been met" — p.122, [BAF Report, SR5 Statutory Public Inquiry, Level 3 (Independent) assurance column]. The same risk entry defines its own consequence as "Failure to respond resulting in the risk of a section 21 notice being issued to the Trust and the loss of confidence by the population of Essex" — i.e. the consequence the risk was written to avoid has already materialised. Current risk score 16 (C4 x L4) against a target of 8. [INFERENCE] The pack does not say when the notice was issued or which Rule 9 requests it covered.

[LEAD] The CQC's well-led inspection report on EPUT is written and with the trust — factual accuracy response due the day after this board meeting.
[Evidence] "The Trust has received the draft report from the CQC following the Well Led inspection (Jan-March 2026) for factual accuracy with a response due by 6 August 2026." — p.84, [CQC Assurance Report — August 2026, section 3.1].

[LEAD] EPUT issued an internal safety alert because ligature inspections were running late, blamed on estates capacity.
[Evidence] "Safety alert issued due to delays in ligature inspections linked to estates capacity, with ongoing risk tracking." — p.246, [Patient Safety Strategy Q1 2026/27, Learning Collaborative Partnership meeting June 2026]. Same paper records SIP improvement work "in response to PFD themes (e.g. ligature risk, handover, transitions, disengagement)".

[LEAD] The trust is £5.6m in deficit three months into the year and names its £47m efficiency programme as the single biggest threat to its finances, with delivery already £1.7m behind.
[Evidence] "The Month 3 in month deficit is £1.0m, £0.2m adverse to plan. The YTD deficit is £5.6m, £1.5m adverse to plan. Adverse variances is driven by Out of Area Placement costs, Pay costs - including unfunded pay award, the impact of past industrial action, and shortfalls on efficiency programme. The position includes £1.6m of Deficit Support Funding (DSF) relating to the annual DSF entitlement of £6.3m in FY2627." — p.57, [Quality and Performance Scorecard]. And: "The most significant risk to financial sustainability continues to be the delivery of the £47 million efficiency programme." — p.114, [BAF Report, SR8 Use of Resources]. Note the same BAF gives a different number in the action detail: "The target efficiency for FY26/27 is £43.1m with 7 key themes identified for delivery" — p.128, [BAF Report, SR8 action 14]. Efficiency is "£0.3m adverse to plan in Month 3 (£1.7m adverse YTD)" — p.58, [Quality and Performance Scorecard].

[LEAD] EPUT is being run almost entirely by interims: five separate leadership changes have had to be registered with the CQC since the last reporting period, including two joint interim CEOs, an interim CFO and an interim COO.
[Evidence] "Five (5) Leadership updates have been registered with the CQC since the last reporting period: - CQC Nominated Individual - Trust Chair - Interim Chief Executive Officers - Interim Chief Finance Officer and - Interim Chief Operations Officer." — p.84, [CQC Assurance Report, section 2.1]. Cause of the chair vacancy: "Following the death in May of our Chair, Hattie Llewelyn-Davies, the Board has put interim leadership arrangements in place in accordance with the Trust's constitution... The process to appoint an interim Chair is under way." — p.34, [Chair's Report (including Governance Update), section 2.1]. Previous CEO Paul Scott left to become CEO of East Suffolk and North East Essex FT (p.26, [Board Part 1 Minutes 3 June 2026, item 078/26]) and gave evidence to the Lampard Inquiry on 6 July (p.41, [CEO Report, 1.2]).

[LEAD] EPUT cut back the mortality data it reported to its board for Q4 2025/26, leaving the full dashboards and historic review progress incomplete — at the trust subject to a statutory inquiry into inpatient deaths.
[Evidence] "To maximise capacity for other key mortality priorities and to progress the development of new processes for automating the production of mortality data into the future, a reduced data process was undertaken for Q4. This reduced process has focused predominantly on Q4 and the mandated data reporting requirements under the national guidance. As a result, the full data dashboards and progress with historic reviews have not been completed this quarter. It is intended to retrospectively prepare this information in the Q1 2026/27 data process." — p.96, [Learning from Deaths Q4 2025/26 Report]. The quarter records 197 deaths, of which "6 deaths were inpatient deaths and 8 were nursing homes deaths", with 33 Stage 1 reviews still outstanding (p.99–100).

[WORTH WATCHING] 103 EPUT patients were in out-of-area beds at the end of June, and the trust has pushed its target date for eliminating inappropriate placements out to March 2029.
[Evidence] "At the end of June, 103 patients were placed out of area; 26 were deemed Inappropriate... The annual planning commitment has been reviewed and is now set to reduce Inappropriate Out of Area Placements to 0 by March 2029, following receipt of regional feedback on our planning submission." — p.56, [Quality and Performance Scorecard]. The measure was also reclassified: "From 27th March, OOA placements have been reclassified based on NHSE criteria. Where a patient is placed within EPUT boundaries, it will now be deemed Appropriate due to continuity of care principles being delivered." The finance committee noted this produced "a significant decrease in inappropriate out of area placements from March 2026" — p.64, [Committee Chairs Report, Finance & Performance Committee].

[WORTH WATCHING] EPUT's dispute with NHS Property Services over West Essex is heading towards arbitration, with DHSC and NHSE regional team at the table.
[Evidence] "The Trust continues to progress discussions in relation to the NHS Property Services disputes in West Essex. A pre-arbitration meeting has been agreed between EPUT, the Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC), NHS Property Services and the NHS England Regional Team." — p.47, [CEO Report, section 2.4].

[WORTH WATCHING] Ward staffing fill rates are deteriorating year on year and temporary staffing is running roughly 150 WTE above plan.
[Evidence] "June reported 25 wards having less than 90% fill rates against the target of <13, this remains outside of target. 2024-25 had an average of 15 wards not achieving target and in 2025-26, the average is 28 wards." And: "usage remains significantly above the planned trajectory, equating to approximately 150 WTE above target, indicating that the pace of delivery of the workforce efficiency programme is not yet sufficient to achieve the required reductions." Sickness absence "increased to 6.0% in June 2026" against a vacancy rate of 8.9%. — p.57, [Quality and Performance Scorecard].

[FOI] Request the terms of reference and report of the independent review EPUT has commissioned into its use of contactless patient monitoring technology — the exact subject the Lampard Inquiry took evidence on in July.
[Evidence] "Use of Contactless Patient Monitoring Technology - The Committee noted the revised SOP and the approved 6-8-week implementation plan, and that an independent review had been commissioned. Further assurances will be provided to the Audit Committee." — p.66, [Committee Chairs Report, Quality Committee]. The Inquiry "heard evidence around important issues they are considering making interim recommendations about, including observations and the use of technology" — p.41, [CEO Report, 1.2]. Ask EPUT for: the commissioning/scoping document, the reviewer's identity and fee, the revised SOP, and the report itself.

[FOI] Request EPUT's Cultural Review — commissioned externally, headline findings taken to a private board seminar in May 2026, final report still not delivered as of the June board.
[Evidence] "RS expressed support for the strategy and sought an update on the publication of the Trust's Cultural Review. AM advised that the Board Development Seminar scheduled for May would provide an opportunity to discuss the headline findings; however, the final Cultural Review report had not yet been received." — p.24, [Board Part 1 Minutes 3 June 2026, item 072/26]. Ask for the review report, the supplier and contract value, and the board seminar slide pack.

[FOI] Request EPUT's spend on external legal and programme support for the Lampard Inquiry response — the pack names the suppliers but not the money.
[Evidence] The BAF lists "Browne Jacobson" and "Essex Chambers" as key controls for the Inquiry response (p.122), and records: "A substantial programme redesign has been undertaken to strengthen the oversight and capacity in the team. This has included: The appointment of a dedicated Senior Responsible Officer; several staff secondments and transfers; increased legal, programme and clinical support; and development of a more structured Programme Management Office approach with further external support commissioned." — p.123, [BAF Report, SR5 actions]. Ask for contract values, terms and deliverables for Browne Jacobson, Essex Chambers and the "further external support"; and the breakdown of the £17.1m cumulative spend between legal fees, staff costs and other categories.
