# Shrewsbury and Telford Hospital NHS Trust (RXW) — Boards in Common (meeting in public) — 9 July 2026

- **Source:** https://www.sath.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/01-BiC-Meeting-in-Public-09.07.2026-COMBINED-PAPERS.pdf (combined papers) + https://www.sath.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/02-Public-Board-Information-Pack-09.07.2026.pdf (information pack)
- **Agenda:** https://www.sath.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/00-BiC-Public-Agenda-09.07.2026.pdf
- **Analysed:** 2026-07-06
- **NOTE — joint "Boards in Common":** SaTH (RXW) now meets jointly with Shropshire Community Health NHS Trust (SCHT) under a "Boards in Common"/alliance model, shared Group Chair (Andrew Morgan), Group CEO (Joanne "Jo" Williams) and Group exec team. Papers cover both trusts; this summary flags SaTH-relevant items. SaTH remains post-Ockenden with continuing maternity scrutiny. Correspondent: Caitlin.

## Top lines

[LEAD] SaTH has exited NHSE's Recovery Support Programme and jumped from the worst National Oversight Framework segment (5) to segment 3, after delivering its financial plan for the first time in a decade — but only on the back of £45.1m of national deficit-support funding that NHSE has warned must now be phased down.
[Evidence] "This risk score reduction was supported by the Finance Assurance Committee on 28 April 2026 due to the Trust exiting the Recovery Support Programme (RSP) and having delivered the 2025/26 financial plan." — p.14, [Minutes 14/5/26, item 017/26b]. "Congratulations also to SaTH for their improvement to Segment 3, up from Segment 5 previously, and a league table position of 78 out of 134, up from 96 previously." — p.4, [Minutes, item 009/26]. "This in-year delivery is in line with forecast after £45.1m of deficit support funding... this is the first time in 10 years that SaTH has delivered its financial plan... financial planning in future years would need to reflect a phased reduction in the Trust's deficit support figure." — p.12, [Minutes, item 015/26c].

[LEAD] SaTH's underlying finances remain deep in the red: a £30.49m in-year deficit plan pre deficit-support for 2026/27, an efficiency target of nearly £40m, and it is already £1.76m adverse to plan at month 2 (May), driven by industrial action and CIP slippage.
[Evidence] "The Trust submitted a finance plan to NHSE on 18th March which showed a deficit plan of £30.49m pre deficit support (DSF) which moves to breakeven with deficit support for the year. At the end of May (month two), the Trust has delivered a deficit position of £7.37m against a deficit plan of £5.61m pre deficit support funding, an unfavourable variance of £1.76m... The predominant driver of the variance is industrial action (£0.75m) and slippage against various efficiency schemes (£1.76m)." — p.96, [SaTH Integrated Performance Report, item 039/26d, Executive Summary]. Efficiency target "£39.98m in FY26/27... At the end of May, £4.13m has been delivered which is £1.76m less than plan." — p.162 region, [SaTH IPR finance].

[LEAD] An independent external review, commissioned to validate SaTH's progress against all 210 Ockenden maternity recommendations, is due to report "later this summer" — the trust will share its findings with families.
[Evidence] "working with families the Trust committed to an independent external review to validate our progress to date and to confirm that all recommendations have been fully delivered. This has been a substantial undertaking, involving more than 700 individual actions and over 1,200 pieces of supporting evidence. The report is due later this summer, and we will share the findings with families." — p.31, [Group Chief Executive's Report, item 035/26]. Also: "an independent external review has been commissioned to validate our progress and confirm that all actions recommendations have been fully delivered. The report is anticipated later this summer." — p.207, [SaTH Integrated Maternity & Neonatal Report, item 043/26].

[LEAD] SaTH's Ockenden action plan is now reported as 93% complete (196 of 210 evidenced and assured), with nine actions "de-scoped" as nationally-led (NHSE/CQC) and outside the trust's control; two actions have reverted to "not yet delivered" after the national Maternity & Neonatal Independent Senior Advisor role ended.
[Evidence] "Evidenced and Assured 196 (1) 93%... De-scoped 9 (2) 3.5%... two actions, linked to the National Maternity & Neonatal Independent Senior Advisor (MNISA) ending, have reverted to Not Yet Delivered and been descoped until National guidance is received on how to progress those actions again." — p.209-210, [SaTH Integrated Maternity & Neonatal Report, item 043/26].

[LEAD] SaTH's Type 1 (major A&E) four-hour performance sat at just 45% in May 2026 and average time to be seen rose to 129 minutes — despite the trust's headline UEC-recovery narrative on 12-hour waits and ambulance handovers.
[Evidence] "Type 1 performance decreased to 45% and Type 3 performance decreased to 90.7%." — p.95, [SaTH IPR, item 039/26d, Executive Summary]. "The average wait to be seen time increased to 129 minutes." — p.[SIIP Appendix 4 / IPR action plan], [Information Pack, SaTH IIP Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] SaTH has engaged external consultancy Nexus Consulting to advise on the "key drivers impacting ED performance"; three named UEC improvement actions are flagged "at risk".
[Evidence] "The Non-Admitted working group is awaiting feedback from Nexus Consulting on key drivers impacting ED performance which will help inform the group's work." — p.205, [SaTH Integrated Improvement Plan (SIIP), item 042/26]; same action listed "At Risk" — [Information Pack, SaTH IIP Report Appendix 4].

[WORTH WATCHING] A SaTH culture workstream is standing up a sub-group specifically to address NHS staff survey feedback on race discrimination — landing alongside the trust's response to Lord Mann's national NHS racism review, oversight of which sits with the People Committee.
[Evidence] "The Culture workstream is forming a sub-group to address the staff survey feedback relating to race discrimination. This is being worked on in collaboration with the Group Chief People Officer." — p.205, [SIIP, item 042/26] / [Information Pack SaTH IIP Report]. "The publication of Lord Mann's Review on Racism in the NHS, finds that racism, including Antisemitism and Islamophobia, remains a systemic issue in the NHS... NHS England has accepted all recommendations." — p.33, [GCE Report, item 035/26].

[WORTH WATCHING] Data-quality gap: SaTH reports "no change in the reporting of income due to the data warehouse issues at present which will be resolved during the first half of FY26/27" — activity/income figures are being run through workarounds while the warehouse is fixed.
[Evidence] "There is currently no change in the reporting of income due to the data warehouse issues at present which will be resolved during the first half of FY26/27. The Trust is actively making Commissioning Data Set (CDS) submissions through to the Secondary Uses Service (SUS) and completing Non-SUS aggregated contract monitoring." — p.162 region, [SaTH IPR finance, item 039/26d].

[WORTH WATCHING] SaTH is carrying a large capital programme — £160.9m total for 2026/27, of which £113.07m is the Hospitals Transformation Programme (Future Fit successor) — reported "on schedule, ahead of budget", but M2 spend is running well behind straight-line (9.5% vs expected 16.66%).
[Evidence] "External allocations have been set at £135.36m; this includes £113.07m for HTP... giving an overall capital programme for FY26/27 of £160.90m. At M2 FY26/27... total expenditure of £15.34m. This represents 9.5% of the overall spend required across FY26/27 against an expected 16.66%." — p.166 region, [SaTH IPR capital]. "assurance was provided to the Committee that the Programme remains on schedule, ahead of budget, and with no major issues or concerns." — p.7, [Minutes, item 012/26e, HTP Assurance Committee].

[WORTH WATCHING] Group CEO Jo Williams was invited to 10 Downing Street on 9 June to meet the Prime Minister and Health Secretary in recognition of SaTH's elective-waiting-time reduction; the trust reports zero English patients over 52 weeks and is briefing this as top-quartile RTT.
[Evidence] "On 9 June, I had the privilege of representing the Trust at 10 Downing Street, where I was invited to meet with the Prime Minister and the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care. The invitation recognised the significant contribution our Trust has made in reducing elective waiting times." — p.33, [GCE Report, item 035/26]. Note: Welsh-commissioned patients still show "11 x 78 weeks, 52 x 65 weeks, 171 x 52 weeks" (May 2026) — p.96, [SaTH IPR Executive Summary].

## Routine pack note

Not routine. The pack is dominated by a turnaround-success narrative (RSP exit, segment 5→3, first financial plan delivered in 10 years, Downing Street visit, top-quartile RTT), but the reporter-relevant substance sits in the caveats: the underlying £30.49m pre-support deficit and £40m efficiency ask, the still-pending independent Ockenden validation review, 45% Type 1 A&E, external consultants (Nexus) on ED, and a race-discrimination staff-survey workstream. Maternity remains the central watch item post-Ockenden and ahead of the Amos review (due 30 July 2026).
