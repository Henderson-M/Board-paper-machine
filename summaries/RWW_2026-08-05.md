# North Cheshire and Mersey NHS FT (Warrington and Halton Teaching Hospitals + Bridgewater Community Healthcare) board — 5 August 2026

**Scope note:** this is a SHARED pack. From 1 April 2026 Warrington and Halton Teaching Hospitals NHS FT (RWW) acquired Bridgewater Community Healthcare NHS FT (RY2) and renamed itself North Cheshire and Mersey NHS Foundation Trust (NCM). This single Part 1 (public) board pack covers both predecessor organisations. Findings below say which organisation they relate to where the pack distinguishes them ("WHH" = the acute trust, "BCH"/"Bridgewater" = the community trust, "NCM" = the merged body).

- **Source:** https://northcheshireandmersey.nhs.uk/about-us/how-we-work/board-meetings-and-papers/
- **Pack:** 1 file (combined Part 1 public pack, 482 pages / "Overall page N of 509" numbering in-document)
- **Analysed:** 2026-08-06

## Top lines

[LEAD] External auditors gave Warrington and Halton's 2025/26 accounts two value-for-money significant weaknesses — on financial sustainability and on urgent and emergency care performance — while Bridgewater's audit was clean.
[Evidence] "The Auditor's Annual Report identified two significant weaknesses: financial sustainability, including medium-term financial planning and CIP delivery, and performance, particularly urgent and emergency care. Governance arrangements were assessed as strong with no significant weaknesses in that domain." — p.164, [Audit Committee assurance report, BM/26/08/049d]. On the community trust: "KPMG intended to issue an unqualified audit opinion... No significant weaknesses were identified for value for money" — p.164, same paper.

[LEAD] The trust planned a £30.5m deficit for 2026/27 with no deficit support funding at all, and only got to a notional breakeven after "national escalation", £5m more income out of the ICB and NHSE agreeing to loosen its control total by £8.8m.
[Evidence] "The Trust submitted a deficit plan of £30.5m. The Trust was not allocated any deficit support funding (DSF) this year. Following national escalation, the Trust has negotiated an improved income position of £5m with C&M ICB which will reduce the deficit to £25.5m. NHSE have also indicated that the Trust's Control Total will move from £16.7m deficit to £25.5m deficit. The Trust will then be able to access DSF of £25.5m to reach a breakeven position." — p.84, [Integrated Performance Report, BM/26/08/049]

[LEAD] WHH ended 2025/26 £40.7m in deficit before deficit support, with an underlying deficit of £45.5m; Bridgewater ended £4.4m in deficit, £2.9m worse than plan.
[Evidence] "The Trust reported a M12 deficit of £40.7m before deficit support funding. This was primarily due to the stretch CIP target not being delivered (£11.2m) and unfunded integration costs (£0.8m)... The Trust's underlying deficit stood at £45.5m" and "[BCH] The Trust reported a M12 deficit position was £2.9m worse than plan at £4.4m due to stretch CIP not delivered." — p.385, [Committee Chairs Annual Reports — Finance, Sustainability and Performance Committee, BM/26/08/056]

[LEAD] PwC is embedded in the trust's finances: its deadlines gate access to NHSE deficit support, its "work" has forced new sign-off controls on bank staffing, its cost is itself listed as a risk to the plan, and it now runs the framework for oversight of enforcement undertakings.
[Evidence] "Q2 – All material contracts signed and 100% of the value of planned CIP is fully developed or implemented – Contracts signed and planned CIP is fully developed or implemented in line with PwC deadline therefore Q2 DSF should flow" and, in the same risk list, "Cost of support from PwC via the FPRM process continues." — p.84, [Integrated Performance Report]. Also "A number of additional controls have been put in place as a result of the PwC work including bank sign off by the Chief Nurse and Deputy Chief Nurse" — p.134, [IPR by Exception, indicator 81]. And, from the June minutes: NK "confirmed that oversight of enforcement undertakings would now be managed through a single PwC framework" — p.17, [Minutes of 3 June 2026]. PwC's own findings are summarised at p.386: "PwC reports for WHH highlighted a need for stronger financial discipline, recurrent savings delivery and productivity management... significant risks remained around delivery of a £30m+ savings programme (£21.5m BAU CIP and £13.1m stretch target)... Governance arrangements were viewed positively, but deliverability risks remained high."

[LEAD] The board is being asked to approve a further £7.177m of NHSE cash support for Q3, with the trust paying only 51 per cent of supplier invoices on time against a 95 per cent target and holding just 16 days of cash.
[Evidence] "The Trust Board is asked to approve up to £7.177m cash support for Quarter 3 2026/27... Increased cash management measures continue in line with NHSE guidance. As a result, BPPC remains low (51% against a target of 95%, 65% cumulative position in March 2026). Cash days are at 16 days" — p.85, [Integrated Performance Report]. At month 3 the deficit is "£14.3m which is £0.9m worse than plan" — p.130, [IPR by Exception, indicator 74].

[LEAD] The finance committee chair says the acquisition of Bridgewater was accelerated in part to ease the acute trust's financial pressures, and describes the position as "extremely constrained".
[Evidence] "I must acknowledge the significant financial challenges the Trust is currently facing in delivering recovery plans and improving productivity... the financial position remains extremely constrained... It is anticipated that the proposed acceleration of the Trust's acquisition of Bridgewater, subject to ICB approval, will allow some benefits to be realised during the 2026/27 financial year to help ease these pressures." — p.387, John Somers, Chair of FSPC, [Committee Chairs Annual Reports, BM/26/08/056]

[LEAD] Bed closures are being delivered as part of the cost improvement programme, signed off with "substantial assurance" after a single closure.
[Evidence] "Hot Topic – Bed Closures. [LEAD] The trust remains a national "alert outlier" for hip fracture mortality despite recent improvement, and expects to stay one.
