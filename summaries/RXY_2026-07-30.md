## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust dropped from NHS oversight segment 1 to segment 3 on the latest National Oversight Framework position, and fell from 13th to 33rd nationally among community and mental health trusts.
[Evidence] "The Trust's overall segmentation moved from 1 to 3 based on quarter 4 performance (25/26), with an average metric score of 2.31 (down from 2.02 in Quarter 3). This is primarily driven by the Trust's benchmarking position in the national Community Mental Health Survey and ranks the Trust 33rd nationally for community and mental health trusts, down from 13th in Quarter 3." — p.107, [Integrated Quality and Performance Report]; corroborated in CEO report: "this confirmed a change in the trust's segment rating from segment 1 to segment 3. This reflects a combination of staff and patient experience survey feedback, and sustained operational pressures." — p.25, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] Trust is one of only two nationally rated "worse than expected" in the 2025 CQC Community Mental Health Survey; its benchmarked overall experience fell from "about the same" to "worse than most trusts", with 16 questions below the expected range (up from one in 2024).
[Evidence] "Overall experience is now rated 'worse than most trusts', having been 'about the same' in 2024, and 16 questions fall below the expected range... The overall experience score is 6.1 out of 10, the second lowest in six years, and the Trust is one of only two nationally rated 'worse than expected' across the survey as a whole." — p.141, [CQC Community Mental Health Survey]. Employment support scored 1.4/10; the paper offers "reasonable assurance on diagnosis and limited assurance on response." — p.142

[LEAD] A Governance Improvement Programme is running in response to a March 2026 CQC Well-Led inspection and a preceding independent governance review, both of which found the trust's board assurance framework and risk register underdeveloped, with significant risks missing from the register.
[Evidence] "The GIP remains the Trust's response to the March 2026 CQC Well-Led inspection and the preceding independent governance review... Both the CQC and preceding independent reviews found the BAF and Corporate Risk Register underdeveloped, with some significant risks not on the register, and that risk mitigations lacked detail and did not show clear ownership." — p.201 & p.204, [Governance Improvement Plan]. Programme monitored weekly by a working group of the CEO, Acting Chief Nurse, Trust Secretary "and external support"; first full progress report due September 2026.

[WORTH WATCHING] Colin Lynch has become joint chair of the trust and Kent Community Health Foundation Trust — a shared chair across the county's mental health and community providers — yet a planned joint committee-in-common between the two trusts has now been dropped.
[Evidence] "I am delighted to welcome Colin Lynch, who joins us as joint Chair of our trust and Kent Community Health NHS Foundation Trust." — p.24, [Chief Executive's Report]. But: "A joint committee-in-common with KCHFT was expected in 2027/28 and it has been decided that this will not be taken forward now." — p.203, [Governance Improvement Plan]

[WORTH WATCHING] Kent and Medway providers have collectively signed off, via the system Joint Committee, that their current care models are "clinically and financially unsustainable" and that incremental change won't be enough — moving to a four-layer model with acute providers expected to work "significantly more closely together".
[Evidence] "The current model has reached its limits and is no longer sustainable... Incremental change will not deliver the scale of improvement required... There was a clear and consistent articulation from provider organisations that their current models are both clinically and financially unsustainable in their present form." — p.195, [System Governance and Joint Committee Alignment]. The Joint Committee is confirmed as "the primary forum for system-level decision making and accountability", with a public report to all partner boards after each meeting (ICB CEO Adam Doyle named as author).

[WORTH WATCHING] Children and young people's mental health and all-age eating disorder services transferred from North East London Foundation Trust to KMMH on 1 April 2026, and are carrying agency and cost pressures the trust is still working through with NHSE.
[Evidence] Time-limited CYPMH & AAED Services Committee set up "to give the Board assurance on the transitional risks arising from the transfer of Children and Young People's Mental Health (CYPMH) and All-Age Eating Disorder (AAED) services from NELFT to KMMH on 1 April 2026. These risks cover quality, safety, performance, workforce stability, and financial and operational resilience." — p.204, [Governance Improvement Plan]. Agency in June "GBP 0.49m relates to agency staff used in CYP/AAED services"; capital "CDEL pressures due to CYP transfer being worked through with NHSE." — p.161, p.156, [Finance Report – Month 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] Trust is on plan at Month 3 (£0.01m surplus against breakeven) but forecasts a £4.5m underlying gap fully dependent on delivering CIP, and is over its NHSE agency and bank caps.
[Evidence] "Total forecast pre CIP (4.5) / Planned CIP 4.5 / Likely Forecast 0.0 / ... Worst Case (CIP non delivery) (4.5)." — p.159. Agency "Forecast GBP 7.16m against cap of GBP 6.46m"; bank "Forecast GBP 21.79m against cap of GBP 16.90m." — p.156. Finance paper flags: "Risk of delivery of finance targets may result in sanctions from NHSE." — p.152, [Finance Report – Month 3]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has flagged an unexplained drop in reported patient deaths from February 2026 and is investigating whether deaths are being missed, while suspected suicides rose in Q1.
[Evidence] "There has been a marked reduction in deaths reported in Q1... As Trust activity and referral levels have remained stable, there is currently no clear explanation for this change... A comprehensive review has been undertaken by both the Business Intelligence and RiO teams to verify the integrity of data flows... with no issues identified to date... Further investigation is therefore ongoing, including engagement with Integrated Care Board (ICB) colleagues to explore whether additional insight can be obtained from primary care records." — p.75/p.79. "Despite the overall reduction in reported deaths, suspected suicides have increased in Q1." — p.76, [Quarterly Mortality Report Q1]. One inpatient death met the PSII criteria; two homicides where the perpetrators were patients. — p.78/p.79

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust proposes standing down its dedicated Mental Health Act Committee and moving MHA oversight to quarterly Quality Committee slots — while itself flagging that MHA compliance was a past CQC finding.
[Evidence] "The Board should particularly confirm whether quarterly Mental Health Act oversight within Quality Committee provides sufficient scrutiny, given the previous CQC finding." — p.200. "Mental Health Act compliance, including record-keeping, was a CQC finding from several years ago, so the Board should confirm that quarterly oversight within the Quality Committee provides sufficient scrutiny before the change takes effect." — p.204, [Governance Improvement Plan]

[FOI] Request the independent governance review that sits behind the Governance Improvement Programme, and identify the "external support" firm advising it.
[Evidence] The GIP is "the Trust's response to the March 2026 CQC Well-Led inspection and the preceding independent governance review" and the weekly working group includes unnamed "external support" — the firm and the review report/terms of reference are not disclosed in the pack. — p.201, [Governance Improvement Plan]. FOI to Kent and Medway Mental Health Trust: terms of reference, final report and cost of the independent governance review; name/contract of the external support provider.

[FOI] Request the CQC S29A warning notices and the March 2026 Well-Led inspection report/correspondence.
[Evidence] BAF records completed actions "Addresses contents of the S29a warning notice" following community and Place of Safety inspections; the Quality Committee reported "all CQC warning notices have been lifted." — p.36, p.73. The notices themselves and the underlying inspection findings are not reproduced. FOI to the trust and/or via CQC: the S29A warning notices, inspection reports and the trust's action plans.
