## Top lines

[LEAD] CEO report names Sandwell & West Birmingham (Midland Met) as one of 12 trusts individually visited by the Amos national maternity investigation, with a neonatal mortality rate more than 5% above comparable trusts and "widely reported racism and discrimination".
[Evidence] "Sandwell and West Birmingham NHS Trust (Midland Metropolitan University Hospital) were one of 12 trusts to receive an individual site visit and a published report. The findings are serious: a neonatal mortality rate more than 5% above comparable trusts, persistent staffing shortfalls, an executive team seen as insufficiently visible and witnessed and widely reported racism and discrimination affecting both families and staff, which the investigation treats as safety critical." — p.30, [CEO Report, Item 7]

[LEAD] CEO uses a single case to make a sharp public criticism of unwarranted variation in urgent care across the cluster, naming specific localities where out-of-hours community response is absent.
[Evidence] "an identical 80-year-old who falls at 9pm receives a 24/7 community response in Wolverhampton, a response until 10pm in Dudley, nothing in Sandwell after 6pm, and nothing at all in Solihull at any hour. The variation is not clinical; it has grown out of organisational choice." — p.28, [CEO Report, Item 7]

[LEAD] Board asked to approve an £84.32m three-year investment in autism/ADHD pathways, against 33,891 people waiting over 52 weeks (13,000+ beyond 104 weeks) and a stated circa 58% capacity shortfall.
[Evidence] "Investment of £84,320,000 over 3 years (inclusive of Right to Choose commitment, waiting list initiative and transformation)"; "33,891 people reported as waiting more than 52 weeks for autism or ADHD assessment. The business case indicates a circa 58% capacity shortfall". — p.183 (finance table) / p.184, [Neurodevelopmental Pathway paper, Item 14]

[LEAD] Both ICBs' 2025/26 accounts closed with unqualified audit opinions and delivered surplus positions; F&P committee nonetheless gave only "qualified assurance" on the 2026/27 opening position, calling delivery "fragile".
[Evidence] 25/26 year end: "BSOL ICB at year end surplus of £14.9m and BC ICB at £46k surplus"; F&P 26 June: "RECEIVED qualified assurance, recognising that although system grip is strong, delivery remains fragile and continued focus on improvement is required". — p.47 / p.46, [Joint F&P Committee Escalation Report, Item 9]

[LEAD] CEO discloses three live and "material" contract disputes with named providers totalling roughly £13m, all under NHSE-supported management.
[Evidence] "Three contract disputes remain live and material: a £6.89m gap with Birmingham Women's and Children's, £1m with South Warwickshire, and the circa £5m Section 117 dispute with Black Country Healthcare, all are under active management through engagement with the providers and NHS England." — p.29, [CEO Report, Item 7]

[LEAD] CEO sets out the ICB-merger footprint question, flagging that the future decision could pull Coventry City Council into a merged Birmingham & Solihull / Black Country ICB coterminous with the West Midlands Combined Authority.
[Evidence] "The question is whether to extend the current cluster footprint to include Coventry City Council at the point Birmingham & Solihull and the Black Country ICBs merge... it is proposed that on 1 April 2027, ICB clusters will be replaced by merged ICBs serving populations of at least 1.5 million". — pp.27–28 / p.23, [CEO Report, Item 7 / Chair's Report, Item 6]

[WORTH WATCHING] Confirmed left-shift capital allocation of c.£244.9m across 2026/27–2029/30, but CEO cautions the baseline "is not yet spendable cash".
[Evidence] "NHS organisations across our area have secured a confirmed allocation for 'left-shift' of approximately £244.9m across 2026/27 to 2029/30 — around £194.6m of programme capital and £50.3m for primary care. With the Heartlands Emergency Village (£49.7m) the largest single scheme... a confirmed baseline is not yet spendable cash". — p.29, [CEO Report, Item 7]

[WORTH WATCHING] EPRR risk register carries four red risks (score 16+) unchanged since first opened in April 2023, including business continuity and cyber attack both at 20.
[Evidence] EP004 "Lack of embedded business continuity arrangements" 20/20; EP005 "Cyber attack" 20/20; EP007 infectious disease 16/16; EP008 "Pandemic Outbreak" 16/16 — "While the risk scores have not yet reduced". — p.128–129, [EPRR Annual Report, Item 11]

[WORTH WATCHING] Audit Committees escalated that ISFE2 financial-ledger workarounds "remain necessary but were not sustainable long term", and that Freedom to Speak Up Guardians reported staff raising concerns about the Management of Change process.
[Evidence] "the ICBs' Freedom to Speak Up Guardians had reported staff raising concerns in relation to the Management of Change process. The committees had been assured that the concerns of staff had been appropriately escalated and responded to". — p.11, [Minutes 11 May 2026, Item 10 record]

[FOI] Sir Jim Mackey letter to all systems on strategic-commissioning ambition — appended to the CEO report but not reproduced in the public pack; ICB drafting a response for its private session.
[Evidence] "Sir Jim Mackey, CEO of the NHS had written to all systems asking them to set out their ambition in relation to strategic commissioning. A copy of the letter was appended to the report and a draft response was due to be discussed in the Board's private session". — p.14, [Minutes 11 May 2026]

## FOI leads

- **Mackey strategic-commissioning letter and the cluster's response.** Request from NHS Black Country ICB and/or NHS Birmingham & Solihull ICB: the letter from Sir Jim Mackey to systems on strategic commissioning ambition, plus the ICB cluster's draft/final response. Referenced (p.14) as appended to a board paper and discussed in private session, but not in the public pack. Why it matters: reveals the national "test your plans" framework and what this cluster committed to.
- **NHSE / Karin Smyth footprint correspondence.** Request the "recent letters from Sir Glen Burley and Karin Smyth MP" on the merged-ICB footprint (p.28), and the ICB's 14 July response to NHSE on future footprint. Why: sets out the Coventry / WMCA coterminosity decision ahead of NHSE's September ruling.
- **Amos investigation site report for Sandwell & West Birmingham (Midland Met).** Request the individual published site-visit report referenced at p.30, and the ICB/LMNS action plan responding to the neonatal mortality and racism findings. Why: names one of 12 trusts singled out; findings described as "safety critical".
- **Section 117 dispute with Black Country Healthcare.** Request correspondence, value and basis of the "circa £5m Section 117 dispute" (p.29) between the ICB and Black Country Healthcare NHS FT. Why: named c.£5m dispute under NHSE-managed resolution.
- **Management of Change / FTSU concerns.** Request the Freedom to Speak Up Guardians' report(s) referenced at Item 10 (p.11) covering staff concerns about the reorganisation, and the Remuneration Committee papers on the "specific workforce issue" it received assurance on (p.12). Why: staff-raised concerns during a running-cost-reduction restructure.
