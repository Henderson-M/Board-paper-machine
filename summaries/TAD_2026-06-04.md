## Top lines

[LEAD] Bradford District Care and Airedale Foundation Trust are creating a shared chief people officer post — the first joint executive role under their new chair-in-common arrangement, with further shared executive roles flagged for feasibility work.
[Evidence] "Establish a shared Chief People Officer (CPO) role across AFT and BDCT… Single post-holder, dual accountability to both CEOs." and "Shared executive roles considered: Parallel track for feasibility scan and lessons learned." — p.49, p.51, p.50, [AFT and BDCT People and OD Collaboration]

[LEAD] The joint CPO move was triggered by the Bradford District Care chief people officer resigning in December 2025; the shared role spans a five-phase plan running to a 12-month review in March–May 2027 and "potential place-wide expansion."
[Evidence] "The BDCT CPO gave notice of resignation from the position in December 2025" and "12-month effectiveness review to confirm sustainability of the model and readiness for Year 2 benefits and potential place-wide expansion." — p.48, p.56, [AFT and BDCT People and OD Collaboration]

[LEAD] The trust's People and Culture Committee escalated an "alert" on ethnic disparity in recruitment and disciplinary processes, plus higher reported bullying of disabled staff.
[Evidence] "Recruitment data showed disparity, with white candidates more likely to be appointed after shortlisting, and disproportionality remained in disciplinary processes for ethnically diverse staff." and "Disabled staff reported higher levels of perceived bullying and harassment, particularly from colleagues and managers." — p.78, [AAAD People and Culture Committee 21 May 2026]

[LEAD] The trust is forecasting planned breakeven at month one but reports its efficiency programme already off track, with downside risk of up to £6.6m and a position needing c.£2.8m of de-risking.
[Evidence] "The efficiency programme is offtrack at Month 1 by £118k and is forecast to underdeliver by £780k for the year." and "the financial position still requires de-risking by c£2.8m." Risk-assessed delivery "ranges from £776k to £8,632k, with a likely case additional risk of £6,629k (equates to a 5.2% delivery risk of the target)." — p.114, p.115, [Finance Report Month 1]

[LEAD] The trust breached its new bank-staff spending cap by £0.4m year-to-date in month one (though forecasts recovery), alongside continued financial risk from out-of-area placements running above plan.
[Evidence] "There is a new Bank cap this year which we have breached by £0.4m YTD but forecast to achieve by the year end." and "current numbers reported at 34 against a plan of 33" out-of-area placements — p.114, [Finance Report Month 1]; p.22, [Public Board minutes 2 April 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The integrated performance report rates Access & Flow, Financial Sustainability and Estates as "Low" assurance, naming dental waits at 52.2 per cent against a 92 per cent target.
[Evidence] "Access & Flow – Limited / Low assurance / Financial Sustainability – Low assurance / Estates and Environment – Low assurance" and "Reduced access performance (e.g. Dental RTT 52.2% vs 92% target)" — p.121, [Board Integrated Performance Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust says it has moved up the NHS Oversight Framework from Segment 4 to Segment 3, but that further progression depends on financial delivery, and CYP access metrics are challenged.
[Evidence] "the Trust had improved its National Oversight Framework position from Segment Four to Segment Three" and "progression beyond Segment Three was dependent on financial delivery." — p.23, [Public Board minutes 2 April 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Mental Health Legislation Committee escalated tribunal delays leaving some patients undischarged without a hearing, and extended use of the Section 136 suite reducing availability.
[Evidence] "Concerns were also raised regarding tribunal delays, which had resulted in some patients not receiving a hearing before discharge, and highlighted extended use of the Section 136 suite impacting availability." — p.71, [AAAD Mental Health Legislation Committee 21 May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] The Finance and Performance Committee approved a 2026/27 contract with private mental health provider Cygnet and a "Somerset surplus asset disposal" — both recommended to the board.
[Evidence] "The Committee approved and recommended Board: 2026/27 Cygnet contract to (contracts update) / Somerset Surplus Asset Disposal" — p.112, [AAAD Finance and Performance Committee 27 May 2026]

[WORTH WATCHING] West Yorkshire mental-health provider collaborative contracts expire March 2027 and NHSE is reshaping collaborative "footprints" and lead-provider geographies under the Provider Selection Regime.
[Evidence] "The current contracts for the MHLDA Provider Collaboratives end in March 2027. National discussions are underway regarding changes to the footprints of the collaboratives. This in turn will lead to a change in Lead Providers." — p.114, [Finance Report Month 1]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's Freedom to Speak Up guardian logged 130 cases in 2025/26 (up 18), with two completed fact-finds both confirming detriment was caused to staff who spoke up.
[Evidence] "A total of 130 cases have been reported to the BDCT Guardian team during 2025/26 (an increase of 18 cases compared to the previous year)." and "Two fact finds have been completed during the year and both found that detriment had been caused." — p.81, [Freedom to Speak Up Guardian Annual Report 2025/26]

[FOI] The CEO report references a coroner's narrative report identifying GP-to-community-mental-health pathway issues, and three new patient safety incident investigations into three suspected suicides — but the pack does not publish the coroner's findings or the PSII scope.
[Evidence] "a Coroner's narrative report, confirming that it identified pathway issues between general practice and community mental health services" and "three new Patient Safety Incident Investigations (PSIIs) reported since the last report relating to three suspected suicides all in the mental health care group." — p.20, [Public Board minutes 2 April 2026]; p.45, [Chief Executive's Report] — request the coroner's regulation 28 / narrative report and the PSII terms of reference from the trust.

[FOI] The People and Culture Committee noted "six employment tribunals" being closely monitored, with no detail in the public pack on subject matter or claimants.
[Evidence] "six employment tribunals were being closely monitored, and that a formal review of e-rostering was underway." — p.21, [Public Board minutes 2 April 2026] — request the list of live employment tribunal claims (subject category, e.g. discrimination/whistleblowing/unfair dismissal) from the trust.
