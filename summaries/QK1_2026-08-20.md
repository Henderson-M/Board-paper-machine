## Top lines

[LEAD] LLR ICB's external auditors issued a section 30 letter over its 2025/26 year-end deficit position — disclosed in a single line of an assurance paper, with no detail of what the letter says.
[Evidence] "The LLR ICB Board approved the LLR Annual Report and Accounts for 2025/26, noting the section 30 letter issued by the external auditors in relation to the year-end deficit position." — p.19/147, [ICBIC-26-41, LLR ICB and N ICB Annual Assessment of Performance and Annual Report 2025/26]. The equivalent sentence for Northamptonshire ICB records approval with no such letter. The pack does not reproduce the letter, name the auditor, quantify the deficit, or describe the auditor's findings. `[UNVERIFIED]` The statutory basis and effect of a "section 30 letter" is not stated in the pack and is not characterised here.

[LEAD] NHS England has instructed clustered ICBs to merge into single statutory organisations by 1 April 2027, in a letter from deputy chief executive Glen Burley — with a population floor of 1.5 million.
[Evidence] "Toby Sanders noted the appendix to the paper, which is a letter from Glen Burley, Deputy Chief Executive of NHS England, dated 1 April 2026, setting out the need for clustered ICBs to merge by 1 April 2027 … The message in the letter is clear, in that organisations need to merge to where they cover a population of 1.5m." — p.9/147, [ICBIC-26-38, Draft Minutes of 18 June 2026]. Chief executive Toby Sanders also flagged a structural problem: "One potential issue was noted, in that the cluster does not align with LAs or strategic authorities."

[LEAD] The cluster's restructure puts roughly a third of staff at risk, with 70 already gone on voluntary redundancy by end of June and about 20 more to follow.
[Evidence] "Staff are split roughly into thirds, 1/3 slotted in, 1/3 ringfence and 1/3 at risk. At the end of June there will have been 70 staff left due to voluntary redundancy and a further 20 later in the year. This has created gaps in teams" — p.8/147, [ICBIC-26-38, Draft Minutes of 18 June 2026].

[LEAD] Secure and specialist services at St Andrew's Healthcare in Northampton transfer to Northamptonshire Healthcare FT in September, with the independent provider still under enhanced oversight.
[Evidence] "St Andrew's Healthcare remains under significant quality pressure and continues to be subject to enhanced oversight arrangements through the Integrated Oversight and Assurance Group (IOAG) … Earlier this month, St Andrew's Healthcare and NHFT confirmed that a number of secure and specialist services at the Northampton site will transfer to NHFT in September." — p.131/147, [ICBIC-26-47, Board Briefing – St Andrews Healthcare Northamptonshire Quality Position]. Background: NHSE told all ICBs "that all patients should be repatriated in March 2026. This was a national decision made, however, due to the complexity of patients at the site, has been very hard to enact, with slow progress being made" — p.4/147, [Draft Minutes].

[WORTH WATCHING] Northamptonshire Healthcare FT has been confirmed eligible to progress an Integrated Health Organisation model for Northamptonshire following a nationally assessed process.
[Evidence] "Following a nationally assessed process, Northamptonshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust (NHFT) has been confirmed as eligible to progress an Integrated Health Organisation (IHO) approach for Northamptonshire." — p.13/147, [ICBIC-26-40, Chief Executive Report].

[WORTH WATCHING] The cluster reports a break-even 2026/27 position, but only after £5.1m of non-recurrent support — all of it going to LLR, none to Northamptonshire — against £5.2bn of planned spend.
[Evidence] "The full year reported forecast is a breakeven position across LNR ICBs of £0.0m after £5.1m non-recurrent support (£5.1m LLR and £0m Northamptonshire), which is in line with planned spend of £5.2 billion. There are currently three contracts still being disputed, which could impact the budgets" — p.8/147, [Draft Minutes, reporting the Month 1 position]. The August pack carries the Month 3 position at [ICBIC-26-48, M3 Finance Assurance Report, p.136/147].

[WORTH WATCHING] Board members were warned that workforce loss from the restructure is itself an ongoing risk to the ICBs' ability to function.
[Evidence] "Simone Jordan noted the size and scale of the changes taking place and praised the executives for managing the process, along with attending additional committees. The loss of the workforce and capacity needs to be recognised as an ongoing risk." — p.8/147, [Draft Minutes].

[WORTH WATCHING] Children's speech and language therapy and ADHD waits are long enough that children are ageing out of the children's list before being seen.
[Evidence] "there is a national shortage of therapists who work in SALT, and this is causing some children to transition to the adult lists before receiving treatment" — p.5/147, [Draft Minutes].
