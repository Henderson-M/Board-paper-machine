## Top lines

[LEAD] Independent well-led review describes the Christie board as having moved on from an "adversarial" culture marked by a NED/executive divide, crediting the new chair.
[Evidence] "the Board culture has transitioned from an adversarial environment characterised by a divide between Non-Executive Directors (NEDs) and executives to one described to us as having trust, candour, and feeling safer. This shift is largely attributed to the appointment of the new Chair" — p.171, [Independent well-led development review, ConsultOne, Item 18/26c]

[LEAD] The Christie is pursuing Advanced Foundation Trust status and "Integrated Health Organisation" status, with four named consultancies engaged on the bid — ConsultOne/AuditOne (well-led + application), PwC (financial modelling), The PSC (medium-term plan/cancer standards) and Deloitte (EPR).
[Evidence] "an assessment & assurance of our ability to deliver our Medium Term Plan (MTP) including achievement of Cancer standards being undertaken by The PSC, and an assessment of our financial plans and modelling by PWC. In addition to these assurance reviews a compliance and quality assessment of our AFT application, has also been carried out by ConsultOne." — p.164, [Independent well-led development review, Item 18/26c]; "Achieve Advanced Foundation Trust status" listed as a 2026/27 annual objective — p.160, [Strategic & Annual Objectives 2026/27, Item 18/26c]

[LEAD] The trust's flagship electronic patient record programme is flagged as at risk of slipping beyond May 2028, with delivery dependent on Cabinet Office approval.
[Evidence] "Delivery timeline for the Electronic Patient Record (EPR) may slip beyond May 2028, compounded by uncertainty regarding Cabinet Office approval and procurement timelines." — p.143, [Senior Management Committee AAA Report (May 2026), Item 18/26ai]

[LEAD] The well-led review warns the financially "outlier" trust relies heavily on non-recurrent savings, including bank interest that may fall as capital spending rises.
[Evidence] "Financially, the Trust is an outlier, delivering a surplus and maintaining segment one performance within the National Oversight Framework... However, the Trust relies heavily on non-recurrent savings including bank interest, which may decrease as capital spending increases as the Trust seeks to deliver its strategic ambitions." — p.170-171, [Independent well-led development review, Item 18/26c]

[LEAD] The trust's financial-balance strategic risk score rose to 15 in Q4 2025/26 (from 5 in Q3), driven by the 2026/27 value improvement programme, which remains high risk with red-rated elements.
[Evidence] BAF Risk 10 Financial balance: "Q3 25/26 [5]... Q4 25/26 [15]... Current Risk Score 15 / Relates to VIP 26/27" — p.146, [Board Assurance Framework, Item 18/26b]; "The VIP programme remains rated high risk (score 16) with multiple red-rated elements" — p.143, [SMC AAA Report, Item 18/26ai]

[WORTH WATCHING] The 2026/27 cost-improvement (VIP) target of £25.5m carries a recurrent shortfall, with a risk-adjusted gap of £7.7m unidentified at month 2.
[Evidence] "Identified in-year VIP is £25.3m against a target of £25.3m. The VIP shortfall against the recurrent VIP target is £1.9m (RAG rated shortfall of £7.7m)... Non-recurrent identified VIP is £14.6m against a target of £12.6m" — p.40, [Integrated Performance Quality & Finance Report, Item 17/26b]; "the Christie has a challenging cost improvement target of £25.5m" — p.69, [Value Improvement Programme, Item 17/26c]

[WORTH WATCHING] Well-led review found ~500 active clinical audit/improvement projects open without clear outcomes, short of the trust's 90% completion goal.
[Evidence] "We noted a significant challenge in relation to closing the loop of audit and improvement projects with approximately 500 active projects and clinical audits without clear outcomes at the time of our review, falling somewhat short of the strategic goal to complete 90% of registered initiatives." — p.174, [Independent well-led development review, Item 18/26c]

[WORTH WATCHING] IPQFR flags rising reliance on temporary staffing, with agency and bank spend both rising month-on-month, particularly in medical staffing.
[Evidence] "Workforce-related indicators show increasing reliance on temporary staffing, with both agency and bank spend rising month-on-month, particularly within medical staffing, indicating underlying capacity pressures." — p.25, [Integrated Performance Quality & Finance Report, Item 17/26b]

[WORTH WATCHING] Well-led review identifies a perceived conflict of interest in the Freedom to Speak Up Guardian being line-managed by the workforce director, and notes a "significant increase" in FTSU cases in 2025.
[Evidence] "there is a noted risk regarding the Guardian being line managed by the Director of Workforce, which can create a perceived conflict of interest... FTSU cases saw a significant increase in 2025, which the Trust tentatively attributes to the high profile of the current Guardian." — p.172, [Independent well-led development review, Item 18/26c]

[FOI] Two separate well-led recommendations — review the rationale for not having a finance committee, and how the audit committee demonstrates its delegated duties — sit alongside two undisclosed external assurance reviews (PwC financial modelling; The PSC MTP/cancer assessment). Request from the trust: the full PwC and The PSC reports, the ConsultOne AFT application compliance assessment, and any board minutes/terms of reference for the proposed Transformation Assurance Committee.
[Evidence] "Review rationale for not having a finance committee." / "Consider how the Audit Committee can more effectively demonstrate fulfilment of its delegated duties." — p.176, [Independent well-led development review recommendations, Item 18/26c]; "Transformation Assurance Committee in place – first meeting 1st October" — p.165, [same paper]
