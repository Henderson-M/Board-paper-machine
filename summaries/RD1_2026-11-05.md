## Top lines (RUH-relevant highlights — full set in the RN3 summary)

[LEAD] BSW Hospitals Group has formally entered turnaround and told NHS England it will end the year £62m adverse to plan (£78m deficit post-DSF), with a 12-week recruitment freeze; RUH's own scenarios run to a £48m worst case against plan.

[LEAD] The Group's BAF warns liquidity is "severe" and all three trusts could run out of cash within six months; RUH expects cash to become "critical" in Q4 without recovery, commissioner cash movement or revenue support.

[LEAD] Fire safety is non-compliant at every site; at RUH specifically, none of 1,512 fire doors passed, the estimated remedial cost is £47.6m (£35.7m unfunded) plus £2m for residences, and the executive is taking legal advice on the Board's liability.

[LEAD] RUH has a confirmed 95-bed winter gap against a modelled 135-bed deficit (up from a previously reported 72-bed gap) and says UEC is "not considered locally recoverable without system delivery"; mental-health ED waits over 24 hours rose from 9 to 26 in a month.

[LEAD] Leadership changes at RUH: Simon Sethi is now Chief Operating Officer after Bernie Bluhm's retirement, and Rebecca Maxwell has become Chief Medical Officer.

[WORTH WATCHING] RUH's shared-EPR cutover is expected to require a six-week reduction in activity in 2027/28, logged as a risk to income and elective waits; its finance committee cautioned against fixing the reduction at 25% before specialty modelling.

[WORTH WATCHING] RUH has the highest C. diff rate in the South West and an NHSE/UKHSA external infection-control review has been requested.

[WORTH WATCHING] RUH is standing up a Commercial Programme including dedicated "Private Care" capacity and an external Commercial Advisory Group.

[FOI] Request RUH's revenue-support PDC application and the "£20.4m submission" referenced in the Chief Executive's report.

[FOI] Request from RUH the number, nature and outcome (including any settlement sums) of the six employment tribunal claims and current staff suspensions referred to in the September People and Culture report.
