## Top lines

[LEAD] Royal Papworth, invited by the national team in July to apply for Advanced Foundation Trust status, is being asked by its executives whether to defer the application by four to eight weeks because it cannot give assurance on diagnostic (DM01) performance, a risk the NHSE regional team has flagged.
[Evidence] "The Trust was approached by the national team in July 2026 to enter the application process for AFT." p.26, [Application for Advanced Foundation Trust Status, Item 3.i]. "The Trust has been working to a submission date of 2 October 2026, targeting Trust Board sign-off on 1 October 2026. NHS England/DHSC had previously suggest an indicative Board-to-Board date in early November". p.26. "The Trust recognises that the combination of the current level of underperformance and the relative maturity of the recovery plan means that the Board cannot yet provide the same level of assurance for DM01 as it can for a number of the other access standards. This represents a risk to the AFT application and has been raised by the NHSE regional team as a key consideration for our application." p.29. "There is a tangible risk that submitting an application before sufficient evidence is available could result in challenge, deferral or rejection through the national process." p.29. Recommendation: "If the Board does not have sufficient levels of assurance, it is asked to consider whether to defer the AFT application by 4-8 weeks". p.30. The paper also lists the 34-statement board certificate across six domains (p.26) and says the "Access and delivery of services" domain is "where our collective assurance is most likely to be challenged through the national process" (p.29).

[LEAD] The executive has commissioned unnamed external specialist support on radiology diagnostics after admitting it lacks robust analysis of its DM01 position, with outputs due early November.
[Evidence] "DM01 delivery represents a more significant challenge... we do not have enough robust enough analysis and clarity on the position within radiology. To address this, the Executive have commissioned external specialist support has to provide additional diagnostic capacity, independent challenge and rapid solution development." p.27, [Application for Advanced Foundation Trust Status, Item 3.i]. "This diagnostic and solution-development phase is expected to take approximately four to six weeks, with the outputs expected in early November." p.28. "Until then we will be in a position where we are unable to fully assure both our Board, and the external regulator, that our DM01 plan is sufficiently robust to deliver shared aspirations of performance against this standard." p.28. The supplier is not named and no cost is given.
