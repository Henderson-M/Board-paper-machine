## Top lines

[LEAD] West London ended 2025/26 with a £3.8m adjusted surplus — but only because NHSE distributed unused deficit funding; without it the trust would have reported a £0.5m surplus.
[Evidence] "The Trust delivered a £3.8m adjusted surplus in 2025/26 against a planned breakeven position excluding impairment. The surplus position was driven by a £3.3m cash bonus support distributed by NHSE via the ICB from unused deficit funding, and the Trust was required to show this in the final position; without that bonus funding, the Trust would have reported a £0.5m surplus, broadly aligned with the plan." — p.121, [2025/26 Finance report]

[LEAD] The trust has identified only £5m of its £16.3m 2026/27 savings requirement, leaving an £11.3m gap against a plan that assumes full CIP delivery.
[Evidence] "The 2026/27 new CIP target is £11.5m plus carry forward CIPs of £4.8m, bringing the total internal CIP target to £16.3m, of which £5m has been identified, leaving a gap of £11.3m." — p.134, [M1 2026/27 Finance report]

[LEAD] The trust must cut bank-staff spend by around £10m (25%) in 2026/27 to meet the NHSE bank ceiling, after spending £40.4m on bank against a £34.5m plan last year.
[Evidence] "The Trust spent £40.4m on bank in 2025/26 against a plan of £34.5m. The Trust will need to reduce bank spend by c£10m (or a 25% reduction) in 2026/27 to meet the NHSE bank limit." — p.121, [2025/26 Finance report]

[LEAD] A coroner issued a Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths report following an Ealing mental health inquest, criticising "unacceptably long" ASD/ADHD waits and a failed crisis-team referral pathway, and is raising the waits concern with the DHSC minister.
[Evidence] "the Trust was issued with a Regulation 28 (PFD) report relating to an inquest which concluded on 5th February 2026 (Ealing MH). The Coroner raised two separate concerns in the PFD requiring Trust response 1) The 'unacceptably long wait' for referrals, assessments and diagnosis of ASD and ADHD. The Coroner noted that this was a longstanding national issue and will also raise the concern with the Minister for the DHSC. 2) The failure in the Crisis team referral pathway; as the coroner believed the pathway failed as the crisis team still was able to reject the referral" — p.81, [PSIRF, Quality and Safety Report Q4 2025/26]

[LEAD] Paediatric audiology 52-week waits are deteriorating, with a look-back exercise under way on 2,300 children after the Ealing service was suspended for much of last year.
[Evidence] "Paediatric audiology 52-week wait performance (community health IPR2) is deteriorating and expected to worsen further during Q1. The Ealing service, which was suspended for much of the previous year, is generating growing numbers of 52-week and diagnostic pathway breaches... A look-back exercise on 2,300 children is under way, with 3 out of the first 400 reviewed requiring recall (all administrative rather than clinical concerns). Discussions with NHS England are ongoing regarding the appropriateness of dual reporting of this service across two National Oversight Framework metrics." — p.118-119, [Finance and Performance Committee Chair's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's ICB contracts were still unsigned at the end of May; the position has been reported to NHSE region and the finance committee called it "not good practice".
[Evidence] "ICB contracts remain unsigned as at end of May 2026, though no material financial disputes exist. Signatures are expected within two weeks. The Committee noted this is not good practice and the position has been reported to Region." — p.118, [Finance and Performance Committee Chair's report]

[WORTH WATCHING] An employer National Insurance cost pressure of £2.4m above funding received is being absorbed across the trust via vacancies.
[Evidence] "the actual increase for NI is £2.4m above the funding received, resulting in a cost pressure to the Trust. This is currently being absorbed across the Trust, via vacancies." — p.121, [2025/26 Finance report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Senior leadership reshuffle: COO for High Secure and Forensic Services Leeanne McGee retiring (moving to interim Director of Improvement), Neil Ragoobar appointed Director of Forensic and Specialist Services, and Dr Christopher Hilton taking the trust-wide COO role.
[Evidence] "Leeanne McGee will take up the interim role of Director of Improvement at West London after she retires from her role of Chief Operating Officer for High Secure and Forensic Services in June... Neil Ragoobar was appointed to the role of Director of Forensic and Specialist Services and will be joining the Board in June. Following these changes, Dr Christopher Hilton will take on the overall Trust role of Chief Operating Officer." — p.31-32, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The ICB has approved investment in a new Assertive Outreach model for complex serious mental illness, with the trust drawing early learning from the Nottingham Inquiry.
[Evidence] "the ICB has approved investment in an Assertive Outreach model for complex patients with serious mental illness. This follows the paper presented to both the Trust and ICB Boards last year highlighting gaps in provision across the two NWL mental health providers and builds on national learning after the Nottingham events in 2023... ahead of any formal recommendations, the Trust is considering what early learning can be drawn from the evidence emerging through the Nottingham Inquiry." — p.35-36, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust has applied to take ownership of eight NHS Property Services sites under the new DHSC estate-transfer policy; board asked to approve the principle, subject to full business case.
[Evidence] "The Trust currently has an occupation in eight NHS PS properties. An expression of interest has been submitted to the ICB for the transfer of these assets to the Trust." Named assets include Brentford Health Centre, Chiswick Health Centre, Feltham Centre for Health, Heston Health Centre, Isleworth Centre, The Meadows Centre for Health (all Hounslow), Martin House (Ealing) and Wandsworth Bridge Road (Hammersmith & Fulham). — p.160, p.162, [Transfer of properties from NHS Property Services to NHS Trusts]

[WORTH WATCHING] Two formal Stage 3 sickness hearings in April both ended in termination on ill-health grounds, against a backdrop of rolling sickness stuck at 5.43% (target 4%) and a fifth of the workforce off sick at least once in March.
[Evidence] "2 Formal Stage 3 hearings took place in April 2026 both of which resulted in terminations on grounds of ill-health." — p.183, [Workforce Performance Report April 2026]; "Sickness absence remains stubbornly high (one fifth of the workforce had at least one sickness episode in March)." — p.165, [Workforce & OD Committee Chair's report]
