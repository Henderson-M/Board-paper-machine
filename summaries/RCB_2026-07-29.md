## Top lines

[LEAD] Trust's most likely forecast is a £30.7m year-end deficit — £8m worse than a plan NHSE has still not accepted — and it is not eligible for deficit support funding; worst-case modelled outturn is £46.9m.
[Evidence] "The most likely scenario is an outturn deficit of £30.7m, which is £8m worse than plan... The worst-case scenario... would be a deficit of £46.9m." — p.228, [Finance / TPR]; "This plan does not currently meet NHSE requirements and remains subject to further discussion. The Trust is not eligible for additional Deficit Support Funding" — p.225, [Finance / TPR]

[LEAD] Trust is now in NHSE's Challenged Provider Programme, with two NHSE-identified staff being placed in to help hit its financial commitments.
[Evidence] "Additionally we are receiving support from NHSE through the Challenged Provider Programme who have assisted us in identifying two colleagues to join us for a period of time to support us in achieving our financial commitments." — p.43, [Chief Executive's Report]; minutes confirm "the Trust was now part of the Challenged Provider Programme and was in discussions with NHS England about external support for delivery of the WRAP programme" — p.11 (minute item 9), [Minutes 24 June 2026]

[LEAD] CEO Clare Smith states it is the first time the trust has been unable to agree its annual plan with NHSE this far into the year.
[Evidence] "This is the first time the Trust has been unable to agree its annual plan with NHS England this far into the financial year which reflects the level of complexity and challenge the organisation is working through." — p.42, [Chief Executive's Report]

[LEAD] An independent NHS Providers well-led developmental review (Final Report, December 2025) — the trust's first for "some years" — is reproduced in full in the pack and finds "historic and deep seated" problems on culture, speaking up and EDI, and "a deep seated suspicion of 'management'".
[Evidence] "On the remaining domains of culture, freedom to speak up and Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) there is more work to do to 'shift the dial' on what are clearly historic and deep seated issues." — p.96; "the leadership team need to grip a number of legacy and deep seated concerns; notably a deep seated suspicion of 'management' including a clear disconnect and lack of visibility between the Board and executive team and the rest of the organisation, historic failure to address a number of cultural concerns, poor communication and feedback loops and a lack of clarity over accountability" — p.96, [Q1 Well-Led Action Plan / Well-Led Developmental Review]. Action plan status: "54 of 99 actions have been completed - 55% by the end of Q1." — p.92

[LEAD] Mortuary incident audit prompted by the Fuller inquiry / Nottingham review found half the trust's potential Human Tissue Authority reportable incidents since 2015 were never reported to the HTA and now need retrospective reporting; the HTA is running a formal compliance assessment of both mortuaries.
[Evidence] "Since 2015 there have been 20 incidents that may be considered an HTARI, 10 have been reported to the HTA with 10 requiring an in-depth assessment and retrospective reporting to the HTA." — p.254; "the HTA have issued a notice informing trusts that they will undertake an Evidence Compliance Assessment (ECA) of licensed premises. For YSTHFT the premises under license include the mortuaries at York Hospital and Scarborough Hospital... Details of this ECA will be sent on 10th August and must be completed by 18th September." — p.254, [Trust response to post-death care / Fuller enquiry]

[WORTH WATCHING] Heavy recent CQC activity: maternity re-assessed in April 2026 following a 2023 "inadequate" rating (draft report awaited); six legal regulatory breaches each at Scarborough and York urgent/emergency care; Section 31 conditions still in place on care of ED patients with mental health needs; 19 open CQC cases.
[Evidence] "The inspection formed part of a planned re-assessment following the 2023 inspection, during which services were rated as inadequate" — p.241; "Six legal breaches in regulation were identified" (Scarborough UEC, published 20 March 2026) — p.242; "Section 31: Care and Assessment of Patients with Mental Health Needs in the Emergency Department. An application to remove the conditions has been drafted" — p.242; "As of 1 July 2026, there are 19 open CQC cases." — p.242, [CQC Compliance Update]

[WORTH WATCHING] Freedom to Speak Up Q1 2026/27: 52 concerns (down from 101 in Q4), but more than half were people escalating because concerns had not been dealt with locally; concerns include racism/race discrimination (5) and sexually inappropriate behaviour (4); Guardian states "culture is a patient safety issue".
[Evidence] "More than half of concerns involved workers reporting that concerns had not been adequately addressed through existing routes." — p.245; "Concerns not addressed through other routes (28 cases)" — p.248; "5 concerns related to racism or race discrimination... 4 concerns related to sexually inappropriate behaviour." — p.248, [Freedom to Speak Up]

[WORTH WATCHING] Malton Hospital fully evacuated after a fire at the on-site Springwood Unit (run by Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys FT); services since restored.
[Evidence] "we experienced a significant incident following a fire in the Springwood Unit on the Malton Hospital site... its proximity required the full evacuation of our services as a precaution." — p.42, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] RTT waiting list running above trajectory at 56,537 (vs 54,210 trajectory) at Month 3, though zero 65-week waits maintained; GP referrals up 5% YTD.
[Evidence] "Total waiting list was off trajectory with 56,537 against a 54,210 trajectory; zero waits over 65 weeks was maintained." — p.143 area, [Trust Priorities Report — Operational Activity and Performance]

[Evidence] "The draft CQC inspection report is awaited" (maternity, Easingwold, breast screening) — pp.241-242, [CQC Compliance Update]. Request from CQC/trust: the Section 31 notice and conditions, and reports on publication.
