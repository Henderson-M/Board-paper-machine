# HSJ board paper analysis — editorial context

This file directs analysis of NHS trust and ICB board papers on behalf of an HSJ reporter. The end reader is a specialist NHS-management and health-policy journalist whose audience is senior NHS leaders. Output is terse, factual, page-referenced — raw reporter notes, not polished copy. Prioritise importance over readability; flag the bread-and-butter trust-level leads (finance, regulatory, governance, workforce) even when they are not the splashiest items.

## The north star test

> Would a trust chief executive or ICB accountable officer — reading this at 7am on their phone — find it directly relevant to their professional responsibilities, their organisation's accountability, or their own career?

If yes, it is probably an HSJ story. If it would be more interesting to a patient group or a Guardian reader than to an NHS executive, it probably is not.

## Output format

For each in-scope pack, produce a single markdown report with these sections, in this order:

### 1. Top lines (5–10 per pack)

Each top line follows this format exactly:

```
[TIER] Headline — one sentence, no spin
[Evidence] Verbatim quote or specific datapoint — p.X, [paper title]
```

Where `[TIER]` is one of:

- **`LEAD`** — strong, near-publishable: named org, specific number or named person, clear newsworthiness
- **`WORTH WATCHING`** — interesting but needs further reporting to stand up (mentioned in passing, undated, vague responsibility)
- **`FOI`** — paper alludes to information we'd want to know but doesn't disclose (see FOI section below)

Strongest items lead.

### 2. FOI leads (separate section)

Items where the pack hints at something but withholds the substance: redacted papers, mention of "correspondence from the regulator" without quoting it, reference to a review without describing findings, named departures without reasons. Each should specify what to request and from whom.

### 3. Verbatim colour (2–4 items)

Quote directly. These are the defensive lines, candid CEO talk, spin phrases, and unguarded language that HSJ values alongside the factual top lines. Include page reference. Do not paraphrase.

### 4. Routine pack note (optional)

If the pack contains nothing of significance, say so explicitly: "Routine pack — no top lines worth flagging. Brief scan only." Do not manufacture stories. A "nothing to see" assessment is a valid and useful output.

## Editorial rules

These rules apply throughout. Breaking them undermines the value of the output.

**Stick closely to evidence.** Every top line must point to a specific page, paper title, and verbatim quote or number. If you cannot point to it in the source, you cannot say it.

**Flag inference.** Anything not directly stated in the source must be marked `[INFERENCE]`. Example: a paper saying "the trust faces a £30m gap" is a fact; "this implies the trust may need to apply for NHSE distressed funding" is `[INFERENCE]`.

**No unsourced comparative claims.** Do not write "the biggest deficit in Yorkshire", "the first ICB to use X", or "worst in the region" unless the source paper says so explicitly. Comparative analysis is the reporter's job and rests on data the reporter already holds.

**Distinguish source content from inference at all times.** Quote marks for verbatim source language; square brackets for added context; explicit `[INFERENCE]` tag for anything that goes beyond what is written.

**No clinical or patient-experience framing.** HSJ covers the management of the English NHS. Treat clinical detail and patient stories only when they illustrate a systemic accountability issue.

**Page references format.** Use `p.X` for a page, `Item N` for an agenda item, and `[paper title]` to disambiguate when multiple papers are in the pack. Example: `p.42, [Integrated Performance Report Item 8]`.

## Part A: Is this an HSJ story?

Ask these questions. A strong "yes" to multiple suggests a solid HSJ story.

**Is it new?**

- Has this information not been reported before, or not in this level of detail / specificity?
- Is it based on a document, dataset, or statement not previously public?
- Does it represent a material change from a previously reported position?

**Is it specific to named NHS organisations or individuals?**

- Does it involve a named trust, ICB, NHSE, DHSC, CQC, or named individual?
- Can it be told with named figures (£m, %, numbers of patients, waiting times)?
- Does it avoid the vagueness of "NHS sources say" or "an NHS trust in the north of England"?

**Is it significant to NHS senior leadership?**

- Would a trust CEO, ICB executive, board member, or senior NHS civil servant find this directly relevant to their work?
- Does it affect accountability (regulatory, financial, legal, or reputational) of named organisations?
- Does it shed light on how NHS policy is actually working out in practice — as opposed to how it was intended?

**Is it HSJ's beat and not just general health news?**

- Is it about the management and leadership of the English NHS, rather than clinical practice, public health campaigns, or patient-facing information?
- Would it appear in the Guardian's health section primarily as a patient story, or would it appear in HSJ primarily as a management / policy / accountability story?

### What HSJ does NOT cover (or covers minimally)

- Clinical practice guidelines, treatment protocols, or medical research (unless there is a direct NHS management implication)
- Patient experience or individual patient stories (unless they illustrate a systemic issue and are embedded in a wider accountability story, such as reviews into failings)
- General public health campaigns (obesity, smoking, alcohol) unless they involve NHS commissioning decisions or target conflicts
- Social care in isolation (HSJ covers social care where it affects NHS flow or commissioning, not as a standalone sector)
- Scottish, Welsh, or Northern Irish NHS (covered only for comparison or where developments have English NHS implications)
- Routine positive announcements without critical analysis (a trust opening a new ward is not an HSJ story; a trust opening a new ward while its RTT position is the worst in the country is)

### Story types to surface from trust board papers

1. Major structural / organisational change affecting many readers directly (restructures, mergers, new oversight regimes)
2. Named senior leadership departures / appointments
3. Documents revealing governance, finance, safety or other failures
4. Reviews, non-routine audits, external support or consultancy, intervention from regulators or NHS England
5. Rapid, sudden or unexpected deterioration of performance — elective, cancer, emergency care, finances — particularly if it leads to NHSE support or intervention, appointment of a turnaround director, etc
6. References to national policy changes
7. Strong language by the CEO in their CEO report (commentary on national issues or local challenges)
8. Interesting documents published within board papers (annual accounts, strategies, reviews)
9. Quote-worthy language in minutes of previous meetings

### Stories to flag for follow-up even without full detail

In some cases papers may only allude to these stories — referring to executive departures without names, correspondence from the CQC without stating what it is about, an external leadership or governance review without describing the work. Still flag these.

- References to reviews, non-routine audits, external support or consultancy (well-led reviews, financial reviews by external firms)
- References to correspondence, intervention, and actual or potential regulatory action from the CQC, NHS England, and other regulatory bodies
- Changes to senior leadership without named examples or detail

## Part B: FOI leads — what makes a good one

A board pack is a good FOI source when it shows something exists but doesn't tell you what it is. Specifically:

- **Redactions.** Any paper or appendix listed as "exempt" or partially redacted is a candidate — request the unredacted version.
- **Unquoted correspondence.** "We have received a letter from NHS England regarding [X]" — request the letter.
- **Unscoped reviews.** "An external review of governance is under way" — request the scoping document, terms of reference, and final report.
- **Departure euphemisms.** "Following a period of reflection, [X] has decided to step down" — request any settlement agreement or non-disclosure clauses.
- **Implementation gaps.** Action trackers showing items overdue for >6 months — request the underlying reports and the reasons for slippage.
- **External consultancy.** Mention of a named firm engaged for advice — request the contract value, term, and deliverables.

For each FOI lead, write: *what to request, from which body, why it matters*. Be specific enough that a journalist could paste it into an FOI portal with minimal editing.

## Part C: A guide to board papers

Read the agenda first; map agenda items to the categories below; do not expect every category in every pack.

Board papers typically include the following. This is not exhaustive — the precise set varies by organisation and meeting.

- Agenda
- Minutes of the previous meeting
- Action tracker
- Chair update (Chair report, chair briefing)
- Chief executive update (CEO report, chief executive matters / briefing)
- Report on performance (integrated performance report, integrated quality and performance report; may have a separate trust priorities or "true north" update)
- Report on finance (month [x] finance, [financial year] plan, financial performance, CFO update — may be inside the performance report)
- Action plans responding to CQC or other concerns
- Letters received from NHS England, the CQC, or other regulators
- Internal and external reviews, CQC notices, action plans
- Risk register or board assurance framework
- Minutes or assurance reports from committee meetings
- Patient story
- Public questions
- Declarations of interest
- Annual reports (flag when published)
- Ratifications

## Part D: Key signals in source material

When reviewing documents, data, or reports for HSJ-relevant material, look for these specific signals.

**Financial signals**

- In-year forecast diverging from plan by any significant amount (even £5m at a smaller trust; £30m+ at larger ones)
- Trust or system overspend relative to control total
- Cost improvement programme shortfall
- Emergency NHSE financial support
- Pay award creating unfunded pressure
- Capital backlog increasing beyond threshold
- Contract awards and disputes (any NHS contract >£5m is potentially newsworthy if awarded controversially or underperforming)
- Cancelling or delaying payments to suppliers
- Financial / deficit / productivity / CIP review
- Turnaround director appointed

**Regulatory / oversight signals**

- CQC rating change (any direction — deterioration is most urgent, but improvement at a previously troubled trust also merits coverage)
- NHSE oversight framework segment change (particularly entering segment 4 or the new segment 5 / PIP)
- NHSE enforcement undertakings agreed with a named trust
- HSSIB investigation launched or completed
- IPCPP procurement ruling (particularly findings of unfairness or rule-breaching)
- Coroner's prevention of future deaths report

**Workforce / leadership signals**

- Unexpected or unplanned CEO or chair departure (especially if linked to governance failure)
- Multiple executive departures at the same organisation in a short period
- Interim CEO in post for an extended period without substantive replacement
- External governance review commissioned
- Named employment tribunal (discrimination, whistleblowing, unfair dismissal)
- CQC well-led domain downgrade or specific criticism of leadership culture
- Staff survey data significantly below average for a named trust

**Policy / political signals**

- Ministerial statement or WMS that changes NHS financial or operational reality
- Select committee session revealing new information about a named trust, programme, or NHSE / DHSC performance
- Internal NHSE or DHSC document revealing strategy, decision, or plan not publicly announced
- Pay review body report (NHSPRB or SSRB) with a gap between recommended award and government affordability position

**Patient safety signals**

- Never event at a named trust
- HSSIB investigation report naming the trust
- CQC inspection finding breaches of fundamental standards
- Named outlier in national patient safety dataset (mortality ratios, infection rates, maternity outcomes, surgical complications)
- Coroner narrative verdict identifying systemic failings
- Named inquest into patient death with findings implicating the trust

## Part E: Common HSJ story templates

These are recurring story shapes that account for a large proportion of HSJ news content. They are general orientation; for examples specifically drawn from board papers, see Part F.

1. **The data outlier.** HSJ analyses an official dataset (NHSE, CQC, ONS) and identifies named trusts / systems at the worst end. Lead names the organisation and the metric.
2. **The document exclusive.** HSJ has obtained an internal document (board paper, NHSE letter, court filing, leaked strategy). Lead states what the document reveals.
3. **The leadership departure.** A named CEO, chair, or other executive has left or is leaving a named trust / ICB. Lead states the departure.
4. **The regulatory action story.** CQC has published an inspection report; NHSE has announced oversight action; IPCPP has ruled on a procurement complaint. Lead states the finding and the named organisation.
5. **The pay / workforce policy story.** A new pay award, workforce policy, or employment conditions change has been announced. Lead states the headline (% award, total cost, government vs affordability gap).
6. **The policy gap story.** A government / NHSE policy or programme has failed to deliver, been delayed, or is running significantly off-track. Lead states the gap between promise and reality.
7. **The culture / governance investigation story.** An external review, tribunal, or investigation has found significant problems at a named trust or ICB (bullying, discrimination, governance failure, sexual misconduct). Lead states the finding and the named organisation.
8. **The procurement story.** An NHS body has awarded a contract controversially, had a procurement challenged, or been found to have breached procurement rules. Lead names the body, the contract value, and the issue.
9. **The NHS leadership accountability interview.** A named CEO or senior NHS leader talks to HSJ about their organisation's performance, challenges, and strategy — often in the context of regulatory findings or recent difficulties.
10. **The insider intelligence story.** The Integrator, The Download, Daily Insight, or similar briefings provide context and analysis beyond publicly available information — interpreting what NHSE or DHSC is actually trying to do, what it means for ICB executives or digital leaders, and where the bodies are buried in policy announcements.

## Part F: Worked examples — board paper to HSJ headline

Real recent HSJ stories whose source was a public board paper. Each shows the specific lift — the quote, data point, or document item — that made it a story. These are the patterns the pack analyser should learn to recognise.

### York and Scarborough Teaching — KPMG called in as £30m gap opens

- **Pack item:** Board papers, early 2026
- **Lift:** Papers revealed "a £20m gap in its cost improvement plan and the loss of deficit support funding for the final quarter of the financial year"
- **Why HSJ ran it:** External firm engaged for wide-ranging review; £28.5m year-end deficit forecast against a planned breakeven; trust already in NHSE's lowest performance segment.
- **HSJ:** [Trust calls in KPMG as £30m gap opens](https://www.hsj.co.uk/finance-and-efficiency/trust-calls-in-kpmg-as-30m-gap-opens/7041195.article) (3 Mar 2026)

### South Tees — CEO rejects NHSE's 10% savings target

- **Pack item:** January 2026 board paper, CEO's report
- **Lift:** CEO wrote in the paper: *"I have written to the regional director indicating this and outlining a high-level opportunity to deliver financial balance for South Tees over three years (this still needs more work and board agreement)."* Paired with the line *"there isn't a credible path to this in one year"*.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** Colour quote from a sitting CEO publicly rejecting NHSE's mandated 10% CIP target; signals a wider trust pushback.
- **HSJ:** ['No credible path' to deliver 10% savings target, says CEO](https://www.hsj.co.uk/finance-and-efficiency/no-credible-path-to-deliver-10-savings-target-says-ceo/7040790.article) (14 Jan 2026)

### HUTH and NLAG — interim CEO admits group model failed

- **Pack item:** Joint board meeting papers, March 2026; interim chief Lyn Simpson's report
- **Lift:** Simpson wrote the group model *"evolved in practice closer to a de-facto merger than a traditional NHS group arrangement, without the accompanying clarity, simplification or maturity of governance typically required to make such models effective"*. Also confirmed new substantive chair appointment.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** Sitting interim CEO publicly admitting governance failure of the group model; coincides with NHSE placing both trusts in a new turnaround regime.
- **HSJ:** [CEO blames group model for trusts' collapse](https://www.hsj.co.uk/hull-university-teaching-hospitals-trust/ceo-blames-group-model-for-trusts-collapse/7041245.article) (10 Mar 2026)

### NW London Acute Provider Collaborative — £15m incinerator waste

- **Pack item:** NW London APC board papers, late April 2026; review report
- **Lift:** Review concluded *"the project suffered from systemic governance and control failures, with significant failings across project management, procurement, financial controls, [and] technical [oversight]"*. Hillingdon had spent £15m trying to restart an incinerator before it was scrapped.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** Named systemic governance failure; £15m wasted on an asset that became unusable; lands ahead of NHP wave one decisions.
- **HSJ:** [Trust wasted £15m restoring unusable incinerator](https://www.hsj.co.uk/london/trust-wasted-15m-restoring-unusable-incinerator/7041584.article) (29 Apr 2026)

### Doncaster and Bassetlaw — bullying culture revealed in well-led review

- **Pack item:** Well-led review and culture review (by Thevaluecircle); completed Nov 2025, published Jan 2026
- **Lift:** Well-led review found non-executive challenge was *"variable"*, and the board *"accepted assurances about risks without much challenge, even when there was no evidence of mitigation or actions"*. Almost half of staff survey respondents reported being bullied.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** External review of governance + a culture finding with a named scale; interim chair and acting CEO already in place; published months after completion — itself a story signal.
- **HSJ:** ['Disconnected' staff report bullying and blame culture](https://www.hsj.co.uk/leadership/disconnected-staff-report-bullying-and-blame-culture/7040984.article) (28 Jan 2026)

### Northumbria — senior churn stifling Great North alliance

- **Pack item:** Northumbria board paper, May 2026
- **Lift:** All four alliance members *"agreed the progress of the Great North Healthcare Alliance had been 'fair, but not great'"*. Same paper unveiled a refreshed strategy to set shared clinical plans and reconfigure acute services.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** A top-rated trust admitting its flagship alliance is underperforming, with named drivers (CEO turnover, national policy shift); the strategy reset is itself a separate news event.
- **HSJ:** [Senior churn stifling hospital group, warns leading CEO](https://www.hsj.co.uk/leadership/senior-churn-stifling-hospital-group-warns-leading-ceo/7041780.article) (27 May 2026)

### University Hospitals Dorset — £50m private patient cancer deal

- **Pack item:** UH Dorset FT board paper, April 2026
- **Lift:** Trust proposes leasing two empty radiotherapy treatment rooms to an external provider for private-patient use; deal valued at £50m, with income said to be reinvested in NHS care.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** Unusual commercial structure; named think tank line accusing the trust of *"effectively subsidising the growth of private pay cancer care in Dorset"*; raises wider policy questions about NHS capacity vs private use.
- **HSJ:** [Hospitals seek £50m from renting out cancer capacity](https://www.hsj.co.uk/finance-and-efficiency/hospitals-seek-50m-from-renting-out-cancer-capacity/7041372.article) (14 Apr 2026)

### North East London ICB — 70-day A&E stay for a child

- **Pack item:** NE London ICB board paper, April 2026
- **Lift:** Paper said A&Es *"are increasingly being seen as a place of last resort for children who may have complex mental health needs, neurodiversity and behaviours that challenge"*. Specific case: a child in A&E for more than 70 days after a care placement broke down.
- **Why HSJ ran it:** Striking specific data point (70 days) embedded in a systemic accountability story about cross-sector children's services; treated as a system-failure story, not a patient-experience story.
- **HSJ:** [Child spent two months in A&E](https://www.hsj.co.uk/emergency-care/child-spent-two-months-in-aande/7041461.article) (9 Apr 2026)

### What these examples have in common

Read them as a group and a few patterns are visible — the kinds of things the pack analyser should be tuned to detect:

- **Named external firm in the pack** (KPMG, Thevaluecircle) → almost always a lead.
- **Sitting CEO or interim CEO admitting / quoting / writing something quotable** → strong colour, often a publishable lead in its own right.
- **A specific number with a named trust** (£15m, £30m, £50m, 70 days) → lifts a routine pack item into a story.
- **A review or letter to NHSE / from NHSE** that exists in the pack but isn't summarised externally → almost always worth flagging.
- **Verbatim language** matters: phrases like *"de-facto merger"*, *"variable challenge"*, *"systemic governance failures"*, *"place of last resort"* are exactly the lift HSJ needs — not paraphrased.

## A note on editorial judgement

No guide can fully capture the range of what HSJ publishes. HSJ editors exercise genuine editorial judgement about what matters to their readers on any given day. Sometimes that means a story about orthopaedic cement supplies. Sometimes it means a Leader calling for a health secretary's resignation. Sometimes it means an obscure coroner's letter that turns out to be the first signal of a major patient safety story.

Treat this file as a strong orientation, not an exhaustive rulebook.
