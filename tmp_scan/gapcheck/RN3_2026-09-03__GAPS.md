## MISSED (in the pack, absent from the 10 Sep summary)

- **The 26 June NHS England letter to Great Western was signed by Jim Mackey and Penny Dash, covered operational, financial and governance under-performance, triggered a July extraordinary board, brought in Hunter Healthcare as turnaround support, and GWH has been moved to NHS Oversight Framework segment 4; NHSE has set a board-to-board for 5 October.** The first pass listed the correspondence only as an FOI target "about its financial position" and suggested asking NHSE South West; the pack says it came from the NHSE chief executive and chair, and that GWH has been formally escalated. "GWH received a letter from NHSE Jim Mackey and Penny Dash on the 26th of June, reflecting under performance against plan in both operational and financial metrics. "NHS England correspondence dated 26 June 2026 raised concerns about finance, operational performance and governance at GWH" (AP p.458, Initial Group BAF, Item 8.1). "the RUH had improved from Segment 4 to Segment 3, SFT had remained in Segment 3, and GWH had moved to Segment 4" (AP p.9, minutes of Group Board 2 July). Group risk register names the consequence: "Escalation within the NHS Oversight Framework to segment 3 or 4. Loss of autonomy through the system triple lock" with "productivity running at c.2.5% against the c.7% required" (AP p.384, Group Risk Register GRP-FIN-01).

- **NHS England's regional finance director told Salisbury not to sign its key worker accommodation lease-and-leaseback deal without Treasury approval; the trust says its Month 4 plan "will be missed as a result" and projects negative cash in November.** A Treasury referral blocking a trust's cash-generating property transaction, with no timescale, is a story in itself and undermines the "SFT on plan" line. "In July, the NHSE Regional CFO stated in a Provider Oversight meeting that SFT should not enter into the KWA agreement without HMT approval... we await HMT review of the business case and there are currently no timescales for that. It is certain that our M4 plan position will be missed as a result." (AP p.335, SFT Managing Director's Report, Item 5.2). "Cash remains the single biggest organisational risk with current projections showing negative cash in November (assuming completion of Lease and Lease back in October)." (AP p.349, SFT Finance and Performance Committee report). "The agreed Deficit Support Funding of £3.4m per quarter is at risk." (AP p.386, Group Risk Register).

- **Great Western will have no interventional radiology service from September unless a deal with Gloucestershire is reached; its last consultant retires then. Stroke is locum-run and rated SSNAP E.** A district general hospital losing IR entirely is a patient safety story. "From September without an agreement in place there is no Interventional Radiology service at GWH. The model is likely to lead to a forecast cost pressure." (AP p.317, GWH MD Report). "Only one interventional radiologist with no cover during absences and no out-of-hours/weekend cover" (RR p.407, GWH People and Culture Committee report). "The service remains staffed by Locum Consultants which is unsustainable model for the future. SSNAP data shows the service is underdelivering." (AP p.317).

- **HCRG has withdrawn funding for 42 of RUH's Hospital at Home beds, now the trust's top-scoring risk (20), and the Group's chief medical and nursing officers have written to the ICB about HCRG's service provision.** A named private community provider pulling funding from a flagship virtual ward, in a trust modelling a 72-bed winter deficit, is a system story. "HCRG withdrawing funding for 42 HH@H beds" and "RUH provides insufficient funding to maintain a service above 20 beds" (AP p.386, Group Risk Register RUH-3314). "Group CMOs and CNOs recently wrote to the ICB raising concerns about the impacts of HCRG's service provision and the impact on quality, patient experience and the extra burden being placed back on to group clinicians." (AP p.333, SFT MD Report). "a potential peak-period adult bed deficit of approximately 72 beds" and "The absence of confirmed Ward 4 funding remains a significant risk to winter resilience" (AP p.374, RUH MD Report).

- **Blackstone is taking control of Acacium, parent of Bank Partners, weeks after the Group outsourced its temporary staffing to it; early fill rates fell to 64% from 72% and the risk register records concerns raised by MPs, governors and councils.** Private equity acquiring an NHS staff bank contractor immediately after go-live is newsworthy on its own; the first pass only listed the ownership notice under files reviewed. "on, or around, 24 August 2026 we expect Blackstone Inc. (Blackstone) to acquire a controlling interest in the Acacium Group" (RR p.451, Acacium Group Change of Ownership Notice). "Increased public concerns have been raised via MP, Governors and Council." (AP p.291, Chief People Officer Report).

- **Great Western's workforce plan requires a cut of about 217 WTE with £8.9m of pay savings unidentified; a mutually agreed resignation scheme ran from 20 July; the Group is 183 WTE over plan with £5.7m adverse pay; and GWH "has been in critical incident more than not since January 2026".** Hard headcount reduction numbers are the story the RAC "collision point" line (which the first pass did cover) points to. "The Trust has been in critical incident more than not since January 2026 reflecting the underlying pressure on capacity." (AP p.323, GWH MD Report).

- **RUH is a South West outlier for C. difficile and E. coli and NHS England's regional IPC team is running an external review on the day of the meeting; an NHSE support visit in February found corridor queueing of up to eight patients and an ED with a single side room.** External regulator-led review of infection control, plus a documented link between corridor care and outbreak control, is absent from the first pass. ## WRONG (in the 10 Sep summary but not supported by the pack)

- Colour quote "The most significant strategic risk across the Group is no longer any single performance metric..." is attributed to "p.76, [CEO Report, Item 3.2]". Page 76 sits inside the Group Executive Committee Upward Report (Item 4.1), which begins at p.66; the CEO Report ends at p.64. Minor misattribution of paper, quote itself is accurate (AP p.76).
- LEAD on deficits says "Great Western's own risk committee records a 'likely...deficit of £25m'". The quoted source (p.397) is the Group Risk and Assurance Committee chair's report, not a GWH committee. The GWH figure in GWH's own papers is "a most likely scenario of £25.3m" (RR p.411, GWH Finance, Performance and Infrastructure Committee report).
- Pack assessment says "Annual accounts / annual report present: yes". The accounts and annual report are not in either pack file; the only content is the GWH Audit, Risk and Assurance Committee report describing their approval and the audit opinion (AP p.478). Should read "no, but audit outcome reported".
