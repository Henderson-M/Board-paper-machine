## Top lines

[LEAD] MPFT is being lined up as the "integrator" / anchor organisation for neighbourhood health across Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent, with a formal board decision still to come and an options paper scheduled for this October board that is not on the public agenda.
[Evidence] "The ICB Cluster has asked MPFT to consider taking on an integrator role in Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent to support the development of neighbourhood health and the wider shift towards prevention and care closer to home... This work will inform a formal proposal to the Trust Board, which will ultimately determine whether MPFT should take on the role." - p.29, [Chief Executive's Corporate Report, Enc C]. The IBPC report goes further: "MPFT have been appointed as Anchor organisation for Neighbourhood Health in Staffordshire/Stoke-on-Trent." - p.113, and "MPFT is scrutinising the evolution of its ICS-designated position as Integrator/anchor organisation for neighbourhood health - options paper scheduled for October Board." - p.112, [IBPC Highlight Report, Enc K]. [INFERENCE] The options paper does not appear on the public agenda (pp.2-3), so it is presumably being taken in the confidential session. The CEO's "asked to consider" and IBPC's "have been appointed" are not the same claim; worth asking which it is.

[LEAD] The trust's break-even forecast to NHSE still depends on £2m of high-risk savings and £3.9m of contingency releases; the underlying deficit is now put at £14.9m, £2.3m worse than the planned exit position, and the "raw" efficiency trajectory is £25.0m against a £30.8m target.
[Evidence] "The latest assessment of the 2026/27 underlying deficit within the updated medium-term plan is £14.9m, which is £2.3m behind the planned exit deficit of £12.6m." - p.98, [Financial Performance 2026-27 M5, Enc J]. "To deliver the breakeven forecast reported to NHSE, the Trust is assuming the delivery of £2m of the transformation savings target which is currently rated as high risk (stretch procurement opportunities, and securing additional ICB funding for service growth), and the release of planned non-recurrent contingencies amounting to £3.9m." - p.105. "The position also assumes the alignment of some existing underspends (e.g. pay vacancies), together with the non-recurrent release of contingencies against the efficiency target. For the purposes of highlighting financial risk in the efficiency plan, the raw trajectory (excluding the assumptions above) amounts to a forecast outturn saving of £25.0m" - p.106, [Enc J]. Year to date the trust reports an adjusted surplus of £716k against a £710k plan (p.98). Change since the August pack: at M3 the high-risk savings assumption was £3.8m and the contingency release £2.6m (per RRE_2026-08-13.md); at M5 it is £2.0m and £3.9m. The August "unmitigated adjusted deficit of £6.4m" line does not reappear in the M5 report. Also at M5: cash £134.3m, "£7.6m behind plan" (p.103); capital spend £3.4m against a £6.8m plan (p.103); non-NHS BPPC 82% against a 95% target (p.104).

[LEAD] Combined ADHD and autism waiting lists in Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent exceed 6,900 referrals against commissioned capacity of 650 assessments a year, and the care group says its own prioritisation approach is "not in line with the service specification".
[Evidence] "Demand within ADHD and Autism services continues to present a significant concern, with combined waiting lists exceeding 6,900 referrals. Estimated waiting times are excessive and unacceptable, modelled at current commissioned capacity of 650 assessments a year. The service continues to prioritise referrals according to clinical need to minimise risk. Whilst this approach is not in line with the service specification, it ensures that we minimise the risk of harm for people on the waiting list... Applicable escalations have been made in to the Integrated Care Board." - p.77, [Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent Care Group Key Issues Report, Enc F]. [INFERENCE] 6,900 referrals at 650 a year is more than ten years of commissioned capacity; the paper does not do this arithmetic.

[LEAD] A Legionella outbreak closed the seclusion facility at Norbury, the trust's PICU, forcing patients into out-of-area beds; the care group calls it "an ongoing issue" with no long-term fix identified.
[Evidence] "Norbury - There was a temporary closure of the seclusion facility due to Legionella outbreak which resulted in patients requiring their level of care to be placed in 'out of area' beds. This is an ongoing issue and a long term resolution needs to be identified due to impact on service continuity, quality and experience of patients." - pp.77-78, [SSOT Care Group Key Issues Report, Enc F]. Norbury is identified as the PICU at p.141 ("Norbury (PICU)", [Safer Staffing Establishment Review, Enc N]).
