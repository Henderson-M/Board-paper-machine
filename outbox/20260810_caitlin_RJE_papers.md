## Top lines

[LEAD] An NHS England-commissioned independent review by Ethical Healthcare Consulting has concluded UHNM cannot afford to replace its electronic patient record, and blames continuing system instability on the Careflow system and iPortal.
[Evidence] "A key conclusion of the review was that an immediate, organisation-wide replacement of the EPR system is neither affordable nor deliverable within current timescales." The review "identified the integration as a significant technical risk and recognised that iPortal, whilst highly valued by clinical teams, is becoming increasingly difficult to sustain", with instability "now predominantly linked to the current Careflow system and iPortal rather than the underlying infrastructure" — p.22, [Chief Executive's Report – August 2026, Item 08]

[LEAD] £53m of the trust's cost improvement programme is rated high risk at Q1, with the in-year financial risk scored "extreme" and executive assurance downgraded to partial.
[Evidence] "At Q1, the risk remains extreme, with financial performance £6.1m behind plan. Delivery of the Cost Improvement Programme continues to represent a significant risk, with £53m currently assessed as high risk." Risk score 20 (likelihood 4 × consequence 5), forecast to "remain at an extreme level throughout 2026/27". "Executive assurance has reduced to Partial." — p.~103, [Board Assurance Framework – Q1, BAF 6: Inability to Deliver In-Year Financial Position, Item 09]

[LEAD] The trust's deficit nearly doubled between months one and two, running £5m adverse to plan by M2.
[Evidence] "Month 1 had delivered a deficit of £6.5m, which was £3.0m adverse to plan… primarily driven by industrial action and under-delivery of the Cost Improvement Programme (CIP), with £2.8m planned but £1.1m below target." "He reported that the Month 2 position had deteriorated to an £11.0m deficit, £5.0m adverse to plan… indicating that progress in delivering CIP had not yet translated into financial improvement." — p.14–15, [Minutes of Trust Board Part 1, 10 June 2026, Item 04]

[WORTH WATCHING] Deloitte has been commissioned to carry out root cause analysis on sickness absence among nursing and midwifery staff.
[Evidence] "Deloitte had been commissioned to support root cause analysis, with further work to be progressed to prioritise and focus improvement actions" — following a note that nursing and midwifery are "the largest workforce group and highest users of bank and agency staff" — p.~11, [Minutes of Trust Board Part 1, 10 June 2026, People Dashboard]

[WORTH WATCHING] UHNM's 2025/26 accounts again require a statutory referral to the Secretary of State over its historic cumulative deficit, despite the trust meeting its break-even duty.
[Evidence] "due to the historic cumulative deficit, a referral to the Secretary of State would still be required" — and, in response to a question on next steps, the CFO "explained that the referral had been required in previous years and confirmed that the position remained unchanged… although the Trust had met the break-even duty since 2019/20 and historic debt had previously been written off, the legislative requirement for referral remained in place." — p.18–19, [Minutes of Trust Board Part 1, 24 June 2026, Items 5 and 6]
