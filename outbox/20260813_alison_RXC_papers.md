## Top lines

[LEAD] The CQC has withdrawn a section 29A warning notice on East Sussex's urgent and emergency care after the trust formally challenged it — the regulator accepting the notice "was not an appropriate or proportionate regulatory response".
[Evidence] "A significant development during the reporting period was the Care Quality Commission's decision to withdraw the Section 29A Warning Notice issued following the March inspection of our urgent and emergency care services. After careful review, the Trust submitted a detailed representation challenging both the accuracy and proportionality of the Notice. The CQC subsequently accepted our representation and confirmed that the Warning Notice was not an appropriate or proportionate regulatory response... We continue to await publication of the final inspection report." — pp.45-46, [Chief Executive's Report, Agenda Item 8/9]

[LEAD] The trust asked NHSE for £25m of cash PDC support; the region has backed only £14.4m, with cash down to a £2.5m closing balance at the minimum threshold.
[Evidence] "Cash remains very constrained and is reported at the minimum threshold, with a closing balance of £2.5m. An application has been submitted to NHSE for £25.0m of cash PDC support. The Regional team have supported £14.4m of the application and we are due to receive a response on 7 August 2026." — p.81, [Integrated Performance Report Month 3 (June), Finance Executive Summary, Item 10(v)]

[LEAD] East Sussex's 2026/27 contract income envelope has fallen by around £40m.
[Evidence] "The 26-27 contract income envelope has reduced by approximately £40m, increasing the need to deliver efficiencies and lower the cost base." — p.81, [Integrated Performance Report Month 3, Finance, Item 10(v)]

[LEAD] The board is being asked to endorse ESHT becoming an "IHO-type" host provider and "steward of health and care" across East Sussex, taking delegated responsibility for pathways beyond its own services.
[Evidence] "This thinking does not seek to replace the Neighbourhood Alliance or partner accountability, which is the current evolving state within our ICB. It aims largely to consider the next stage of development and what we call the 'IHO-type' model develops the thinking further re: responsibility for agreed pathways and outcomes, with ESHT potentially acting as host provider and convener. For ESHT, this means evolving from organisational stewardship alone towards stewardship of health outcomes across East Sussex, while retaining its duties & provider responsibilities." — p.153, [Integration and Strategic Direction, Item 12]. The recommendation asks the board to "Agree that ESHT should explore a host-provider and convener role for agreed pathways, subject to clear delegation, due diligence and Board approval" — p.162.

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust is using "pre-emptive boarding in corridors" as a planned response to occupancy, stated in the board's own nursing workforce reporting.
[Evidence] "During June occupancy remained high and we have opened additional capacity and used pre-emptive boarding in corridors alongside this we have significant numbers of patients requiring enhanced observation in relation to high risk of falls or patients with challenging/violent behaviour." — p.53, [Integrated Performance Report Month 3, Nursing & Midwifery Workforce, Item 10(ii)]

[WORTH WATCHING] Cancer 62-day performance is 64%, "significantly below trajectory", with recovery to 75% not expected until September.
[Evidence] "Cancer 62-day standard was significantly below trajectory at 64%. A targeted recovery programme is in place, including tumour site-specific actions to recover performance to 75% trajectory by September." — p.52, [Integrated Performance Report Month 3, Chief Executive Summary, Item 10(i)]

[WORTH WATCHING] Workforce is now the board's highest-rated assurance risk at 20 — above quality — and the board committee openly asked whether wider organisational pressure is surfacing through workforce indicators.
[Evidence] "The Committee reviewed the Quarter 1 BAF and supported maintaining the People and Culture risk rating at 20, recognising continued pressures relating to workforce, recruitment, sickness absence and staff experience. The Committee noted that workforce risks were currently presenting at a higher level than quality risks and considered whether organisational pressures were increasingly manifesting through workforce indicators." — p.41, [Board Committee Chairs' Reports, Item 8]

[WORTH WATCHING] Early modelling suggests well over half of the trust's band 5 nurses could be re-banded under the national "fairer deal for band 5 nurses" job evaluation — a cost the pack does not quantify.
[Evidence] "The Trust has begun a significant programme of work following the Secretary of State's commitment to ensure a fairer deal for Band 5 nurses across the NHS. This national initiative requires all organisations to review Band 5 nursing posts through the national job evaluation framework... Early modelling suggests that well over half of our Band 5 nurses may benefit from the programme." — p.47, [Chief Executive's Report]

[WORTH WATCHING] Maternity red flags: 60 incidents in Q4 (down from 113 in Q3), driven mainly by delays between admission and starting induction of labour, plus nine shifts where the labour ward coordinator could not start supernumerary.

The second most common reason reported was due to the labour ward coordinator being unable to maintain supernumerary status... there were 9 occasions when this Red Flag occurred at the start [of a shift]." — pp.212-213, [Maternity Overview Q4, Acuity & Red Flag Report, Item 11]
