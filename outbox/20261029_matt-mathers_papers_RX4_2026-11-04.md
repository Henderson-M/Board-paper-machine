## Top lines

[LEAD] CNTW is telling NHS England it will break even this year while its own "mid case" position — the one reported to the regional team — is a £6.9m deficit, and its underlying deficit has nearly doubled against plan to £15.7m.
[Evidence] "The Trust has submitted a forecast of financial break-even as part of the Month 6 national submission. The national submission forecast is based on the best-case scenario included in the regional submission" and "The forecast which supports the mid case position presented to the Regional Team is a £6.9m deficit" — p.212, [Finance Report (Month 6)]; "The plan included an underlying deficit of £8.6m at the end of 2026/27. The table below this has increased to £15.7m." — p.214, same paper. The regional worst-case model shows £14.6m ("Worst Case 14.6"), though the trust says it does "not acknowledge this level of risk so internally report a worst-case of £8.9m" — p.213. The trust is on the Northern region's "watch list" of providers (p.212), its plan requires "a reduction in whole time equivalent (WTE) of over 230 WTE" against a "Cost Improvement Plan (CIP) of £43.3m" (p.212), and the Resource and Business Assurance Committee gave only limited assurance: "An unadjusted position forecasts a gap of c.£7.3m." — p.206, [RBAC Triple A report]. [INFERENCE] The gap between the national break-even submission and the regionally reported mid-case deficit is the story: the board is being asked to "Note the limited assurance on financial delivery at this stage and the mid-case £7.3m forecast gap to break-even for the year" (p.206).

[LEAD] The longest wait at CNTW's gender dysphoria service has reached 97 months — over eight years — and NHS England has ordered a transformation plan, due 2 November, for a "substantially different" service model.
[Evidence] "The number of people awaiting assessment increased from 2,676 to 2,970 over the year and the longest wait increased from 83 to 97 months. NHS England has requested a transformation plan by 2 November 2026 for a substantially different assessment, prescribing and monitoring model." — p.50, [Quality Committee Triple A report, October 2026]. The committee flagged "significant workforce, financial, service quality and patient communication risks" (p.50).

[LEAD] CNTW says its Oxevision patient-monitoring procurement left it with "an inflexible, provider favourable contract"; it has declined to extend beyond March 2028 and is market-testing alternatives.
[Evidence] "The Committee identified significant learning from the historical transition from pilot to wider procurement, including an inflexible, provider favourable contract and the need for clearer Board-level oversight. The Trust has moved to explicit opt-in consent, has declined an extension beyond March 2028 and is market-testing alternatives." — p.50, [Quality Committee Triple A report, October 2026]. July minutes record "the contract renewal process had been paused pending further engagement" after "concerns raised by patients, carers and staff" (p.11). [INFERENCE] Oxevision's use in mental health wards is nationally contentious; a large MH trust publicly calling the contract "provider favourable" and walking away is a strong line.

[LEAD] CNTW has moved up a segment in the NHS Oversight Framework, from segment 4 to segment 3, in its Q1 2026/27 rating.
[Evidence] "The Trust has received its Quarter 1 National Oversight rating and has improved from Segment 4 to Segment 3." — p.285, [Chief Executive's Report].

[LEAD] CNTW is lobbying NHSE to stop the "Deconstructing the Blocks" pricing exercise being used in 2027/28 planning, saying the process has been "widely problematic nationally" with some organisations "reportedly refusing to participate".
[Evidence] "the process has been widely problematic nationally, with some organisation's reportedly refusing to participate. A key issue highlighted was the use of an outdated 'deflator' applied to pricing" and trust leads have "supported meetings with regional and national teams to resist this being used in 27/28 planning" — pp.207-208, [RBAC Triple A report]. [INFERENCE] A national story candidate: a provider committee putting on record that trusts are refusing to take part in an NHSE costing exercise.

[WORTH WATCHING] CNTW says the national target of 50 per cent of children's mental health referrals getting help within four weeks by March 2027 is "forecasting not achievable" — it is currently at 12 per cent, with 1,505 children waiting over two years.
[Evidence] "12% of Children and Young People's mental-health related referral spells received help within four weeks. The NHSE target of 50% of CYPS referral spells receiving help within four weeks by March 2027 is forecasting not achievable" and "1,505 spells, or 38.0%, had waited more than 104 weeks" — p.24, [Q2 Performance Report]. The trust says it is "on track to eliminate" the 104-week waits by March 2027 (p.24).
