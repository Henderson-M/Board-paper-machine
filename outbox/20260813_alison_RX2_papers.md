## Annual reports — deep read (added 2026-08-10, second pass)

Three annual reports were published in this pack and are analysed in full below. All three are image-only in the source and were read by page rendering.

### 9.1d — Learning from Mortality Deaths Annual Report 2025/26 (pp.150–166)

[LEAD] Sussex Partnership cannot evidence whether it is reducing premature death among people with serious mental illness, learning disability or autism — the trust says it has no reliable baseline, and its own board paper rates it only "partially assured".
[Evidence] "The Trust is partially assured in relation to learning from mortality deaths, with identified gaps in accessibility of system wide data, family and carer involvement and triangulation with wider safety intelligence." "Data limitations continue to constrain assurance. Current restrictions in system-wide mortality data, data-sharing arrangements and the recent Electronic Patient Record (EPR) transition mean the Trust cannot yet provide complete population-level analysis, establish a reliable baseline, or evidence sustained reductions in avoidable or premature mortality." The stated strategic focus "remains reducing premature and avoidable mortality for people with Serious Mental Illness, Learning Disability and Autism". — p.150, [Learning from Mortality Deaths Annual Report 2025/26, Item 9.1d]

[LEAD] The trust recorded 2,300 deaths of people in its care in 2025/26, of which 139 met the criteria for a mortality review — but only 101 reviews were completed, a 73% completion rate the report attributes to reviewer capacity.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, 2,300 deaths were recorded for individuals known to SPFT who were receiving care from Trust services, or who had been discharged from those services within the preceding six months. This was consistent with number of deaths recorded in 2024/25. 139 deaths met the criteria for a mortality review, equating to 6% of deaths recorded in the year." "Of the 139 deaths requiring mortality review, 101 reviews were completed by year end, giving a completion position of 73%. Completion was below 100% due to capacity within the mortality review function". Outstanding reviews "will be recovered through the 2026/27 mortality programme, with cases prioritised by risk and age". — p.155

[LEAD] Basic mortality data the trust is expected to monitor is simply unavailable to it, including age-at-death for its own serious mental illness and learning disability registers.
[Evidence] "There remain significant data-quality and information-governance constraints affecting mortality reporting. The Sussex Integrated Dataset (SID) does not currently provide sufficiently reliable mortality data for this purpose." Not available for routine reporting: "Total number of deaths across Sussex for 2025/26"; "Comparative population ethnicity data and ethnicity data for people who died"; "Crude age-at-death analysis for people on the Serious Mental Illness and Learning Disability registers"; "Analysis of primary prevention factors, including the proportion of deaths where smoking was a contributing factor." The chief medical officer "has requested NHS England support to enable SPFT to access real-time mortality surveillance data." — p.154–155

[LEAD] Just eight LeDeR reviews were carried out in the year, and none at all for autistic people.
[Evidence] "In the year April 2025 - March 2026 eight LeDeR reviews were undertaken" — Learning Disability 8, **Autism 0, Learning Disability and Autism 0**, total 8. This is against a policy in which "All deaths of people with Autism" is a mandatory review category. — p.156, p.160

[LEAD] A February 2026 electronic patient record changeover cost the trust two months of its own life-expectancy data.
[Evidence] "Due to an Electronic Patient Record (EPR) system change implemented in February 2026, the required data was not available for the full reporting period; therefore, this analysis covers April 2025 to January 2026. The excluded data can be reviewed when available." — p.156

[WORTH WATCHING] More than nine in ten deaths of people with a learning disability known to the trust were of people under 80.
[Evidence] Graph 2, "Average age of deaths across Sussex 25/26 April 2025-Jan 2026": Learning Disability **92.59% under 80 / 7.41% over 80**, against Brighton and Hove 54.79%/45.21%, East Sussex 41.72%/58.28% and West Sussex 34.05%/65.95%. "in Brighton and Hove there were a total of 261 deaths of people known or open to SPFT, (118 over 80 and 143 under 80)". — p.156

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust's thematic review covered only 31 cases and concluded the main learning is systemic, not clinical.
[Evidence] "A thematic review of 31 mortality cases (15 non-learning disability; 16 learning disability/autism) identified that the most significant learning relates to system and process issues rather than isolated clinical factors. The principal themes and consistent themes since mortality reviews have been undertaken on the trust relate to quality of documentation (including timely entries) adherence to care pathways, and coordination across services." Non-LD deaths were "concentrated in mid-life (45–64 years), indicating a younger mortality profile"; LD/autism themes were "epilepsy, antipsychotic use, infection and dysphagia". — p.158

[WORTH WATCHING] The trust delivered 3,136 physical health checks against a 4,000 ambition — 78%.
[Evidence] "during 2025/26, the Trust completed 3,136 physical health checks against the 4,000 ambition, achieving 78% compliance." — p.162

### 9.1c — Health and Safety Annual Report 2025/26 (pp.121–149)

[LEAD] First aid is the single area where Sussex Partnership has **no** assurance against its health and safety standards, with wards lacking sufficient on-duty first aiders.
[Evidence] "The Trust has assessed compliance against 29 Health and Safety standards. Of these, the Trust is fully assured in 21, partially assured in 7, and has no assurance in 1. The principal area of no assurance relates to First Aid compliance." "First Aid compliance remains the only area of no assurance and requires urgent recovery oversight." The 2026/27 action requires the trust to "agree recovery actions for areas without sufficient on-duty First Aiders" by October 2026. — p.121–122, [Health and Safety Annual Report 2025/26, Item 9.1c]

[LEAD] Violence, abuse and hate crime are the highest staff-reported incident themes, and support for staff afterwards is inconsistent.
[Evidence] "Violence, abuse and hate crime remain the highest staff-reported incident themes, with inconsistent support arrangements before, during and after incidents." Training compliance "remains below target in PMVA, First Aid, ILS/BLS and Moving and Handling Level 2." — p.122

[WORTH WATCHING] Seven standards are only partially assured, including ligature management, violence and aggression, and medical devices; oxygen storage and DSEAR arrangements are not standardised across the trust.
[Evidence] Partial assurance on 2A.4 Health and Safety Audit Programme, 2A.7 Training, 2E Musculoskeletal disorders/manual handling, 2F Electric Profiling Beds, 2G Violence and Aggression and Challenging Behaviours, 2L Work Equipment and 2M.4 Ligature Management. "Medical device governance requires stronger attendance, wider membership, completion of self-audits and clearer arrangements for flat lifting equipment. DSEAR and oxygen storage risk assessment arrangements are not yet standardised across the Trust. Ligature assessment practice requires continued standardisation as the new CQC environmental ligature tool is embedded." — p.121–122

[WORTH WATCHING] No inpatient ligature-related deaths were reported in the year.
[Evidence] "No inpatient ligature-related deaths were reported." Listed among key achievements alongside rollout of "The CQC environmental ligature tool". — p.122

### 9.1e — Mixed Sex Accommodation Annual Declaration and Sexual Safety Report 2025/26 (pp.167–176)

[LEAD] Internal single-sex accommodation breaches at Sussex Partnership have risen for a third consecutive year, to 176, with two wards accounting for almost half.
[Evidence] "In the year there were 176 internal single sex accommodation breaches (deemed as breach in single sex corridors on MSA wards), compared to 174 breaches in 24/25 and 146 breaches in 23/24. Rowan and Larch Wards in West Sussex accounted for 49% of all 2025/26 MSA breaches." "Chief Nursing Officer has requested assurance from Divisional Directors of Nursing and Quality about breaches in internal mix sex accommodation." No breaches were reported externally: "In the year there were zero externally reported breaches to NHSE." — p.167, [Mixed Sex Accommodation Annual Declaration and Sexual Safety Report 2025/26, Item 9.1e]

[LEAD] Two male-only wards generated the most sexual safety incidents in the trust, and female staff are disproportionately the targets.
[Evidence] "the top ward for reporting sexual safety is Regency and Thyme wards which are male only wards. These incidents relate to a small number of individuals on Regency (three patients) and Thyme (one patient). This indicates that the higher reporting volume is driven by repeat incidents rather than a widespread issue." "Female staff are more frequently targeted in patient-on-staff incidents on male only wards. This reflects behavioural symptoms such as disinhibition and boundary impairment in acute settings, rather than a gender-driven risk alone". Regency and Thyme recorded 15 incidents each. — p.174

[WORTH WATCHING] Reported inpatient sexual safety incidents fell 36% year on year, to 79.
[Evidence] "In 2025/26, there were 79 inpatient sexual safety incidents reported, compared to 124 reported in 24/25, representing a 36% decrease in reported incidents." "Out of 79 incidents reported, 49 were no harm and 30 low harm." The report notes "There are notable spikes in certain months, which may warrant further investigation into specific causes or locations." — p.172, p.174. [INFERENCE] The report treats the fall as a reduction in incidents; it does not address whether reporting behaviour changed, and it separately says 2025/26 is characterised by "Clearer categorisation of sexual safety incidents" and "More explicit recognition of: Staff as victims" — which could cut either way.

[WORTH WATCHING] A ward has been closed since December 2025 and the trust has reconfigured others, opening a new female-only ward.
[Evidence] "Chalkhill Mixed sex (West) - temporary closed since December 2025 (All Ensuite Bedrooms)". "In the year several wards were reconfigured; Caburn was repurposed as a mixed sex ward and a new female only ward (Palmeira) opened in Brighton and Hove." — p.167, p.169

[WORTH WATCHING] New national mixed-sex accommodation guidelines are due this month, which will change how justifiable breaches are determined.
[Evidence] "New national MSA guidelines are expected to be published in August 26, to support determining justifiable and non-justifiable breaches. Once published the MSA policy and associated procedures will be reflected." — p.167

### Additional top lines

[LEAD] Sussex Partnership's own safe staffing review found several inpatient wards had baseline establishments set below safe staffing levels, with wards kept safe only by temporary staff and extra shifts.
[Evidence] "The review identified several wards where baseline establishment templates were below safe staffing levels. However, fill rates and additional shifts indicate that wards were operating at a safe level. In some cases, baseline establishment templates have been amended to reflect the findings. In other areas further work is required to determine the sustainable establishment." — p.135, [Annual Safe Staffing Establishment Report 2025/26, Item 9.1b]. And: "Unmitigated, this would create an operational and clinical risk on the wards. The position has been mitigated in-year with temporary staffing, particularly HCSWs at night, as evidenced through fill rates being over 100%." — p.136.

[LEAD] Four forensic wards and the Selden learning disabilities unit ran with a single registered mental health nurse overnight until July 2026, when establishments were doubled to two.
[Evidence] "Initial action focused on areas where triangulated evidence identified the greatest staffing risk, particularly services operating with single RMN cover overnight, high levels of patient dependency, or increasing service complexity. As a result, substantive overnight RMN establishments were increased from one to two RMNs on four forensic wards (Hazel, Pine, Ash and Elm, now Cedar) and on the Selden Learning Disabilities Unit, with changes implemented from July 2026." — p.136, [Annual Safe Staffing Establishment Report 2025/26, Item 9.1b]

[LEAD] The trust's own benchmarking puts it bottom of a seven-trust mental health peer group on care hours per patient day — 9.14 overall and 2.97 registered nurse hours — while running the highest temporary-staffing fill rates in its sample.
[Evidence] "Peer benchmarking (January 2026) shows that SPFT's overall Care Hours Per Patient Day (CHPPD) remains below that of comparable mental health trusts, particularly within the registered nurse workforce. The Trust demonstrates a comparatively higher reliance on HCSW than some peer organisations. These findings are consistent with national reference cost benchmarking, which indicates that SPFT's cost per occupied bed day remains below the national average." — p.135. The table gives Sussex Partnership 9.14 CHPPD overall / 2.97 registered nurses / 5.81 HCSW, against Berkshire Healthcare 13.39, Oxford Health 13.39, Avon and Wiltshire 12.37, Surrey and Borders 12.08, Kent and Medway 10, Dorset Healthcare 9.97 — p.135. On fill rates: "Though a small sample, it does show SPFT as having the highest fill rate – particularly in healthcare support workers at night" — Sussex Partnership 136% HCSW night fill, 112% overall average — p.135.

[WORTH WATCHING] The psychiatric intensive care unit at Langley Green is staffed 6.4 WTE below its own recommended establishment.
[Evidence] Amber ward (Langley Green Hospital, West Sussex, 12-bed mixed PICU): "Recommended Whole FTE 44.6 / Actual Whole FTE 38.21" — p.126, [Annual Safe Staffing Establishment Report 2025/26, Item 9.1b]. The comparator Pavilion ward at Millview, Brighton (10-bed male PICU) is above recommendation at 64.79 actual against 40.5 recommended.

[WORTH WATCHING] The annual establishment review presented to this board covers Q3 2025/26 — the previous one was reported in April 2025 — and the trust says it will only be "fully compliant" with National Quality Board and Delivering Workforce Safeguards expectations after the December 2026 board.
[Evidence] "The staffing establishment review was last reported to the Board in April 2025 and considered the findings of the November 2024 Mental Health Optimal Staffing Tool (MHOST) review." — p.123. "The expectation is that the Annual Safe Staffing Establishment review will commence in September 2026 starting with the MHOST data collection, with professional judgement review meetings in October 2026 and a completed report presented to QC in the November 2026 and presented at the December 2026 Trust Board. This will ensure that we are now fully compliant with the National Quality Board and DWS expectations for monitoring and setting annual establishments." — p.137. The report records that the improvement programme followed a July 2025 Delivering Workforce Safeguards gap analysis undertaken "supported by NHS England oversight" — p.121.
