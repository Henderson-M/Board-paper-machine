## Top lines

[LEAD] NHS England has formally asked for Thames Valley ICB's chief executive Nick Broughton to spend four days a week on his national secondment, leaving a minimum of one day a week for the ICB, and the ICB has created an interim deputy chief executive post for a year to cover.
[Evidence] "The Committee noted that NHS England has formally requested an increase in the secondment commitment of the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of NHS Thames Valley Integrated Care Board to four days per week, to support delivery of national priorities. The CEO would retain overall accountability for the ICB, with a minimum of 20% dedicated time to provide strategic leadership, governance and system oversight. To support executive leadership capacity during this period, the Committee approved the establishment of an interim Deputy Chief Executive Officer role, for 1 year from 1 October 2026. The Committee approved the appointment of Sam Burrows to the role on an interim basis" – Item 14, [Remuneration & Culture Committee AAA report, 11 September 2026 via correspondence]. The declarations register lists Broughton's NHS England role as "National Priority Programme Director for Mental Health, Learning Disabilities, and Neurodevelopmental conditions (part time seconded role)" – Item 2, [ICB Board Declarations of Interest Register v 9 Nov]. The committee also "agreed the proposed executive leadership and operating model for the duration of the extension to the CEO secondment arrangements" (6 October). The pack does not say when the secondment ends.

[LEAD] NHS England's mid-year review letter identifies an £18.6m run-rate gap at the ICB, and the chief finance officer describes the position as "highly challenged" with a financial recovery plan being drawn up.
[Evidence] "NHS England's letter of 2 November 2026 following the 6 October mid-year review recognised the ICB's commitment to its 2026/27 financial plan and recovery work. It highlighted an £18.6m run-rate gap, subject to other assumptions holding, mainly across all-age continuing care, prescribing and neurodiversity right-to-choose pathways, alongside operational delivery and winter preparedness risks. Agreed actions cover quantified financial and continuing care recovery plans; outstanding contracts, settlements, reconciliation and Frimley disaggregation arrangements; elective recovery impact, particularly at Oxford University Hospitals; Royal Berkshire urgent care demand; and winter discharge and flow" – p.8 of 10, Item 4, [Chief Executive and Chief Officers' Report]. CFO section: "The financial situation in the current year is highly challenged and immediate financial recovery actions are underway, with a fuller financial recovery plan being drawn up for implementation... not all of those actions will have immediate impact and not all of the pressures are within the ICB's direct control" – p.3 of 10. The planned workstreams include "benchmarking, contract and recent investment reviews, decommissioning, grants, joint funding and block deconstruction" – p.4 of 10. The letter itself is not in the pack ("A copy of the letter is available in the reading room").

[LEAD] The ICB is still forecasting break-even at month 6, but only because cost pressures "have been fully mitigated by non-recurrent benefits", and its own run-rate analysis shows a £14.2m deficit before further mitigations.
[Evidence] "The cost pressures at Month 6 have been fully mitigated by non-recurrent benefits" – p.2 of 3, Item 5, [Finance Report cover sheet]. Forecast outturn adverse variances listed: All Age Complex and Continuing Care "YTD adverse £8.6m and FOT £14.5m adverse"; Mental Health, LD, CYP and Maternity "YTD adverse £5.6m and £8.4m FOT"; Primary Care Prescribing "YTD adverse £3.7m and FOT £7.2m adverse"; UEC, Community and LTC "£3.7m YTD and £3.6m FOT" – pp.1-2 of 3. Run rate: "After reflecting delegated reserves, phasing adjustments, undelivered efficiencies and cost pressures, the position moves to a £14.2m deficit. This gap is expected to be closed through AMPs, further CIPs and mitigating actions, resulting in a break-even forecast outturn" – [Financial Performance Report Month 06, Run Rate Analysis slide]. On savings: "Current forecasts indicate a realistic year-end delivery of £60.0m, reducing to £48.7m under the current risk assessed worst case forecast" against a £65.0m CIP target – [Month 6 Efficiency Plan Delivery Summary slide]. Committee view: "Increased financial rigour has been introduced and urgent mitigations will be implemented, but year-end position remains at risk" – Item 14, [Quality, Performance and Finance Committee AAA report, 22 October 2026]. Check before use: the cover sheet says the £2.9m CIP adverse variance is "a improvement of £1.1m from the £0.5m adverse variance reported at Month 5", which is internally inconsistent.
