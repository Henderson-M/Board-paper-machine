## Top lines

[LEAD] The trust is £2.2m in deficit at month 5 against a break-even plan; the chief executive blames the unfunded pay award, operational workforce pressures and the adult secure provider collaborative, and concedes it will be "challenging" to hold on to its segment 1 NHS Oversight Framework rating — under which any trust in deficit is barred from segments 1 and 2.
[Evidence] "The Trust's financial position remains challenging with a deficit of £2.2m recorded at the end of month 5. The main contributory factors to the deficit are the unfunded pay increase, workforce pressures in operational services and the adult secure provider collaborative. Further grip and oversight are taking place to drive a reduction in our run rate without adversely impacting on service quality." — p.30, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 9]; "It is recognised, particularly given the financial pressures we face, that it will be challenging to maintain this rating." — p.29, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 9]; "Any organisation determined to be in financial deficit is automatically prevented from being allocated to segment 1 or segment 2 until the deficit has been satisfactorily resolved." — p.162, [NHS Oversight Framework 2026/27, Item 12.7]; The Brief (August) shows the July YTD deficit of £2.0m "in line with plan" with a full-year forecast of "£0m" and the bank-spend forecast "updated to amber, at £0.6m above target" — p.46, [Chief Executive's Report, Item 9 — appended staff briefing "The Brief"]. NHSE has separately told the trust it is "lower risk": "our Trust has been identified as lower risk and therefore we will not require a face-to-face mid-year review with the NHSE regional leadership team." — p.29. [INFERENCE] The July board minutes in this pack record the CEO proposing "a revised financial delivery plan be developed with a revised trajectory aimed at returning the Trust to a break-even position" (p.14) — i.e. the original trajectory is no longer being relied on; the month 5 IPR that would show the forecast is not in the public pack.

[LEAD] Out-of-area placements in the West Yorkshire adult secure collaborative (which SWYPFT co-ordinates) have hit 57 — the highest since 2022 — with 24 men out of area purely because there is no bed at Newton Lodge; the collaborative is forecasting a £1.2m deficit.
[Evidence] "The overall number of OOA cases increased to 57 and has remained at this level for the last 5 months. This is the highest number since 2022, whilst some cases are within their natural clinical flow, some are OOA purely due to no capacity in West Yorkshire. 24 men are currently OOA due to no bed availability within Newton Lodge (medium Secure)." — p.182, [Specialised Provider Collaboratives and Alliances, Item 13.3]; "The WY PC continue to experience the highest number of patients cared for in secure services, at 275 since the PC went live. Also: a new executive oversight group has been set up "in the context of the potential lead provider contract and associated KPI indicated by NHSE post March 2027" — p.182 (policy signal: NHSE moving Yorkshire secure services towards a lead-provider contract from April 2027).

[LEAD] The South Yorkshire and Bassetlaw adult secure collaborative (also SWYPFT-led) is £1.5m in deficit at month 5 with a forecast pressure of "over £2m", driven by inpatient demand that is nine patients above plan and "one of the highest levels of occupancy seen since go live".
[Evidence] "There has been an increase in the SYB inpatient forensic population for the reporting period (+10) with 163 patients in inpatient treatment in total, this is 9 patients higher than planning estimated and one of the highest levels of occupancy seen since go live.
