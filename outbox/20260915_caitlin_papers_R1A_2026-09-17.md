## Top lines

[LEAD] The trust's own papers say most leavers from its children's neurodiversity service are going to work for the independent and Right to Choose providers doing the same assessments, and that it has lost the clinician who was due to train the extra prescribers the ICB had just funded.
[Evidence] "although the ICB have committed to non-recurrent investment into ISP outsourcing (approx. 1000 CYP split between ASD and ADHD assessment in 2026/27) and an additional 2 substantive Non-Medical Prescribing posts, the service remains fragile. To date ISP providers have not been selected, and ND staffing is reduced significantly with the majority of leavers choosing to take up employment with ISP and RtC providers for ND. Of particular concern is that one of the leavers is the identified staff member to train the additional NMP trainees, meaning that the service is currently unable to commit to recruiting into these roles." – p.34, [Enc E, Performance Report, Physical Healthcare division executive summary, July 2026]

[LEAD] The trust tells its board the neurodiversity service is completing about 30 autism assessments a month against about 120 new referrals, and has "planned temporary waiting-list controls/restricted service arrangements".
[Evidence] "Neurodiversity service capacity is under significant pressure: approximately 30 autism assessments can be completed each month against around 120 new referrals. The neurodiversity service have planned temporary waiting-list controls/restricted service arrangements while the backlog and caseload mix are reviewed. An ICB quality summit and mid-September workshop with local providers will consider workforce solutions, including a possible training academy, workforce sharing and alternative skill mix of staff." – p.3, [Enc M, People and Culture Committee Report, meeting of 2 September 2026]
[Note on the ICB account] The Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB pack for 16 September says this trust's children's neurodivergence service is "temporarily suspended from 14 September 2026" (and elsewhere that the ADHD assessment service is suspended from 7 September for four weeks). **The trust's own 17 September pack does not say that.** The words "suspend", "suspended" and "suspension" do not appear anywhere in the trust pack in relation to the service; the strongest formulation the trust uses is the Enc M line quoted above, and the Enc E performance narrative (p.4) still describes the service as operating and working with the ICB on solutions. The trust pack also gives no date, no duration and no scope for any restriction. That gap between the two accounts is itself the story to put to the trust.

## FOI leads

[FOI] The RoSPA external health and safety audit report. Request the full audit report completed in May 2026, plus the resulting trust-wide action plan, from Herefordshire and Worcestershire Health and Care Trust. The annual report says the audit found failures similar to those that have led to NHS trust prosecutions but does not reproduce a single finding, and the report is explicitly held back ("This audit report will be available separately"). Also worth requesting the six fire summit workstream documents and the Fire Safety Training Needs Analysis referenced in the same paper.

[FOI] The neurodiversity "temporary waiting-list controls / restricted service arrangements". Request from the trust: the decision paper, equality impact assessment, clinical risk assessment and communications to families covering any restriction, pause or suspension of the children's neurodiversity or ADHD assessment service from August 2026 onwards, plus the start date and planned duration. The trust pack refers to planned controls but gives no scope or date, while the ICB pack for 16 September describes a suspension. Request the same from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB, including the papers and minutes of the ICB quality summit and the mid-September workshop with local providers referred to at p.3 of Enc M.

[FOI] The NHS England exchange on 104-week Category D clock stops. Request from the trust and from NHS England Midlands all correspondence, meeting notes and guidance about stopping the waiting-time clock for Category D children and young people's mental health spells where the wait is caused by an ADHD or autism referral. The trust says advice from NHSE "is required" and a meeting is scheduled. A national decision on how these waits are counted would move the reported figures at many trusts.

[FOI] Independent-sector neurodiversity assessment outsourcing and staff movement. Request from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB the specification, value, provider shortlist and award decision for the approximately 1,000 outsourced ASD and ADHD assessments for 2026/27 (the trust says "To date ISP providers have not been selected"), and from the trust the number of neurodiversity staff leaving in 2025/26 and 2026/27 to date, with exit-interview destination data. The trust asserts the majority of leavers are going to ISP and Right to Choose providers; the underlying numbers are not in the pack.

[FOI] The stroke services outline business case. Request from Herefordshire and Worcestershire ICB the Stroke services outline business case and the trust's formal response of 31 July 2026. The pack says the business case is now going to the ICB board before submission to NHS England, with feedback expected late in quarter three, but the document itself is not public. – p.6, [Enc E]; p.2, [Enc N]
