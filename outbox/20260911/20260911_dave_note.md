Note on the 10 September board paper sweep, and what it missed.

## What happened

The sweep hit the account session limit twice on 10 September. The second hit killed 16 pack-analyser agents mid-run, so 16 of the 24 packs were re-run on Sonnet rather than the intended Opus. Those 16 went out to correspondents without anyone knowing the analysis tier had changed.

## The model test

Three packs, three models, scored against the source text rather than against each other.

Substantive items found: Sonnet 15 of 23, Opus 21 of 23, Fable 20 of 23.
Factual errors: Sonnet 2, Opus 0, Fable 0.
Em dashes, where house style is none: Sonnet 91, Opus 167, Fable 0.

Recommendation is to make Fable the default analysis tier. It matched Opus on recall, made no errors, ran shorter, and was the only model that produced no em dashes without being told twice.

Sonnet is not uniformly worse. On Nottingham it alone carried the Ockenden review's "more than 2,500 cases" figure, and it went deeper on maternity generally. Its weakness is breadth on long packs: where a pack has one dominant story it holds up, where it has several it loses most of them.

## What was actually missed

I re-checked 6 of the 16 packs. Verified against the source packs:

**Gloucestershire, Joe.** A patient died of pseudomonas caught from the hospital environment in August 2022. The trust has pleaded guilty at a prosecution brought by the CQC, and the Health and Safety Executive is bringing a separate case against its subsidiary Gloucestershire Managed Services. The deceased is named in the minutes. None of this reached Joe. Separately, the CQC has raised the trust's well-led rating from requires improvement to good, and maternity at Gloucestershire Royal from inadequate to requires improvement, which was also missed, so he got a one-sidedly negative read of a trust that has improved.

**BSW group board, Joe.** A 26 June letter to Great Western signed by Jim Mackey and Dr Penny Dash over underperformance on finance and operations, which triggered an extraordinary board. Great Western has dropped into segment 4 while Bath climbed out of it. An NHS England board-to-board is set for 5 October. Also the Group's temporary staffing provider being bought by Blackstone weeks after it outsourced to them.

**Nottingham, Annabelle.** A statutory section 30 referral to the health and social care secretary over breach of the breakeven duty, quoted in the KPMG report at p.424. Plus the £80.6m deficit, a £16m cash application with suppliers facing delayed payment, a fifth consecutive hip fracture mortality alert, and a 1,176 WTE reduction.

**Leicester, Annabelle.** Moved out of segment 4, ranking 105 of 134 to 65 of 134. Kettering and Northampton submitted non-compliant operational plans and monthly cash support was being withheld. Chief nurse Julie Hogg seconded to Northamptonshire, Dr Andy Haynes interim chair.

**Berkshire, Mimi and Sheffield, Henry.** Errors rather than misses. Berkshire: the summary said the first court hearing in the bribery prosecution was held in early August, when the minutes say scheduled, and the committee met before the date. Sheffield: the summary said the trust issued a prevention of future deaths report, when Paper G says "The Trust received no Regulation 28 Reports during Q1" and the report went to NICE and the RCOG about a national guidance gap. That one is close to the opposite of what was sent.

## The pattern

Two categories get dropped repeatedly. First, statutory and regulatory triggers recorded as one line in minutes rather than headlined: prosecutions, section 30 referrals, NHS England letters, segment changes, HSE cases, Regulation 28 reports. Second, named board-level people changes. Four packs so far had an unreported chair or chief nurse change, and all three models missed a new chair at Berkshire, so that one is a gap in the analyser's prompt rather than a model failure.

Both belong in the pack-analyser skill as explicit things to hunt for. Small edit, not yet made.
